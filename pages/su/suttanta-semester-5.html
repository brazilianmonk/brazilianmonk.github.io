        • AI: vipassanā reduces self-love, attachment to aggregates, and the notion of "I", enabling monks to endure pain, sickness, famine, or attacks while upholding training rules. advanced vipassanā ñāṇa prevents breaching sīla to protect impermanent, non-self, suffering aggregates. virtue must be strengthened with insight wisdom, elevating mundane sīla to supramundane. contemplating three universal characteristics, monks weaken latent defilements, develop nibbidā (dispassion), and incline toward nibbāna. at lokuttara magga, virtuous monks eradicate defilements (led by ignorance) via supramundane sammā diṭṭhi across four stages. ñāṇa saṃvara in four magga cittas destroys defilements; three virati cetasikas in magga and phala cittas form lokuttara adhisīla, eradicating defilements and pacifying the mind, leading to arahantship. #editing
          • book: through this practice, self love and attachment toward one’s own aggregates diminish. The idea of “I” lessens, making the monk brave enough to tolerate pain and adhere to the training rules even amidst severe sickness, famine, or attacks by enemies. A monk with advanced vipassanā ñāṇa does not consider breaching the sīla to protect impermanent (anicca), non-self-aggregates (anatta) that lead to suffering (dukkha). Therefore, a monk must strengthen his virtue with insight wisdom. Finally, a monk should not be satisfied with mundane sīla but must strive to elevate it to the supramundane level. Through vipassanā, contemplating the three universal characteristics of realities, a monk weakens the latent defilements. Gradually, he develops nibbidā (dispassion) toward all conditioned realities, which is considered a powerful insight wisdom. Furthermore, he inclines toward Nibbāna. At the moment of attaining the lokuttara magga, the virtuous monk eradicates latent defilements (samuccheda pahāna) led by ignorance (avijjā) through supramundane sammā diṭṭhi on four distinct occasions. The wisdom in the four magga cittas that destroys latent defilements is called ñāṇa saṃvara. The three virati cetasikas associated with the four supramundane paths and their fruitions are the cetasika sīla and also the lokuttara adhisīla. While the three viratis in magga cittas involve in eradicating the latent defilements, the same in phala cittas engage in pacifying the mind stream. This is how a monk enriches his sīla until the attainment of Arahantship. #editing

At that moment, poison like ghee in the pot oozed from its mouth.” The female devotee thinking “It is not possible to trick this baldheaded monk” gave the sugarcane stick to him, cooked rice and offered it with salted fish, ghee and molasses.
