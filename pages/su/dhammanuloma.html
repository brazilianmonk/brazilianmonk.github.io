          • (Brasil) Ariyañāṇa Bhikkhu
          • What are the important conditions including the verbal utterance for the taking of refuge in the Triple Gem (tisaraṇāgamana) to be established?
            • The conditions to be met for the refuge (saraṇa) to be established are faith in the Triple Gem as the ultimate shelter, refuge, and assistance, faith in the genuine qualities of the Triple Gem such as faith in the enlightenment of the Buddha, faith in the Dhamma as being the truth and the path, and faith in the Saṅgha as possessed of higher virtues and as practicing the right way. The verbal utterance that follows and expresses such mental refuge is:
            • "Buddhaṁ saraṇaṁ gacchāmi
            • Dhammaṁ saranaṁ gacchāmi
            • Saṅghaṁ saraṇaṁ gacchāmi
            • Dutiyampi Buddhaṁ saraṇaṁ gacchāmi
            • Dutiyampi Dhammaṁ saranaṁ gacchāmi
            • Dutiyampi Saṅghaṁ saraṇaṁ gacchāmi
            • Tatiyampi Buddhaṁ saraṇaṁ gacchāmi
            • Tatiyampi Dhammaṁ saranaṁ gacchāmi
            • Tatiyampi Saṅghaṁ saraṇaṁ gacchāmi"
          • Describe what pollutes and what breaks the triple refuge.
            • The triple refuge is twofold as mundane (lokiyasaraṇāgamana), the refuge taken by a puthujjana, and supramundane (lokuttarasaraṇāgamana), the refuge of a Noble disciple. The mundane refuge is polluted and weakened by impure understanding of the Triple Gem and by holding wrong views about the Triple Gem. It is completely broken by rejecting the Triple Gem, by taking other teachers as refuge and by death. Of these three cases the first two are blameworthy and connected with unwholesome states while the third is blameless. The supramundane refuge of a Noble disciple is established with the first Noble Path and cannot be polluted or broken.
          • Mention the types of refuge-taking in the Buddha and describe the advantages (results) of going to the Triple Gem for refuge.
            • The taking of refuge can be classified into 4 kinds:
              • Attasannīyātana saraṇagamana (Self-relinquishment to the Triple Gem)
              • Tapparāyana saraṇagamana (verbal utterance taking refuge in the Triple Gem)
              • Sissabhāvopagamana saraṇagamana (Determination to be a disciple of the Triple Gem)
              • Paṇipātana saraṇagamanaṁ (Prostration accepting the Triple Gem as the greatest refuge)
            • The puthujjana who goes to the Triple Gem for refuge accrues as result worldly benefits such as possessions, good destinations and the support for supramundane attainments. Supramundane refuge on its turn has supramundane dhammas (paths, fruits and Nibbāna) as benefit.
          • How do you explain and define the Gem of the Dhamma according to the Theravāda standpoint?
            • The Gem of the Dhamma is the body of teachings expounded by the Buddha, the Truths, the aggregates, the faculties, the enlightenment factors, the inspiring accounts, exhortations, examples, the monastic discipline, and so on. The Dhamma is also the mundane and supramundane paths, the three trainings consisting of morality, concentration and wisdom in their mundane and supramundane manifestations. Above all Dhamma is the goal, the cessation of suffering, Nibbāna.
          • Write and essay on behaving towards the Triple Gem as a part of inner development.
            • The good person possessed of merit who approaches the Triple Gem respectfully, listens carefully and attentively to its message, investigates its meaning, establishes confidence in it and takes the triple refuge should cultivate such association often. This good person should repeatedly reflect about such truths as the Four Noble Truths, ultimate dhammas, the triple training, and so on, and should approach well versed bhikkhus respectfully and pose them intelligent questions. This person should as well associate the Triple Gem bodily and verbally by visiting holy places, supporting the Saṅgha with the necessities of their practice, making offerings of material things and offerings of Dhamma by printing Dhamma books, organizing Dhamma talks and study groups, and by performing such activities connected with what is wholesome and conducive to the Path. While doing so the good person should do it faithfully and respectfully by rising to greet venerables, paying respect, paying close attention to Dhamma discussions in a respectful posture, wearing suitable attire avoiding covering the head and wearing shoes, and by performing such good bodily and verbal deeds which are the natural practice of wise individuals. This good person should furthermore put the teaching into practice by diligently developing samatha objects such as mindfulness of breathing, kasiṇas, foulness meditation, four elements meditation and so on, by suppressing the hindrances, dwelling in the jhānas, discerning ultimate dhammas, their specific and general characteristics, and by penetrating the Noble Truths with insight cutting off defilements.
            • With such respect, with such faith, with such practices one would surely achieve growth and fruition in their path of inner development as a Noble disciple.

          • Musāvāda
            • definition
              • intentionally misrepresenting the truth by body, speech or by ordering another and being understood by the listener.
            • 4 sambhāra
              • atathaṃ vatthu: Being a false information according to what one perceives to be the truth
              • visaṃvādanacittaṃ: Intention of deceiving the other person
              • tajjo vāyāmo: Effort (bodily, verbal, by giving a command) to convey what is not the truth to the other person.
                • When ordering another if the messenger knows it is a lie both lied, if the messenger does not know it is a lie only the one who ordered lied.
                • in the case of āṇattika (by ordering another) if the messenger does not know it is a lie he does not commit this kamma, only the sender. If the messenger knows it is a lie he also commits the kamma together with the sender
              • parassatadatthavijānanaṃ: The listener accepts the conveyed meaning
                • According to Ven. Janakābhivaṁsa (Myanmar) if the listener understands but is not deceived (knows it is a lie) the precept is broken but the kammapatha is not complete.
            • Factors for being appasāvajja or mahāsāvajja:
              • virtue of the being deceived
              • means the method used for conveying the lie
              • intensity of the defilements involved
              • purpose
                • lies uttered for fun, to avoid parting with something one owns, to lull children and to hide wrong deeds are less blameworthy;
                • making false promises, uttering lies that cause injustice, loss or harm to others are more blameworthy

              • mouth ailments: mute, have bad breath, deformed teeth, teeth fall early, mouth ailments, disfigurements, unpleasant voice

        • Micchādiṭṭhi (wrong view)
          • definition as kammapatha→holding on with strong conviction to a wrong idea that rejects mainly kamma and vipāka (including past and future lives, merit and demerit, hell and heavens and nibbāna) - ahetukadiṭṭhi, akiriyadiṭṭhi or natthikadiṭṭhi
          • 2 sambhāra
            • vatthuno ca gahitākāraviparītatā (holding on to a distorted view about such matters as kamma and kammavipāka)
            • yathā ca taṃ gaṇhāti tathābhāvena tassupaṭṭhānaṃ (strong conviction in such wrong view after much consideration)
          • 3 levels of micchādiṭṭhi according to what they hinder (āvaraṇa)
            • saggāvaraṇaṁ maggāvaraṇaṁ ca (it hinders being reborn in the heavens and attaining the path): ahetukadiṭṭhi, akiriyādiṭṭhi and natthikadiṭṭhi
            • na saggāvaraṇaṁ maggāvaraṇaṁ (it doesn't hinder heaven but hinders the path): antaggāhikadiṭṭhi (sassata / uccheda)
            • nevasaggāvaraṇanamaggāvaraṇa (it neither hinders heaven nor the path): sakkāyadiṭṭhi
          • 3 views which are akusalakammapatha
            • ahetukadiṭṭhi (uncausedness): no cause for the defilement or purification of beings, kusala and akusala do not lead to good and bad results respectively; beings go through saṁsāra a certain period (84.000 kappas) and end suffering without a cause. (expounded by Makkhali Gosala)
            • akiriyadiṭṭhi (inefficacy of action): no demerit accrues by doing bad deeds, no merit accrues by doing good deeds (expressed by Pūraṇa Kassapa)
            • natthikadiṭṭhi (anihilation): no consequences of kusala or akusala kamma; no living being is reborn after death, no threefold training (expressed by Ajitakesakambala)
          • sutta related to the topic: apannakapaṭipadā
          • Pāḷi about micchādiṭṭhi as mahāsāvajja: "nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ mahāsāvajjaṃ yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, micchādiṭṭhi. micchādiṭṭhiparamāni, bhikkhave, mahāsāvajjānī"ti This is said with regard to the 3 niyatamicchādiṭṭhis: ahetuka, akiriya and natthikadiṭṭhi
          • some vipākas and adīnavas of micchādiṭṭhi
            • sugati and nibbāna is not achievable and only rebirth in the 4 apāya can occur
            • based on such view one commits many unwholesome kammas
            • these views result in consequences that may be even worse than anantariyakamma (heinous crime) since ānantariyakamma leads to rebirth in hell until the end of the kappa but niyatāmicchādiṭṭhi may go even beyond that.
          • The 3 views which are akusalakammapatha are also called niyatāmicchādiṭṭhi since they certainly lead to rebirth in apāya.
          • "akalyāṇajanaṃ, āsīvisamivoragaṃ, tasmā ārakā parivajjeyya, bhūtikāmo vicakkhaṇo"ti.
        • surāmerayapānaṃ (using intoxicants)
          • definition→intentionally consuming any substance that causes intoxication. Intoxication here is a mental state of pride, delusion and heedlessness
          • 4 sambhāra

          • vipāka and adīnavas of surāmerayapānaṁ
            • rebirth in 4 apāya (when associated with other kammapathas)
            • if born as a human one is ummattaka (crazy)
            • this is a anekantika (not definite) kamma that may or may not cause rebirth. It produces rebirth when associated with other kammapathas (example: drinking before, while or after killing - this drinking can cause rebirth)
