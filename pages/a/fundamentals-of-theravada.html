        • what are hīnatthas?

          • Lesson 12 - The eight wholesome consciousnesses of the sensuous sphere - Questions
            • by (Brasil) Ariyañāṇa Bhikkhu
            • What is wholesome consciousness?
              • It is a citta which has the capacity to produce good results in the future. It also associates the wholesome roots alobha, adosa and occasionally amoha. It tranquilizes the mental stream suppressing defilements. It also produces higher quality materiality which supports physical health.
            • According to what matters are the wholesome consciousnesses of the sensuous sphere divided.
              • They are divided according to feeling (somanassa or upekkhāsahagata), sampayoga (ñāṇasampayutta or ñāṇavipayutta) and according to saṅkhāra (asaṅkhārika or sasaṅkhārika).
            • What is the other name for the wholesome consciousness of the sense sphere?
              • They are collectively called mahākusalacittas because they are the kusalacittas that arise most frequently in beings.
            • What are the factors that make wholesome consciousness stronger?
              • The greatness of a kusalacitta varies according to vedanā (somanassa being superior), saṅkhāra (asaṅkhārika being superior), cetanā (stronger cetanā being superior) and above all, the association with kammassakatañāṇa (ñāṇasampayutta).
            • How does the wholesome action of the sensuous sphere vary with volition?
              • The intensity of the volition affects the strength of the citta but they can also be divided according to which of the ten bases of merit volition is directed towards: dāna, sīla, bhāvanā, apacāyana, veyyāvacca, pattidāna, pattānumodanā, dhammassavaṇa, dhammadesanā and diṭṭhujukamma. When divided thus the mahākusalas amount to 80.
            • How is the strength of a wholesome action measured? Explain based on volition and knowledge.
              • Volition: the stronger the volition the stronger is the citta and the merit accumulated. A weaker volition arises when engaging in one of the ten bases of merit such as dāna with weak intent such as when giving little when one could give plenty. A strong volition arises when giving plenty.
              • Knowledge: knowledge meant here is the knowledge of kamma and its result. When engaging in meritorious acts such as undertaking sīla knowledge is not present if one does not know his act of undertaking precepts produces good future results. If done so with this understanding (right view) the merit is much superior. This is the most important factor in determining the strength of a wholesome deed.
