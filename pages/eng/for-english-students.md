This page collects the material that is useful for **students** of the IIT English Language Course: how the course works (outline and grading), how writing is graded, which words to avoid, what to write at each level, and how vocabulary is studied.

## Course Outline

The course takes students from the basics (A1) to an advanced level (C1), following the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR), with emphasis on grammar, vocabulary, and writing.

### Foundational Course (3 years / 6 semesters)

-   **Target Students**: IIT English Medium Students
-   **Duration**: 3 years (6 semesters)
-   **Goal**: learning the English grammar and vocabulary according to the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFRL) emphasizing on grammar, vocabulary, and writing skills
-   **Grammar Source**:
    -   A1--B2: [Test-English.com](https://test-english.com/)
    -   [Grammar summaries](https://test-english.com/grammar-points/contents/)
-   **Vocabulary Source**: [Oxford Learner's Dictionaries word lists](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/wordlists/oxford3000-5000)
    -   American Oxford 3.000 by CEFR level
    -   American Oxford 5.000 by CEFR level
    -   Oxford Phrase List 750 phrases
    -   OPAL: 2420 words/phrases (~150/week)
        -   Written Single words: 1200 (8 weeks)
        -   Written Phrases: 370 (2 weeks)
        -   Spoken Single Words: 600 (4 weeks)
        -   Spoken Phrases: 250 (2 weeks)
-   **Listening Source**: Audiobooks: 3 h / week

### Advanced Course (2 years)

-   **Target Students**: students who have completed the Foundational Course and aspire for mastery of the language, for teaching positions in IIT or outside, for studying, teaching, or living abroad in English speaking countries, etc.
-   **Duration**: 2 years
-   **Status**: still being designed -- the outline below is provisional.
-   **Goal**: overall practice and application of the knowledge acquired in the Foundational Course, in various ways, to help students master different skills.

The Advanced Course makes the student practice and apply their knowledge in various ways, e.g.:

-   **Teaching** English Language
-   **Writing**: academic writings and narratives
-   **Speaking**: developing speaking skills (public speaking)
-   **Video essays**
-   **Review** of weak grammar points, etc.

Besides the common practice, the Advanced Course gives room for each student to emphasize the skills he wishes to develop more: some may prefer writing, others public speaking.

## Grading Method

The grading system rewards:

- **Participation**: graded based on the attendance, attitude, and homeworks. This is to encourage and reward continuous effort and dedication, not only for a test.
- **Academic Results**: graded based on tests and final exams. This is to encourage and reward knowledge acquisition and content mastery.
- **Beyond-expected Results**: Bonuses can be given for exceptional, beyond-expected results, such as perfect attendance and so on. This is to encourage and reward going beyond what is expected.

- **40 % Participation**
  - Attendance X Attitude: Sick and duty days are not counted
  - Homeworks
  - Listening Homeworks
- **60 % Academic Results**
  - Tests During Semester
	- Vocabulary Test
	- Writing Tests
	- Speaking Tests
	- Listening Comprehension Tests
  - Final Exam
- **0-5% Bonus**
  - Perfect Attendance
  - Perfect Test Scores, etc.

The **ideal passing grade** is a 70% Global Total. If you do not reach it, see the [Repeat-Protocol](#repeat-protocol) below.

Writing is graded with a rubric: see [Writing Grading System](#writing-grading-system). Per-component weights may vary per student according to join date and attendance frequency (e.g. late joiners: mid-term 30% + final 70%).

## Repeat-Protocol

If a student does not reach the ideal passing grade (70% Global Total):

-   **Repeat the level** if a teacher is available.
-   **Remedial Lessons** based on the weak lessons:
    -   Weak students **teach** the lessons they failed, and other weak students attend.
    -   Teaching a topic is one of the best ways to consolidate it.
-   **Retake the exams** after the remedial lessons.

## Writing

### Serious Writing Mistakes

These mistakes are heavily penalized in all writing. The full list with explanations lives [**here**](/summaries/eng/serious-writing-mistakes).

-   **Incomplete Sentence**: main clause left incomplete.
    -   Wrong: *The man, whom I met yesterday.*
    -   Correct: *The man, whom I met yesterday, is called Robert.*
-   **Comma Splices**: joining two independent clauses with only a comma.
    -   Wrong: *I am a monk, he is a worker.*
    -   Correct: *I am a monk **and** he is a worker.*
-   **Missing Verb**:
    -   Wrong: *He good. I happy.*
    -   Correct: *He **is** good. I **am** happy.*
-   **Subject-verb Agreement**:
    -   Wrong: *He do.* -- Correct: *He does.*
-   **Singular / Plural Agreement**:
    -   Wrong: *I see many **dog** there.* -- Correct: *I see many **dogs** there.*
-   **Unclear Pronouns**: it is not clear what the pronoun refers to.
    -   Wrong: *The man came and took it from her.* (what is "it"?)
    -   Correct: *She had a book. The man came and took it from her.*
-   **Tense consistency**: do not change the tense of the text.
    -   Wrong: *...a man ordained as a monk and **practices** meditation.*
    -   Correct: *...and **practiced** meditation.*
-   **Wrong Tense / Aspect** for the situation.
    -   Wrong: *I am needing this.* (stative verbs are not continuous) -- Correct: *I need this.*
-   **Mixing Direct and Indirect Speech**:
    -   Wrong: *He said that "I will go there".*
    -   Correct: *He said, "I will go there."* / *He said that he would go there.*
-   **2 Subjects**:
    -   Wrong: *John he did it.* -- Correct: *John did it.*
-   **Lack of Capitalization**: first letters, proper names, etc.
    -   Wrong: *his name is john and he is from laos.*
    -   Correct: *His name is John and he is from Laos.*

### Writing Grading System

Writing is graded with the same components across all levels, but the **weights shift by level**: earlier levels focus on grammatical accuracy; as students advance, the focus moves toward organization, reasoning, insight, and originality.

#### Level A (A1--B1)

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Grammar (serious mistakes + grammar according to level) | 50% |
| Organization / structure of sentences, paragraphs & text | 10% |
| Follows instructions | 15% |
| Minimum length | 10% |
| Legibility | 5% |
| Content: reasoning, insight & originality | 10% |

#### Level B (B1+--B2)

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Grammar | 35% |
| Organization / structure of sentences, paragraphs & text | 15% |
| Follows instructions | 15% |
| Minimum length | 10% |
| Content: reasoning, insight & originality | 25% |

#### Level C (C1)

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Grammar | 25% |
| Organization / structure of sentences, paragraphs & text | 20% |
| Follows instructions | 15% |
| Minimum length | 10% |
| Content: reasoning, insight & originality | 30% |

A **late submission** deducts 10%.

### Writing Topics According to Level

-   **A1 (Beginner)** -- *Kind of writing*: short sentences, lists, simple forms. *Topics*: introducing oneself, daily routines, basic needs, describing a simple picture.
-   **A2 (Elementary)** -- *Kind of writing*: simple paragraphs, short emails/messages, describing personal experiences. *Topics*: hobbies, family, holidays, shopping, directions, simple past events.
-   **B1 (Intermediate)** -- *Kind of writing*: simple connected text, personal letters, short narratives, expressing opinions. *Topics*: travel experiences, future plans, job applications, reviews of films/books, local events.
-   **B1+ (Upper Intermediate)** -- *Kind of writing*: more detailed narratives, short reports, argumentative essays with simple justification, expressing feelings. *Topics*: social issues (healthy living, environmental protection), giving advice, comparing options.
-   **B2 (Upper Intermediate)** -- *Kind of writing*: clear, detailed text, essays, reports, formal letters, opinion pieces, summaries. *Topics*: advantages/disadvantages, abstract topics (e.g. technology's impact), complex problems, cultural differences.
-   **C1 (Advanced)** -- *Kind of writing*: well-structured, detailed text, complex essays, professional reports, academic papers, critical reviews. *Topics*: specialized fields, abstract and complex issues, synthesizing information from multiple sources, nuanced arguments.

### Tabooed Words

**Purpose**: to push students towards more sophisticated and precise language. The goal is not to eliminate these words entirely, but to encourage students to seek out more precise synonyms, vary their vocabulary, and avoid overreliance on generic terms.

1.  **Good / Bad**: vague and overused.
    -   *Good*: excellent, outstanding, beneficial, favourable, positive, commendable, effective, advantageous, superior, pleasant, admirable, remarkable, high-quality.
    -   *Bad*: poor, detrimental, negative, adverse, harmful, unpleasant, inadequate, inferior, flawed, unsatisfactory, severe, dire, appalling.
2.  **Happy / Sad**
    -   *Happy*: elated, glad, joyful, rapturous, zestful, delighted, pleased, satisfied, cheerful, comforted, at ease, blissful, euphoric, felicitous, joyous, gleeful, jubilant.
    -   *Sad*: despondent, grieving, depressed, downtrodden, discouraged, frustrated, disheartened, moody, sorrowful, disappointed, miserable, unhappy, dejected, in low spirits.
3.  **Very + adjective**: a stronger adjective makes the sentence more impactful and concise.
    -   *Very big*: enormous, colossal, gigantic, vast.
    -   *Very small*: tiny, minute, microscopic.
    -   *Very hungry*: ravenous, famished.
    -   *Very tired*: exhausted, fatigued, drained.
    -   *Very important*: crucial, vital, essential, paramount.
    -   *Very often*: frequently, regularly, constantly.
    -   *Very interesting*: fascinating, captivating, compelling, engaging.
4.  **Big / small**
    -   *Big*: huge, monumental, immense, great, tall, enormous, gigantic, oversized.
    -   *Small*: minute, short, little, mini, pocket-size.
5.  **Thing**: the ultimate generic noun; nearly always has a more specific alternative.
    -   *A new thing*: development, phenomenon, trend, innovation, concept.
    -   *This thing is important*: issue, matter, aspect, element, factor, concern, object, item.
    -   *Many things*: various elements, numerous factors, several aspects, countless items.
6.  **Stuff**: very informal and sometimes slightly condescending.

Studied from the official Oxford Learner's Dictionaries word lists, in this order:

1.  [**Oxford 3000**](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/wordlists/oxford3000-5000) -- the 3,000 most important words to learn in English, organized by CEFR level.
2.  [**Oxford 5000**](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/wordlists/oxford3000-5000) -- the 5,000 most important words to learn in English, organized by CEFR level.
3.  [**Oxford Phrase List**](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/wordlists/oxfordphraselist) -- 750 of the most important phrases to learn in English.
4.  [**OPAL**](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/wordlists/opal) -- Oxford Phrasal Academic Lexicon: 2,420 words and phrases for academic English (see [OPAL](#opal--oxford-phrasal-academic-lexicon) below).

### OPAL -- Oxford Phrasal Academic Lexicon

OPAL contains **2,420 words/phrases**, studied at approximately **150 items per week** over 16 weeks, in the specified order:

| Part | Items | Weeks | Weekly target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Written Single Words | 1,200 | 3--10 | 150/week |
| Written Phrases | 370 | 11--12 | 185/week |
| Spoken Single Words | 600 | 13--16 | 150/week |
| Spoken Phrases | 250 | 17--18 | 125/week |

**Method**:

-   Go through the list and mark the words you already know; focus on the unfamiliar ones.
-   Pay attention to the part of speech (noun, verb, adjective, adverb).
-   Create flashcards and write example sentences for each new word.
-   For spoken items: focus on pronunciation, listen to examples, and use them in conversation.
