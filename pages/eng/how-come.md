### How come

-   It's a grammatical shortcut that English adopted because it's efficient and carries a specific conversational nuance.
-   **"How come"-questions don't require subject-verb inversion.**
    -   Normal Question (with inversion):\*\* "Why **are you** so dirty?"
    -   "How Come" Question (no inversion): "How come **you are** so dirty?" (Same word order as the statement: "You are so dirty.")
    -   Reason: "How come" is a shortened, colloquial form of "How did it come about that&#x2026;" or "How comes it that&#x2026;". Over time, the phrase got shortened and fossilized into the fixed expression. 
        -   "How come you are so dirty?"
        -   "How did it come about that you are so dirty?"
            -   inversion happens in the first clause ("How did it come&#x2026;").
-   **Differences from "Why":**
    -   Inversion:
        -   "Why":  with inversion (unless the subject is a question word itself).
            -   Why **is she** late?
        -   "How come": no inversion.
            -   How come **she is** late?
    -   Usage/Tone:
        -   "How come":
            -   more informal
            -   often shows surprise, skepticism, or a request for an explanation that the speaker finds puzzling
