### Lay down vs. Lie down

**Lie down** (intransitive verb)

**Meaning:** To recline or rest in a flat position. It does **not** take a direct object (you don't "lie down" something).

Conjugations:

-   Present Simple: lie (down) / lies (down)
    -   I lie down after work.
    -   He lies down for a nap.
-   Past Simple: lay (down)
    -   Yesterday, I lay down for an hour.
    -   She lay down on the grass.
-   Past Participle: lain (down)
    -   I have lain down all afternoon.
    -   He had lain down before dinner.
-   Present Participle (Gerund): lying (down)
    -   She is lying down right now.
    -   Lying down helps my back.

**Lay down** (Transitive Verb)

**Meaning**: To place something down, to set something down gently. It always takes a direct object (you "lay down" something).

Conjugations:

-   Present Simple: lay (down) / lays (down)
    -   Please lay down the book on the table.
    -   She lays down the baby gently.
-   Past Simple: laid (down)
    -   He laid down his tools after work.
    -   They laid down the carpet yesterday.
-   Past Participle: laid (down)
    -   I have laid down the groundwork for the project.
    -   The chicken has laid down its eggs.
-   Present Participle (Gerund): laying (down)
    -   She is laying down the plates.
    -   Laying down your burdens can be freeing.
