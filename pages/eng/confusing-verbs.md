### Some Confusing Verbs

- **Deny**: state that something is not true, or that one is not responsible.
    - *He denied eating the last piece of cake.*
- **Blame**: assign responsibility for a fault or wrong to someone.
    - *She blamed her sister for breaking the vase.*
- **Accuse**: charge someone with a wrongdoing or offense.
    - *The manager accused him of taking office supplies.*
- **Complain**: express dissatisfaction or annoyance about something.
    - *They complained that the coffee was too cold.*
- **Criticize**: point out faults or express disapproval of someone or something.
    - *The reviewer criticized the film's weak plot.*
- **Refuse**: decline to do something requested or accept something offered.
    - *He refused to help me with my homework.*
- **Reject**: dismiss something as inadequate, unacceptable, or unsuitable.
    - *The editor rejected her manuscript.*
- **Object** (v.): express opposition or disapproval towards something.
    - *Several board members objected to the proposed budget cuts.*

#### Group 1: Responding to truth, actions, or suitability

**Deny vs. Refuse vs. Reject**

- **Deny**: concerns **truth or responsibility**. You deny a fact (it's not true) or involvement (I didn't do it). It's about what is or was.
    - *He denied the rumor.* (said it wasn't true)
- **Refuse**: concerns **willingness to act or accept**. You refuse to do something or to take something offered. It's about a future action or acceptance.
    - *She refused to answer the question.* (wouldn't do it)
- **Reject**: concerns the **acceptability or value** of something. You reject an idea, proposal, gift, application, or person because you deem it unsuitable, inadequate, or undesirable. It's about the merit of what's presented.
    - *The committee rejected their proposal.* (found it unacceptable)
- **Nuance**: you *deny knowing* something (a fact). You *refuse to know* something (a choice not to learn). You *reject the idea of knowing* something (find the concept bad).

#### Group 2: Assigning fault

**Blame vs. Accuse**

- **Blame**: focuses on **assigning responsibility** for a negative outcome or fault. It can be a personal conclusion or an informal statement. You can blame someone in your head, or state it.
    - *I blame the rainy weather for the cancelled picnic.* (assigning cause)
    - *He blamed his colleague for the error.* (assigned fault)
- **Accuse**: focuses on **formally stating that someone committed a wrongdoing**, crime, or offense. It's stronger, often implying a charge, confrontation, or need for justification. It's an assertion that needs proof.
    - *The police accused him of theft.* (made a formal charge)
    - *She accused him of lying.* (challenged his honesty directly)
- **Nuance**: you might *blame the system* for a problem, but you *accuse a specific person of sabotaging* the system. Blame is about fault; accuse is about commission of a wrong.

#### Group 3: Expressing dissatisfaction

**Complain vs. Criticize vs. Object**

- **Complain**: expresses **general dissatisfaction**, annoyance, or unhappiness about a situation or a problem. It's often about one's personal feelings regarding a circumstance, without necessarily offering solutions or deep analysis.
    - *He always complains about the food in the cafeteria.* (expresses general unhappiness)
- **Criticize**: involves **pointing out specific faults**, flaws, or shortcomings in something or someone. It implies an evaluation, often with an analytical or judgmental tone, and can be constructive or destructive.
    - *She criticized the report's methodology.* (identified specific flaws)
    - *The coach criticized the team's poor performance.* (evaluated their actions negatively)
- **Object** (v.): expresses **formal opposition** or disagreement to a specific plan, proposal, rule, or action. It's usually more direct and often requires a reason or alternative. It's about taking a stance against something being decided or implemented.
    - *The community objected to the construction of the new factory.* (expressed formal opposition to the plan)
    - *"I object, Your Honor!"* (formal opposition in a legal context)
- **Nuance**: you might *complain about the difficulty* of a task (expressing your personal struggle). You might *criticize the design* of the task (pointing out its flaws). You might *object to the task being assigned* in the first place (formally opposing the decision).
