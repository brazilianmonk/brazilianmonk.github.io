### Shared Lessons That Add New Information at B1+

These B1+ lessons introduce new structural rules, finer semantic distinctions, or additional target structures not taught in B1:

- **Gerund or Infinitive:**
    - B1 (Do, to do, doing): covers basic patterns like verb + gerund (*enjoy swimming*) vs. verb + infinitive (*want to go*).
    - B1+ (Verb patterns): adds complex structures such as verb + object + infinitive (*advise him to go*), prepositions + gerunds, and verbs where the meaning changes depending on the choice (*remember doing* vs. *remember to do*).
- **Passive Voice:**
    - B1 (Passive verb forms / Active & passive): focuses on present simple, past simple, and basic continuous passive forms.
    - B1+ (The passive voice: All tenses): extends passive structures to perfect tenses (*has been written*), future tenses (*will be delivered*), modal verbs (*should be done*), and continuous forms across all tenses.
- **Conditionals:**
    - B1 (First, Second, Third conditionals): teaches each conditional structure independently using standard forms (if + present, will; if + past, would).
    - B1+ (Zero & First / Second & Third unreal conditionals): combines zero conditional with future time clauses using time expressions like *as soon as*, *in case*, *unless*, *until*, and introduces mixed variations and nuance distinctions (*if* vs. *in case*).
- **Expressing Preference:**
    - B1 (Would rather & Would sooner): focuses strictly on would rather / would sooner + base verb and would rather + subject + past simple (*I'd rather you left*).
    - B1+ (Would rather, would prefer): formally introduces and contrasts would prefer + to-infinitive with would rather, detailed comparative patterns (*would prefer to X rather than Y*), and formal vs. informal usage.
- **Quantifiers:**
    - B1 (All, both / Both, either, neither / Any, no, none): splits quantifiers across separate lessons, introducing their baseline meanings and pronoun usage.
    - B1+ (All, most, both, either, neither, any, no, none): unifies all of them in a single comprehensive lesson, adding *most*, and focusing heavily on subject-verb agreement (*neither of them is/are*) and formal determiners (quantifier + *of* + the/my).
- **Present Perfect:**
    - B1 (Past simple vs. present perfect / Present perfect simple & continuous): establishes when to use present perfect instead of past simple, and introduces basic continuous duration (for/since).
    - B1+ (Present perfect simple or continuous): shifts focus to contrast actions with visible present results vs. completed actions, emphasizing state verbs, duration vs. quantity (*how long* vs. *how much*), and temporary vs. permanent situations.

### Shared Lessons with Near-Identical Core Rules (Reinforcement)

These B1+ lessons cover the same fundamental concepts introduced in B1, but use different practice exercises, expanded examples, and a broader vocabulary context:

- **Used to / Habitual Forms** (Used to, be used to, get used to):
    - B1: introduces the three forms (past habit, familiarity state, process of adjusting).
    - B1+: keeps the core rules identical, but tests them together in mixed exercises to reinforce contrast in more complex contexts.
- **Adjectives in -ed/-ing:**
    - B1: teaches the difference between cause (*boring*) and feeling (*bored*).
    - B1+ (Participles as adjectives): maintains the same rule, using a wider, upper-intermediate vocabulary range.
- **Clauses of Contrast and Purpose:**
    - B1 (Clauses of contrast, purpose and reason): introduces *although*, *despite*, *in order to*, *so that*.
    - B1+: covers the exact same structures (*despite / in spite of* + -ing/noun, *so that* + clause), serving primarily as a consolidative review with longer sentence structures.
- **So, such (a), so much, so many:**
    - B1: establishes *so* + adjective vs. *such* + a + adjective + noun vs. *so much/many* + noun.
    - B1+: uses identical grammar rules, applied within more advanced reading and gap-fill scenarios.
