**Key takeaway for "When I shower":**

*   "When I shower" *could* mean "while I am in the shower" (simultaneous) or "after I have finished showering" (subsequent).
*   If you intend the action to happen *after* the shower is complete, using **"When I have showered..."** makes that completion explicit and unambiguous.
*   If you intend to imply "while I am showering," then "While I am showering..." is a clearer choice.

When the action expressed in the Time Clause happens simultaneous or soon after the action of the main clause, the Present Simple is always used.

| Time clause            | Main clause   | Main action happens                |
| When you come          | I'll call him | simultaneous/soon after            |
| When you have finished | I'll call him | main action only after             |
| When I shower          | I'll call him | ambiguous (use perfect to clarify) |

The key difference lies in the **emphasis on completion**.

1.  **When + Present Simple:**
    *   Focuses on the **point in time** or the **occurrence** of the action in the "when" clause.
    *   It implies that as soon as that action happens, the main action will follow.
    *   This is generally the **most common and natural** choice in everyday English.
    *   **Example:** "When I **arrive** home, I'll call you." (The moment I get home, the call happens.)

2.  **When + Present Perfect:**
    *   Emphasizes the **completion** of the action in the "when" clause *before* the main action begins.
    *   It stresses that the first action is fully finished, and its result or state is established.
    *   This adds a nuance of "only *after* this is completely done..."
    *   **Example:** "When I **have arrived** home, I'll call you." (Once the process of arriving is complete, and I'm settled, then I'll call.)

**When to choose which:**

*   **Use Present Simple (more often):** When the completion is simply understood, or you're focusing on the simple occurrence of the event as a trigger. It's often more concise.
*   **Use Present Perfect (to emphasize completion):** When you want to explicitly highlight that the first action needs to be *fully finished* and its result set before the second action can start. This is useful when the first action is a process that takes time, or where the finished state is crucial.

**In many cases, both are grammatically correct and convey a very similar meaning.** The Present Perfect just adds a slightly stronger emphasis on the "finished" aspect. If in doubt, the Present Simple is usually a safe and natural choice.

# version 3
## Present Simple vs Present Perfect after When / Until / After
## Overview

The difference between **Present Simple** and **Present Perfect** after time conjunctions (*when, until, after, before*) is not mainly about tense. It is about:

1. **When the action occurs**
   - During the action
   - After the action is completed

2. **What meaning the speaker intends**
   - Event happening
   - Completion/result of an event

---

## 1. Based on Occurrence of the Actions (During vs After)

| Time relationship | Grammar | Meaning | Example | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DURING the action** | Present Simple | The second action happens while the first action is happening | I'll move out when I find a job. | Find job → Move out |
| **DURING the action** | Present Simple | The action happens at the same time or around the same event | When I call Tom, I'll invite him. | Call Tom + invite him |
| **AFTER the action is completed** | Present Perfect | The first action must finish before the second action happens | I'll move out when I've found a job. | Find job ✓ → Move out |
| **AFTER the action is completed** | Present Perfect | Completion is more important than the action itself | We'll talk when we've had dinner. | Have dinner ✓ → Talk |
| **AFTER the action is completed** | Present Perfect | A completed condition is required | You can't play until you've done your homework. | Homework finished ✓ → Play |

---

## Simple Rule



DURING = Present Simple

when + subject + verb

Example:
I'll call you when I arrive.




AFTER = Present Perfect

when + subject + have/has + past participle

Example:
I'll call you when I've arrived.



---

## 2. Based on Meaning Intended

| Intended meaning | Grammar choice | What the speaker emphasizes | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sequence only** | Present Simple | The order of events | I'll move out when I find a job. | Finding a job triggers moving out |
| **Completion/result** | Present Perfect | The first action must be finished | I'll move out when I've found a job. | I need a confirmed job before leaving |
| **Habit/general truth** | Present Simple | Repeated situations | When I finish work, I go home. | Every time I finish work |
| **Future event** | Present Simple | A future time reference | I'll call you when I arrive. | After I arrive, I will call |
| **Requirement/precondition** | Present Perfect | A condition must be completed | You can go out after you've cleaned your room. | Cleaning must be finished first |
| **Achievement/result** | Present Perfect | Reaching a goal | I'll celebrate when I've passed the exam. | Passing is the achievement |

---

## Core Difference

| Present Simple | Present Perfect |
|---|---|
| Focuses on the **event happening** | Focuses on the **event being completed** |
| `When I do X → Y happens` | `When I have done X → Y happens` |
| The action triggers another action | The completed action allows another action |
| Answers: "When does it happen?" | Answers: "When is it finished?" |

---

## Compare Examples

## 1. Present Simple

> I'll talk to him when I meet him.

Meaning:

- The meeting is the important event.
- When the meeting happens, I will talk to him.
- Focus = the occurrence of meeting.

Timeline:



Meet him → Talk to him



---

## 2. Present Perfect

> I'll talk to him when I've met him.

Meaning:

- The meeting needs to be completed first.
- Focus = the result of having met him.
- Less common, but emphasizes completion.

Timeline:



Meet him completely ✓ → Talk to him



---

## Mental Model

## Present Simple



WHEN I DO X


    ↓


Y happens

(action triggers another action)



Example:



When I arrive, I'll call you.



Meaning:



Arrival happens → Call



---

## Present Perfect



WHEN I HAVE DONE X

    ↓

Y happens

(completion unlocks next action)


Example:


When I've finished my work, I'll relax.


Meaning:


Work finished ✓ → Relax

## Final Summary

| Form | Main Focus | Question Answered |
|---|---|---|
| **Present Simple** | The action/event | "When does it happen?" |
| **Present Perfect** | Completion/result | "When is it finished?" |

The difference is:

Present Simple = event

Present Perfect = completed event
