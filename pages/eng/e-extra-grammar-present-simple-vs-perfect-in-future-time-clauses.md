Future Time Clauses have a structure similar to that of the 1st Conditional (Present → Future/Modal/Imperative) and can be formed with the Present Simple, Present Continuous, or Present Perfect.

*   **Present Simple:** When **I'm** 40, I'll be a great elder.
*   **Present Continuous:** When **you're talking**, I can't concentrate.
*   **Present Perfect:** When **you've finished** your duty, you can go back home.

---

## Difference Between Present Simple and Present Perfect in Future Time Clauses

### The Present Simple

*   **More common** in everyday usage.
*   The main clause action can happen:
    *   **During** the future time clause action:
        *   When we **have** dinner, we'll talk. (Both actions occur together or overlap.)
    *   **After** the future time clause action:
        *   When I **find** a job, I'll move out. (Finding a job happens first, then moving out.)

### The Present Perfect

*   **Less common** than the Present Simple.
*   The meaning is similar to using the Present Simple, but it specifically **emphasizes that the action in the time clause will or must be completed before the main clause action takes place.**
*   The main clause action happens **after** the future time clause action:
    *   When **we've had** dinner, we'll talk. (First, dinner must be completely finished, then we talk.)
*   It is used to emphasize the **necessity of concluding** the first action before the next can begin.

### When to Prefer the Present Perfect:

Since the Present Simple can be used for both "during" and "after" scenarios, it is often clearer to use the Present Perfect when:

1.  **The time relationship of the actions is ambiguous:**
    *   **Ambiguous:** "When you do your homework, you can watch the movie."
        *   (This could mean *while* you're doing it, or *after* you've finished. It's unclear.)
    *   **Clearer with Perfect:** "When you **have done** your homework, you can watch the movie."
        *   (Clearly means *after* completion.)

2.  **There is a need to emphasize the completion of the Time Clause action:**
    *   "When you **have done** your homework, you can watch the movie." (Stresses that finishing the homework is a prerequisite.)

3.  **Personal Preference:** Some speakers prefer the Present Perfect in cases where the main action strictly occurs after the future time action.

## Rule of Thumb:

*   **Present Simple** can be used in most cases.
*   However, the **Present Perfect** is generally preferred or clearer when:
    *   **Ambiguity:** The action time relationship is unclear with the Simple.
    *   **Emphasis on Completion:** There is a need to strongly emphasize the completion of the future time action before the main action can occur.
    *   **Personal Preference:** You prefer using the Perfect when the main action strictly happens after the future time action.
