### Most vs. Mostly

- **most** = superlative / majority as a noun determiner
- **mostly** = adverb = "mainly / largely / for the most part"

If you confuse them, you're mixing **category** vs. **manner**.

#### "Most" — as a determiner (quantity of a group)

**Correct usage**

- *Most people prefer tea over coffee.*
- *Most students passed the exam.*
- *Most of the water was spilled.*
- *She spent most of her time reading.*

**What it does**: it refers to a majority of a noun group.

**Key test**: if you can replace it with "the majority of ...", then *most* is correct.

#### "Most" — as a superlative

- *This is the most important rule.*
- *He is the most intelligent student in class.*
- *That was the most difficult part.*

**Key test**: if it's ranking one thing above all others → *most*.

#### "Mostly" — adverb (degree / general condition)

**Correct usage**

- *The room is mostly clean.*
- *I eat mostly vegetarian food.*
- *The audience was mostly young people.*
- *The work is mostly done.*

**What it does**: it means "for the most part, but not entirely" — so it allows exceptions.

#### Direct contrast examples (high clarity)

**Wrong vs. correct**

- ❌ *Mostly students passed the exam.*
- ✅ *Most students passed the exam.*

#### Sharp distinction test

- **Sentence A**: *Most people agreed.*
    - talks about **how many** people
- **Sentence B**: *People mostly agreed.*
    - talks about **to what extent** they agreed (generally, but not all)

#### One-line mental model

- **most** = how many
- **mostly** = how much / to what extent
