### 1. "May Well"

*   **Meaning:** Expresses **probability or a strong likelihood**. It suggests that something is quite probable or a logical consequence of something else. It's stronger than just "may" (which suggests possibility) but not as strong as "will definitely."
*   **Structure:** `Subject + may well + base verb`
*   **Usage:**
    *   "He **may well** be late; his train was delayed." (It's quite likely he'll be late.)
    *   "You **may well** be right about that." (It's probable that you are right.)
    *   "If you keep practicing, you **may well** win the competition." (It's a strong possibility/logical outcome.)

---

### 2. "May As Well"

*   **Meaning:** Expresses **resignation, a lack of better alternatives, or doing something because there's no good reason not to**, or to avoid a worse situation. It implies that while you might not be enthusiastic, it's the most sensible or least undesirable thing to do in the circumstances.
*   **Structure:** `Subject + may as well + base verb`
*   **Usage:**
    *   "The bus is late, so we **may as well** walk." (Walking isn't ideal, but it's the only option or better than waiting indefinitely.)
    *   "Since you're here, you **may as well** help me with this." (There's no good reason for you not to help.)
    *   "I've started the project, so I **may as well** finish it." (Even if I don't particularly want to, it makes sense to complete it now that I've begun.)

---

### Key Difference:

*   **May Well = Probability / Likelihood**
*   **May As Well = Resignation / Lack of Better Alternative**
