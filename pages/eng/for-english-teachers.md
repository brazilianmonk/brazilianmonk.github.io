## Suggested Principles

### Pronunciation

#### Step-by-step
- Learn the pronunciation of each word individually
- Learn how to join sounds (some sounds are dropped when words are combined)
- Learn where to stop in the sentence (commas, periods, phrases)
- Learn intonation, pitch (rise and fall) at the right occasions (rise in questions, sustain in commas, descend in periods)
- Gradually increase the difficulty and speed

#### Resources
- [**Tongue Twisters**](/summaries/eng/tongue-twisters)
- [**Difficult Words**](/summaries/eng/difficult-words)

### Speaking

#### Step-by-step
- Start with a small audience and safe environment: just the teacher or another student, no recordings, no presentations.
- Correct the student but also allow the conversation to flow (don't correct too much, which leads to low confidence and mental blockage)
- Gradually increase the audience: a student, the teacher, small group, bigger group, entire class, presentations, recordings, etc.

### Writing

- Teach structure first: sentence → paragraph → text; use the [**Serious Writing Mistakes**](/summaries/eng/serious-writing-mistakes) list as a core correction reference.
- Choose topics according to the student's level: see [Writing Topics According to Level](/summaries/eng/e#writing-topics-according-to-level) in the Student Handbook.
- Enforce the [**Tabooed Words**](/summaries/eng/e#tabooed-words) for the student's level to push vocabulary sophistication.
- Grade writings with the rubric in [How to Grade a Writing](#how-to-grade-a-writing).

## Grading System

### How to Grade a Writing

Evaluate grammar **according to the student's level** (a B1 student is not penalized for not using C1 structures). The components below are the same at every level; the **weights vary per level** -- A levels emphasize grammar (50%), and the weight gradually shifts toward structure, reasoning, insight, and originality as students advance -- as defined in the [Writing Grading System](/summaries/eng/e#writing-grading-system) of the Student Handbook:

| Component | Weight | What is evaluated |
|---|---|---|
| Grammar | 50% | Usage of grammar according to level; serious writing mistakes |
| Structure | 10% | Structure of sentences, paragraphs, and text in general |
| Legibility | 5% | Legibility of handwriting, writing on the line, etc. |
| Instructions | 15% | Following the instructions of the exercise |
| Length | 10% | Minimum length of each answer |
| Others | 10% | Sophistication, reasoning, spelling, and other factors |
| **Late** | **−10%** | Late submission |

### Repeat-Protocol and Remedial Lessons

If a student does not reach the ideal passing grade:

-   **Repeat the level**, if teachers are available.
-   **Remedial lessons** based on the weak lessons:
    -   Identify weak questions from the last test (less than 50% of the marks).
    -   For each weak question, record: **Question** → **Lesson** → who will **Explain** (weak students teach, consolidating their own learning) → who will **Attend** (the other weak students).
-   **Retake the exams** after the remedial lessons.

### An Example of Final Grade Composition

How the global result is composed in practice (3rd year, 2nd semester): Participation /100 → ×40%; Academic Total (vocab tests, listening, mid-term, final exam) /100 → ×60%; Bonus +1–3% for exceptional work; ideal passing grade 70%. Per-component weights vary per student according to join date and attendance frequency (e.g. late joiners: mid-term 30% + final 70%). Full tables: [3rd year 2nd semester final grades](/summaries/eng/3y-2s-final-grades).

## Suggested Exams by Type

-   **Vocabulary Test**
    -   Matching words with their meanings.
    -   Conjugation of irregular verbs.
    -   Spelling (as in a Spelling Bee).
    -   For higher levels: phrasal verbs and idiomatic phrases.
-   **Grammar Test**
    -   Draw questions from the [Question Bank](/summaries/eng/for-english-teachers/question-bank) according to level and lesson.
    -   Mid-term: half of the semester's lessons; Final: all of them.
-   **Writing Test**
    -   One topic according to the student's level (see [Writing Topics According to Level](/summaries/eng/e#writing-topics-according-to-level)).
    -   Minimum length required; graded with the [writing rubric](#how-to-grade-a-writing).
-   **Speaking Test**
    -   Lower levels: reading aloud, describing a picture, answering simple questions.
    -   Higher levels: presentations (on camera), debates, explaining a topic to the class.
    -   Evaluate pronunciation, fluency, and communication; see [Speaking principles](#speaking).
-   **Listening Comprehension Test**
    -   An audiobook excerpt followed by comprehension questions.
    -   Weekly listening homework is tracked separately and counts towards participation.
-   **Mid-term & Final Exams**
    -   Combine grammar + vocabulary + writing (and listening) sections.
    -   Weight each section according to the group and the student's join date.
    -   Use the standard [Exam Heading](#exam-heading) at the beginning of each exam paper.
