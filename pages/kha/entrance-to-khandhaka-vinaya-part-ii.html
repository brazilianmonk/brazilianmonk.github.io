    • pātimokkha
      • word formation: pa + ati + mokkh + a ⇒ pātimokkha
      • meaning (Pāḷi and English): "tattha pātimokkhan'ti paatimokkhaṃ, atipamokkhaṃ, atiseṭṭhaṃ, atiuttaman'ti attho." (the greatest, highest, most excellent thing)
      • pātimokkha refers to two things (dabbattha)
        • sīlapātimokkha: the foundation of all wholesome qualities and is therefore considered excellent and of the highest importance
          • meaning (Pāḷi and English): "ādimetaṃ mukhametaṃ pamukhametaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ, tena vuccati pātimokkhaṃ" (sīlapātimokkha is beginning, the entrance to wholesome qualities, so it is excellent)
        • ganthapātimokkha: the precepts established by the Buddha
          • meaning (English): the means (sikkhāpada) to reach adhisīla, adhicitta and adhipaññā; considered to be associated with excellent qualities
            • IIT - Nissayamuttaka Course
      • sīlapātimokkha
        • definition (Pāḷi and English): (kaṅkhāvitaraṇī): "tattha sīlaṁ yo naṃ pāti rakkhati, taṃ mokkheti mocayati āpāyikādīhi dukkhehi, attānuvādādīhi vā bhayehīti pātimokkhaṃ." (sīla protects individuals who follow it from encountering suffering, such as hardships in awful realms and the dangers of self-reproach and blame from others)
        • in terms of paramatthas sīlapātimokkha is
          • cetanā in abstaining from evil (vāritta) and in performing good (cāritta) (yathālābha)
          • three virati in refraining from evil
        • 2 kinds of sīlapātimokkha

                • ājīvaṭṭhamakasīla bc it is the preliminary paṭipadā practiced in the beginning (ādi) of the development of the supramundane path as expressed in various suttas: “Pubbeva kho panassa kāyakammaṃ vacīkammaṃ ājīvo suparisuddho hoti”

    • sikkhāpada
      • sikkhāpada is the training rules or precepts promulgated by the Bhagavā which are the foundation for achieving higher spiritual states, known as adhisīla, adhicitta, and adhipaññā
      • 2 types of sikkhāpada
        • ovādasikkhāpada: these are exhortations by the Bhagavā that are found mostly in the Suttapiṭaka, where the Bhagavā advises bhikkhus on what actions to perform or avoid such as "pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti" (refrain from taking life) or the ovādapātimokkha
        • āṇāsikkhāpada: these are instructions by the Bhagavā, that are found in the Vinayapiṭaka, that have been established as binding rules
      • the threefold sāsana
        • pariyattisāsana (the study of the doctrine)
        • paṭipattisāsana (the practice)
        • paṭivedhasāsana (supramundane attainments)
      • 10 benefits of promulgating sikkhāpada
        • saṅghasuṭṭhutāya (for the excellence of the Saṅgha): under rājasikkhāpada: “Yo ca tathāgatassa vacanaṃ sampaṭicchati, tassa taṃ dīgharattaṃ hitāya sukhāya hoti” (whoever accepts the Tathāgata's words, that would bring them benefit and happiness for a long time); on the other hand, Bhaddāli Sutta in the Majjhima Nikāya, emphasizes the detrimentals one has to face for not accepting them
          • is this the only meaning meant on this case? what about the excellence of the Saṅgha? #q🙋🏼‍♂
        • saṅghaphāsutāya (for the comfort of the Saṅgha): The establishment of precepts enables the Saṅgha to live together peacefully and in unity
        • dummaṅkūnaṃ puggalānaṃ niggahāya (for restraining obdurate individuals)
        • pesalānaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ phāsuvihārāya (for the comfort of well-behaved monks): when the boundaries of right and wrong are explicitly defined, well-behaved monks can practice their morality with ease, eliminating any doubts about what should or should not be done; furthermore, when immoral individuals are restrained, the observance of community acts is unhindered and moral monks can abide comfortably
        • diṭṭhadhammikānaṃ āsavānaṃ saṃvarāya (for the prevention of suffering here and now): adhering to the precepts prevents sufferings here and now from occurring, such as physical punishment, accusations, defamation, loss of respect or losing followers
        • samparāyikānaṃ āsavānaṃ paṭighātāya (for warding off suffering hereafter): adhering to the precepts prevents sufferings hereafter from occurring, such as bad destinations and various unpleasant results in saṁsāra
        • appasannānaṃ pasādāya (for instilling faith in those without faith): learning about the precepts and observing disciplined bhikkhus and bhikkhunīs can inspire faith even in non-Buddhists such as the case of a non-Buddhist brahmin, well-versed in Vedas, who, developed faith in the Buddhasāsana after studying the Vinaya
        • pasannānaṃ bhiyyobhāvāya (for the increase of faith in the faithful): learning about the precepts and observing disciplined bhikkhus and bhikkhunīs can increase the faith of Buddhists themselves
        • saddhammaṭṭhitiyāya (for the endurance of the true Dhamma); how does the promulgation of precepts lead to the endurance of the three saddhammas? #editing
          • saddhamma comprises three aspects:
            • pariyattisaddhamma: the teachings found in the Tipiṭaka
            • paṭipattisaddhamma: austere practices (dhutaṅga), duties (khandhaka vatta and asīti mahāvatta), morality (sīla), concentration (samādhi), and insight (vipassanā)
            • paṭivedhasaddhamma: supramundane path (magga), fruition (phala), and Nibbāna
          • the promulgation of precepts leads to the study of vinaya and in order to gain a deeper understanding of the vinaya, monks and nuns engage in the study of other teachings of the Buddha which strengthens the pariyattisaddhamma the observance of precepts allows the proper development and fulfilment of the practical aspects of the teaching such as austerities, duties, morality, concentration and insight, strengthening the paṭipattisaddhamma the observance of precepts assists disciples to easily fulfill their practice and achieve the paths, fruitions and Nibbāna, which strengthens the paṭivedhasaddhamma
        • vinayānuggahāya (for the fostering of discipline): The establishment of precepts strengthens the fourfold Vinaya within the sāsana
          • vinaya is fourfold
            • saṃvaravinaya (discipline as restraint)
            • pahānavinaya (discipline as eradication)
            • samathavinaya (discipline as settlement of disciplinary issues)
            • paññattivinaya (discipline as promulgated rules)
            • note: in this list the other ones are not included, they are: 3-/5-fold sampatti, and codanaṁ vā saraṇaṁ vā
        • pāḷi: "tena hi, bhikkhave, bhikkhūnaṃ sikkhāpadaṃ paññapessāmi dasa atthavase paṭicca – saṅghasuṭṭhutāya, saṅghaphāsutāya, dummaṅkūnaṃ puggalānaṃ niggahāya, pesalānaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ phāsuvihārāya, diṭṭhadhammikānaṃ āsavānaṃ saṃvarāya, samparāyikānaṃ āsavānaṃ paṭighātāya, appasannānaṃ pasādāya, pasannānaṃ bhiyyobhāvāya, saddhammaṭṭhitiyā, vinayānuggahāya."

    • uposatha and pātimokkha
      • uposatha
        • the observance of the uposatha is found among other religions as well
        • there are three uposatha days in a fortnight, namely the eighth (aṭṭhamī), fourteenth (cātuddasī), and fifteenth (paṇṇarasī) days ; uposathavinayakamma is to be done on the 14th or 15th, except on the day of pavāraṇā in which the pavāraṇāvinayakamma should be done instead
        • puṇṇamā (full-moon days) are always on the paṇṇarasī
        • is the uposathavinayakamma the same as the pātimokkha recitation?→they are different kammas
        • 9 uposathas
          • divasavasena: 3 uposathas based on the day it is observed
            • cātuddasīuposatha: the one observed on the 14th
            • paṇṇarasīuposatha: the one observed on the 15th
            • sāmaggīuposatha: the one observed on the day a previously disunited saṅgha is reunited; if such day coincides with the pavāraṇa day, the pavāraṇavinayakamma should be performed instead
            • note: one can perform the uposatha on the 14th or 15th but traditionally it is performed on the 15th with the exception of the 3rd and 7th uposatha which is traditionally performed on the 14th
          • puggala- / kāraka-vasena: 3 uposathas based on the individuals who perform it
            • saṅghauposatha: the one observed by four or more bhikkhus/nīs in communion (samānasaṃvāsaka bhikkhu)
            • gaṇauposatha: the one observed by three or two bhikkhus/nīs in communion; it should not be done by obtaining chanda
            • puggalauposatha: the one observed by a single bhikkhu/nī
          • kātabbākāraka vasena: 3 uposathas based on the manner of performance
            • suttuddesauposatha: observed by a saṅgha by reciting the pātimokkha
              • note: any part of the Buddha's teaching can be called sutta
            • pārisuddhiuposatha: observed by three or two bhikkhus/nīs by declaring their purity (pārisuddhi) mutually
            • adhiṭṭhānauposatha: observed by a single bhikkhu/nī by determining, "Ajja me uposatho (paṇṇaraso/cātuddaso)"
            • vocab: #[[pāḷi vocab (1st year) ]]
              • pārisuddhi means purity, while parisuddhi means one who is pure
        • saṅghuposatha - pubbakaraṇādi (tasks to be performed before the saṅghuposatha)
          • pubbakaraṇa: 4 compulsory acts to be performed before gathering for uposatha
            • sammajjanakaraṇaṃ: the uposatha venue should be swept
            • padīpujjalanaṃ: lamps should be lit (during the night)
            • udakaṭṭhapanaṃ: water for drinking and other uses should be made available
            • āsanapaññāpanaṃ: seats should be prepared (at very least with leaves)
            • "sammajjanī padīpo ca, udakaṃ āsanena ca, uposathassa etāni, pubbakaraṇanti vuccati."
            • note:
              • neglecting to do so, when ordered by the theras, result in a dkṭ
              • hitting the gong is traditionally done as well, but not compulsory
          • pubbakicca: 5 compulsory acts to be performed after gathering but before reciting the pātimokkha
            • chandāharaṇaṃ: likewise, the consent must be brought (it is effective for all vinayakammas); it is necessary for the validation of other vinayakammas apart from the uposathavinayakamma
            • pārisuddhiāharaṇaṃ: the claim of purity (pārisuddhi) from offenses of the upasampannas within the sīmā who cannot attend the uposatha must be brought; it is necessary for the validation of the uposatha of the non-attending bhk/ī
            • utukkhānaṃ; 5 things to be announced

    • The Tathāgata emphasized that disciplinary acts such as uposatha and pavāraṇā must be carried out by the united Saṅgha. The unity of the Saṅgha is determined by three main factors:
      • 1. Participation of all bhikkhus: All bhikkhus residing in a specific area should participate in the assembly for vinayakamma and remain within close proximity of hatthapāsa to each other.
      • 2. Consent from absent monks: If any monks remaining in the specific area are unable to participate, they should send their consent (chanda) to the assembly.
      • 3. Non-opposition: Monks physically present in the vinayakamma should not oppose the act, either verbally or physically.
      • If any of these factors are incomplete, such as when even a single monk from the area fails to attend or provide consent, the vinayakamma becomes invalid. Hence, it is crucial to understand the area where the participation or consent of monks is required for a vinayakamma, which is known as sīmā in Pāḷi.

    • Sīmā Nimitta: Boundary Signs of Sīmās The area of a baddha sīmās are marked by signs known as sīmā nimitta, which act as boundaries or boundary points for the sīmā. The Buddha has recommended eight different inanimate physical objects that can serve as sīmā nimitta. These signs are as follows:
      • Pabbata nimitta - A mountain made entirely of rock or soil, or a combination of both, that is the size of an elephant or larger.
      • 2. Pāsāṇa nimitta - A rock or lump of iron that ranges in size from a minimum of a jaggery lump (about 32 pala) to the size of an elephant. If it is a flat rock, it can be even larger.
      • 3. Vana nimitta - Even a thicket with at least four or five trees out of which at least three trees containing heartwood can be considered a vana (forest) nimitta. A group of arecaceae trees (palm family) cannot be considered as vana nimitta.
      • 4. Rukkha nimitta - A tree, even with a slender trunk as thin as a stylus (a pen used to write on palm leafs), can be considered a rukkha nimitta as long as it has grown to a height of eight inches (aṅguli) and contains heartwood.
      • 5. Magga nimitta - A footpath or cart-path that passes through two or three villages.
      • 6. Vammika nimitta - An ant hill that reaches a height of eight inches (aṅguli), even if it was built on the same day, with a circumference resembling an ox horn.
      • 7. Nadī nimitta - Rivers suitable for udakukkhepa sīmas can be considered nadī nimitta (river). More details about them can be found under udakukkhepa sīmā.
      • 8. Udaka nimitta - Places with non-flowing water, such as natural lakes, ponds, oceans, and lagoons. Even a pit dug just before the vinayakamma and filled with water can be used as an udaka nimitta if the water in the pit does not dry out until the vinayakamma is completed. Flowing water or water in a vessel does not suffice as an udaka nimitta.

    • Threefold Sampatti
      • The establishment of a baddha sīmā requires the recitation of a ñatti dutiya kammavācā. Its recitation is called sīmā sammuti vinayakamma – disciplinary act done to establish a baddha sīmā. To ensure its effectiveness, three factors known as sampatti must be present. These factors are as follows:
        • 1. Nimitta Sampatti: The sīmā should be established by correctly naming and connecting the signs (nimitta) in the manner described earlier. When the vinayakamma is performed in this way, it is considered to have nimitta sampatti.It must be one of the eight nimittas.
        • 2. Parisa Sampatti: At least four upasampadā monks are required to establish a sīmā. If the sīmā is established within a village, all the monks residing within the boundaries of the village should either participate in the act or provide their consent. The participation or consent of monks who are in already established baddhasīmās, rivers, seas, or natural lakes within the village is not necessary. Furthermore, it is crucial that the participating monks do not object to the vinayakamma. When the disciplinary act is performed with these conditions fulfilled, it is said to have parisa sampatti. chanda is from the sima where the kamma is done, not the area that will be established;
        • 3. Kammavācā Sampatti: The recommended ñatti dutiya kammavācā must be correctly recited according to the Vinaya by a competent monk. This recitation should be made in the presence of the gathered assembly of monks. When the disciplinary act is performed with this proper recitation, it is considered to have kammavācā sampatti.
    • Types of Baddha Sīmā
      • 1. Samānasaṃvāsaka Sīmā: All baddha sīmās are considered samānasaṃvāsaka sīmā because monks in communion can perform all types of vinayakammas in any baddha sīmā.
      • 2. Avippavāsa Sīmā / ticīvara avippavāsa sīmā: If needed, a baddha sīmā can be designated as an avippavāsa sīmā. In such cases, a bhikkhu can keep the three robes (ticīvara) within the designated baddha sīmā and reside anywhere within its boundaries at dawn. The ticīvara does not need to be within the monk's hatthapāsa. However, when approving a baddha sīmā as an avippavāsa sīmā, the village areas (gāma) and village proximity (gāmūpacāra) within the baddha sīmā should be excluded. These areas, although part of the baddha sīmā, cannot be designated as avippavāsa zones. The avippavāsa sammuti should be given after the proper establishment of the baddha sīmā.
      • 3. Khaṇḍa Sīmā and Mahā Sīmā: When performing a disciplinary act within a baddha sīmā, all bhikkhus present in the area must gather in the assembly or send their consent. However, gathering all the bhikkhus and obtaining their consent can be challenging in large baddha sīmās. To address this, small sīmā zones, known as khaṇḍa sīmā, are established within the larger baddha sīmā to facilitate the performance of vinayakammas other than uposatha and pavāraṇā easily. The larger sīmā that encompasses the khaṇḍa sīmās is called the mahā sīmā.

  • calendar studies #edited
    • days and hours
      • a divasa (day) is the duration between one dawn to the next and comprises 60 ghaṭikā (buddhist hours) with each of them being equivalent to 24 minutes in modern standards
    • fortnights and months
      • one pakkha is the period of fifteen consecutive days either between the full and new moon, or between the new and full moon; the waning period is the kālapakkha while the waxing period is the sukkapakkha; both together constitute a māsa (month); the month commences with kālapakkha and concludes with sukkapakkha, ensuring that each month concludes with a full moon; however, according to the Abhidhānappadīpikā, a month begins with sukkapakkha and ends with kālapakkha in one year there are 18 pakkhas with 15 days and 6 pakkhas with 14 days
    • seasons
      • in one year there are 12 months 4 months collectively form an utu (season) in one year there are 3 seasons: vassāna / vassa / pāvusa(rainy season), hemanta (winter) and gimhāna / gimha / uṇha / nidāgha (summer) each season can be further divided into 2 smaller seasons known as khuddaka utu each small season comprises 2 months totalling 6 small seasons: vassāna, sarada, hemanta, sisira, vasanta, gimhāna
    • names of the months
      • vassāna utu vassānamāsa 1st: first month is called sāvaṇa or nikkhamanīya 2nd: second month is called poṭṭhapāda saradamāsa 3rd: third month is called assayuja or pubbakattika 4th: fourth month is called kattika or pacchimakattika hemanta utu hemantamāsa 5th: first month is called māgasira 6th: second month is called phussa sisiramāsa 7th: third month is called māgha 8th: fourth month is called phagguna gimhāna utu vasantamāsa 9th: first month is called citta or rammaka 10th: second month is called vesākha gimhānamāsa 11th: third month is called jeṭṭha 12th: fourth month is called āsāḷha or āsāḷhī
    • sun's course
      • during the three small seasons, namely vassāna, sarada, and hemanta, the sun follows the dakkhiṇāyana (southern path) during the sisira, vasanta, and gimhāna small seasons, it travels along the uttarāyana (northern path) A vassa (year) is completed when the sun completes its journey along both paths once
      • vocab: #[[pāḷi vocab (1st year) ]]
