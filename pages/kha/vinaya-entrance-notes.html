    • Pabbajjā: Ordination
      • background
        • During the early period ordination (pabbajjā) and higher ordination (upasampadā) were performed concurrently. Later on the Buddha declared them as two separate disciplinary acts.
        • Two types of monk status
          • sāmaṇera/ī
          • upasampadā
          • (for women there is also sikkhamāna in between sāmaṇerī and bhikkhunī)
      • sāmaṇera pabbajjā (ordination of novices)
        • how is the candidate for ordination called?→pabbajjāpekkha
        • 3 main sections and subsections of pabbajjā
          • 1. bhaṇḍukamma / kesacchedana (shaving act)
            • informing the saṅgha
            • shaving
            • bathing
            • requesting ordination
          • 2. kāsāvacchādana (wearing the robes)
            • giving the robes to the teacher
            • requesting the robes back
            • wearing the robes
          • 3. saraṇadāna (undertaking refuge)
            • requesting refuge and precepts
            • giving refuge and precepts
          • after ordination: upajjhāyaggāha
          • note: all 3 can be called pabbajjā
        • who can perform each step of the process of pabbajjā?

          • Is ordination valid if refuge is taken without wearing robes?→no, it is a basic factor for pabbajjā to be valid

            • The robes from the candidate’s hands should be given to the preceptor
              • can pabbajjā be done in another language?→All except the saraṇadāna can be done in another language but it is traditionally done in pāḷi #important

          • Wearing the robes
            • After having done cīvarapaccavekkhaṇa the candidate should wear the robes. The teacher or another bhikkhu or sāmaṇera on his order should assist him.
            • Obtaining the robes from the preceptor (indirectly by a bhikkhu or sāmaṇera under the orders of the teacher is also valid) is a compulsory matter in ordination.

      • 10 sikkhāpada (10 precepts)
        • 1st pāṇātipātā veramaṇī sikkhāpadaṁ samādiyāmi
          • 5 sambhāra (factors): #important
            • pāṇo (object is a living being)
            • pāṇasaññī (perception of a living being)
            • vadhakacittaṁ (intention to kill)
            • upakkamo (means to kill)
            • tena jīvitanāso / tena maraṇaṁ (by that (effort) that life is destroyed) #q🙋🏼‍♂
            • Stanza: "Pāṇo have pāṇasaññī, vadhakacittamupakkamo, tena jīvitanāso ca, aṅgā pañca vadhassime."
          • If the being dies even after several years from a blow struck with the intention to kill the precept will be broken with effect from the very instant of striking.
        • 2nd adinnādānā veramaṇī sikkhāpadaṁ samādiyāmi
          • 5 sambhāra #important
            • 1. parapariggahitaṃ (An object that belongs to another human)
            • 2. parapariggahitasaññitā (perceiving the object as belonging to another human)
            • 3. theyyacittaṃ (intention to steal it)
            • 4. upakkamo (attempt on stealing it)
            • 5. tena haraṇaṃ (taking the object by that attempt)
          • 4 exceptions to stealing: #important
            • sakasaññā (as belonging to oneself)
            • paṁsukūlasaññā (one perceives the object as discarded, ownerless)
            • vissāsaggāhī (taking on trust)
            • tāvakālikasaññā (taking temporarily)
          • There is no freedom to take other's properties because a loss has been cause to you. If a thief steals one's property, forcing it to be returned will break the precept but if one still perceives the property as belonging to oneself one may get it back
