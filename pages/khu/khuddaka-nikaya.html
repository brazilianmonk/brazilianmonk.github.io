    • A brief introduction to Suttanta #edited
      • Suttanta has five nikāyas:
        • Dīgha Nikāya
        • Majjhima Nikāya
        • Saṁyutta Nikāya
        • Aṅguttara Nikāya
        • Khuddhaka Nikāya
      • Khuddhakapāṭha (Short passages) is the first book in the Khuddhaka Nikāya, arranged for newcomers or novices and has 9 short passages that are traditionally commit to memory. The 9 and their functions are:
        • Saraṇagamana (refuge taking) as the entrance to the sāsana
        • Dasasikkhāpadāni (the ten training rules) for the undertaking of virtue
        • Dvattiṁsākāra (the thirty two body parts) for removing lust
        • Sāmaṇerapañhā / Kumārapañhā (the novice's questions) for removing delusion
        • Maṅgalasutta (The discourse on blessings) for self-protection
        • Ratanasutta (The discourse on gems) for protecting onself and others
        • Tirokuḍḍasutta (The discourse on hungry ghosts outside the walls) for showing the dangers of unwholesome
        • Nidhikaṇḍasutta (The discourse on treasure) for showing the benefits of the wholesome
        • Mettāsutta (The discourse on benevolence) for removing hatred

      • What is the Triple Gem and why is it so called?
        • Buddha: the originator, producer, declarer, knower, discoverer of the path, the one skilled in it. He teaches it to others and thus they can attain freedom from suffering.
        • Dhamma: the nature, the law of nature, the truth and the virtue. Weather Buddhas arise or not the Dhamma persists.
        • Saṅgha: the community of those who practice and who have reached the truth.
      • 4 meaning of the word saraṇa (either with the root raṇ or sar)
        • "with conflict": said in Araṇavibhaṅgasutta with regard to the pursuit of self-mortification (attakilamathānuyoga)
          • According to saddattha (meaning of the word): "hiṁsatīti saraṇaṁ" ⇒ That which destroys is saraṇa. Here in the sense of destruction of defilements.
          • raṇaṁ (nt.) √raṇ↔conflict; trouble; harm; (comm) defilement #[[pāḷi vocab (1st year) ]]
          • saraṇa (adj.)↔harmful; malign; unpeaceful; (comm) defiled; lit. with conflict #[[pāḷi vocab (1st year) ]]
        • "dwelling"
        • "something used as protection"
          • According to vohārattha (meaning of the expression) it means something used as protection.
        • "protection"
          • saraṇaṁ (nt.) √sar↔shelter; refuge; help; lit. going to; remembering #[[pāḷi vocab (1st year) ]]
      • 2 types of meaning (attha)
        • saddattha: meaning of the word
        • vohārattha: meaning of the expression
      • Gacchāmi in the sentence Buddhaṁ saraṇaṁ gacchāmi not only means I go but also I know.
      • 2 types of saraṇagamana
        • lokiyasaraṇagamana (the taking of refuge after the suppression of skepticism and wrong views)
          • attasanniyyātanasaraṇagamana (self-dedication/relinquishment)
            • "From today onwards may the Venerable remember me thus: I offer my life to the Buddha, Dhamma and Saṅgha"
          • tapparāyanatāsaraṇagamana (taking the Triple Gem as the main, chief object in one's life)
            • "I rely on the Buddha, Dhamma and Saṅgha."
            • Often appear in the canon: "Esāhaṃ, bhante, taṃ bhagavantaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi dhammañca bhikkhusaṅghañca. Upāsakaṃ maṃ bhagavā dhāretu ajjatagge pāṇupetaṃ saraṇaṃ gata"nti.
            • Aḷavakayakkha: "so ahaṃ vicarissāmi, gāmā gāmaṃ purā puraṃ,namassamāno sambuddhaṃ, dhammassa ca sudhammata"nti.
            • One of the three types of upasampadā
          • sissabhāvūpagamanasaraṇagamana (by becoming a student)
            • "I am an apprentice of the Buddha, Dhamma and Saṅgha"
            • Mahākassapa: "Satthā me bhante bhagavā sāvakohamasmi."
          • paṇipāta saraṇagamana (tappoṇatta) (by means of prostration and adoration)
            • "From today onwards may the Venerable remember me thus: I will salute, revere, pay homage and respect the Buddha, Dhamma and Saṅgha."
            • "Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa"
            • tappoṇa (adj.)↔sloping towards that #[[pāḷi vocab (1st year) ]]
            • poṇa (adj.) [pa + ava + √nam + a]↔(+acc) sloping down (to); inclining down (to); leading (to) #[[pāḷi vocab (1st year) ]]
        • lokuttarasaraṇagamana (supramundane refuge established in the first noble path)
          • 3 defilements eradicated at sotāpattimagga
            • sakkāyadiṭṭhi (personality view)
            • vicikicchā (skepticism)
            • sīlabbataparāmāsa (adherence to rites and rituals)
          • 7 ariyadhana (noble treasures)
            • saddhā (faith)
            • sīla (morality)
            • hiri (moral shame)
            • ottappa (moral dread)
            • suta (learning)
            • cāga (generosity)
            • paññā (wisdom)
      • After having taken refuge in one of those 4 ways (attasanniyātana...etc) one is considered an upāsaka as it is said: "yato kho, mahānāma, buddhaṃ saraṇaṃ gato hoti, dhammaṃ... saṅghaṁ... – ettāvatā kho, mahānāma, upāsako hotī"ti.
      • 4 causes of pollution of mundane refuge
        • Aññāṇa: taking refuge without understanding the Triple Gem
        • Saṁsaya: taking it with doubt
        • Micchāñāṇa = taking the refuge with misunderstanding such as begging or wishing to get something from the Buddha, etc.
        • Anādara = taking the refuge without respect.
      • 2 types of break up of mundane refuge
        • anavajja (blameless): by death
        • sāvajja (blameworthy): by rejecting the Triple Gem and accepting other as refuge.
          • even though one pays homage to other religious people thinking they are our relatives or to learn some arts or knowledge the refuge is not broken. The refuge is broken by taking something else as the ultimate refuge.
      • Four causes of prostration (paṇipāta):
        • ñāti (relative),
        • bhaya (fear),
        • ācariya (teacher),
        • dakkhiṇeyya (one who is worthy of gift): refuge taking is done by prostrating to dakkhiṇeyyas only, not to the others.
      • 5 similes for the Tiple Gem
        • The Buddha like the moon, the sun, a rain cloud, a charioteer, a surgeon
        • The Dhamma like the moonlight, the sunlight, the rain, the method to tame the horses, the method to pull out the dart of wrong views.
        • The Saṅgha like the world receiving the moon/sunlight/rain which settles dust, like tamed horses, like those who have been cured
      • 4 meanings of dhamma
        • dhamma - this refers to truth, nature, principle or reality such as nāmas and rūpas
        • dhammatā - this refers to natural laws which are categorized into 5:
          • utuniyāma - law of temperature
          • bījaniyāma - law of seeds 🌱
          • cittaniyāma - law of consciousness
          • kammaniyāma - law of volitional actions
          • dhammaniyāma - law of dhammas
        • dhammadesanā - refers to the theoretical aspect of the Teaching, the study of texts (pariyatti)
        • dhammacariyā - refers to the practical aspect of the Teaching (paṭipatti)

    • "tīṇi nāma kiṃ? tisso vedanā" (What is three? The three feelings) #[[khuddaka exam]]
      • Vedanākkhandha
        • Definition: "Vedayatīti kho, bhikkhave, tasmā vedanā'ti vuccati" - It feels so it is called feeling
        • vedayati √vid↔(+acc) feels; experiences; senses; lit. causes to know
        • Feeling has the characteristic of experiencing the object and has the function of sensing the flavour of the object
        • Since feeling accompanies every citta it could be said that there are 89 or 121 feelings as well.
        • 3 types of feeling
          • sukha (pleasant)
          • dukkha (painful / unpleasant)
          • adukkhamasukha / upekkhā (neither painful nor pleasant / neutral)
        • Feeling does not like or dislike the object only feels it in a "passive" manner. Liking and disliking are functions of formations.
        • Feeling itself feels the flavor of the object not me, a self, a soul or a being. This shows the doctrine of anatta.
        • The term upekkhā can be used to refer to two dhammas:
          • tatramajjhattatā cetasika (specific neutrality) which is in the aggregate of formations
          • adukkhamasukhavedanā (neither painful nor pleasant feeling) which is in the aggregate of feeling.
        • "cakkhuñca paṭicca rūpe ca uppajjati cakkhuviññāṇaṁ, tiṇṇaṁ saṅgati phasso, phassapaccayā vedanā..."
          • paṭicca↔1: ger. (+acc) dependent; depending (on); because (of); supported (by) #[[pāḷi vocab (1st year) ]]
        • The main cause for vedanā is phassa as it is said: "phassapaccayā vedanā"
        • If the object is desirable pleasant feeling arises; if the object is undesirable painful feeling arises and when the object is not very evident neuter feeling arises.
        • Lust may arise for the desirable object, hatred for the undesirable object and delusion for the obscure object.
        • All feelings are considered as suffering by the Buddha as it is said: “yaṃ kiñci vedayitaṃ, taṃ dukkhasmiṃ" (whatever feeling it may be, it is included in suffering).
        • Why are all three types of feeling suffering? It is because feelings are conditioned states. Conditioned states have the nature of impermanence and whatever is impermanent is suffering (yadaniccaṃ taṃ dukkhaṃ).
          • Vajirā bhikkhunī said:
            • “It’s only suffering that comes to be, Suffering that stands and falls away Nothing but suffering comes to be Nothing but suffering ceases.”
        • Feeling itself should not be abandoned. What should be abandoned is the craving for it.
        • In which of the 4 Noble Truths is feeling classified?→1st Noble Truth which should be fully understood.
        • What should be done with regard to pleasant, unpleasant and neutral feeling?
          • The pleasant feeling should be seen as suffering and the underlying tendency to lust to it should be abandoned. The unpleasant feeling should be seen as as a dart and the underlying tendency to aversion to it should be abandoned. The neutral feeling should be seen as impermanent and the underlying tendency to delusion to it should be abandoned.
    • "cattāri nāma kiṃ? cattāri ariyasaccāni" (What is four? The four noble truths) #[[khuddaka exam]]
      • cattāri ariyasaccāni (4 Noble Truths) #[[khuddaka exam]]
        • What are the Four Noble Truths
          • Dukkhaṁ ariyasaccaṁ (the noble truth of suffering)
            • Rebirth is suffering; old age...death...sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and distress...not getting what you wish for is suffering. In brief, the five grasping aggregates are suffering.
          • Dukkhasamudayaṁ ariyasaccaṁ (the noble truth of the origin of suffering)
            • It’s the craving:
              • kāmataṇhā (craving for sense pleasures)
              • bhavataṇhā (craving for existence)
              • vibhavataṇhā (craving for non-existence)
          • Dukkhanirodhaṁ ariyasaccaṁ (the noble truth of the cessation of suffering)
            • It’s the fading away and cessation of that very same craving with nothing remaining.
          • Dukkhanirodhagāminī paṭipadā ariyasaccaṁ (the noble truth of the way leading to the cessation of suffering)
            • Noble eightfold path: right view, right thought, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration.
        • Why are they called Noble Truths? (3)
          • because the Noble Ones penetrate them
            • i.e. the Buddhas, Paccekabuddhas and Arahants, etc.
          • because they are the Truths of Noble Ones
            • as it is said “sadevake loke samārake sabrahmake sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiyā pajāya sadevamanussāya tathāgato ariyo, tasmā ‘ariyasaccānī’ti vuccanti"." (In this world, with its devas, Māra, and Brahma, in this generation with its ascetics and brahmins, its devas and humans, the Tathāgata is the noble one)
          • because the Noble Truths are always real, truth and cannot be changed
          • mem aid: pen of unchanging
        • The four functions regarding the Four Noble Truths
          • The Noble Truth of suffering should be fully understood (pariññeyya ⇒ pariññāta)
          • The Noble Truth of the origin of Suffering should be abandoned (pahātabba ⇒ pahīna)
          • The Noble Truth of the cessation of suffering should be realized (sacchikātabba ⇒ sacchikata)
          • The Noble Truth of the path leading to the cessation of suffering should be developed (bhāvetabba ⇒ bhavita)
        • The three ñāṇa (knowledges), three parivaṭṭa (rounds/levels) and twelve ākāra (aspects): "yāvakīvañca me, bhikkhave, imesu catūsu ariyasaccesu evaṃ tiparivaṭṭaṃ dvādasākāraṃ yathābhūtaṃ ñāṇadassanaṃ na suvisuddhaṃ ahosi..."
