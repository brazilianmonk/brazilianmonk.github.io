    • 4 features of the passages in the Udāna
      • jinavacanabhūta: they must be uttered by the Buddha Himself
      • gāthāpaṭisaṃyutta: they must be composed of verses
      • pītisaṁvega: caused either by somanassañāṇa or dhammasaṃvega
      • udāna: included in the udāna section among the navaṅgasatthusāsana

        • "When phenomena become manifest to the ardent, concentrated brahmin, all his doubts vanish, for he understands those phenomena and their causes."
      • 1.What does Uruvelā mean?
        • Uruvelā literally means 'the great heap of sand' (mahante vālukarāsimhi) or 'the sand carried due to the transgression of moral boundaries' (velātikkamanahetu ābhatā uru uruvelāti). It is said that before the Buddha’s appearance in the world, ten thousand ascetics lived in this place and established a rule: if any evil thought arose in the mind of one of them, that person would carry a basket of sand to a designated spot. They enacted this rule because transgressions in bodily and verbal actions were visible, but mental transgressions were not. Over time, the place gradually filled with sand. Later, people used this sand to create a pagoda.
      • 2.How many meanings of the term "nerañjarā"? What are they? Nerañjarā is of three explanations:
        • 2.1. The water that is without fault or is pleasant due to being free from mud, moss, algae, and so on (nelaṃ jalaṃ assāti nelañjalā, with the 'r' substituted by 'l').
        • 2.2. It has blue water (nīlaṃ jalāyāti should have been said, but nerañjarāyāti is used).
        • 2.3. It is simply the name of the river.

            • āsaṃso (full of expectation): moral, of good character, possessing good qualities, has the expectation of attaining maggaphala
              • e.g.: like eldest son of a king expects to become a king
            • vitāso (who has overcome expectation): arahant
              • simile: a king no longer wonders when he will become a king
