## Introduction

<a id="orga7c59bd"></a>

### Itivuttaka &#x2013; meaning


the text beginning with "thus (it was) said" &#x2013; **vuttaṃ hetaṃ bhagavatā vuttamarahatāti me suttaṃ** (this indeed was said by the Blessed One, said by the Arahant, thus was heard by me)



<a id="org9e66935"></a>

### Composition Style



-   mixture of prose and verse
-   verses rephrase what was said in the prose resembling the SN sagāthāvagga
    -   st, the verse amplifies an idea briefly mentioned in the prose
        st, only one verse is connected
-   notes:
    -   appears in navaṅgasatthusāsana: **itivuttakaṁ**
    -   resembles AN for following the order of ekakanipāta, dukanipāta, etc
    -   also found in the Chinese version but with only 3 vaggas
        -   some: remaining suttas may have been added later
        -   theravāda: all of them are authentic

      


<a id="org0ba223b"></a>

#### general structure



-   intro: vuttañhetaṁ bhagavatā vuttamarahatāti me sutaṁ (by Kujjuttarā)
    -   preaching place: not mentioned since they were all preched in Kosambī
        -   V. Ānanda's style: evaṁ me sutaṁ ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṁ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme
    -   listener: "bhikkhave" in every sutta seems they were preached to an assembly including monks
        -   V. Ānanda's style: tena kho pana samayena rājā Magadho…
-   descriptions
-   verse: normally rephrasing the prose
-   conclusion

      


<a id="org6701bf2"></a>

#### navaṅgasatthusāsana (extra)

-   **suttaṃ** (prose): tattha ubhatovibhhaṅga-niddesa-khandhakaparivārā suttanipāte maṅgalasutta-ratanasutta-nālakasutta-tuvaṭṭakasuttāni aññampi ca suttanāmakaṃ tathāgatavacanaṃ suttanti veditabbaṃ
-   **geyyaṃ** (prose and verse): sabbampi sagāthakaṃ suttaṃ geyyanti veditabbaṃ
-   **veyyākaraṇaṃ** (detailed expositions): visesena saṃyuttake sakalopi sagāthāvaggo, sakalaṃ abhidhammapiṭakaṃ, niggāthakaṃ suttaṃ, yañca aññampi aṭṭhahi aṅgehi asaṅgahitaṃ buddhavacanaṃ taṃ veyyākaraṇanti veditabbaṃ
-   **gāthā** (verses): dhammapadaṃ, theragāthā, therīgāthā, suttanipāte nosuttanāmikā suddhikagāthā ca gāthāti veditabbaṁ
    -   includes petavatthupāḷi
-   **udānaṃ** (heartfelt exclamations): somanassañāṇamayikagāthāpaṭisaṃyuttā dvāsīti suttantā udānanti veditabbaṃ
-   **itivuttakaṃ** (quotations): vuttañhetaṃ bhagavatā"tiādinayappavattā dasuttarasatasuttantā itivuttakanti veditabbaṃ
-   **jātakaṃ** (birth stories): apaṇṇakajātakādīni paññāsādhikāni pañca jātakasatāni jātakanti veditabbaṃ
    -   note: comm: 550, but we only have 547 nowadays
-   **abbhutadhammaṃ** (marvellous accounts): "cattārome, bhikkhave, acchariyā abbhutā dhammā ānande"ti (DN) -ādinayappavattā sabbepi acchariyaabbhutadhammapaṭisaṃyuttā suttantā abbhutadhammanti veditabbaṃ
-   **vedallaṃ** (analyses / question and answers): cūḷavedalla-mahāvedalla-sammādiṭṭhi-sakkapañha-saṅkhārabhājaniya-mahāpuṇṇamasuttādayo sabbepi vedañca tuṭṭhiñca laddhā laddhā pucchitasuttantā vedallanti veditabbaṃ

### Transmitter, Etadagga Title, Etc


Female lay devotee **Khujuttarā**

-   she had a curved back for having mocked a pckb in the past
-   **etadaggaṃ, bhikkhave, mama sāvikānaṃ upāsikānaṃ bahussutānaṃ yadidaṃ Khujjuttarā**
-   possessor of sekhapaṭisambhidā
-   declared, together with Veḷukaṇṭakī Nandamātā, as an ideal model for female lay followers
-   heard them from the Buddha in Kosambī, where she lived, and prached them to Queen Sāmāvatī and her 500 attendants in the royal chamber
-   later, Khujuttarā taught them to the bhikkhunīs who passed them to the monks (V. Ānanda)
    -   (*:q: what about the condition that the Buddha would repeat everything to V. ānanda? some things, like this case, do not seem to be included*)
    -   (*:q: V. ānanda did not mention source in his explanation of where the 84k dhammakkhandha came from*)
-   she introduced all the suttas with the introductory words (vuttaṁ hetaṁ bhagavatā…), which were preserved by V. Ānanda in the 1st council

#### Sukkadhammasuttaṃ

1.  Hiri Ottappa

    1.  Meaning
    
        
        -a:
        
        -   **Hiri**: that which feels or by which one feels shame of what one should be ashamed of (akusala, misconduct)
            -   dhsgn: shame of the commission of bad, unwholesome states
        -   **Ottappa**: that which feels or by which one feels fear of the consequences of what should be feared (akusala, misconduct)
            -   dhsgn: fear of the commission of bad, unwholesome states
        
        Abhi: lakkhaṇādi:
        
        -   **definition**
            -   kāyaduccaritādīhi hiriyatīti hirī. lajjāyetaṃ adhivacanaṃ.
            -   tehiyeva ottappatīti ottappaṃ. pāpato ubbegassetaṃ adhivacanaṃ.
        -   **lakkhaṇa**
            -   pāpato jigucchanalakkhaṇā hirī: disgust of evil
            -   uttāsanalakkhaṇaṃ ottappaṃ: dread of evil
                -   also toward
                    -   one's own and other's criticism
                    -   resulting suffering in apāya
        -   **kiccarasa**
            -   lajjākārena pāpānaṃ akaraṇarasā hirī: shying away from doing evil
            -   uttāsākārena ottappaṃ: fearing away from doing evil
        -   **paccupaṭṭhāna**
            -   vuttappakāreneva ca pāpato saṅkocanapaccupaṭṭhānā etā: shrinking away from, not engaging in, withdrawing from, avoiding evil
        -   **padaṭṭhāna**
            -   hiri: attagārava; attānaṃ garuṃ katvā hiriyā pāpaṃ jahāti: respect for oneself
                -   kulavadhū viya: like a good daughter-in-law in a good family
                    -   she's shy and afraid of doing anything wrong
            -   ottappa: paragārava; paraṃ garuṃ katvā ottappena pāpaṃ jahāti: respect for others:
                -   vesiyā viya: like a prostitute
                    -   teacher: maybe because they are afraid of societal reproach
            -   **attānaṁ garuṁ katvā, hiriyā pāpaṁ jahāti
                paraṁ garuṁ katvā, ottappena pāpaṁ jahāti**
        
        Vitthatasutta: ariyas have hiri ottappa bala
        
        English renderings
        
        -   moral shame and moral dread/fear
        -   conscientiousness/conscience and embarrassment
        
        
    
    2.  Hiri vs. Ottappa: 4 Differences
    
        
        
        -   **Samuṭṭhāna** (origin)
            -   **ajjhattasamuṭṭhānā hirī** (internal / self-reflection): based on 4 considerations
                -   on **jāti**: evil is for those of low birth, not of good birth like me
                -   on **vaya**: evil is for the immature, not for the mature like me
                -   on **sūrabhāva**: evil is for the weak, not for the brave like me
                -   on **bahussuta**: evil is for the uneducated, not for the learned like me
            -   **bahiddhāsamuṭṭhānaṃ ottappaṃ** (external): avoiding evil deeds by reflecting on others' criticism
        -   **Adhipateyya** (dependence)
            -   **attādhipateyyā hirī** (severeignty of the self): avoiding evil deeds reflecting on one's own qualities
            -   **lokādhipateyyaṃ ottappaṃ** (sovereignty of the world): avoiding evil deeds reflecting on the criticism of those who have dibbacakkhu, cetopariyañāṇa, etc
        -   **Sabhāvasaṇṭhita** (established through)
            -   **lajjāsabhāvasaṇṭhitā hirī** (the nature of shame): disgust toward duccarita like the wise toward the impurity of a cold iron ball smeared with excrement
            -   **bhayasabhāvasaṇṭhitaṃ ottappaṃ** (the nature of fear): fear of woeful realms like the wise toward touching a hot iron ball
        -   **Lakkhaṇa** (characteristic)
            -   **sappatissavalakkhaṇā hirī** (deference): 4 paccavekkhaṇas under vīriyasambojjhaṅga:
                -   **jātimahatta paccavekkhaṇa**: on the greatness of one's lineage
                    -   Kings Ukkāka, Mahāsammata, Suddhodana, Queen Mahāmāyā, Prince Rāhula, etc.)
                -   **satthumahatta paccavekkhaṇa**: on the greatness of the Buddha
                -   **dāyajjamahatta paccavekkhaṇa**: on the greatness of one’s inheritance (ariyadhanas)
                    -   an evil son is disowned by his parents and doesn't get the inheritance;
                        an evil person doesn't get the ariyadhanas
                -   **sabrahmacārīmahattapaccavekkhaṇa**: on the greatness of fellow companions (Vs. Sāriputta, Mahāmoggallāna, etc)
                    vīriya-sambojjhaṅga
            -   **vajjabhīrukabhayadassāvilakkhaṇaṃ ottappaṃ**: seeing danger or faults due to:
                -   **attānuvāda-bhaya**: fear of self-reproach
                -   **parānuvāda-bhaya**: fear of others' reproach
                -   **daṇḍa-bhaya**: fear of punishment
                -   **duggati-bhaya**: and fear of a bad destination
            -   also:
                -   pāpato jigucchanalakkhaṇā hirī
                -   uttāsanalakkhaṇaṃ ottappaṃ
        -   notes:
            -   they become clear (pākaṭabhāva) in different ways in different contexts
            -   niyatayogī: always together

    3.  4 Epithets
    
        
        
        -   **Sukkadhamma**: bright dhammas
            -   **Sukkamūla**: root of brightness (kusala)
        -   **Lokapāladhamma**: protectors of the world
        -   **Devadhamma**: dhammas that lead to the 3 devas
        
        
        
        1.  Sukkadhamma
        
            
            Hiriottappa are called **sukkadhamma** because they:
            
            -   produce sukka (brightness)
            -   lead to
                -   sukkabhāva: state of brightness
                -   paramavodānā: extreme purity
            
            Hiriottappa are also called **sukkamūla** because they are the cause (mūla) for kusala (sukka) to arise and increase
            
            -   all kusala are sukka for
                -   opposing the darkness of akusala
                    -   AN kaṇhasutta: ahirika and anottappa: kaṇhadhamma
                -   makinging the mind
                    -   pabhassara (brilliant)
                    -   parisuddha (pure)
            
            Notes:
            
            -   other factors leading to hiriottappa
                -   AN satisampajaññasutta: kusala => satisampajañña => hiriottappa
                -   AN vijjāsutta: paññā => kusala => hiriottappa
                -   AN samāpattisutta
                    -   no hiriottappa => akusalakamma
                    -   kusala => hiriottappa => no akusalakamma
                -   Combination: **paññā => kusala => satisampajañña => hiriottappa => no akusalakamma**
            -   hiriottappa leading to other factors
                -   hiriottappasutta:
                    -   hiriottappa => saṁvarasīla => samādhi => yathābhūtañāṇadassana => virāga => vimuttiñāṇadassana
                    -   hiriottappa => brahmacariya
                    -   simile: hiriottappa (sprout) => growth of the other factors (tree)
            
            
        
        2.  Lokapāladhamma
        
            
            So called because they
            
            -   safeguard
                -   one's own well-being
                -   society by contributing to decency and orderliness
            -   form the foundation of morality
            
            Without them: hiriottapparahita and bhinnamariyādā => no garucittakāra => promiscuity and incest like animals
            
            -   garucittakāra: mental respect (for social and family roles such as mother, aunt, teacher’s wife, etc)
            -   bhinnamariyādā: broken (moral) boundaries

#### Nibbānadhātusuttaṃ

1.  Purposes for Classifications of Nibbāna

    
    In the ultimate sense, Nibbāna is indivisible, just one, so its classification into types is a figurative didactic tool to explain:
    
    -   those who attain it according to the way it is experienced
    -   various properties of Nibbāna itself
    -   note:
    
    

2.  2 Nibbānadhātus

    
    
    -   **Saupādisesā nibbānadhātu** (element of Nibbāna with remaning khandhas): arahat's extinguishing of defilements leaving remaning khandhas, which allows undefiled sensory experience
        -   **idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu arahaṃ hoti… tassa tiṭṭhanteva pañcindriyāni yesaṃ avighātattā manāpāmanāpaṃ paccanubhoti, sukhadukkhaṃ paṭisaṃvedeti. tassa yo rāgakkhayo, dosakkhayo, mohakkhayo**
            -   5 faculties unimpaired until death, allowing the experience of what is agreeable/disagreeable, pleasurable/painful
                -   shows the arahat’s contact with the world
            -   eradication of rāga, dosa, and moha in him
                -   shows sensory experience without defilements (ariya-iddhi) and that does not form defiled habits
                -   corresponds to kilesa-parinibbāna: arahant's complete extinguishing of defilements
        -   **ekā hi dhātu idha diṭṭhadhammikā,
            saupādisesā bhavanettisaṅkhayā**
            -   experienced in this life and exhausts the craving that leads to future existence
        -   note: term rarely used referring to an event
    -   **Anupādisesā nibbānadhātu** (element of Nibbāna without remaning khandhas): arahat's extinguishing of khandhas, in which all experience is extinguished
        -   **idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu arahaṃ hoti… tassa idheva, bhikkhave, sabbavedayitāni anabhinanditāni sīti bhavissanti**
            -   sabbavedayitāni: all that is felt
                -   kusalākusalavedanā are abandoned with arahattamagga
                -   subsequent abyākatavedanās are abandoned with parinibbāna
                -   vedanā is mentioned but includes all other khandhas
            -   anabhinanditāni: not delighted in with craving
            -   sīti bhavissanti: will be cooled/extinguished
                -   no chance for existence to arise again after the Arahant’s death
            -   corresponds to khandhaparinibbāna: arahat's complete extinguishing of khandhas at death
                -   end of bondage to the khandhas, cessation of rebirth
        -   **anupādisesā pana samparāyikā,
            yamhi nirujjhanti bhavāni sabbaso**
            without residue for the future, in which all forms of existence completely cease
            -   samparāyikā: after the breakup of the khandhas (as opposed to diṭṭhadhammikā)
        -   notes:
            -   term more commonly used
            -   other related passages:
                -   DN Mahāparinibbānasutta: no matter how many attain anupādisesanibbāna, Nibbāna is neither filled nor depleted, like rivers and rain do not fill or deplete the ocean
                -   Ud Lokasutta:
                    -   **Sabbaso taṇhānaṃ khayā, asesavirāganirodho nibbānaṃ,**
                        **Tassa nibbutassa bhikkhuno, anupādā punabbhavo na hoti**
                        Through the total destruction of cravings,
                        the fading away and cessation (of craving) without remainder is Nibbāna;
                        for the bhikkhu who is quenched, due to the absence of clinging, there is no renewal of becoming
    -   notes:
        -   upādi: here, pañcakhandha
        -   saupādisesa: rarely used
            anupādisesa: commonly used
        -   this classification of Nibbāna explains the beings who attain it, not Nibbāna itself

#### Sikkhānisaṃsasuttaṃ

1.  4 Principles Leading to Aññā / Anāgāmitā

    1.  Sikkhānisaṃsā
    
        
        AN sikkhānisaṃsasutta:
        
        -   **Ādibrahmacariyika-sikkhā**: training in mahāsīlas, which are fundamental to the Noble Path
            -   purpose: sabbaso sammā dukkhakkhayāya: for the utterly complete destruction of suffering
            -   -a: 4 mahāsīla (maybe the 4 pārājikā), the foundation (ādibhūta) for the maggabrahmacariya
        -   **Ābhisamācārika-sikkhā**: training pertaining to superior good conduct such as duties
            -   purpose:
                -   appasannānaṁ pāsādāya: so that those without confidence gain confidence
                -   pasannānaṁ bhiyyobhāvāya: those with confidence increase in their confidence
            -   -a: types of virtuous behaviour promulgated by way of duties (vattavasena paññattasīlassetaṃ adhivacanaṃ)
        
        How to live with this quality: keeping it unbroken, flawless, unblemished, and unblotched
        
        Sikkhā in sikkhānisaṃsā:
        
        -   AN-a: adhisīla-sikkhā
        -   Itv-a: adhisīla-, adhicitta-, and adhipaññā-sikkhā
        
        One should live the Noble Life for the purpose of the threefold training, not for the sake of gain, fame, honour, and so forth
        
    
    2.  Paññuttarā
    
        
        AN Sikkhānisaṃsasutta:
        
        -   purpose of the teaching: utter destruction of suffering
        -   how: scrutinize the teachings with wisdom for this purpose
        -   itvtk-a: wisdom: highest and foremost training, especially sahavipassanāmaggapaññā, which eradicates defilements and ends suffering
            -   in N8P: sammādiṭṭhi: first factor because it serves as
                -   the forerunner of the entire path
                -   the guide for all the other factors from beginning to end
        
        
    
    3.  Vimuttisārā
    
        
        AN Sikkhānisaṃsasutta:
        
        -   purpose of the teaching: utter destruction of suffering
        -   how: liberation -> experience of the teaching as it was taught
        
        Itv-a:
        
        -   vimutti (liberation): the goal or essence (sāra) of the Dhammavinaya: here, arahattaphala or Nibbāna
        -   Uposathasutta: ocean has one taste: salt; Dhammavinaya has one taste: vimutti (liberation)
        
        
    
    4.  Satādhipateyyā
    
        
        AN Sikkhānisaṃsasutta: sati as dependence by being well established internally in 3 aspects in order to carry them out:
        
        -   related to sikkhā (under sikkhānisaṁsā)
            -   to **fulfill** the as-yet unfulfilled training
            -   to **assist with wisdom** the fulfilled training
        -   related to paññā (under paññuttarā)
            -   **scrutinize with wisdom** the as-yet unscrutinized teachings
            -   **assist with wisdom** the scrutinized teachings
        -   related to vimutti (under vimuttisārā)
            -   **experience through liberation** the as-yet unexperienced Dhamma
            -   **assist with wisdom** the experienced Dhamma
        -   itvtk-a: mindfulness is foremost / supreme authority
            -   satipaṭṭhānas: **ekāyano … maggo sattānaṁ visuddhiyā … cattāro satipaṭṭhānā**
            -   **satiñca khvāhaṃ, bhikkhave, sabbatthikaṃ vadāmi**: mindfulness as always beneficial

#### Jāgariyasuttaṃ

1.  Jāgara

    1.  Jāgara, Jāgariya, and Jāgariyānuyoga
    
        
        
        -   **jāgara**: one who is awake, devoid of sleep, engaged in wakefulness, or engaged in the contemplation of a meditation object day and night
            -   Pṭs: one who remains awake and does not sleep:
                -   during the 3 divisions of the day (morning, afternoon, and evening)
                -   during the 1st (6-10 pm) and 3rd (2-6 am) watches of the night
                -   engages only in samaṇadhamma, purifying the mind of āvaraṇīya dhamma
                -   middle watch
                    -   lies down on the right side in the lion's posture
                    -   one foot upon the other
                    -   mindful and clearly comprehending
                    -   has noted in his mind the idea of rising: determining to rise at a certain time
        -   **jāgariya**: state or act of being awake
        -   **jāgariyānuyoga**: application or devotion to that wakefulness
        
        
        
        1.  Real Jāgariya
        
            
            AN 9, Saupādisesasutta:
            
            -   Deva asked:
                -   how many are asleep when others are awake?
                    how many are awake when others sleep?
                    by how many does one gather dust?
                    by how many is one purified?
            -   Buddha:
                -   5 are asleep when others are awake
                    -   5 faculties are asleep: 5 hindrances are awake
                    -   5 hindrances are asleep: 5 faculties are awake
                -   5 are awake when others sleep
                    -   one with 5 hindrances: asleep because of negligence and akusala
                    -   one with 5 faculties: awake because of diligence and kusala
                -   by 5 things, one gathers dust
                    -   by 5 hindrances, one gathers defilements
                -   by 5 things one is purified
                    -   by 5 faculties, one is purified
            -   Notes:
                -   awake:
                    -   5 indriyas active
                    -   5 nīvaraṇas asleep
                -   asleep:
                    -   5 nīvaraṇas awake
                    -   5 indriyas asleep
                -   asleep with nīvaraṇas => dust
                -   awake with indriyas => purity

#### Diṭṭhigatasuttaṃ

1.  Taṇhā & Diṭṭhi's Connection

    
    **Taṇhā & Diṭṭhi** are often connected for being closely related and interdependent
    
    -   mechanism: one views something favorably, desires to acquire it (taṇhā), sees it as worthy of acquisition (diṭthi), clings to it (taṇhā) or forms various opinions about it (diṭṭhi)
    -   related to each other:
        -   bhavataṇhā => bhavadiṭṭhi
        -   vibhavataṇhā => vibhavadiṭṭhi
    
       

2.  3 Individuals

    
    
    -   **Olīyanti eke**: some crave for and have eternalist views about existence, don't delight in Dhamma, which is to end existence, and get stuck wandering in saṁsāra
        -   **bhavataṇhā**: bhavārāma, bhavarata, bhavasammudita
        -   **bhavadiṭthi**: view that existence is desirable, a place of stability for the 'self'; sassatadiṭṭhi (eternalism)
    -   **Atidhāvanti eke**: some loathe existence and crave for and have anihilationist views about non-existence, and go too far, falling into ucchedadiṭṭhi (anihilationism)
        -   **vibhavataṇhā**: vibhavaṃ abhinandanti &#x2013; rejoice in non-existence as peaceful
        -   **vibhavadiṭṭhi**: self/world is annihilated after death; ucchedadiṭṭhi (anihilationism)
    -   **Cakkhumanto passati**: those with vision see
        -   **passati**:
            -   **bhūtaṃ bhūtato passati**: sees what has come to be (khandhas) as it really is (sa- and sāmañña- lakkhaṇas) together with their conditions (sappaccayanāmarūpadassana)
                -   bhūta: khandhas because they exist or come to be through the accumulation of causes
                -   encompasses up to taruṇavipassanā
                -   note: salakkhaṇa = sabhāvalakkhaṇa
            -   **bhūtassa nibbidāya virāgāya nirodhāya paṭipanno hoti**: practices disenchantment, dispassion, and cessation of bhūta
                -   nibbidāya: balavavipassanā
                -   virāgāya: magga
                -   nirodha: magga or anupādisesanibbāna

#### Vitakkasuttaṃ

2 thoughts that frequently occur (samudācaranti) to the Buddha:

-   **khemavitakka**
-   **pavivekavitakka**

1.  Khemavitakka

    
    **Khemavitakka**: thought of safety / that brings safety (for beings): abyāpāda- and avihiṁsā- vitakkas that precede (pubbaṅgama) and associate (sampayutta) the 1st jhāna
    
    -   connected with **karuṇā** (mainly), **mettā**, and **muditā**
        -   mettā (ass. abyāpādavitakka): promotes the welfare and happiness of beings
            -   abyāpādavitakka: imāyāhaṃ iriyāya na kiñci byābādhemi tasaṃ vā thāvaraṃ vā (by this posture, may I not oppress anyone, either frail (puthujjana and sekkha) or firm (arahat))
        -   karuṇā (ass. avihiṁsāvitakka): alleviates their suffering
        -   mechanism:
            -   mettā brings sukha, which is the opposing factor to dukkha, which is the object of karuṇā
                -   karuṇāvisayassa hi dukkhassa paṭipakkhabhūtaṃ sukhaṃ sattesu upasaṃharantī mettāpi pavattatīti
            -   abyāpāda- & avihiṁsā- vitakkas oppose the saṅkilesas, which bring byāpāda and vihiṁsā, and brings khema due to freedom from fear
            -   for such a person, everything is always peaceful and free from fear
    -   it is **pubbaṅgama** (it precedes) & **sampayutta** (associates) of the 1st jhāna mahākaruṇāsamāpatti and mettāsamāpatti
        -   only 1st jhāna due to vitakka

#### Sukhāvedanā dukkhato daṭṭhabbā


**Dukkhato**: should be seen as suffering due to change &#x2013; vipariṇāmadukkha

> **Sukhā vedanā ṭhitisukhā vipariṇāmadukkhā** (MN)  
> Pleasant feeling is pleasant as long as it lasts but pain when it changes

-   -a: simile: food mixed with poison; enjoyable while eating, painful when taking effect, so too sukhavedanā

All beings are:

-   **Sukhakāmā**: delight in pleasant feeling
-   **Dukkhapaṭikūlā**: aversed to painful feeling

**Sukhāya vedanāya rāgānusayo anuseti**: rāgānusaya tends toward, is attached to, pleasant feeling

-   if sukha is seen as:
    -   **nicca or sukha**: one cannot see it as dukkha or remove rāga
    -   **anicca or dukkha**: can see it as dukkha and remove rāga

  


<a id="orgc219ee4"></a>

#### Dukkhāvedanā sallato daṭṭhabbā


**Sallato**: dart, spike, thorn, that which pierces &#x2013; dukkhadukkha

-   -a: simile: a dart causes agony when entering, remaining lodged in, and being removed from the body, so too dukkhavedanā
-   Seeing dukkha as a dart:
    -   one is pierced only by the 1st dart, which is small and not poisoned
    -   one becomes disenchanted with feeling
    -   paṭighānusaya does not arise
-   Not seeing dukkha as a dart:
    -   one is pierced by the 2nd dart, which is larger and poisoned, since one constructs the notion of a self as the one who experiences painful feeling
    -   paṭighānusaya arises

**Dukkhāya vedanāya paṭighānusayo anuseti**: aversion tends to be aversed to painful feeling

-   Avoid:
    -   dosa toward dukkhavedanā
    -   escaping dukkhavedanā by seeking kāmasukha, which leads to rāgānusaya
        -   dukkha -> dosa -> seeking for sukha -> rāga

 


<a id="org80d3723"></a>

#### Adukkhamasukhā vedanā aniccato daṭṭhabbā


**Aniccato**: as impermanent &#x2013; saṅkhāradukkha (suffering due to formations)

-   Cūḷavedalla Sutta:
    -   sukha: ṭhitasukhā vipariṇāmadukkhā
    -   dukkha: ṭhitadukkhā vipariṇāmasukhā
        -   dukkha while present
        -   sukha when it changes
    -   adukkhamasukha: ñātasukhā aññātadukkhā
        -   sukha when known
        -   dukkha when not known
        -   notes:
            -   me:
                -   coming from pain, upekkhā feels sukha
                -   coming from sukha, upekkhā feels dukkha
                -   for the wise, upekkhā is sukha (peace, 4th jhāna, etc.)
                    -   for the one who overcomes attachment to sukha, upekkhā is higher sukha (coming from the 3rd jhāna to the 4th)
                -   for the fool, upekkhā is dukkha (lack of sukha, boring, etc.)
            -   Encyclopedia of Buddhism: V. Anālaya:
                -   sukha when known: during contemplation, it can be experienced as pleasurable; a pleasure derived from the presence of knowledge
                -   dukkha when not known: experienced as "boring", or leading to boredom, which leads to the pursuit of sense pleasures
-   **Sukhā & dukkhā**: conspicuous, easily seen (like lobha and dosa)
-   **Adukkhamasukha**: inconspicuous, not easily seen (like moha)
    -   Itv-a: conspicuous but barely obvious like an object in the dark
    -   occurs when sukhā and dukkhā are absent
    -   can be inferred as the opposite of sukha and dukkha or their absence
        -   simile: migapadavalañjananaya (inferring a deer’s footprint): seeing a deer's footprints on either side of a rock, one infers the deer has passed over it; likewise, when sukha and dukkha are absent, one infers adukkhamasukha
-   Why only upekkhā as anicca
    -   all three are anicca, dukkha, anattā but here anicca is emphasized for upekkhā's inconspicuousness

#### Qualities of a Sammaddasa



-   **Ariyo**: noble
-   **Sammaddaso**: who has seen rightly (feelings as they are)
-   **Acchecchi taṇhaṃ**: who has completely cut off craving, which has vedanā as cause
    -   acchejji (sī. pī.), acchijji (ka.)
-   **Vivattayi saṃyojanaṃ**: unravelled the fetters
    -   vāvattayi (sī. aṭṭha.)
-   **Sammā mānābhisamayā**: rightly understood conceit, through complete abandonment
-   **Antamakāsi dukkhassa**: made an end of suffering

> **Yo sukhaṃ dukkhato adakkhi, dukkhamaddakkhi sallato,  
> Adukkhamasukhaṃ santaṃ, adakkhi naṃ aniccato**   
> **Sa ve sammaddaso bhikkhu, yato tattha vimuccati,  
> Abhiññāvosito santo, sa ve yogātigo muni**  
> Whoever sees the pleasant as painful, the painful as a dart,   
> And sees neither-pleasant-nor-painful feeling as peaceful,   
> That monk, who sees rightly, indeed is thereby well released.   
> Accomplished in knowledge, at peace, the sage has overcome all bonds.”




<a id="orgfa0a8ed"></a>

#### 3 Dukkhas vs. 3 Vedanās



-   **Dukkhadukkhatā** (intrinsic suffering)
    -   Suffering that is painful by its very nature
    -   Identical with dukkhavedanā (both kāyika and mānasika)
-   **Vipariṇāmadukkhatā** (suffering due to change)
    -   inherent in sukhavedanā since it is pleasant while it last but painful when it changes
    -   due to being transient, it is dukkhā
    -   as if suffering lied hidden behind pleasure and manifests when pleasure fades away
-   **Saṅkhāra-dukkhatā** (suffering of conditioned phenomena)
    -   inherent in all conditioned phenomena for being oppressed by their conflicting component factors because they are subject to arising and passing away
    -   those who are deluded or attached to it suffer
    -   inherent in adukkhamasukhavedanā for being impermanent and subject to causes and conditions
        -   -a: includes all saṅkhāras because of the oppressive nature of arising and passing away

> Psm:

> - **Sukhā vedanā vipariṇāmena dukkhuppattihetuto vipariṇāmadukkhaṃ.**  
> - **Upekkhāvedanā ceva avasesā ca tebhūmakasaṅkhārā udayabbayapaṭipīḷitattā saṅkhāradukkhaṃ.**

#### Saṃsaggā vanatho jāto asaṃsaggena chijjati



-   **Saṁsaggā**: due to the 5 types of association; e.g., seeing, hearing

-   **Asaṁsaggena**: by non-association, rejection of the 5 associations, by kāya- and citta- viveka

#### 3 Sekhaparihānasaṃvattanadhammas


Dhammas that lead to the decline of sekhas

-   **Kammārāmo kammarato kammārāmatamanuyutto**: enjoys work, spends the entire day making or repairing requisites
    -   if done at the appropriate time with skill: good
        -   includes recitation, studies, duties, meditation

    -   dhammasākacchā: ok
    -   appabhassa: one of little speech, who keeps speech within Dhamma, even if engaging in speaking a lot
        -   Bhikkhus, when you have assembled together, two things should be done:
            -   either discussion of the Dhamma
            -   or noble silence
-   **Niddārāmo niddārato niddārāmatamanuyutto**: enjoys sleep, overeats and indulges in seyya-, passa-, and middha- sukha, constantly overcome by thīnamiddha

            -   obstacles to the attainment of all paths
    -   3 dhammā sekhassa bhikkhuno aparihānāya saṃvattanti (lead to the non-decline of a sekha): their negatives

> Bhabbo so tādiso bhikkhu, phuṭṭhuṃ sambodhimuttamaṁ**  
> Therefore, a monk who has few duties, sleeps little, and is free from restlessness   
> is capable of attaining the supreme enlightenment

### Pañcapubbanimittasuttaṃ


<a id="org590dd81"></a>

#### 5 Pubbanimitta of a Dying Deva


Signs a deva is about to pass away:

-   **Mālā milāyanti**: their garlands wither, losing their splendor as if under the midday sun
-   **Vatthāni kilissanti**: their garments lose their radience and become stained as if cast upon mud and trampled upon
-   **Kacchehi sedā muccanti**: they get a fever and sweat flows from their armpits (and entire body) like they're bedecked with a network of strings of pearls
    -   normally, they do not sweat or get dirty like an image of gold or gems
-   **Kāye dubbaṇṇiyaṃ okkamati**: their lose color, radiance, and power and become deformed
    -   normally,
        -   their radiance pervades 1-12 yojanas acc. to their power
        -   they are free from broken teeth, grey hair, etc
        -   they are not affected by cold or heat
        -   female deity: appears 16 years old
        -   male deity: appears 20 years old
-   **Sake devo devāsane nābhiramati**: they do not delight in their own divine throne
    -   normally, they enjoy and dally with their nymphs there




<a id="orgd8e170d"></a>

##### To Which Devas They Appear

These signs appear only for deities of great power, like meteors, earthquakes, and lunar eclipses appear only for those of great merit

-   some suddenly die and are reborn in hell and such signs may not appear
-   teacher: maybe they only appear to those who die due to āyu- or kamma- khaya maraṇa
-   they occur 7 days before the passing (in human reckoning)


<a id="org3e911e2"></a>

##### Passing Deva's Reaction to the Signs



-   **Understanding**:
    -   some devas don't know those are the signs of death
    -   wiser ones recognize them as signs of death
-   **Reaction**:
    -   **grief**: overwhelming grief arises due to the impending death
    -   **fever**: great fever arises, leading to the sweating
    -   **wailing**:
        -   some wail and lament, “I am burning! I am burning!”
        -   others are unnafected by that but are unable to endure the suffering from separation from what is beloved, and wander about afflicted
    -   **fear**:
        -   one with weak merit fears rebirth
        -   one with much merit does not
-   Bodhisattas
    -   Mv-a:
        -   Vipassī Bodhisatta did not tremble
        -   deities from 10 k world-systems gathered and requested him thus: “Sir, the ten perfections have been fulfilled by you, not by aspiring for the prosperity of Sakka, of Māra, of Brahmā, of a Cakkavattirāja, but for Buddhahood for the sake of leading beings across saṃsāra. Therefore, sir, now is the time; sir, now is the occasion for Buddhahood!”
        -   He did the pañcamahāvilokana, etc.




<a id="org73e12eb"></a>

-   They usher the passing deva to the Nandana forest, which exists in every celestial realm
-   **Tivācānumodanā**
    -   **Ito, bho, sugatiṃ gaccha**: manussatta
        -   admired by the deities because it is a means of acquiring qualities as the root cause for arising again as a deity
    -   **Sugatiṃ gantvā suladdhalābhaṃ labha**: dhammavinaye saddhā: as a human, obtaining faith in the Dhammavinaya of the Buddha
        -   simile:
            -   wealth => prosperity => removes suffering of hunger, etc
            -   saddhā => ariyadhanas => remove suffering of jāti, etc
                -   Dhammavinayasaddhā => sammāpaṭipatti => 3 atthas (diṭṭhadhammika, samparāyika, and parama)
                -   lokiya & lokuttara saddhā => lokiya & lokuttara sukha
        -   saddhādura: those who practise with faith as the chief
    -   **Suladdhalābhaṃ labhitvā suppatiṭṭhito bhavāhi**: asaṁhāriyasaddhā: unshakeable faith
        -   faith of ariyas
        -   niviṭṭha: well established in kusala
        -   mūlajātā: arisen with its root
            -   upāya-manasikāra: methodical reflection, the root-cause of placing trust in an object
            -   or the 4 sotāpattiaṅga:
                -   sappurisasaṁseva
                -   saddhammassavaṇaṁ
                -   analytical reflection
                -   dhammānudhamma paṭipatti
        -   asaṃhāriya: cannot removed by anyone
-   Notes:
    -   encourage them to make abundant merits (dāna, sīla, etc.)
        -   with the wish for the diminution of the host of Asuras and the augmentation of the host of deities
    -   they provide him with the means to perform almsgiving
    -   encourage him to establish others in the true Dhamma and the holy life
    -   wise deities remove his sorrow, saying, “Come again and again among the deities”

    -   Anaññasādhāraṇa: not shared with others

    -   AN: saddhesiko, bhikkhave, ariyasāvako akusalaṃ pajahati, kusalaṃ bhāveti, sāvajjaṃ pajahati, anavajjaṃ bhāveti, suddhaṃ attānaṃ pariharati.

2.  Dammavinaya Definitions

    
    It-a:
    
    -   Dhamma because of not departing from nature
        Vinaya: because of guiding those able according to their dispostions
    -   Dhamma: because of not departing from nature because of the attainment of upanissayasampatti
        Vinaya: because it disciplines beings of little dust in their eyes (apparajakkhajātika) by means of the Dhamma
    -   Vinaya: by means of the Dhamma alone, not by rods or weapons
    -   Dhammavinaya: discipline accompanied by the Dhamma
    -   Dhammavinaya: discipline for the sake of the Dhamma (with magga, phala, Nibbāna)
    -   Dhammavinaya: discipline that proceeds from mahākaruṇā and sabbaññutaññāṇa
    -   Dhamma: the Blessed One
        Vinaya: having become Dhamma, the embodiment of Dhamma (dhammakāya), the master of Dhamma; the discipline belonging to that Dhamma, not to mere speculation (takkiyānaṃ), is Dhamma-Vinaya
    -   Dhamma: maggaphala
        Vinaya: discipline that operates within the sphere where the maggaphala is to be realized

#### Appamāṇa & Nirūpadhi Kusala



> **Kāyena kusalaṃ katvā, vācāya kusalaṃ bahuṃ;   
> Manasā kusalaṃ katvā, appamāṇaṃ nirūpadhiṃ**   
> Having done wholesome through body, speech, and mind,    
> that is immeasurable and non-substrate

-   **Appamāṇaṁ**: kusala that cuts off pamāṇa &#x2013; gross defilements (kāmarāga with diṭṭhi)
-   **Nirūpadhiṁ**: kusala that abandon the 3 upadhis and depends on Nibbāna
    -   because it involves the abandonment of
        -   khandhūpadhi, which would otherwise arise after the seventh existence
        -   abhisaṅkhārūpadhi that produces it
        -   kilesūpadhi that is abandoned by the respective path
    -   because it depends on Nibbāna, also known as nirūpadhi due to its non-arising of all upadhi

> **Tato opadhikaṃ puññaṃ, katvā dānena taṃ bahuṃ;  
> Aññepi macce saddhamme, brahmacariye nivesaya**  
> Then, having performed so much substratal merit through giving,    
> may you establish others in the true Dhamma and the holy life.

### Kāmūpapattisuttaṃ


<a id="orgf1b1168"></a>

#### 3 Kāmūpapatti


**Kāmūpapatti**:

-   kāmapaṭilābha: the attainment of sensual pleasure
-   kāmapaṭisevana: indulgence in sensual pleasures

Saṅgīti Sutta:

-   **Paccupaṭṭhitakāmā**: those whose sensual objects are fixed (nibaddhakāma) (they cannot make them anew or modify them)
    -   Manussā: take control (vasaṁ vattenti) of fixed sensual objects.
        -   e.g., paying for enjoying sex with whatever woman one wants
    -   Ekacce devā: devas in the 1st 4 deva realms
        -   e.g., deva Pañcasikha
    -   Ekacce vinipātikā: beings in apāya except nerayikas
        -   e.g., a male fish controls a female fish; a male turtle a female turtle
-   **Nimmānaratino**: those who delight in creating them (nimmitakāmā) &#x2013; nimmānarati devas
    -   -a: they create various forms (nimminitvā nimminitvā) (blue, yellow, etc.) and control them
        -   e.g., Manāpakāyika deities and V. Anuruddha (AN Anuruddha Sutta)
            -   Kosambī, Ghosita's Park
            -   manāpakāyika-devatā came and said they can control their
                -   colour
                -   pleasure
                -   voice
            -   he thought "may they become blue", and they did, etc.
            -   one sang, one danced, one snapped her fingers, like a well-trained musical quintet
            -   V. drew in his sense faculties
            -   they thought "Master Anuruddha is not enjoying this", and disappeared on the spot
            -   he approached the Buddha, informed what happened, and asked how many qualities a woman should possess to be reborn as a manāpakāyika devi
            -   8 qualities:
                -   She serves her husband respectfully and dutifully, showing agreeable conduct and pleasing speech
                -   She honors and respectfully welcomes those whom her husband respects
                -   She skillfully and diligently manages the household duties entrusted to her
                -   She carefully supervises the household workers and ensures that each person receives proper care and food
                -   She carefully protects and manages her husband’s wealth without wasting or squandering it
                -   She has gone for refuge to the Buddha, Dhamma, and Saṅgha
                -   She observes the five precepts and abstains from the five forms of misconduct
                -   She is generous, free from miserliness, and delights in giving and sharing
-   **Paranimmitavasavattino**: those who exercise control over objects created by others (paranimmitakāmā) &#x2013; paranimmitavasavattī devas
    -   paranimmitesu kāmesu vasaṃ vattenti
    -   -a: other devas, knowing the minds of the Paranimmitavasavattī deities, create sensual pleasures according to their desires, who then control and enjoy them through sex, etc.
        -   How do they know their minds?
            -   pakatisevanāvasena: by natural association, like a skilled cook who knows what a king prefers while eating

> **Paccupaṭṭhitakāmā ca, ye devā vasavattino;  
> Nimmānaratino devā, ye caññe kāmabhogino;**  
> **Itthabhāvaññathābhāvaṃ, saṃsāraṃ nātivattare.**  
> Those whose sensual objects are existent, those deities who exercise control, Those who delight in creating, and others who enjoy sensual objects—Whether in this present existence or in another, they do not pass beyond saṃsāra.

<a id="orgd57c510"></a>

##### Anavaññattipaṭisaṃyutta Vitakka


A (wrong) thought concerned with not being despised, with being praised or recognized or that occurs with it as its object

-   "mā maṃ pare avajāniṃsu" based on jāti, gotta, kulaputtiya, vaṇṇapokkharatā, dhana, ajjhena, kammāyatana, sippāyatana, vijjāṭṭhāna, suta, paṭibhāna, etc.
    -   A-Vbh: idhekacco jātiyā vā gottena vā kolaputtiyena vā vaṇṇapokkharatāya vā dhanena vā ajjhenena vā kammāyatanena vā sippāyatanena vā vijjāṭṭhānena vā sutena vā paṭibhānena vā aññataraññatarena vatthunā mā maṃ pare avajāniṃsūti. yo tattha gehasito takko vitakko micchāsaṅkappo – ayaṃ vuccati anavaññattipaṭisaṃyutto vitakko
-   **Anavaññatti** (na + ava + √ñā + ti)
    -   state of not being despised or looked down upon by others
    -   sambhāvanakamyatā: desire for others:
        -   not to despise one
        -   to praise or recognize one




<a id="org397aa7f"></a>

##### Lābhasakkārasilokapaṭisaṃyutta Vitakka


A (wrong) thought with the following as object:

-   gain: such as robes
-   honour
-   and fame / praise




<a id="org0b94fe8"></a>

##### Parānuddayatāpaṭisaṃyutta Vitakka


A thought concerned with involvement in the affairs of others

-   **Parānuddayatā**: a thought that is associated with compassion toward others
    -   para + anuddayatā (the state of compassion or sympathy)
    -   -a: thought associated with
        -   gehasita-pema: household-based affection, which resembles compassion
        -   or anuddayatāpaṭirūpaka: affection that is a counterfeit of compassion
    -   how is one "intimate in the village"?:
        -   lives in association with laypeople
        -   rejoices and sorrows with them
        -   he is happy when they are happy and sad when they are sad
        -   he involves himself in their affairs and duties

#### 3 Outcomes of Examinining Things Properly (2 Methods)


MN Uddesavibhaṅga Sutta method: V. Mahākaccāyana
Buddha: Examine things in such a way that while he is examining them, his consciousness is:

-   **Bahiddhā viññāṇaṃ avikkhittaṃ avisaṭaṃ**: not distracted and scattered externally because of following after rūpādinimitta and of being shackled by kāmassāda
    -   note: same for the other faculties
-   **Ajjhattaṁ asaṇṭhitaṁ**: not stuck internally due to attachment to jhāna due to following after the blissful jhāna experiences and being fettered by their assāda
    -   1st jhāna: not following the "vivekajaṁ pītisukhaṁ" (rapture and pleasure born of seclusion) or being fettered by its assāda
    -   2nd jhāna: not following the "samādhijaṁ pītisukhaṁ" (rapture and pleasure born of concentration) or being fettered by its assāda
    -   3rd jhāna: not following the equanimity or being fettered by its assāda
    -   4th jhāna: not following the neither-pain-nor-pleasure or being fettered by its assāda
-   **Anupādānā aparitassanā**: agitation due to clinging (-a: through views)
    -   andhabālaputhujjana
        -   seeing khandhas as attā: 20 sakkāyadiṭṭhis
            -   rūpaṃ attato samanupassati: regards rūpa as self
                -   like the flame is identical with its colour
            -   rūpavantaṃ vā attānaṃ samanupassati: regards the self as possessing rūpa
                -   like a tree possesses a shadow
            -   attani vā rūpaṃ samanupassati: regards rūpa as being *in* the self
                -   like a scent is *in* the flower
            -   rūpasmiṃ vā attānaṃ samanupassati: regards the self as being *in* rūpa
                -   like a jewel is in a casket
            -   note:
                -   same for the other 4 khandhas
                -   2 additional ones from yamakasutta: 
                    -   rūpaṃ vedanaṃ saññaṃ saṅkhāre viññāṇaṃ attato samanupassati: regards all 5 aggregates as the self
                    -   arūpiṃ avedanaṃ asaññaṃ asaṅkhāraṃ aviññāṇaṃ attānaṃ samanupassati: regards the self to be something other than the 5 khandhas
        -   when the khandhas change, he is preoccupied, agitated, obsessed, anxious, distressed, concerned
    -   ariyasāvaka: not so

Itv-a method: in vipassanā context: discern formations starting with samathanimitta and then sammasanañāṇa

-   **Bahiddhā viññāṇaṃ avikkhittaṃ avisaṭaṃ**: his vipassanācitta is not distracted externally taking saṅkhāras apart from the meditation object
    -   accāraddhavīriya: overly aroused energy, which leads to such distraction
-   **Ajjhattaṁ asaṇṭhitaṁ**: doesn't shrink into the meditation object
    -   Ajjhatta: gocarajjhatta; meditation subject
    -   Asaṇṭhita: doesn't shrink (balanced effort & concentration)
        -   if effort is weak and concentration is strong: shrinking due to laziness
    -   4 Ajjhattas
        -   Gocarajjhatta: meditation subject (meant here)
        -   Niyakajjhatta
        -   Visayajjhatta
        -   Ajjhattajjhatta
-   **Anupādānā aparitassanā**: not agitated because he doesn't grasp saṅkhāras with craving and diṭṭhi as "etaṃ mama, eso me attā"
-   Notes:
    -   balance effort by avoiding
        -   uddhacca-pakkhiyadhamma
        -   and kosajja-pakkhiyadhamma
    -   purifies vipassanūpakkilesa
    -   paṭipadāñāṇadassanavisuddhi begins
    -   magga destroys defilements
    -   birth, etc. removed

If so, for him, there is no origination of suffering in the future

-   method 1: of birth, ageing, and death
-   method 2
    -   origin of suffering: birth
