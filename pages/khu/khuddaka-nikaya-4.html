      • background
        • a rich faithful and devoted man in Sāvatthi offered a donation to the bhikkhusaṅgha headed by the Buddha; the king was in need of wealth and sent for him, but the rich man said to the messenger that he was burying the hidden treasure and would go later; after the meal, the Buddha showed appreciation by expounding the nidhikaṇḍasutta; at the end the devotee and many others became sotāpannas; he informed the king who was delighted and made him great honor
    • nidhikaṇḍasutta
      • 1. nidhiṃ nidheti puriso,→gambhīre odakantike, atthe kic ce samuppanne, atthāya me bhavissati. "A man buries a treasure in a deep place where there is water at the end, (thinking that) when an urgent matter arises, it will be for my benefit"
        • 4 types of nidhi (NB)
          • external
            • thāvaranidhi (stationary treasure): properties which are not able to move by themselves (land, paddy field, gold, silver, houses, etc) note: this is the one meant by the 1st verse
              • saṁhārima: something that can be carried
              • asaṁhārima: something that cannot be carried
            • jaṅgamanidhi (mobile treasure): properties which are able to move by themselves (slaves, elephants, horses, cows, goats, sheep, chicken, pigs, etc)
          • internal
            • aṅgasamanidhi (limb-like treasure): knowledges that after obtained, remain with one like limbs
              • knowledge of work (kammāyatana)
              • knowledge of crafts (sippāyatana)
              • arts and sciences (vijjāṭṭhāna)
              • education (bāhusacca), etc
            • anugāmikanidhi (following treasure): merits that follow one from one life to another as inheritance (kammassakomhi kammadāyādo...)
          • related suttas:
            • vaḍḍhisutta - the noble disciple who grows with these ten growths grows with noble growth and gets the essence and takes the very best of body / life: growth in fields and land, wealth and grain, children and wives, slaves, workers and employees, quadrupeds (cattle), saddhā, sīla, suta, cāga and paññā.
            • andhasutta: internal and external wisdom ⇒ wisdom about what is good and bad, and wisdom about other crafts and arts
        • gambhīre (deep) and udakantike (in the place where there is water as the end)
          • There is a place that is deep but has no water at the bottom, such as a hundred-fathom pit or a hole on a plateau in the jungle. There is a place where there is water at the bottom but it is not deep, such as a one- or two-span pit or a hole in a lowland or pond. Here, it means a place that is deep and has water at the bottom.
        • 3 types of person
          • Three types of person in the world: 4.1. Andho (blind person): one who lacks the eye to obtain wealth that has not yet been acquired and increase the wealth that has already been obtained. Moreover, one lacks the eye to discern between wholesome and unwholesome, blameworthy and praiseworthy, inferior and superior, as well as dark and bright qualities. 4.2. Ekacakkhu (one-eyed person): one who has the eye with which one can obtain wealth that has not yet been acquired and increase the wealth that has already been obtained. However, one lacks the eye with which one can discern between wholesome and unwholesome, blameworthy and praiseworthy, inferior and superior, as well as dark and bright qualities. 4.3. Dvicakkhu (two-eyed person): one who has the eye with which one can obtain wealth that has not yet been acquired and increase the wealth that has already been obtained, and one who has the eye with which one can discern between wholesome and unwholesome, blameworthy and praiseworthy, inferior and superior, as well as dark and bright qualities.

      • 2.rājato vā duruttassa,→corato pīḷitassa vā, iṇassa vā pamokkhāya, dubbhikkhe āpadāsu vā, etadatthāya lokasmiṃ, nidhi nāma nidhīyati. "When I am accused by opponents (enemies), it will be for the freedom from the ruler. When I am harassed by thieves, it will be for the freedom from the thieves. When I am blamed by creditors, it will be the freedom from the debt. When there are famine and calamities, it will be for the freedom from them. For this benefit (reason), such a hidden treasure is buried in the world."
        • 4 reasons for burying a treasure - according to the 2nd verse (NB)
          • paccāmittehi me duruttassa, rājato vā pamokkhāya bhavissati: To be free from the ruler if one gets slandered. When one is accused by enemies, labeled as 'the thief,' 'the adulterer,' or 'the tax evader,' he/she may be spared from punishment by a king.
          • corehi me pīḷitassa, corato vā pamokkhāya bhavissati: To be free from thieves if harassed. When the thief harasses or takes as hostage, saying, "Give me this much of gold or silver," then he will be free from getting hurt or killed.
          • iṇāyikehi me codiyamānassa, iṇassa vā pamokkhāya bhavissati: To release from debt. When the creditor comes and pressures for repayment, saying 'Pay me the debt,' then he will be free from it.
          • dubbhikkhe āgate āpadāsu (vā) uppannāya, tesaṁ pamokkhāya bhavissati: To be free from hunger in case of famine or losses, etc. When there is famine or a bad harvest, making it difficult to obtain food, one cannot live easily. Therefore, this hidden treasure will help. In times of calamity or disaster, such as one's house being burnt by fire or swept away by water, the hidden treasure will save him.
        • bhogavibhāga: fourfold division of wealth (with gāthā)
          • Ekena bhoge bhuñjeyya - on one part he should live and do his duties towards others' welfare.
          • Dvīhi kammaṃ payoje - with two portions he should spend for his business.
          • Catutthañca nidhāpeyya apadāsu bhavissati - he should have the fourth path deposited for times of calamities.

        • bhogādīya (benefits which one should get from external wealth)
          • Ādittasutta: that when one's house is burning, whatever belonging is taken out will be beneficial, not what is burnt - likewise, when the world is burnt by the fires of old age and death, one should save one's wealth by giving.
          • that belonging will be beneficial for him/her, not the one that being burnt. In the same way, when the world is burned by [fire of] old age and death, one should take out one's wealth by giving. Whatever is given, that is well taken out. What is given brings pleasant result, unlike withholding, as thieves can steal, kings can confiscate, and fire can destroy. Then in the end, one abandons one's body and belonging. Therefore, the wise, having realized that, should utilize by himself/herself and should give to the others. Having given and utilized according to one's ability, then he/she is not blamed by anyone and goes to heaven after death.

      • 3.tāvassunihito santo,→gambhīre odakantike, na sabbo sabbadā eva, tassa taṃ upakappati. "Even though the hidden treasure is buried deep until [it reaches] water in the end. Not all hidden treasure will indeed be beneficial for him all the time"

        • The Buddha said in bhogasutta6 that there are five dangers in wealth: the wealth is shared by fire (aggisādhāraṇā bhogā), water (udakasādhāraṇā bhogā), kings (rājasādharaṇā bhogā), thieves (corasādhāraṇā bhogā), and unbeloved heirs (appiyehi dāyādehi sādhāraṇā bhogā).

          • kalyāṇamittatā (spiritual friendship): in whatever village or market town, the son of good family enters, in that place they are householder or householder's son, young ones mature in virtue or adult ones mature in virtues and they are endowed with faith, moral virtue, generosity and wisdom.           • samajīvitā (balanced life): the son of the good family, knowing his income and expenses, lives with his means, being neither extravagant nor stingy (samaṃ jīvika kappeti nāccogāḷhaṃ nātihīnaṃ), considering thus his income will stand in excess of his expenses, but not his expenses in excess of his income. (udumbarakhādika, when one desiring to eat figs, shakes the tree, but much fruit falls and a large amount is wasted), and if a son of good family with a large income leads a mean life, there will be those who say, 'This son of good family will die of starvation!"
