## Lesson 1 - declension of 'ta', sandhi
- 1. Reading
  - Sīhaladīpe kira uddalolakavihāro nāma ramaṇīyo eko vihāro ahosi. Tadā vihāropavane bahū migasūkarā vasanti. Athekasmiṃ gāme nesādaputto tasmiṃ bahū migasūkare disvā ekadivasaṃ ekasmiṃ passe koṭṭhakaṃ katvā vanapariyante paṇṇaṃ bandhitvā dhanukalāpaṃ paggayha migāgamanaṃ olokento koṭṭhake aṭṭhāsi. Atheko migo tattha gocaraṃ gaṇhitvā pānīyaṃ pātuṃ titthaṃ gacchanto tasmiṃ assamapade dhammasavaṇatthāya ghuṭṭhasaddaṃ sutvā pasāritagīvo uttuṅgakaṇṇo anikkhittadakkhiṇapādo dhammakathikassa sare nimittaṃ gaṇhitvā aṭṭhāsi.

  - 2.1 Grammar
    - declension of the pronoun ‘ta’ (sabbanāmagaṇa)
      - masculine declension

        | case | singular | plural |
        |---|---|---|
        | Nom. | so | te, ne |
        | Acc. | taṃ, naṃ | te, ne |
        | Ins. | tena, nena | tehi, tebhi, nehi, nebhi |
        | Dat/gen. | tassa, nassa, assa | tesaṃ, tesānaṃ, nesaṃ, nesānaṃ |
        | Abl. | tasmā, nasmā, asmā, tamhā, namhā | tehi, tebhi, nehi, nebhi |
        | Loc. | tasmiṃ, nasmiṁ, asmiṃ, tamhi, namhi | tesu, nesu |

        - memory aid:
          - 1 - t ⇒ n
          - 2 - sa, sma, smiṁ: ta ⇒ a
      - neuter declension

        | case | singular | plural |
        |---|---|---|
        | Nom./Acc. | taṁ, naṃ | te, tāni, ne, nāni |

        - (the rest as masculine)
      - feminine declension

        | case | singular | plural |
        |---|---|---|
        | Nom. | sā | tā, tāyo, nā, nāyo |
        | Acc. | taṃ, naṃ | tā, tāyo, nā, nāyo |
        | Ins. / Abl. | tāya, nāya | tāhi, tābhi, nāhi, nābhi |
        | Dat/gen. | tāya, nāya - tassā, nassā, assā, tissā - tassāya, nassāya, assāya, tissāya | tāsaṃ, tāsānaṃ, nāsaṃ, nāsānaṃ |
        | Loc. | tāyaṃ, nāyaṃ, tassaṃ, nassaṃ, assaṃ, tissaṃ | tāsu, nāsu |

        - memory aid:
          - 1 - t ⇒ n
          - 2 - sa, smā, smiṁ, sā, saṁ: ta ⇒ a
          - 3 - dat. gen.: ending in sā: add ya
          - 4 - ta ⇒ ti, sa ⇒ saṁ
    - sandhi: euphonic combination
    - what is a sandhi (euphonic combinations)? (English definition): The linguistic act of combining two words (actually letters) for the sake of an easier pronunciation
    - saññā (f.) (grammar) √ñā: (gram) grammatical term; technical term; definition; lit. name
      - 1.Forty-one letters (41) starting with ‘a’ and ending with ‘ṃ’ are the letters – akkhara.
      - 2. Out of the 41 letters, first eight letters: ‘a’, ‘ā’, ‘i’, ‘ī’, ‘u’, ‘ū’, ‘e’ and ‘o’, are vowels – sara.
      - 3. Among vowels, three vowels, ‘a’, ‘i’ and ‘u’ are called short vowels – rassa sara.
      - 4. The remaining five letters, ‘ā’, ‘ī’, ‘ū’, ‘e’ and ‘o’ are called long vowels – dīgha sara.
      - 5. Thirty-three (33) letters starting from ‘k’ till ‘ṃ’ are called consonants – vyañjana/byañjana.
      - 6. Out of the consonants, twenty-five letters starting from ‘k’ and ending with ‘m’ are called group letters – vaggakkhara
      - 7. Vagga letters are divided into five groups: ka-vagga, ca-vagga, ṭa-vagga, ta vaggga and pa-vagga
      - 8. Last letter of each vagga (group) is called group-endings – vagganta. There are five vagganta letters: ‘ṅ’, ‘ñ’, ‘ṇ’, ‘n’ and ‘m’.
      - 9.The last consonant of the alphabet is called niggahīta (ṁ)
      - 9 saññās under sandhi:
        - 41 letters – akkhara
        - 8 vowels – sara
          - 3 short vowels – rassa sara
          - 5 long vowels – dīgha sara (be careful: vuddhis are only ā, e, o)
        - 33 consonants – vyañjana / byañjana
        - 25 group letters – vaggakkhara
          - 5 groups: ka-vagga, ca-vagga, ṭa-vagga, ta vaggga and pa-vagga
          - group-endings – vagganta ‘ṅ’, ‘ñ’, ‘ṇ’, ‘n’ and ‘m’
        - mem aid: akkhara, saras, vyañjana, vagga
    - 3 types of sandhi
      - sara sandhi: letter + vowel (yassa + indriyāni ⇒ yassindriyāni)
      - vyañjana / byañjana sandhi: letter + consonant (ava+vādo = ovādo)
      - niggahīta sandhi: ṁ + letter / the insertion of niggahīta is also included in niggahītasandhi (yaṁ+hi=yañhi)
    - two consonants together are called a saṁyoga / saññoga (conjunct)
    - the vowels e and o are shortened before double consonants (conjunct) but the remaining vowels may remain long
    - when two words are separated it is called visandhi (disjoining)
    - 3 grammar traditions
      - Kaccāyana
      - Moggallāna
      - Saddanīti (Myanmar)
## Lesson 2 - declension of 'kiṁ', sarasandhi
- 2.1. Reading
  - Tasmiṃ khaṇe nesādo taṃ ekappahāreneva vijjhitvā jīvitakkhayaṃ pāpesi. Atha so kālaṃ katvā tattheva vihāre vasantassa mahābhayattherassa kaṇiṭṭhikāya kucchiyaṃ paṭisandhiṃ gaṇhitvā dasamāsaddhamāsaccayena mātukucchito nikkhamitvā kamena abhivaḍḍhanto sattavassiko ahosi. Tato taṃ mātāpitaro abhayattherassa santikaṃ nesuṃ. So na cirena kālena pabbājesi. So pana kumāro pubbe migabhūtenattanā dhammasavaṇānubhāvena khuraggeyeva arahattaṃ pāpuṇi.
  - The suffix ‘-to’ in the word matukucchito is a suffix with which meaning? the ablative meaning (most often). It can be used in both singular and plural according to the context. It is a suffix, not a case ending. It turns the noun into a nipāta.

  - 2.2 Grammar
    - declension of the pronoun 'kiṁ' (sabbanāmagaṇa)
      - masculine

        | case | singular | plural |
        |---|---|---|
        | Nom. | ko | ke |
        | Acc. | kaṁ | " |
        | Ins. | kena | kehi, kebhi |
        | Dat/Gen. | kassa, kissa | kesaṁ, kesānaṁ |
        | Abl. | kasmā, kamhā | kehi, kebhi |
        | Loc. | kasmiṁ, kismiṁ, kamhi, kimhi | kesu |

        - mem aid: extra 'i' form, but not in abl.
      - neuter

        | case | singular | plural |
        |---|---|---|
        | Nom./Acc. | kaṁ, kiṁ | ke, kāni |

        - (the rest as the masculine)
      - feminine

        | case | singular | plural |
        |---|---|---|
        | Nom. | kā | kā, kāyo |
        | Acc. | kaṁ | " |
        | Ins./Abl. | kāya | kāhi, kābhi |
        | Dat/Gen. | kāya, kassā | kāsaṁ, kāsānaṁ |
        | Loc. | kāyaṁ, kassaṁ | kāsu |

        - note: no 'i' form, 'a' takes over
      - If one does not know which gender the word kiṁ refers to the gender used is neuter kiṁ.
    - Sara sandhi is further divided into following categories.
      - 1). Lopa (elision) – Elision of a vowel
        - pubba sara lopa sandhi (elision of the previous vowel)
          - paññā + indriyaṁ ⇒ paññindriyaṁ
          - loka + aggo ⇒ lokaggo
            - In traditional Pāḷi grammar, on most of such occasions the combination is considered to have happened by eliding the previous vowel (pubbasara)
        - para sara lopa sandhi (elision of the following vowel / succeeding vowel) It only occurs between dissimilar vowels. Dissimilarity of vowels is based on the difference of sounds. (a and ā, i and ī, u and ū are similar; the others are dissimilar)
          - cattāro + ime ⇒ cattārome
          - saññā + iti ⇒ saññāti
      - 2) Ādesa – substitution
      - 4) Dīgha – lengthening
      - 3) Āgama – augmentation or insertion
## Lesson 3 - declension of 'aggi', dhātugaṇa
- 3.1. Reading
  - Tassa pana mātulatthero paṭiladdhapañcābhiñño appattaarahattamaggaphalo ahosi. Athekasmiṃ divase sāmaṇero padoseva upajjhāyassa santikaṃ agamāsi. Athassa upajjhāyo hatthaṃ vaḍḍhetvā pāṇinā candamaṇḍalaṃ parimaddanto aṭṭhāsi. Sāmaṇero taṃ disvā “Bhante, etaṃ rakkhituṃ vaṭṭatī”ti āha. Thero pana tassa aggaphale arahatte patiṭṭhitabhāvaṃ ajānanto tassa vacanaṃ na sammā manasākāsi. Atha sāmaṇero iddhiyā candasahassaṃ āharitvā therassa dassetvā “Bhante, candasataṃ vā sahassaṃ vā satasahassaṃ vā āharaṇaṃ na bhāriyaṃ. Yo pana ekameva taṇhaṃ pajahati, so eva uttamo, taṃyeva bhāriya”nti āha.

  - 3.2. Grammar
    - *declension of aggi (m.) (rattādigaṇa)

      | case | singular | plural |
      |---|---|---|
      | Nom. | aggi | aggī, aggayo |
      | Voc. | (bho) " | (bhonto) " |
      | Acc. | aggiṃ | " |
      | Ins. | agginā | aggīhi, aggībhi, aggihi, aggibhi |
      | Dat/gen. | aggino, aggissa | aggīnaṃ, agginaṃ |
      | Abl. | agginā, aggismā, aggimhā | aggīhi, aggībhi, aggihi, aggibhi |
      | Loc. | aggismiṃ, aggimhi | aggīsu, aggisu |

      - mem aid:
        - no aggini (as it is itself a noun)
      - note: aggini and gini (synonyms) are also so declined (saddanīti)
    - Some masculine nouns of ratti-group ending in ‘i’

    - Dhātugaṇa (root groups)
      - Roots (dhātu) are the fundamental sounds (morphemes) which have verbal meanings. Verbs are made by adding a vikaraṇa paccaya (conjugational sign) and a verbal termination to the roots. All the roots in Pāḷi are classified into eight groups called dhātugaṇa. They are classified according to conjugational signs (vikaraṇa paccaya).
      - The suffix added between the dhātu and the verbal termination is called the conjugational sign – vikaraṇa paccaya. There are thirteen such signs in Pāḷi.
      - Following are the eight dhātugaṇa together with their vikaraṇa paccaya
        - 1. bhūvādigaṇa – a (most common)
        - 2. rudhādigaṇa – ṃ … a
        - 3. divādigaṇa – ya
        - 4. svādigaṇa (√su) – ṇā, ṇu, uṇā
        - 5. kiyādigaṇa – nā
        - 6. gahādigaṇa – ṇhā, ppa
        - 7. tanādigaṇa – o, yira
        - 8. curādigaṇa – (ṇ)e, (ṇ)aya (2nd most common)
        - mem aid: the ghost (bhū) cries (rudh), the deva (div) is happy (su), the kiṇṇara (ki) holds (gah) with stretched (tan) paws the thief (cur)
      - Curādi group
        - Though it is mentioned in traditional Pāḷi books that the conjugational sign of the curādi group is ‘ṇe’ and ‘ṇaya’ the actual signs are ‘e’ and ‘aya’. The lingual ‘ṇ’ is just an indicator called anubandha. It indicates that when the conjugational sign is added to the root the first vowel gets strengthened (vuddhi). After that the anubandha sign ‘ṇ’ is dropped.
        - During the process of vowel strengthening (vuddhi) a light vowel turns into its corresponding heavy vowel: a > ā, i and ī > e, u and ū > o When a light vowel is followed by a double consonant (saññoga) vuddhi normally does not occur
        - A few roots belonging to the curādigaṇa
          - cinteti / cintayati: thinks
          - peseti / pesayati: sends
          - pāleti / pālayati √pāl: protects
          - poseti / posayati: nourishes, brings up
          - vedeti / vedayati: knows
## Lesson 4 - declension of 'daṇḍī', bhikkhu, sayambhū
- 4.1. Reading
  - Taṃ sutvā thero vipassitvā tadaheva arahattaṃ pāpuṇi. Atha dutiyadivase therakaṇiṭṭhikā sāmaṇerena saddhiṃ theraṃ nimantesi. Thero bhaginiṃ evamāha. “Upāsike, tayājja bahū bhikkhū disvā cittaṃ pasādetabbaṃ. Paṭivīso pana dvinnaṃyeva hotū”ti taṃ pesetvā pubbaṇhasamayaṃ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya bahūhi bhikkhūhi saddhiṃ agamāsi. Tesaṃ ānubhāvena dvinnaṃyeva paṭiyāditaṃ sūpabyañjanādi sabbaṃ bahūnaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ pahonakaṃ ahosi. Bhuttāvasāne kho upāsikā bhuttānumodanatthaṃ sāmaṇerassa pattaṃ gaṇhi. Sopi tesaṃ majjhe madhurabhāratiṃ nicchārento dhammaṃ desesi. Desanāvasāne mātāpitaro ādiṃ katvā bahū sotāpattiphale patiṭṭhahiṃsu. Bahuno janassa sā desanā sātthikā ahosi.

## Lesson 5 - declension of 'atta', bhavalakkhaṇa, pubbasaradīghasandhi
- 5.1. Reading
  - Atīte jambudīpe ajitaraṭṭhe eko gopālako vasi. Tassa gehe eko paccekabuddho nibaddhaṃ bhuñjati. Tasmiṃ gehe eko kukkuro ca ahosi. Paccekabuddho bhuñjanto tassa nibaddhaṃ ekaṃ bhattapiṇḍaṃ adāsi. So tena paccekabuddhe (loc.sg) sinehamakāsi. Gopālako divasassa dve vāre paccekabuddhassa upaṭṭhānaṃ gacchi. Sunakhopi tena saddhiṃ gacchi. Gopālo ekadivasaṃ paccekabuddhaṃ āha: “Yadā me bhante, okāso na bhavissati, tadā imaṃ sunakhaṃ pesessāmi. Tena saññāṇena āgaccheyyāthā”ti. Tato paṭṭhāya anokāsadivase sunakhaṃ pesesi. So ekavacaneneva pakkhanditvā paccekabuddhassa vasanaṭṭhānaṃ gantvā tikkhattuṃ bhussitvā attano āgatabhāvaṃ jānāpetvā ekamantaṃ nipajji. Paccekabuddhe velaṃ sallakkhetvā nikkhante bhussanto purato gacchi. Paccekabuddho taṃ vīmaṃsanto ekadivasaṃ aññaṃ maggaṃ paṭipajji. Atha sunakho purato tiriyaṃ ṭhatvā bhussitvā itaramaggameva naṃ āropesi.
    - English translation:
      - In the past in the island of Jambudīpa in a country called Ajita dwelt a herdsman. In his house a silent Buddha regularly ate. In that house there was also a dog. The silent buddha would frequently give a lump of food to it. The dog because of that became fond of the silent buddha. The herdsman twice a day would go to silent buddha for serving him. The dog as well would go with him. One day the herdsman said to the silent buddha: "Whenever, bhante, I have no opportunity (to come) I will send this dog. By this sign may bhante come". From then onwards whenever he had no opportunity to go he would send the dog. The dog would spring forward on a single command and after having approached the dwelling place of the private buddha would bark thrice to make known its presence and lie down at a side. When the private buddha departed after having considered the time it would go ahead while barking. The private buddha one day took a different path in order to test the dog. Then the dog stood horizontally and while barking got the silent buddha back on the right path.

    - In Pāḷi adverbs are called kiriyā visesana – verb modifiers. They are adjectives mostly written in neuter gender accusative singular form or sometimes instrumental. There are also some nipātas which function as adverbs as well.
  - 5.2. Grammar
    - declension of atta (m.) (rājādigaṇa) (not for exam)

      |---|---|---|
      | Nom. | attā | attāno |
      | Voc. | (bho) atta, attā | (bhonto) attāno |
      | Acc | attaṃ, attānaṃ | attāno |
      | Ins. | attanā, attena | attanehi, bhi |
      | Dat/Gen. | attano, attassa | attānaṃ |
      | Abl. | attanā, attamhā, attasmā | attanehi, bhi |
      | Loc. | attani | attanesu, attesu |

      - note: ātuma is declined as atta in nom., voc., and acc., but the rest is like purisa
    - bhāva is a Pāḷi word for action
    - bhāvalakkhaṇa: The act of denoting the time of occurrence of a certain action with another action
    - mostly locative and genitive cases are used in the bhāvalakkhaṇa
    - how is bhavalakkhaṇa constructed?: the present/past participle and its agent are declined in either locative or genitive case expressing the sense of 'when' (rukkhe patante sakuṇā palāyanti - rukkhassa patantassa sakuṇā palāyanti) when the tree falls the birds flee). The main verb of the sentence could be in different verbal tenses
    - sara sandhi
      - pubbasara dīgha sandhi: type of sara sandhi in which the following vowel is elided and the previous is lengthened (gacchanti + iti = gacchantīti)
