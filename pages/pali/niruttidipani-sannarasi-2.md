## <i><b>ṭhānaṃ, karaṇaṃ, payatanaṃ</b></i>

- <i><b>cha ṭhānāni – kaṇṭhaṭṭhānaṃ, tāluṭṭhānaṃ, muddhaṭṭhānaṃ, dantaṭṭhānaṃ, oṭṭhaṭṭhānaṃ, nāsikaṭṭhānaṃ. </b></i> (<i><b>exam</b></i>)
- <i><b>kaṇṭha </b></i>includes the back of the tongue
- <i><b>tesu byattaṃ </b></i>(fluent, skillful)<i><b> vadantena yattha "akkha"nti vuccati, taṃ kaṇṭhaṭṭhānaṃ. </b></i><br><i><b>yattha "iccha"nti, taṃ tāluṭṭhānaṃ. yattha "raṭṭha"nti, taṃ muddhaṭṭhānaṃ. </b></i><br><i><b>yattha "sattha"nti, taṃ dantaṭṭhānaṃ. </b></i><br><i><b>yattha "puppha"nti vuccati, taṃ oṭṭhaṭṭhānaṃ. </b></i><br><i><b>nāsapadeso nāsikaṭṭhānaṃ.</b></i>
- <i><b>katthaci </b></i>(in some places)<i><b> pana uraṭṭhānaṃ, siraṭṭhānaṃ, jivhāmūlaṭṭhānantipi āgataṃ </b></i>(3 other ṭhānas given in other places)<i><b>. tattha siraṭṭhānaṃ nāma muddhaṭṭhānameva. jivhāmūlaṭṭhānaṃ pana sabbavaṇṇānaṃ sādhāraṇanti vadanti </b></i>(the back of the tongue is common to all letters, some say)<i><b>.</b></i>
- <i><b>karaṇaṃ </b></i><i>(instrument)</i><i><b> catubbidhaṃ – jivhāmajjhaṁ </b></i><i>(such as cavagga)</i><i><b>, jivhopaggaṃ, jivhaggaṃ, sakaṭṭhānanti.</b></i>
- <i><b>payatanaṃ </b></i><i>(effort)</i><i><b> catubbidhaṃ – saṃvuṭaṃ, vivaṭaṃ, phuṭṭhaṃ, īsaṃphuṭṭhanti. tattha karaṇānaṃ sakasakaṭṭhānehi saddhiṃ saṃvaraṇādiko visesākāro saṃvuṭādi nāma.</b></i>

### map of <i><b>ṭhāna</b></i>

- ekaṭṭhānika
  - khaṇṭhaja
    - a, ā, h\*
      - \*vagganta / antaṭṭhā + h: urasa (uraja vadanti)
      - (i, ī, u, ū)
  - tāluja
    - i, ī, y
  - muddhaja
    - r, ḷ
  - dantaja
    - l, s
  - oṭṭhaja
    - u, ū
  - nāsikaja
    - ṁ
- "dveṭṭhānika"
  - kaṇṭhatāluja: e, ē
  - kaṇṭhoṭṭhaja: o, ō
  - dantoṭṭhaja: v
  - sakaṭṭhāna + nāsikaṭṭhāna: vagganta
- note:
  - the 5 vaggas are produced in the first 5 ṭhānas respectively
  - pañcavaggantā = anunāsikā
- <i><b>tattha kaṇṭhapadesānaṃ </b></i>(of the throat areas)<i><b> aññamaññaṃ saṅghaṭṭanena </b></i>(by pressing together)<i><b> uppannā </b></i>(born, appear)<i><b> avaṇṇa </b></i><i>(a, ā)</i><i><b>, kavagga, hakārā </b></i><i>(h)</i><i><b> kaṇṭhajā </b></i><i>(produced on the throat)</i><i><b> nāma. </b></i><i>(</i><i><b>exam</b></i>)
- <i><b>note</b></i>:
  - instrument is also the throat (sakaṭṭhāna)
- **vocab:** see the [Part I Vocabulary](/summaries/pali/niruttidipani-part-1-vocab) page
- <i><b>tālumhi jivhāmajjhasaṅghaṭṭanena uppannā ivaṇṇa, cavagga, yakārā </b></i><i>(y)</i><i><b> tālujā nāma. </b></i>(<i><b>exam</b></i>)
- karaṇa: jivhāmajjhaṁ
- **vocab:** see the [Part I Vocabulary](/summaries/pali/niruttidipani-part-1-vocab) page
- <i><b>mukhabbhantaramuddhamhi </b></i>(in the top area inside of the mouth)<i><b> jivhopaggasaṅghaṭṭanena uppannā ṭavagga, ra, ḷakārā muddhajā nāma. </b></i>(<i><b>exam</b></i><i>)</i>
- <i>karaṇa: jivhopaggaṁ</i>
- here the ḷ is different from l
- muddha is explained in myanmar books as the top of the protuberance
- jivhāmajjhaṁ is slightly under the tongue
- <i><b>upari dantapantiyaṃ </b></i>(in the row of upper teeth)<i><b> jivhaggasaṅghaṭṭanena uppannā tavagga, la, sakārā dantajā nāma.</b></i>
- karaṇa: jivhagga
- **vocab:** see the [Part I Vocabulary](/summaries/pali/niruttidipani-part-1-vocab) page
- <i><b>oṭṭhadvaya</b></i>(pair)<i><b>saṅghaṭṭanena uppannā uvaṇṇa, pavaggā oṭṭhajā nāma.</b></i>
- <i>karaṇa: sakaṭṭhāna (tbc)</i>
- **vocab:** see the [Part I Vocabulary](/summaries/pali/niruttidipani-part-1-vocab) page
- <i><b> niggahītaṃ nāsikajaṃ nāma. </b></i>(<i><b>exam</b></i>)
- <i><b>note</b></i>:
- each vagga is pronounced in the order of the ṭhānas
  - kavagga, a, ā, h: kaṇṭhaja, sakaṭṭhāna
  - cavagga, i, ī, y: tāluja, jivhāmajjha
  - ṭavagga, r, ḷ: muddhaja, jivhopaggaṁ
  - tavagga, l, s: dantaja, jivhaggaṁ
  - pavagga, u, ū: oṭṭhaja, sakaṭṭhānaṁ
- <i><b>pañcavaggantā pana nāsikaṭṭhānepi </b></i>(the nose as well) <i><b>sakaṭṭhānepi jāyanti.</b></i>
- **vocab:** see the [Part I Vocabulary](/summaries/pali/niruttidipani-part-1-vocab) page
- <i><b>ekāro </b></i>(e) <i><b>kaṇṭhatālujo.</b></i>
- <i><b>okāro </b></i>(o)<i><b> kaṇṭhoṭṭhajo.</b></i>
- <i><b>vakāro </b></i>(v)<i><b> dantoṭṭhajo.</b></i>
- <i><b>apica </b></i>(furthermore, but)<i><b> ivaṇṇuvaṇṇā kaṇṭhepi </b></i>(also on the throat)<i><b> jāyantiyeva.</b></i>
- they can also be pronounced there
- **vocab:** see the [Part I Vocabulary](/summaries/pali/niruttidipani-part-1-vocab) page
- <i><b>yadā hakāro vaggantehi vā ya, ra, la, vehi vā yutto hoti, tadā urajoti vadanti.</b></i>
- <i><b>hakāraṁ pañcameheva</b></i><br><i><b>antaṭṭhāhi ca saṁyutaṁ</b></i><br><i><b>orasanti vadantettha</b></i><br><i><b>kaṇṭhajaṁ tadasaṁyutaṁ</b></i> (rūpasiddhi)<br>when h is with the 5th letters (vaggantas)<br>combined with antaṭṭhā letters<br>grammarians say that it is born in the chest<br>if not combined with them, it is born in the throat
- **vocab:** see the [Part I Vocabulary](/summaries/pali/niruttidipani-part-1-vocab) page

### map of <i><b>payatana</b></i>

- <i><b>kaṇṭhaṃ saṃvaritvā uccārito </b></i>(pronounced) <i><b>akāro </b></i>(a)<i><b> saṃvuṭo nāma.</b></i>
- closing the throat
- <i><b>sakasakaṭṭhāna </b></i>(one's own place)<i><b> karaṇāni vivaritvā uccāritā sesasarā </b></i>(except a)<i><b> ca sa, hakārā ca vivaṭā nāma.</b></i>

- gāḷhaṁ
- <i><b>thokaṃ phusāpetvā uccāritā ya, ra, la, vā </b></i>(y, r, l, v)<i><b> īsaṃphuṭṭhā nāma.</b></i>
- thokaṁ
- it includes ḷ
- <i><b>tattha oṭṭhajesu tāva </b></i>(first)<i><b> pavaggaṃ vadantānaṃ oṭṭhadvayassa gāḷhaṃ phusanaṃ icchitabbaṃ </b></i>(should be preferred)
- tāva ind.
- <i><b>kasmā? phuṭṭhapayatanikattā pavaggassa </b></i>(due to the pavagga having the state of having the effort of touching)

- <i><b>kasmā? vivaṭapayatanikattā uvaṇṇassa.</b></i>

- <i><b>note</b></i>:
- in SL 'a' is the saṁvuta (as in b<i><b>u</b></i>t), vivaṭa is like among
- in grammar books there is only one version of 'a' and it is saṁvuta (the a in father)
- <i><b>cūḷaniruttiyaṃ pana sabbe rassasarā saṃvuṭā nāma, sabbedīghasarā vivaṭā nāmāti vuttaṃ. tathā saddasāratthajāliniyaṃ </b></i>(name of a book)<i><b>, katthaci sakkaṭaganthe </b></i>(sanskrit books)<i><b> ca. idaṃ yuttataraṃ </b></i>(this is more suitable)<i><b>. aññaṭṭhānikabyañjanehi yuttā sarā </b></i>(the vowels which are combined with consonants which have other places)<i><b> attano ṭhāna karaṇāni jahantāpi </b></i>(abandoning their own place and instrument)<i><b> payatanaṃ na jahanti </b></i>(do not abandon their own effort)<i><b>. tasmā nānāvaṇṇānaṃ saṃsagge payatanānaṃ saṃsaggabhedopi veditabboti. </b></i> therefore the combination of diferent letters to/for/of ? the effort the division of combinations too should be known.
- <i><b>another opinion preferred by niruttidīpanī</b></i> (cūḷanirutti, saddasāratthajālini and some sanskrit books)<br>short vowels <i><b>saṁvuta</b></i><br>long vowels <i><b>vivaṭa</b></i>
- when letters are combined and form syllables, some letters can change their ṭhana and karaṇa but not the payatana<br>a single syllable may have multiple payatana
- question: explain the two versions and say which is more suitable according to niruttidipani
- extra information:
  - saddanīti says:
    - in sāsana vohāra phuṭṭha is used for aspirates, while apuṭṭha for inaspirates
- <i><b>tattha "suṇātu me"ti vadanto yadi ṇā-kāraṃ jivhaggena dantaṭṭhāne katvā vadeyya, dantajo nā-kāro eva bhaveyya. </b></i>  (here, if the "ṇā" in "suṇātu me" were to be pronounced on the location of the teeth, it would become the dental 'nā') (ṇā ⇒ nā)
- <i><b>tu-kārañca jivhopaggena muddhaṭṭhāne katvā vadeyya, muddhajo ṭu-kāro eva bhaveyya. </b></i> (tu ⇒ ṭu) (if "tu" were pronounced with the area close to the tip of the tongue on the location of the peak, it would become the retroflex "ṭu")
- <i><b>evañca sati </b></i>(when this is so) <i><b>akkharavipatti nāma siyā. esa nayo sesesu muddhajadantajesu. </b></i>(ṭ, ṭh, ḍ, ḍh, ṇ)<i><b> tasmā kammavācaṃ sāventehi </b></i>(by the ones who recite)<i><b> nāma ṭhāna, karaṇa, payatanesu suṭṭhu kusalehi </b></i>(skillful) <i><b>bhavitabbanti.</b></i>

- the air with sound heading towards the nasal path without having come out from the cavity of the mouth. Likewise, by not opening the mouth (by closing the throat), the consonant called niggahīta should be pronounced.

- When words are combined with an euphonic combination they are called sambandha. Those that should be pronounced by splitting the word are called vavatthita.
