## Lesson 5 - declension of 'atta', bhavalakkhaṇa, pubbasaradīghasandhi

- Atīte jambudīpe ajitaraṭṭhe eko gopālako vasi. Tassa gehe eko paccekabuddho nibaddhaṃ bhuñjati. Tasmiṃ gehe eko kukkuro ca ahosi. Paccekabuddho bhuñjanto tassa nibaddhaṃ ekaṃ bhattapiṇḍaṃ adāsi. So tena paccekabuddhe (loc.sg) sinehamakāsi. Gopālako divasassa dve vāre paccekabuddhassa upaṭṭhānaṃ gacchi. Sunakhopi tena saddhiṃ gacchi. Gopālo ekadivasaṃ paccekabuddhaṃ āha: “Yadā me bhante, okāso na bhavissati, tadā imaṃ sunakhaṃ pesessāmi. Tena saññāṇena āgaccheyyāthā”ti. Tato paṭṭhāya anokāsadivase sunakhaṃ pesesi. So ekavacaneneva pakkhanditvā paccekabuddhassa vasanaṭṭhānaṃ gantvā tikkhattuṃ bhussitvā attano āgatabhāvaṃ jānāpetvā ekamantaṃ nipajji. Paccekabuddhe velaṃ sallakkhetvā nikkhante bhussanto purato gacchi. Paccekabuddho taṃ vīmaṃsanto ekadivasaṃ aññaṃ maggaṃ paṭipajji. Atha sunakho purato tiriyaṃ ṭhatvā bhussitvā itaramaggameva naṃ āropesi.
- English translation:
  - In the past in the island of Jambudīpa in a country called Ajita dwelt a herdsman. In his house a silent Buddha regularly ate. In that house there was also a dog. The silent buddha would frequently give a lump of food to it. The dog because of that became fond of the silent buddha. The herdsman twice a day would go to silent buddha for serving him. The dog as well would go with him. One day the herdsman said to the silent buddha: "Whenever, bhante, I have no opportunity (to come) I will send this dog. By this sign may bhante come". From then onwards whenever he had no opportunity to go he would send the dog. The dog would spring forward on a single command and after having approached the dwelling place of the private buddha would bark thrice to make known its presence and lie down at a side. When the private buddha departed after having considered the time it would go ahead while barking. The private buddha one day took a different path in order to test the dog. Then the dog stood horizontally and while barking got the silent buddha back on the right path.

## Lesson 6 - declension of 'eta', causative, niggahītasandhi

- Athekadivasaṃ aññaṃ maggaṃ paṭipajjitvā sunakhe tiriyaṃ ṭhatvā vārentepi anivattitvā taṃ pādena apanetvā pāyāsi. Sunakho tassa anivattanabhāvaṃ ñatvā nivāsanakaṇṇe ḍaṃsitvā ākaḍḍhanto gantabbamaggameva pāpesi. Evaṃ so sunakho tasmiṃ paccekabuddhe balavasinehaṃ uppādesi. Aparabhāge paccekabuddhassa cīvaraṃ jīri. Athassa gopālako cīvaravatthāni adāsi. Paccekabuddho “Phāsukaṭṭhānaṃ gantvā cīvaraṃ kāressāmī”ti gopālakaṃ āha. Sopi “Bhante mā ciraṃ bahi vasitthā”ti avadi. Sunakhopi tesaṃ kathaṃ suṇanto aṭṭhāsi. Paccekabuddhe vehāsaṃ abbhuggantvā gacchante bhuṅkaritvā ṭhitassa sunakhassa hadayaṃ phali. Tiracchānā nāmete ujujātikā honti akuṭilā. Manussā pana aññaṃ cintenti, aññaṃ vadanti.
- English translation:
  - Then, one day after following another path when the dog  stood sideways preventing him he went on without stopping moving it away with his foot. The dog realizing he did not stop bit the edge of his robe and while dragging him made him reach the right path. In this way that dog gave rise to strong affection for the private buddha. Afterwards the private buddha's robe got worn out. Then the herdsman offered him cloth for a robe. The paccekabuddha said to the herdsman: "I will go to a comfortable place and have a robe made" and the herdsman said thus: "Bhante, may bhante not dwell away for long". The dog too stood listening to their conversation. When the private buddha rose up in the sky and was going away the dog barked and its heart was broken. Such animals are of an honest nature, not crooked, but humans think one thing and say another.

## Lesson 7 - saṅkhyānāma, tulyatthaliṅgatthavākya, yākārādi āgamasandhi, dvebhāvasandhi

- Kūṭavāṇija Jātakaṃ – 1 Atīte bārāṇasiyaṃ brahmadatte rajjaṃ kārente bodhisatto bārāṇasiyaṃ vāṇijakule nibbatti. Nāmaggahaṇadivase assa paṇḍitoti nāmamakaṃsu. So vayappatto aññena vāṇijena saddhiṃ ekato hutvā vāṇijjaṃ karoti. Tassa atipaṇḍitoti nāmaṃ ahosi. Te bārāṇasito pañcahi sakaṭasatehi bhaṇḍaṃ ādāya janapadaṃ gantvā vaṇijjaṃ katvā laddhalābhā puna bārāṇasiṃ āgamiṃsu. Atha tesaṃ bhaṇḍabhājanakāle atipaṇḍito āha: “Mayā dve koṭṭhāsā laddhabbā”ti. “Kiṃkāraṇā?”ti. “Tvaṃ paṇḍito, ahaṃ atipaṇḍito, paṇḍito ekaṃ laddhuṃ arahati, atipaṇḍito dve”ti. Nanu amhākaṃ dvinnaṃ bhaṇḍamūlampi goṇādayopi samasamāyeva. Kasmā tvaṃ dve koṭṭhāse laddhuṃ arahasī?”ti. “Atipaṇḍitabhāvenā”ti. Evaṃ te kathaṃ vaḍḍhetvā kalahaṃ akaṃsu.
- Quick English translation:
  - In the past, when Brahmadatta was reigning in Bārāṇasī, the bodhisatta was born in a family of merchants. In his naming day he was given the name Paṇḍita. After growing up he joined another merchant in business whose name was Atipaṇḍita. After taking goods from Bārāṇāsī with five hundred carts they went to the countryside, did business and after making profit came back to Bārāṇasī. Then when it was the time for sharing the goods Atipaṇḍito said: "I should receive two portions", "for what reason?" asked Paṇḍita. "You are Paṇḍita and I am Atipaṇḍita. Paṇḍita deserves one share while Atipaṇḍita deserves two". "Isn't our investments, oxen and so on, the same indeed?
