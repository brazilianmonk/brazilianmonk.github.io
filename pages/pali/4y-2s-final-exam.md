### Tappurisasamāsa


<a id="org427e709"></a>

#### Definition



-   in general
    -   **visesana-visesya**: compound formed from a qualifier and a qualified term
-   excluding kammadhāraya and digu
    -   **2nd meaning is predominant**: meaning of 2nd term (visesya) is predominant
    -   **bhinnādhikaraṇe**: they don't have the same referent
    -   **amādayo parapadehi** (kcyn): visesana bears a case ending beginning with the accusative (aṃ ādi (any other than paṭhamā), leading to 6 types
-   e.g.:
    -   tassa puriso tappuriso (his man)
-   notes:
    -   includes kammadhāraya and digu as subtypes
    -   called Dependant Determinative Compound

<a id="org2494215"></a>

#### 6 types


acc. to the vibhatti of the first term:

-   **dutiyā tappurisa** (accusative tappurisa) 
    -   araññaṁ gato araññagato (one who has gone to the forest)
-   **tatiyā tappurisa** (instrumental tappurisa
    -   buddhena bhāsito buddhabhāsito (the dhamma spoken by the buddha)
-   **catutthī tappurisa** (dative tappurisa) 
    -   kaṭhinassa dussaṃ kaṭhinadussaṃ (cloth intended for the kaṭhina robe)
-   **pañcamī tappurisa** (ablative tappurisa) 
    -   corato bhayaṃ corabhayaṃ (fear arising from thieves)
-   **chaṭṭhī tappurisa** (genitive tappurisa) 
    -   rañño putto rājaputto (the king’s son)
    -   tassa puriso (ta + purisa) tappuriso (his man)
-   **sattamī tappurisa** (locative tappurisa)
    -   vikāle bhojanaṃ vikālabhojanaṃ (eating at an improper time)
-   notes:
    -   **paṭhamā tappurisa** (kammadhāraya and digu): 1st member bears the nominative case
    -   also
        -   amādiparatappurisa
        -   aluttatappurisa




<a id="orgc744bf2"></a>

#### Gender, Number, and Declension


Determined by the **2nd term**

-   e.g.: araññagata
    -   purisādigaṇa declension
        -   when referring to a single man: araññagato (m. sg.)
        -   when referring to many men: araññagatā (m. pl.)
    -   kaññādigaṇa declension
        -   when referring to a single woman: araññagatā (f. sg.)
    -   etc.
-   notes:
    -   abhidheyya: the meaning denoted by a word




<a id="orga3b56a4"></a>

#### Viggaha



-   look at the meaning of the samāsa
    -   e.g.: tappurisa (his man)
-   determine the case ending that has been omitted from the qualifier
    -   e.g.: chaṭṭhīvibhattī
-   place the qualifier in that case
    -   e.g.: tassa
-   place the qualified in the nominative
    -   e.g.: puriso
-   final: tassa puriso tappuriso




<a id="org39f9c1d"></a>

#### Kitanta Upapada Samāsa


**kitanta tappurisa / kitanta samāsa / upapadasamāsa**: compound in which

-   **upapada**: the 1st member is an upapada (close word): preceding noun
-   **kitaka**: ends in a kitaka that cannot occur by itself
-   **niccasamāsa**: always occurs in the compounded form, never as 2 separate words
-   e.g.: kammakāro (one who does work; worker)
    -   kamma (upapada) kar + ṇa (kitaka); NOT kammaṁ kāro
-   notes:
    -   mostly found in tappurisa
    -   gato (kitaka): can occur by itself, so saraṇagato is not kitanta



1.  Kitanta Upapada Viggaha

    
    
    -   **assapada viggaha**: uses a substitute word since the kitaka cannot be by itself
    -   kitakaviggahas always use **verbs**
    -   e.g.:
        -   kammaṃ karotīti kammakāro (one who does work; worker)
            -   kamma (upapada) + kara + ṇa (kitaka)
            -   NOT kammaṁ kāro kammakāro (not a verb)
            -   NOT kammaṁ kārako kammakāro (not a verb)
        -   kumbhaṃ karotīti kumbhakāro (one who makes pots; potter)
        -   alaṁ karotīti alaṅkāro (one who makes beauty)
        -   atthaṃ kāmetīti atthakāmo (one who desires benefit)
        -   dhammaṃ kāmetīti dhammakāmo (one who desires the Dhamma)
        -   dhammaṃ dhāretīti dhammadharo (one who bears or retains the Dhamma)
        -   vinayaṃ dhāretīti vinayadharo (one who bears or retains the Vinaya)

#### Forms That Do Not Form Compounds


forms ending in the following as the 2nd member do NOT form compounds

-   **-vantu**
    -   **-tavantu**
    -   **-tāvī**
-   **-anta**
    -   **-māna**
-   **-tvā**
    -   **-tvāna**
    -   **-tuna**
-   **-tuṃ**
    -   **-tave**

-   e.g.:
    -   odanaṃ bhuttavā (a person who has eaten rice); NOT odanabhuttavā
    -   dhammaṃ suṇanto (one who hears the dhamma)
    -   dhammaṃ suṇanto (one who hears the Dhamma)
    -   dhammaṃ suṇamāno (one who is listening to thle dhamma)
    -   dhammaṃ suṇamāno (one who is listening to the Dhamma)
    -   dhammaṃ sutvā
    -   dhammaṃ sutvāna
    -   dhammaṃ sotuna
    -   dhammaṃ sotuṃ
    -   dhammaṃ sotave

    Although the meaning is that of an object (kammattha), the grammatical case is the genitive. Since the 
    determining factor is the case ending rather than the semantic role, this is still a chaṭṭhī tappurisa 
    compound. 
    
    Then it declines like a regular a-stem masculine noun (purisa): devarājo, devarājā, 
    devarājaṁ, devarāje, etc. 
    The same suffix may also be added to sakha, another member of the rājādi class. 
    Thus, from: Devānaṁ sakhā → devasakhā, the compound may decline either:  
    according to the rājādi class: devasakhā, devasakhāno, etc. or,  
    according to the purisādi class: devasakho, devasakhā, etc.

3.  Shortening of ī & ū

    **ī & ū at the end of the first member are often shortened** 19 
    
    -   hatthino padaṁ hatthipadaṁ (elephant's footprint)
    -   itthiyā rūpaṁ itthirūpaṁ (female form)
    -   bhikkhuniyā saṅgho bhikkhunisaṅgho (community of nuns)
    -   jambuyā sākhā jambusākhā (branch of a rose-apple tree)
    -   sabbaññuno sāsanaṁ sabbaññusāsanaṁ

4.  Gamaka Compounds

    -   a type of tappurisa
    -   not compounded
        -   **aññamañña anapekkha** (mutually independent): words that, though close to each other, do not compound because they are mutually independent
            -   bhaṭo rañño putto devadattassa (the servant of the king is the son of Devadatta)
                -   NOT rājaputto
        -   **aññasāpekkha** (dependent on another word): words that, though close to each other, do not compound because they depend on another word
            -   devadattassa kaṇhā dantā (the black teeth of Devadatta)
                -   NOT devadattakaṇhā
        -   **gamakasamāsa**
            -   an aññasāpekkha term may be compounded if the intended meaning can still be clearly understood after compounding
                -   devadattassa *gurukulaṁ* (the family of Devadatta's teacher)
                    -   devadattassa guruno kulaṁ 
                        -   guru: depends on both:
                            -   kula (the teacher's family)
                            -   devadattassa (devadatta's teacher)
                -   bhagavato *sāvakasaṅgho*
                -   devadattassa *bhaginiputto*
                -   tassa *pādamūlaṁ*
    
    -   gamaka: that which makes something understood
    -   sāpekkhatte satipi gamakattā samāso &#x2013; even though dependence exists, compounding occurs because the meaning can still be understood
    
    19 This change takes place according to the Kaccāyana rule “Kvacādimajjhuttarānaṁ dīgharassā 
    paccayesu ca.” Under this rule, the long and short vowels occurring at the beginning, middle, or end of certain words are subject to alteration when suffixes or stems are succeeding.

-   Chando ca pārisuddhi ca chandapārisuddhi (consent and purity)
-   Paṭisandhi ca pavatti ca paṭisandhipavatti (rebirth-linking and continued existence)

#### Dvanda &#x2013; Meaning

The word dvandva is formed by combining two occurrences of the word dvi (“two”). 
A dvandva, in Sanskrit, is anything that occurs in pairs. Thus, a duel between two persons is 
called a dvandva battle.  

In Pāli, the word is dvanda. Its viggaha is: Dve ca dve ca dvandā. 
Just as the word dvanda is formed from dvi + dvi, all compounds formed by joining two or 
more coordinate words, such as Sāriputta + Moggallāna, are called Dvandva compounds.

### Bahubbīhisamāsa


<a id="org3099fc9"></a>

#### Definition

**aññapadatthesu bahubbīhi** (kcyn): expresses the meaning of another word, which is different from the meaning of the two compounded words
e.g., āgatā samaṇā imanti āgatasamaṇo (saṅghārāmo) (the monastery to which monks have come)

-   āgatā: those who have come
-   samaṇā: contemplatives
-   imaṁ: monastery (referent)

**aññapadatthappadhāno bahubbīhi** (rpsd): principal meaning is that of the another word

aka: Relative Compound or Attributive Compound


<a id="orgb7231de"></a>

#### Bahubbīhi: Meaning

**bahavo vīhayo yassa so bahubbīhi**: he who has much rice is called bahubbīhi

-   bahu + vīhi (much grain)
-   yassa: to whom there is much grain

1.  Dvipada-tulyādhikaraṇa Bahubbīhi

    
    2 terms in tulyādhikaraṇa
    e.g. āgatā samaṇā imanti āgatasamaṇo
    
    -   before compounding: both are tulyādikaraṇa (refer to the same referent)
        -   āgatā: those (contemplatives) who have come
        -   samaṇā: contemplatives
    -   after compounding: both refer to a single another referent
        -   ima: the place to which the contemplatives have come
    
    
    
    1.  Gender and Number of Bahubbīhi Compounds
    
        
        **bahubbīhi cāyaṁ abhidheyyaliṅgavacano** (rpsd): meaning, gender, and number acc. to the referent
        
        -   āgatā samaṇā imeti āgatasamaṇā (saṅghārāmā) (monasteries to which monks have come)
        -   āgatā samaṇā imanti āgatasamaṇā (sāvatthī) (sāvatthī to which monks have come)
        -   āgatā samaṇā imanti āgatasamaṇaṁ (jetavanaṁ) (jetavana to which monks have come)
        
        
    
    2.  Aññapada
    
        
        Word that expresses the referent to which the compound refers
        
        -   **ya, ta, eta, and ima (/ vā)** are usually used in its place so are also called the external word
            -   note: in saṅkhyobhayapada bahubbīhi, "vā" is the external word
        -   case
            -   it is put in any vibhatti except the ālapana
            -   depends on the meaning intended
        
        e.g., āgatā samaṇā imaṁ (saṅghārāmaṁ) āgatasamaṇo
        
        -   external word: saṅghārāma, ima
        -   here, dutiyā because it expresses the place to which the monks came
        
        
    
    3.  Viggaha of Bahubbīhi Compounds
    
        -   put the terms in the **nominative** case (singular or plural)
            -   e.g., *āgatā samaṇā*
        -   put the **external word** (ya, ta, eta, and ima (/ vā)) in the appropriate case followed by "iti"
            -   e.g.,
                -   āgatā samaṇā *yanti* āgatasamaṇo
                -   āgatā samaṇā *tanti* āgatasamaṇo
                -   āgatā samaṇā *etanti* āgatasamaṇo
                -   āgatā samaṇā *imanti* āgatasamaṇo
        -   optional
            -   add **referent word** after pronoun to clarify its meaning
                -   e.g. āgatā samaṇā imaṁ *saṅghārāmanti* āgatasamaṇo (saṅghārāmo)
            -   add **ta and ima** to express the meaning "that is" (if so, remove "iti")
                -   e.g.
                    -   āgatā samaṇā imaṁ saṅghārāmaṁ, *so* āgatasamaṇo
                    -   āgatā samaṇā imaṁ saṅghārāmaṁ, *soyaṁ* āgatasamaṇo (saṅghārāmo)

                        -   sāyaṁ (sā + ayaṁ)

            -   āgatā samaṇā *tanti* āgatasamaṇo

            3 Bahubbīhi compounds are classified into two types: Tagguṇa-saṁviññāṇa and Atagguṇa
            saṁviññāṇa. 
            Here, guṇa means the qualifier. Since the constituent words of a Bahubbīhi compound qualify the 
            meaning of another (external) word, the constituent words are called qualifiers (guṇa). 
            Saṁviññāṇa means “that which conveys together” (saha + viññāpaka). 
            If the meaning of the external word (aññapada) inherently includes the meaning expressed by the 
            qualifier, the compound is called Tagguṇasaṁviññāṇa. 
            For example: Lambo kaṇṇo yassa so lambakaṇṇo - one who has long ears. 
            Here, the long ears belong to the person. Therefore, the meaning of yassa (“the one who has”) inherently 
            includes the meaning of the qualifier, namely, long ears. 
            If the meaning of the external word does not inherently include the meaning expressed by the qualifier, 
            the compound is called Atagguṇasaṁviññāṇa. 
            For example: Bahu dhanaṁ yassāti bahudhano - one who possesses much wealth. 
            Here, the person and the great wealth are two distinct entities. Merely referring to the person does not 
            simultaneously convey the meaning of much wealth. Therefore, this is an Atagguṇa-saṁviññāṇa Bahubbīhi compound.

## Taddhita


<a id="orgf5108ab"></a>

### Introduction


<a id="org62b7ec7"></a>

#### Definition



-   **Taddhitapada** (secondary derivatives): word formed by a nominal stem + taddhita suffix
-   **Taddhitapaccaya** (secondary-derivative suffix): suffixes that bear particular meanings and are added after nominal stems to form secondary derivatives

e.g., nominal base + taddhitapaccaya + vibhattipaccaya

-   vasiṭṭha + ṇa => Vāsiṭṭha + si => Vāsiṭṭho (vasiṭṭha’s child or a person belonging to vasiṭṭha’s lineage)
-   nāvā + ṇika => nāviko (one who crosses by ship; a sailor)
-   alasa + ṇya => ālasyaṃ (the state of a lazy person; laziness)
-   guṇa + vantu => guṇavā (one who possesses virtues; a virtuous person)
-   pañca + ma => pañcamo (one who completes a group of five; the fifth)
-   eka + kkhattuṃ => ekakkhattuṃ (once)




<a id="orga038e57"></a>

#### 5 Nāmas



-   kitakanāma
-   taddhitanāma
-   nāmanāma
-   sabbanāma
-   samāsanāma




<a id="org642362c"></a>

#### Taddhita &#x2013; Meaning



-   **Tasmā tividhaliṅgato paraṃ hutvā hitā sahitāti taddhitā** (rpsd): they are called taddhita because
    -   they occur after and in connection with the three kinds of nominal bases
        -   suddhanāma (primary nouns)
        -   samāsanāma (compound nouns)
        -   taddhitanāma (second derivative nouns)
        -   notes: rarely also to
            -   ākhyātas: ehipassiko (ehi passa ṇika)
            -   nipātas: 
                -   sāhasikaṁ (ṇika)
                -   ajjatana: something that happens today (ajja tana)
                -   purātana: something that happened in the past (pura tana)
                -   svātana: something that happens tomorrow (suve tana)
                -   sanātana: something that happens forever (sanaṁ tana)
                -   cirantana: something that happens for long time (ciraṁ tana)
    -   **Tasmā hitā taddhitā**: pañcamītappurisasamāsa
        -   ta + hita = taddhita
            -   hita / sahitā / dhita: applied, placed (r/dhā)
            -   tasmā (from that): tividhaliṅgato
    -   The statement that taddhita suffixes occur after nouns describes the usual situation. In instances such as ehipassiko (the Dhamma that is worthy of the invitation “Come and see”), a taddhita suffix is also added to a verbal form.  Particles are also treated as nouns in this context. Thus, in instances such as sāhasika (sahasā + ṇika, meaning “deed that is accidentally or forcefully done”) and ajjatana (ajja + tana, meaning “that which happens today”), taddhita suffixes are added to particles as well. Accordingly, taddhita suffixes are added to nouns and particles and, occasionally, to verbal forms. No words formed by adding taddhita suffixes to prefixes are found
-   **Tesaṃ nāmikānaṃ hitā upakārakāti taddhitā** (rpsd): called taddhita because they are beneficial or useful to those nominal bases
    -   **Tesaṃ hitā taddhitā**: catutthī/chaṭṭhī-tappurisasamāsa

primary denotation of the word taddhita: the taddhita suffixes such as ṇa



<a id="org575d849"></a>

#### Paccaya &#x2013; Meaning


**Paṭicca etasmā attho etīti paccayo** (rpsd): called paccaya because, depending on them, a meaning arises

-   paccaya: kitakanāma (pati √i)




<a id="orge710069"></a>

#### Usage


Main: to express concisely a meaning otherwise expressed in two or three words 

-   vasiṭṭhassa apaccaṃ vāsiṭṭho (Vasiṭṭha’s child or a person belonging to his lineage)
-   vinayaṃ adhīte venayiko (one who studies the vinaya; a person learned in the vinaya)
-   guṇo assa atthi guṇavā (a virtuous person)
-   alasassa bhāvo ālasyaṃ (the state of a lazy person; laziness)




<a id="org97f9f3f"></a>

#### Samāsa vs. Taddhita



-   **Samāsa**: two or more nominal forms are combined 
    -   rañño puriso rājapuriso (the king’s man; a royal servant)
-   **Taddhita**: a nominal base and a suffix are combined
    -   Vasiṭṭhassa apaccaṃ → Vāsiṭṭho (Vasiṭṭha + ṇa; Vasiṭṭha’s child)
-   Thus, in a compound, two or more words combine to form a single word, whereas in taddhita formation, a nominal base and a suffix combine to form a new nominal base.

**Samāso padasaṅkhepo, padappaccayasaṃhitaṃ,
Taddhitaṃ nāma hotevaṃ, viññeyyaṃ tesamantaraṃ** (rpsd) 
A compound is a word in which words have been combined and condensed. A taddhita is a word in 
which a word and a suffix have been combined. Their difference should be understood in this way



<a id="orgee0775a"></a>

#### How Taddhita Words Are Formed



-   Meaning: Vasiṭṭhassa apaccaṃ / putto (Vasiṭṭha’s child)
    -   Vasiṭṭha: person named Vasiṭṭha
    -   ṇa: apaccaṁ (son)
-   vasiṭṭhassa apaccaṃ > vasiṭṭhassa + ṇa
-   vibhattipaccayas are dropped
    -   vasiṭṭha + ṇa
-   ṇ is applied and elided
    -   ṇ: anubhanda (vuḍḍhi of the initial vowel); indicatory letter
    -   vāsiṭṭha + a 
        -   sometimes, vāseṭṭha  (medial vowel i > e)
-   base + suffix = taddhita base
    -   vāsiṭṭha
-   vibhattipaccaya is added for it to be used
    -   vāsiṭṭha + si = vāsiṭṭho, etc.
-   noun is declined




<a id="org294c158"></a>

#### Anubandha (Ṇ)



-   does not become part of the final form of the word
-   after the suffix has been applied, this indicatory ṇ is elided. The letter is called an anubandha (indicatory letter) because it is attached closely to the suffix
-   ṇa → ṇ + a
-   ṇika → ṇ + ika
-   ṇeyya → ṇ + eyya
-   ṇava → ṇ + ava
-   ṇera → ṇ + era

The indicatory ṇ is attached to a suffix to show that vuddhi is to take place in the base. 
3 Apaccaṃ is a neuter word bearing the meaning “son.” 



<a id="orgb46db22"></a>

#### Vuddhi


Vuddhi: strengthening

-   a > ā
-   i, ī > e
-   u, ū > o

e.g.,

-   vasiṭṭha + ṇa > vāsiṭṭho (Vasiṭṭha’s child or a person belonging to his lineage)
-   vinaya + ṇika > venayiko (one who has studied the Vinaya; a person learned in the Vinaya)
-   uḷumpa + ṇika > oḷumpiko (one who crosses by raft)

vuddhi doesn't happen

-   if followed by a conjunct
    -   kacca + ṇāyana > kaccāyano (Kacca’s child or a person belonging to Kacca’s lineage)
-   if the base begins with ā, e, or o
    -   bhāradvāja + ṇa > bhāradvājo (Bhāradvāja’s child or a person belonging to Bhāradvāja’s lineage)
    -   vesāmitta + ṇa > vesāmitto (Vesāmitta’s child or a person belonging to Vesāmitta’s lineage)
    -   gotama + ṇa > gotamo (Gotama’s child or a person belonging to Gotama’s lineage)




<a id="org40b52d2"></a>

#### 3 Vidhis of Vuddhi



-   **niccavidhi** (mandatory arrangement): vuddhi necessarily takes place
    -   e.g. Vāsiṭṭho
-   **aniccavidhi** (optional arrangement): vuddhi takes place optionally
    -   e.g. oḷumpiko / uḷumpiko (one who crosses by raft)
-   **asantavidhi** (non-arrangement): vuddhi does not take place
    -   e.g.
        -   nīlaṃ (something blue)
        -   pītaṃ (something yellow)

**Vāsiṭṭhādīsu niccā’yaṃ, anicco’ḷumpikādisu;
Na vuddhi nīlapītādo, vavatthitavibhāsato** (rpsd)
This vuddhi is obligatory in words such as Vāsiṭṭha, optional in instances such as oḷumpika, and absent in words such as nīla and pīta. This is due to vavatthita-vibhāsā: that is, the instances in which vuddhi does or does not occur are systematically determined within the grammar



<a id="org330e5e6"></a>

#### Gender



-   all three genders
-   determined by the referent that it denotes or qualifies
    -   vāsiṭṭho puriso (a man belonging to vasiṭṭha’s lineage)
    -   vāsiṭṭhī kaññā (a young woman belonging to vasiṭṭha’s lineage)
    -   vāsiṭṭhaṃ apaccaṃ (a child belonging to vasiṭṭha’s lineage)
        -   words formed with certain suffixes, however, occur only in a prescribed gender. For example, abstract nouns such as lahutā and mudutā, formed with the suffix tā, are always feminine. Special points concerning gender will be explained under the respective suffixes




<a id="org9644cbd"></a>

#### 3/6 Types



-   **sāmañña taddhita** (general taddhita)
    -   **apacca** (descendant): meaning: someone’s child or a person belonging to someone’s lineage
        -   e.g. vāsiṭṭho
    -   **anekattha** (miscellaneous meanings): meanings: various
        -   being mixed with something
        -   having studied something
            -   e.g. venayiko (vinaya + ṇika)
        -   being made from something
        -   being born in a particular place
    -   **assatthi** (possession): meaning: the possession of something
        -   e.g. guṇavā (guṇa + vantu)
    -   **saṅkhyā** (number): meaning: numbers
        -   e.g. pañcamo (pañca + ma)
-   **bhava taddhita** (abstract taddhita): meaning: the state or quality of being
    -   e.g. ālasyaṃ (alasa + ṇya; the state of a lazy person; laziness)
-   **abyaya taddhita** (indeclinable taddhita): forms taddhita words regarded as indeclinable particles
    -   e.g.
        -   dvikkhattuṃ (dvi + kkhattuṃ; twice)
        -   dvidhā (dvi + dhā; in two ways)
        -   yathā (ya + thā; in a certain manner; as)

**sāmaññavuttibhāvatthā, byayato taddhitaṃ tidhā,
tassādi catudhā’paccā'nekattha’ssatthisaṅkhyāto** (rpsd) 
Taddhita is threefold: general-function taddhita, abstract taddhita, and indeclinable taddhita. The first of these, general-function taddhita, is fourfold: descendant, miscellaneous meanings, possession, and number.

**Visesa Taddhita**: taddhita suffixes used to indicate a greater or more distinctive quality within a group greater or more distinctive than that of the others

-   Rūpasiddhi: considers it a separate class
-   most common are tara and tama

Distinctive or Comparative sense: "more than", "the most"
