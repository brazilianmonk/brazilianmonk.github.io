- 1. Word-by-Word Translation:
  - I include a word-by-word translation in my notes to make everything clear for you, but I do not expect you to follow this format in your own work.
- 2. Your Translations vs. My Notes:
  - I am happy with the way you are translating now. Differences between your translations and my notes do not mean your work is wrong or weak. My notes are based on years of practice with Pali texts, so use them as a guide to improve your understanding and skills.
- 3. Approach to Translation:
  - In response to Venerable Ariyañāṇa's question today: Last year, I asked you to translate freely to help you gradually familiarize yourself with the language. Free translation works for stories, but it does not always work well for the deeper explanations of the Buddha's words found in the commentaries.
  - Now, we should aim for literal translation as much as possible. Since Pali and English are very different languages, literal translation may not always be possible. In such cases:
  - Use alternative expressions in English without distorting the meaning.
  - If the literal translation seems awkward in English, provide a free translation in brackets for clarity.
  - The goal is to make the translation as literal as possible while ensuring it remains meaningful.
  - As you continue to learn grammar and gain familiarity with Pāl̥i, you will discover more ways to translate freely without distorting the intended meaning.
