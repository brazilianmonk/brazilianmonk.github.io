    - Niggahīta Sandhi
      - 2 types of niggahīta sandhi according to the letter that substitutes ṁ when followed by a vowel (ṁ + vowel)
        - makāra ādesa sandhi (substitution of ṁ for m): gantabbamaggaṃ + eva = gantabbamaggameva
        - dakāra ādesa sandhi (substitution of ṁ for d): this happens only with the pronouns:
          - ya (yaṁ + idaṁ = yadidaṁ)
          - ta (taṁ + eva = tadeva)
          - eta (etaṁ + ahosi = etadahosi)
          - note: philologically, this is the recurrence of the ending consonant d of those pronouns found in Sanskrit (yad, tad, etad)
      - ādeso (m.) √dis: (gram) substitution; replacement; change

- From Kaccāyana: "yavamadanataralā cāgamā" > yava (as long as) mada (one is intoxicated) na (one cannot) Tara (cross) la (there)
  - dvebhāva sandhi
    - sometimes the consonant in the beginning of the second word is duplicated, which is called augmentation of a consonant; the consonant that is thus augmented is called dvebhāva
      - philologically, most of such doubling is due to the assimilation of the two beginning consonants of the Sanskrit counterpart of those words. For example, vaya + prāpta > vayaprāpta > vayappātta > vayappatta; nāma + grahaṇa + divasa > nāmagrahaṇadivasa > nāmaggahaṇadivasa
      - dvebhāva sandhi is twofold:
        - sadisa dvebhāva sandhi (similar augmented consonant): pp, gg
          - sappuriso
          - duggati
        - asadisa dvebhāva sandhi (dissimilar augmented consonants): bbh, pph
          - when the beginning letter of the second word is an aspiration (kh, gh, ch, jh, ṭh, ḍh, th, dh, ph, bh), the augmented consonant is its non-aspirated counterpart (k, g, c, j, t, d, p, b) since in pāḷi two aspirated consonants never come close to each other without a vowel in between them

    - Passive voice sentences
      - In passive voice sentences the subject is written in the instrumental case whereas the object is written in the nominative case because the word that indicates the verb refers to the object. The object is utta kamma (as opposed to utta kattu). The word referred to by the verb or the verbal-word should always be written in the nominative case.
      - if a word made in the passive meaning is in the place of a verb the question that should be asked must be passive. if a word made in the active meaning is in the place of a verb the question that should be asked must be active. One cannot ask an active voice question regarding a passively constructed word which is in the place of a verb and vice-versa.
      - Sentences in which the object is expressed by either the verb or the verbal word are called passive voice sentences Sentences in which the subject is expressed either by the verb or by the verbal word are called active voice sentences
      - Syntax
        - Subject (kattu): Said by the verb or verbal-word (utta): nominative Unsaid by the verb or verbal-word (anutta): instrumental Object (kamma): Said by the verb or verbal-word (utta): nominative Unsaid by the verb or verbal-word (anutta): accusative
      - Niggahīta sandhi
        - Parassara lopa sandhi
          - When niggahīta is followed by a vowel sometimes the vowel is elided (tvaṃ + asi > tvaṃsi (tvaṃsi ācariyo mama)
        - vaggantādesa sandhi
          - When a niggahīta is followed by a group-letter (vaggakkhara), the niggahīta is substituted by the ending letter of the group (vagganta byañjana) to which that group-consonant belongs. (taṇhaṅkaro > taṇhaṃ + karo)

    - English translation:
      - Then the owners of a female slave who did not give the money back made her sit down by the door and were hitting her with a whip. Her name was "The one who protects wealth". Walking on the middle of the road he saw her being beaten and asked: "why are you beating her?", "She did not give back the money", "What is her name?", "The name is The one who protects wealth", "Even being named "The one who protects wealth" she is not able to give back a mere fee". "Those (who are named) those who protect wealth as well as those who are named those who do not protect wealth can be poor. The name is just a designation. I think you are a fool". Becoming more indifferent to his name he left the city, took the road and on it saw a man who had lost his way and asked him "Hey there, what are you doing wandering around?", "I have lost my way, sir", "What is your name?", "'One who knows the way' is my name", "Even those named 'One who knows the way' can also lose their way", "Those named 'one who knows the way' as well as those who are named 'one who does not know the way' can both lose their way. The name is just a designation. I think you are a fool!". Becoming more indifferent to his name he went to the presence of the future-buddha who said: "...only by deeds there is success. Enough of other names for me. May this indeed be my name". The future-buddha comparing what was seen and done by him uttered the verse: "Having seen the one who lives dead, the wealthy-one poor, the skilled in the way lost in the woods, pāpaka came back"
