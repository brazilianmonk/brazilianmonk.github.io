- Paragraph 5
  - tattha phandananti rūpādīsu ārammaṇesu vipphandamānaṃ. capalanti vipphandamānaṃ ekairiyāpathena asaṇṭhahanto gāmadārako viya ekasmiṃ ārammaṇe asaṇṭhahanato capalaṃ. Cittanti viññāṇaṃ, bhūmivatthuārammaṇakiriyādivicittatāya (5th) panetaṃ “Citta”nti vuttaṃ.
  - translation
    - There, "phandana" means shaking while taking (lit. in) objects such as rūpa, etc. "Capala" means moving to and fro because of not remaining still in one object like a village boy who does not remain still in one posture Consciousness is awareness. This consciousness (citta) is called so because of the diversity (vicittatā) of spheres, bases, objects, and functions.
  - grammar
    - 7 meanings of 'iti' (gāthā)
      - iti hetu (i) pakāresu (ii), ādimhi (iii) cāvadhāraṇe (iv) nidassane (v) padatthassa, vipallāse (vi) samāpane (vii)
        - hetu (i): "because of" in definitions‒"cintetīti cittaṁ"
          - cintanamattaṁ cittaṁ: there is no capacity to perform an action, so no 'iti' is used
        - pakāresu (ii): "similar to"
        - ādimhi (iii): "beginning with"‒"evaṁ me sutanti..."
        - cāvadhāraṇe (iv): emphasis‒"
        - nidassane (v): showing, pointing out‒"aṁ iti niggahītaṁ"
        - padatthassa vipallāse (vi): describing the meaning of a word‒"phandananti"
        - samāpane (vii): concluding‒"bhavagāti"
    - some words are said to have an inherent meaning and are not separated into roots, etc
      - khandha: collection
      - citta: diversity
      - piṭaka: basket
  - sandhis
    - phandananti
    - rūpādīsu
    - vipphandamānaṃ

  - usukārova tejananti yathā nāma usukāro araññato ekaṃ vaṅkadaṇḍakaṃ āharitvā nittacaṃ katvā kañjiyatelena makkhetvā aṅgārakapalle tāpetvā rukkhālake uppīḷetvā nivaṅkaṃ ujuṃ vālavijjhanayoggaṃ karoti, katvā ca pana rājarājamahāmattānaṃ sippaṃ dassetvā mahantaṃ sakkārasammānaṃ labhati, evameva medhāvī paṇḍito viññū puriso phandanādisabhāvametaṃ cittaṃ dhutaṅgāraññāvāsavasena, nittacaṃ apagataoḷārikakilesaṃ katvā
    - translation

Likewise, a knowledgeable, wise and discerning man, having made this consciousness, which has natures such as wavering, barkless, making it free from gross defilements by the power of austere practices and forest dwelling...

- Paragraph 9
  - vārijovāti maccho viya, thale khittoti hatthena vā pādena vā jālādīnaṃ vā aññatarena thale chaḍḍito. Okamokataubbhatoti “Okapuṇṇehi cīvarehī”ti ettha udakaṃ okaṃ (hoti / nāma), “Okaṃ pahāya aniketasārī”ti ettha ālayo; ettha ubhayampi labbhati. “Okamokataubbhato”ti hi ettha okamokatoti udakasaṅkhātā ālayāti ayamattho. Ubbhatoti uddhaṭo.
    - translation
      - Vārijova: like a fish. Thale khitto: thrown on dry ground with hands, feet, or something like nets, etc. Okamokataubbhato: “Okapuṇṇehi cīvarehi (with robes full of water)": here, "oka" is water; “Okaṃ pahāya aniketasārī (one who wanders homeless, having abandoned the house)": here, abode; In this case, both are found. Okamokataubbhato: here, the meaning "okamokato" is "from an abode called water". Ubbhato means pulled out.
    - grammar
      - hatthena vā pādena vā jālādīnaṃ (niddhāraṇa meaning) vā aññatarena: by a certain thing among things like nets, etc

- Paragraph 10
  - paripphandatidaṃ cittanti yathā so udakālayato ubbhato thale khitto maccho udakaṃ alabhanto paripphandati, evamidaṃ pañcakāmaguṇālayābhirataṃ cittaṃ tato uddharitvā māradheyyasaṅkhātaṃ (what is called) vaṭṭaṃ pahātuṃ vipassanākammaṭṭhāne khittaṃ kāyikacetasikavīriyena santāpiyamānaṃ paripphandati, saṇṭhātuṃ na sakkoti. Evaṃ santepi dhuraṃ anikkhipitvā medhāvī puggalo taṃ vuttanayeneva ujuṃ kammaniyaṃ karotīti attho.
    - translation
      - "paripphandatidaṃ cittaṁ": just as a fish trembles, having been taken out of its watery abode, thrown on the ground, and not getting water; in the same way, this mind that delights in the abode of the five strings of sense pleasures trembles, having been taken out from there and thrown in the development of insight to abandon the cycle called the domain of māra, being heated with the bodily and mental heat, and is unable to remain still. In the same way, even, when it exists like this, not having abadoned the responsibility, the wise man, by the means mentioned, makes that (mind) straight and fit (suitable for work [burning the defilements, realizing the truth]). This is the meaning.
    - grammar
      - santāpiyamānaṃ (i + ya): being heated (passive present participle)
        - bhuñ ya te ⇒ bhuñj i ya te
        - santāpayati ⇒ santāpayamāna
      - Evaṃ santepi: bhavalakkhaṇa: sante "when it exists"; pi "even": even when it exists like this
    - sandhis

- Paragraph 11
  - aparo nayo – idaṃ māradheyyaṃ kilesavaṭṭaṃ avijahitvā ṭhitaṃ cittaṃ so vārijo viya paripphandati, tasmā māradheyyaṃ pahātave, yena kilesavaṭṭasaṅkhātena māradheyyeneva paripphandati, taṃ pahātabbanti. Gāthāpariyosāne meghiyatthero sotāpattiphale patiṭṭhito, aññepi bahū sotāpannādayo jātāti (iti marks the end of the comm of these two gāthās). Meghiyattheravatthu paṭhamaṃ.
    - translation
      - Another method‒the mind, which remains without having abandoned this round of defilements, this domain of māra, trembles like that fish; therefore, "the domain of māra should be abandoned": whatever makes it tremble, which is known as the round of defilements, the domain of māra, that should be abandoned (lit. by whatever is known as...). At the conclusion of the verse, the Elder Meghiya was established in the fruit of stream-entry. Many others too became sotāpannas and so on.
    - grammar
      - pahātave: to abandon (but here: should be abandoned)
        - -tave = -tuṁ
        - tave here means -tabba: it is explained in "taṁ pahātabbaṁ"
      - yena paripphandati, taṁ pahātabbaṁ: "by what" "due to what" (the mind shakes), that should be abandoned
    - sandhis
      - paripphandati, meghiyatthero, patiṭṭhito: pari phandati (asadisa dvebhāva)
      - māradheyyeneva: māradheyyena eva (pubbasaralopa)
      - pahātabbanti: pahātabbaṁ iti (vaggantādesa)
      - jātāti: jātā iti (parasaralopa)
- Paragraph 12
  - 2.aññatarabhikkhuvatthu dunniggahassa lahunoti (beginning with) imaṃ dhammadesanaṃ satthā sāvatthiyaṃ jetavane viharanto aññataraṃ bhikkhuṃ ārabbha kathesi. Kosalarañño kira vijite pabbatapāde mātikagāmo nāma eko ghanavāso gāmo ahosi. Athekadivasaṃ saṭṭhimattā bhikkhū satthu santike yāva arahattā kammaṭṭhānaṃ kathāpetvā taṃ gāmaṃ gantvā piṇḍāya pavisiṃsu. Atha ne yo tassa gāmassa sāmiko mātiko nāma; tassa mātā (nom.) disvā gehe nisīdāpetvā nānaggarasena yāgubhattena parivisitvā, “Bhante, kattha gantukāmā”ti pucchi. “Yathā phāsukaṭṭhānaṃ mahāupāsike”ti.
    - translation
      - The story of a certain monk: "Difficult to restrain, rapid...": this Dhamma discourse the Teacher gave while dwelling in Jetavana Monastery in Sāvatthi concerning a certain monk. It is said that in the kingdom of the King Kosala, at the foot of a mountain, there was a certain thickly settled village called the village of Mātika. Then, one day, about sixty monks, having had the meditation subject until arahatship explained in the presence of the Buddha, went to that village and entered it for alms. Now, the master of that village, was (also) called Mātika. His mother saw them, made them sit in the house, served rice porridge with various superb flavors, and asked, "Where do you want to go, bhantes?" "Wherever we feel comfortable, great devotee".
    - grammar
      - which vibhatti is used together with "yāva"?

      - 2 usages of kira
        - marks the beginning of an explanation (not that it was just heard)
        - sometimes: it is said that
      - Jetavana: became the name of the monastery
      - Atha ne yo tassa gāmassa sāmiko mātiko nāma, tassa mātā disvā gehe nisīdāpetvā: Now, the master of that village, was (also) called Mātika. His mother saw them
        - yo sāmiko: whoever is the master
        - tassa gāmassa: of that village
        - mātiko nāma: called Mātika
        - why is "ne" so far away from disvā?
          - bhante: to convery that we are still talking about the monks...
    - sandhis

- Paragraph 17
  - “Ko esa, bhante, samaṇadhammo nāmā”ti? “Dvattiṃsākāre (acc.) sajjhāyaṃ karoma (samatha), attabhāve ca khayavayaṃ paṭṭhapema (vipassanā), mahāupāsike”ti. “Kiṃ pana, bhante, dvattiṃsākāre sajjhāyaṃ kātuṃ, attabhāve ca khayavayaṃ paṭṭhapetuṃ tumhākameva vaṭṭati, udāhu amhākampī'ti "kassacipi avārito esa dhammo, mahāupāsike”ti. “Tena hi, bhante, mayhampi dvattiṃsākāraṃ detha, attabhāve ca khayavayapaṭṭhapanaṃ ācikkhathā”ti. “Tena hi uggaṇha, mahāupāsike”ti sabbaṃ uggaṇhāpesuṃ.
    - "What is this so called contemplative practice, bhante?" "We do the recitation in the 32 parts, and we establish the wasting away and vanishing in (of) the individual being (aggregates), great devotee." "Bhante, is it suitable only for you to do the recitation in the 32 parts, and to establish the (contemplation on the) wasting away and vanishing in the individual being, or for us too? "This practice is not prohibited for anyone at all, great devotee." "If it is so, bhante, please, give the 32 parts to me as well, and teach (me) the establishment of the wasting away and vanishing in the individual being." "If that is so, great devotee, learn it." They made her learn all (of it).

- Paragraph 18

    - “Tena hi attano cittameva rakkhāhi, sakkā dukkhā muccitu”nti (it is possible to be freed from suffering) imaṃ ovādaṃ datvā imaṃ gāthamāha – 36. “Sududdasaṃ sunipuṇaṃ, yatthakāmanipātinaṃ; cittaṃ rakkhetha (attanopada sattamī in active meaning) medhāvī, (hi: because) cittaṃ guttaṃ sukhāvaha”nti. Tattha (there [in the verse]) sududdasanti suṭṭhu (nip.) duddasaṃ (extremely difficult to see). Sunipuṇanti suṭṭhu nipuṇaṃ (subtle; shows the separation of the words) paramasaṇhaṃ (the most soft; shows the meaning of each word; suṭṭhu = parama; nipuṇa = saṇha). Yatthakāmanipātinanti jātiādīni anoloketvā (having not seen race, and so on [referring to the previous passage]) labhitabbālabhitabbayuttāyuttaṭṭhānesu (among the places that should be gotten, not gotten, [cognized] suitable and unsuitable; niddhāraṇa) yattha katthaci (in whatever [place]) nipatanasīlaṃ (habit of falling). Cittaṃ rakkhetha medhāvīti andhabālo (blind fool) dummedho (unwise) attano cittaṃ (his own mind) rakkhituṃ samattho (capable of protecting) nāma (called, recognized as; emphasis) natthi (there is no), cittavasiko hutvā anayabyasanaṃ (misfortune and disaster) pāpuṇāti.
