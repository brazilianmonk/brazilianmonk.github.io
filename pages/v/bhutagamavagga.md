# Bhūtagāmavagga
## 11 - Bhūtagāmasikkhāpada 

> **Bhūtagāmapātabyatāya, pācittiyaṃ.**  
> In the destroying of vegetation, [there is a case] involving expiation.

### Background

**Buddha:** Aggalāva Stupa, city of Āḷavi
- in the city of Āḷavi, bhikkhus were building near the Aggalāva stupa, causing destruction
- despite a deity's warning, a monk cut down a tree, angering the tree spirit
- the deity sought help from the Buddha, who praised her patience and offered a new divine abode
- people criticized the monks for their actions
- the Buddha rebuked the monk and established a training rule against harming 

**Note:**
- it does not mention how the deva's arm got cut; maybe they created a gross form and became susceptible to gross attack
- bhummādevas can get pregnant
- petas as well
- rock peta that lasted for the time of 4 Buddhas
- Buddhadeyya mansion: she could stay in her mansion inside Jetavana even when higher deities would come to listen to the Dhamma

---

### Description

**Purpose of the rule:** people think plants are living and criticize monks for damaging them

**bhūtagāma:** plants that grow from bījagāma

**Types of bhūtagāma:**
- **aquatic plants:**
  - those that have the **soil** as **ṭhāna** (habitat) such as lotuses
  - those that have the **water** as **ṭhāna**
- **ground plants:** those that have the **soil** as **ṭhāna**

**6 ways of damaging bhūtagāma:**
- **chindati**, chindāpeti
- **bhindati**, bhindāpeti
- **pacati**, pacāpeti

**Note:** removing the entire plant is also included

---

**bījagāma:** parts of a plant that after detached can propagate - it is of 5 types starting with mūlabīja

**5 types of bījagāma:**

1. **mūlabīja** - roots, bulbs, rhizomes or tubers that can propagate such as haliddi, siṅgivera and so on
   - **exception:** roots such as ginger that kept on wet sand have propagated: **bhūtagāma**, not bījagāma

2. **khandhabīja** – stem or branches that can propagate such as assattha, nigrodha, pilakkha and so on
   - **exception:**
     - haritakhāṇu (tree stump) that **can** grow: **bhūtagāma** (such as kakudha (arjuna tree), karañja (indian beech), piyaṅgu, panasa (jack-fruit tree), etc)
     - haritakhāṇu that **cannot** grow: **bījagāma** (such as those of coconut or palm trees)
     - haritakhāṇu of a **banana / bamboo tree:**
       - if cut **before** having yielded fruit: **bhūtagāma**
       - if cut **after** having yielded fruit:
         - if the leaves are **green**: **bhūtagāma**
           - *how can there be leaves if it is a stump?* #q 🙋🏼‍♂
         - if the leaves have begun to dry from the tips: **bījagāma** (the entire tree)

3. **phaḷubīja** - joints, knots, nodes from which new shoots can propagate (such as those of bamboo and sugarcane)

4. **aggabīja** – buds that can propagate
   - Bud: a small protuberance on a stem or branch, sometimes enclosed in protective scales and containing an undeveloped leaf, flower, or leafy shoot.

5. **bījabīja** - seeds that can propagate (such as grains)

**Note:**
- **exception:** **aquatic plants** that grow freely floating on the water, if taken out of water and put on soil become **bījagāma**

---

**haritakhāṇu:** tree stump

**Exceptions put together:**
- roots, such as ginger, that after kept on wet sand have propagated: **bhūtagāma**
- haritakhāṇu that **can** grow: **bhūtagāma** (such as kakudha (arjuna tree), karañja (indian beech), piyaṅgu, panasa (jack-fruit tree), etc)
- haritakhāṇu that **cannot** grow: **bījagāma** (such as those of coconut or palm trees)
- haritakhāṇu of a **banana / bamboo tree:**
  - if cut **before** having yielded fruit: **bhūtagāma**
  - if cut **after** having yielded fruit:
    - if the leaves are **green**: **bhūtagāma**
    - if the leaves have begun to dry from the tips: **bījagāma** (the entire tree)
- **aquatic plants** that grow freely floating on the water, if taken out of water and put on soil become **bījagāma**

---

**Difference between bhūtagāma and bījagāma:**
- **Note:** as long as bījagāma is attached to the plant, it is bhūtagāma
- **bhūtagāma** is the entire plant that grew from bījagāma (such as a tree)
- **bījagāma** is a part of a plant that has been detached and can propagate (such as a seed); it is of 5 kinds (such as mūlabīja)

---

**kappiya karaṇa** (making allowable)

**What is it?** → a minor vinayakamma to make fruits and bījagāmas allowable for consumption; its purpose is not to halt the potential for propagation; maybe it is a way for the anupa to take responsibility

**Note:** if the bījagāma was cooked even a little bit, it is allowable

**Pāḷi:**
- upa: "**kappiyaṁ karohi**"
- anupa: "**kappiyaṁ (bhante)**" and then damages

**Can anything be used?**
- no, only fire (cinder / hot metal), sharp weapons, or finger-/toe-nails (hooves are also mentioned)
- wood / stones cannot be used and the item must be able to cause some damage
- *if hooves are ok, why not anything?* #q 🙋🏼‍♂

**How to do it for multiple items:** if packed into a bundle, doing kappiyakaraṇa on one item makes the entire bundle allowable as long as they are in contact with each other; water does not count as connection here

**How to do it for seeds (kernel) loose in the shell?** the shell should be broken first, but if they are still connected, doing the procedure to the shell is enough

**Must roots that were made allowable and then propagated (such as ginger) be made allowable again?** yes

**Procedure according to Srthdpn and other teachers:**
- **srthdpn:** after the upa says "kappiyaṁ karohi" the anupa must say "kappiyaṁ (bhante)" and proceed to damage it - if the anupa damages first and says "kappiyaṁ (bhante)" after having damaged it, it is not made allowable
- **some teachers:** saying "kappiyaṁ" while on the fire / before pulling out the knife (during the act of damaging): ok; any **language** could be used
- **note:**
  > "Kappiya"nti vatvāva kātabbanti yo kappiyaṃ karoti, tena kattabbākārasseva vuttattā bhikkhunā avuttepi kātuṃ vaṭṭatīti na gahetabbaṃ. Puna "kappiyaṃ kāretabba"nti kārāpanassa paṭhamameva kathitattā bhikkhunā "kappiyaṃ karohī"ti vutteyeva anupasampannena "kappiya"nti vatvā aggiparijitādi kātabbanti gahetabbaṃ. "kappiyanti vacanaṃ pana yāya kāyaci bhāsāya vattuṃ vaṭṭatī"ti vadanti. "kappiyanti vatvāva kātabba"nti vacanato paṭhamaṃ "kappiya"nti vatvā pacchā aggiādinā phusanādi kātabbanti veditabbaṃ. "paṭhamaṃ aggiṃ nikkhipitvā nakhādinā vā vijjhitvā taṃ anuddharitvāva 'Kappiya'nti vattumpi vaṭṭatī"ti vadanti. (Srd-ṭ. III. 22).

### Offenses

**Damaging bhūtagāma or having it damaged in any way:**
- with bījasaññā: **pc 11**
- with doubt: **dkṭ**
- with abījasaññā: **anāpatti**

**Damaging abhūtagāma or having it damaged in any way:**
- with bījasaññā: **dkṭ**
- with doubt: **dkṭ**
- with abījasaññā: **anāpatti**

**Damaging bījagāma or having it damaged in any way:**
- with bījasaññā: **dkṭ**
- with doubt: **dkṭ**
- with abījasaññā: **anāpatti**

**Damaging abījagāma or having it damaged in any way:**
- with bījasaññā: **dkṭ**
- with doubt: **dkṭ**
- with abījasaññā: **anāpatti**

Therefore this is **ekaka**-pācittiya and **tika**-dukkaṭa offence (**catukadukkaṭa** - not mentioned in the text)

The saññās involved are **bīja-/abīja-saññā** (thinking that it is a bhūtagāma or not)

### Anāpatti

- damaging bhūtagāma / abhūtagāma with **abījasaññā**
- using **kappiya vohāra**
- **asañcicca:** damaging without thinking "I shall break this," etc; rolling stones, dragging branches, using a walking stick...
- **asatiyā:** damaging with the mind elsewhere; while speaking to someone one plucks grass or a vine using toenails or hands without being aware that he is doing so
- **ajānantassa:** damaging without knowing it is bhūta/bījagāma or not knowing it is being damaged; keeping a spade within a patch of grown grass for protection or dropping a fire torch on the ground when the flame begins to burn the hands
- **paṭaggidāna:** making a counter-fire when a wildfire is approaching - "**Anujānāmi, bhikkhave, davaḍāhe ḍayihamāne paṭaggi dātuṃ**"
  - davaḍāha [dava + ḍāha] masc.↔wildfire, forest fire; lit. running fire
- **to save another monk, but not to save oneself**
  - smtpsdk says all ancient commentaries say so
  - bdghs questions it
  - argument: for another it will be kusalacitta but maybe not for oneself
  - counter-argument: but it can be done with both kusala or akusalacitta
  - conclusion: accept it out of confidence in the commentaries

  > Manussaviggahapārājikavaṇṇanāyaṃ pana sabbaaṭṭhakathāsu "sace bhikkhu rukkhena vā ajjhotthaṭo hoti, opāte vā patito sakkā ca hoti rukkhaṃ chinditvā bhūmiṃ vā khaṇitvā nikkhamituṃ, jīvitahetupi attanā na kātabbaṃ. Aññena pana bhikkhunā bhūmiṃ vā khaṇitvā rukkhaṃ vā chinditvā allarukkhato vā daṇḍakaṃ chinditvā taṃ rukkhaṃ pavaṭṭetvā nikkhāmetuṃ vaṭṭati, anāpattī"ti vuttaṃ. Tattha kāraṇaṃ na dissati – "anujānāmi, bhikkhave, davaḍāhe ḍayhamāne paṭaggiṃ dātuṃ, parittaṃ kātu"nti (cūḷava. 283) idaṃ pana ekameva suttaṃ dissati. Sace etassa anulomaṃ "attano na vaṭṭati, aññassa34 vaṭṭatī"ti idaṃ nānākaraṇaṃ na sakkā laddhuṃ. Attano atthāya karonto attasinehena akusalacitteneva karoti, paro pana kāruññena, tasmā anāpattīti ce. III. 33-4).

## 12 - Aññavādakasikkhāpadaṃ 

> **Aññavādake vihesake, pācittiyaṃ.**
> In evading, in vexing, [there is a case] involving expiation.

---

### Background

The Buddha was residing at the **Ghosita** monastery in the city of **Kosambhi**. During this time the Venerable **Channa** had been **misbehaving** and was being **questioned** by the **Saṅgha** in relation to the offence committed. He was then trying to hide the true story by asking cross questions such as: "Who committed an offence? What offence have I committed? Upon what object have I committed an offence? How did I commit an offence? To whom are you telling? What are you telling?" When this was informed, the Buddha declared the aññavādaka training rule prohibiting monks from thus confusing the Saṅgha by **making unrelated statements**. aññavādake pācittiyaṁ

Later on, Venerable Channa misbehaved, and he was again questioned by the Saṅgha in relation to the offence committed. At that time, **he kept silent** without stating anything. This annoyed the Saṅgha. Having known the incident, the Buddha added an amendment to the training rule prohibiting monks from keeping silence while being questioned in the midst of the Saṅgha. The amendment is like another training rule. **vihesake**

**Note:**
- like another rule inside the first one
- miracle at birth, just an instance a sign of his attaining Buddhahood, after that he could not talk and walk, he was not aware his mother died, when he saw the 4 signs Channa explained

---

### Description

**aññavādaka:**
- saying **unrelated things** in order to **avoid answering** when questioned by the Saṅgha **about a vatthu (act) or āpatti**

**vihesaka:**
- troubling the Saṅgha by **remaining silent** intending not to answer when being questioned **about a vatthu or āpatti**

**aññāvādaka sammuti:**
- ñattidutiya kammavācā that is done upon the upa who does aññāvādaka; it declares the upa in question as **aññāvādaka**

**vihesaka sammuti:**
- ñattidutiya kammavācā that is done upon the upa who does vihesaka; it declares the upa in question as **vihesaka**

---

### Instances of Offenses

**Does aññavāda / vihesaka:**
- before sammuti: **dkṭ**
- sammuti is done, he does it again: **pc**
- doing so unintentionally: **anāpatti**

**Does aññāvādana / vihesana having been given a dhammakamma:**
- dhammakammasaññī: **pc**
- vematiko: **pc**
- adhammakammasaññī: **pc**

**Does so having been given an adhammakamma:**
- dhammakammasaññī: **dkṭ**
- vematiko: **dkṭ**
- adhammakammasaññī: **dkṭ**

Therefore this is a **tika**-pācittiya and **tika**-dukkaṭa offence

The perception involved are dhammakamma-/adhammakamma-saññā (perception that the vinayakamma was done according to Dhamma and Vinaya or not)

---

### Nature

This is **sacittaka**, **saññāvimokkha** offence.

---

### Anāpatti

- **inquiring** about one's mistake or offense **not knowing** about it
- unable to answer due to **sickness**
- refraining from answering thinking it will cause a **bhaṇḍana, kalaha, viggaha, vivāda, saṅgharāji or saṅghabheda** among the Saṅgha
- *refraining from answering knowing that an adhamma vinayakamma will be done (**adhammena**), without the proper unity of the Saṅgha (**vaggena**) or to a person who does not deserve a vinayakamma (**akammārahassa**)* #q 🙋🏼‍♂

---

### Aṅga

1. aññavādaka or vihesaka **sammuti** has been given
2. being questioned based on a **vatthu** or **āpatti**
3. **aññāvādake vihesake**

---

### Note

- this could only escalate as **ukkhepanīya** for not correcting himself
- saṅgha unity is more important than doing an ukkhepanīyakamma
- **tassapāpiyasikā:** declaration to someone who acts like that when being questioned about a pārājika or something close to a pārājika
## 13 - Ujjhāpanakasikkhāpadaṃ 

> **Ujjhāpanake khiyyanake, pācittiyaṃ.**
> In making [another bhikkhu] find fault, in criticising, [there is a case] involving expiation.

---

### Background

The Buddha was residing at the **Veḷuvana** monastery in the city of **Rājagaha**. During this time Venerable **Dabbamallaputta** Thera was preparing lodgings for the Saṅgha (**senāsana paññāpaka**). He was also in charge of distribution of meals (**bhattuddesaka**). Two monks named **Mettiya** and **Bhummajaka**, who were among the group of **Chabbaggiya** monks, at the time were juniors and lacked good kamma to obtain good lodgings and food. They received inferior lodgings and food of the Saṅgha. Then **they got other bhikkhus to think and say, "Dabbamallaputta Thera favours when preparing quarters. He also favours, when distributing food"**. Based on this incident the Buddha imposed the ujjhāpanaka training rule prohibiting monks from getting other monks to think and say bad things about officials appointed by the Saṅgha.

When this rule was imposed, Mettiya and Bhummajaka bhikkhus started **accusing** Dabbamallaputta Thera in the presence of the other bhikkhus saying, "Dabbamallaputta Thera favours when preparing lodgings and distributing food". Having known this, the Buddha added an amendment to this rule prohibiting monks from speaking ill about Saṅgha officials in the presence of other monks. This amendment, too, is like another training rule.

**Types of anuppaññatti:**
- **anāpattikara:** creates an exception
- **daḷhīkaraṇa:** makes it stricter
- **āpattikara:** makes another offense, extends the scope of the rule (vihesaka under aññavādaka and this one)

---

### Description

**Purpose:** unjustly blaming a bhikkhu who has received an official position from the Saṅgha and making others detest him is a great barrier to the well-being of the Saṅgha - this rule counteracts that

**ujjhāpanaka:** making others think low of or devalue someone

**khīyanaka:** stating an individual's bad qualities in front of someone else

**3 intentions behind ujjhāpanaka / khīyanaka:**
- to criticize (**avaṇṇaṃ kattukāmo**)
- to defame (**ayasaṃ kattukāmo**)
- to silence (**maṅku kattukāmo**)
- **Note:** **tuṇhī** means keeping silence willingly, **maṅku** is when one is silent because one was defeated

---

**6 saṅgha officials under this rule:**
1. **senāsana paññāpaka** – one who arranges lodgings to the monks
2. **bhattuddesaka** – one who distributes food
3. **yāgubhājaka** – one who serves porridge
4. **phalabhājaka** – one who serves fruit
5. ***khajjabhājaka** – one who serves sweetmeats* #q 🙋🏼‍♂
6. **appamattaka vissajjaka** – one who distributes minor items

**Note:** there are altogether 13 saṅgha officials

---

**4 individuals object of ujjhāpanaka / khīyanaka:**

1. **sammata upasampanna:** an upa who was authorized as one of the 6 officials
2. **asammata upasampanna:** an upa who voluntarily attends the functions of the position - in most monasteries this is what happens
3. **sammata anupasampanna:** an upa who was an authorized official and directly became a sāmaṇera by saying "Sāmaṇeroti maṃ dhārehi"
   > Anupasampannaṃ saṅghena sammataṃ vā asammataṃ vāti ettha pana kiñcāpi anupasampannassa terasa sammutiyo dātuṃ na vaṭṭanti. Atha kho upasampannakāle laddhasammutiko pacchā anupasampannabhāve ṭhito, taṃ sandhāya "saṅghena sammataṃ vā"ti vuttaṃ. (V-a. III. 37). Sāmaṇero hotīti upasampannabhāvaṃ jahitvā sāmaṇerabhāvaṃ upagacchati. Tattha pārājikappattabhāvena vā "gihīti maṃ dhārethā"tiādinā sikkhāpaccakkhānena vā gihī hoti. Tesu paṭhamena puna upasampadāya abhabbattā puna parivāso na ruhatiyeva, dutiyena pana puna upasampadāya bhabbattā "so ce puna upasampajjatī"ti vuttaṃ. Itaro pana pārājikappattabhāvena sāmaṇero na hoti. Kasmā? Saraṇagamanādīnaṃ vinassanato. Vuttañhi vimativinodaniyaṃ "upasampannānampi pārājikasamaāpattiyā saraṇagamanādisāmaṇerabhāvassapi vinassanato senāsanaggāho ca paṭippassambhati, saṅghalābhampi tena labhantīti veditabba"nti, gihī pana hutvā puna sāmaṇerabhāvamattaṃ laddhabbaṃ hoti. "sāmaṇeroti maṃ dhārethā"tiādinā pana sikkhāpaccakkhāne kate siyā sāmaṇerabhāvo, tatopi puna upasampajjitukāmatāya sati siyā upasampannabhāvo. "gihīti maṃ dhārethā"tiādinā sikkhāpaccakkhānaṃ katvā gihibhāvaṃ upagatepi puna sāmaṇerapabbajjaṃ pabbajitvā sāmaṇero hoti. Tato puna upasampajjituṃ laddhabbattā "puna upasampajjatī"ti vutto. (Vnl. II. 254-5).
4. **asammata anupasampanna:** a sāmaṇera who voluntarily attends the functions of the official positions

---

### Offenses

**An upa criticizes a sammata upa to an upa:** **pc**

**All other scenarios:** **dkṭ**

**The upa does ujjhāpanaka / khīyanaka to a sammata upasampanna:**
- dhammakammasaññī: **pc**
- vematiko: **pc**
- adhammakammasaññī: **pc**

**The upa does ujjhāpanaka / khīyanaka to an asammata upasampanna:**
- dhammakammasaññī: **dkṭ**
- vematiko: **dkṭ**
- adhammakammasaññī: **dkṭ**

Therefore this is a **tika**-pācittiya and **tika**-dukkaṭa offence

---

### Anāpatti

- **with a base:** if the appointed individual does his tasks out of any of the wrong courses (agati) - chanda, dosa, moha and bhaya - it is fine to make another upasampanna to think low of him or to criticize his activities in front of another upasampanna

---

### Nature

This is **sacittaka**, **saññāvimokkha** offence

---

### Aṅgāni

1. **dhammakamma**
2. **upasampanna** (the official)
3. him not acting under any of the **agati**
4. **desire** to speak bad qualities to someone
5. **upasampanna** (the listener)
6. **ujjhāpanake khīyanake:** making that person to think low or stating bad qualities in front of him

---

### Note

**Some issues:**
- no rule to remove the position, only ukkhepanīya
- normally given for lifetime
- if the official is not acting well, normally the saṅgha asks him to resign
- or leaves the monastery permanently (ṭhānissaro)
## 14 - Paṭhamasenāsanasikkhāpadaṃ 

> **Yo pana bhikkhu saṅghikaṃ mañcaṃ vā pīṭhaṃ vā bhisiṃ vā kocchaṃ vā ajjhokāse santharitvā vā santharāpetvā vā taṃ pakkamanto neva uddhareyya na uddharāpeyya anāpucchaṃ vā gaccheyya pācittiyaṃ.**
> If any bhikkhu, having [himself] laid out a bed or seat or mattress or stool belonging to the community in the open air, or having [someone else] laid [it] out, [and] then, when departing, should not take [it] away or should not have [it] taken away or should go without asking [someone to put it back], [this is a case] involving expiation.

---

### Background

The Buddha was residing in the Jetavana monastery near the city of Sāvatthi. During the winter season bhikkhus prepared chairs and beds outdoors and heated their bodies under the sun. When they were leaving the premises neither they replaced the beds and chairs under a shelter, nor did they get another to do so. They did not hand over the responsibility of the chairs and beds they kept outside to someone else either. Furniture was soaked due to the snowfall. When the Buddha was informed of this, he set the paṭhama senāsana rule to prevent such irresponsible activity.

---

### Description

## 15 - Dutiyasenāsanasikkhāpadaṃ 

> **Yo pana bhikkhu saṅghike vihāre seyyaṃ santharitvā vā santharāpetvā vā taṃ pakkamanto neva uddhareyya na uddharāpeyya anāpucchaṃ vā gaccheyya pācittiyaṃ.**
> If any bhikkhu, having [himself] laid out or having [someone else] lay out, bedding in a dwelling belonging to the community, [and] then, when departing, should not take [it] away or should not have [it] taken away, or should go without asking [someone to put it back], [this is a case] involving expiation.

---

### Background

The Buddha was residing in the **Jetavana** monastery near the city of **Sāvatthi**. During this time the **Sattarasavaggiya** monks had laid out their **seyya** (beddings) at one **temple** that had been offered to the Saṅgha and had **left** the place without putting them away. They did not even get someone else to remove their seyya. Nor did they hand the responsibility of those seyya to someone else. The beddings were later attacked by **termites**. Knowing this the Buddha laid down the dutiya senāsana training rule prohibiting monks from such irresponsible behavior.

---

### Description

**Purpose:** to minimize damage to seyyas caused by termites

**How to avoid the offense:**
- keeping (/c) them in a protected place
- handing over the responsibility to a bhikkhu, sāmaṇera, any resident, the donor of the vihāra, or any members of their family

**saṅghika vihāra here means:**
- **antogabbho** (a room) in a saṅghika building
- **sabbaparicchanna guttasenāsana** (saṅghika building covered all around)
- **Note:** "Vihāroti antogabbho vā aññaṃ vā sabbaparicchannaṃ guttasenāsanaṃ veditabbaṃ"

---

**10 seyya under this rule (identify on exam):**

1. **bhisi** – mattress (cushions) laid on beds and chairs
2. **cimilikā** – mats that are made to protect the floor that has been plastered
3. **uttarattharaṇa** – mat that is laid upon chairs and beds to protect them
4. **bhūmattharaṇa** – carpets
5. **taṭṭikā** – mat made of palm leaves or bark
6. **cammakaṇḍa** – animal skin (leather) that is used as a part of the dwelling
7. **nisīdana** – sitting cloth with borders
8. **paccattharaṇa** – a blanket used to cover the body (But in Gaṇṭhipada, paccattharaṇa are the cloths that are spread upon beds and chairs other than uttattharaṇa)
9. **tiṇasanthāra** – spread made out of grass
10. **paṇṇasanthāra** – spread made out of leaves

**Note:** the term seyya, in other places, can refer to **beds** and **chairs** as well

**Vocab:**
- cimi→masc. name of a fibrous plant from which cloth is made ~
- skins of animals such as lions and tigers are not allowed for personal use
- santhāra masc. covering, flooring

---

**Places in which if the seyya is left, one is subject to dkṭ:**
- **vihārūpacāra** – within the proximity of the vihāra
- **upaṭṭhānasālā** - dining hall
- **maṇḍapa** – bounded or unbounded shed where usually many monks gather
  - maṇḍapa masc.↔tent; pavilion; marquee
- **rukkhamūla** – under a tree

**Distance for an offense:** going beyond the **monastery boundaries**

---

### Offenses

**Keeping (/c) seyyas in a saṅghika vihāra subjected to termite attacks and leaving beyond the monastery boundaries without the proper procedure:** **pc, as it poses a threat to both the seyya and the vihāra**

**Doing so in a vihāra that is not subjected to termite attacks** - such as those built on rocks or rock pillars (**siluccaya leṇa**), well plastered structures (**sudhālitta senāsana**): **anāpatti, though it is customary to hand over responsibility to someone else**

**If termites are seen climbing from one side of the dwelling:** **the procedure should be done**

**Doing so in the vihārūpacāra, upaṭṭhānasālā, maṇḍapa or rukkhamūla:** **dkṭ, because it only poses a threat to the seyya**

**Doing so to a bed or chair in any place:** **dkṭ**

As with the paṭhamasenāsana, this is also a **tika**-pācittiya and **tika**-dukkaṭa offence

The perception in this regard is **saṅghika-/puggalika-saññā** (about the seyya)

As with the paṭhama, leaving **someone else's** personal property: **dkṭ**

To **one's own** property: **anāpatti**

**Vocab:**
- siluccaya [silā + uccaya]→masc. hill; mountain; lit. pile of stones
- leṇa [√lī + *aṇa] nt.↔cave; 2 safety; security; lit. hiding

---

### Nature

This is **acittaka**, **nosaññāvimokkha** offence

---

### Anāpatti

- if they are **personal belongings**
- if the **proper procedure** is done
- **intending to relocate** it upon return
- doing so due to a **paḷibodha / āpadā**

---

### Aṅga

1. **saṅghike vihāre...santharitvā vā santharāpetvā vā**
2. **seyyaṁ** (seyya being **saṅghika**)
3. no **paḷibodha**
4. no **āpadā**
5. leaving without the **intention of relocating** the seyya upon return
6. crossing the **boundaries of the monastery**
## 16 - Anupakhajjasikkhāpadaṃ 

> **Yo pana bhikkhu saṅghike vihāre jānaṃ pubbupagataṃ bhikkhuṃ anupakhajja seyyaṃ kappeyya 'Yassa sambādho bhavissati, so pakkamissatī'ti, etadeva paccayaṃ karitvā anaññaṃ, pācittiyaṃ.**
> If any bhikkhu, having encroached upon a bhikkhu who has arrived before, should knowingly use a sleeping place in a dwelling belonging to the community [saying]: "He for whom it is [too] cramped, will leave," having done [it] for just this reason, [and] not another, [this is a case] involving expiation.

**Vocab:**
- anupakhajja [anu + pa + √khand + ya]↔ger. (+acc) sneaking in; encroaching; intruding; forcing oneself in; 2 ger. (+acc) entering; going among; going near

---

### Background

The Buddha is residing in the **Jetavana** monastery near the city of **Sāvatthi**. During this time **Chabbaggiya** monks arrive at a good dwelling (senāsana) and take over the entire place. The Theras get the Chabbaggiya monks to hand the dwellings over to them. Then the Chabbaggiya monks thought, "By what means shall we stay in these good dwellings?" Then they thought, "Those who experience inconvenience will leave". Having thought thus, they went and **slept amongst the Theras** saying "Venerable Sir, you only get the bed or the chair, we can stay in the remaining areas of the vihāra". This caused **inconvenience** to the elderly monks. The Tathāgata having known this laid down the anupakhajja training rule.

---

### Description

**anuṭṭhāpanīya:** a monk who should not be moved from his dwelling or hindered by others

**Hindering:** stay too close to the dwelling of an anuṭṭhāpanīya monk or to block its roadway thinking that he may vacate the dwelling due to being obstructed

**anuṭṭhāpanīya monks:**
- **vuḍḍha** (a senior): cannot be moved by a junior
- **gilāna**: should not be moved out of compassion
- those who have been offered permanent dwellings by the saṅgha through a vinayakamma:
  - **bhaṇḍāgārika** (storekeeper)
  - **dhammakathika**
  - **vinayadhara**
  - **gaṇavācaka / ganthavācaka** (responsible for teaching a certain book, one who teaches a group)

**upacāra here means:**
- the **proximity of** the chair and bed
- the **path** from the **place one washes the feet** to the chair and bed
- the **path** from the chair and bed to the **bathroom**

**3 upacāras of the bed / chair:**
- upacāra **of the bed or chair:** 1½ hattha around them - if the vihāra is smaller, whatever space available around them up to 1½ hattha
- upacāra **of the way to** the bed or chair: a **1½ hattha** wide path from the place one washes the feet before entering (**pādadhovana pāsāṇato**) until the bed and chair
- upacāra of the way **from** the bed or chair: **1½ hattha** wide path from the bed or chair to the toilet

---

### Offenses

**With the intention of moving an anuṭṭhāpanīya monk from a saṅghika vihāra, a monk, within one of the 3 upacāras of the bed / chair:**

- lays a seyya: **dkṭ**
- sits or lies down:
  - saṅghikasaññī: **pc**
  - vematiko: **pc**
  - puggalikasaññī: **pc**

**Outside the upacāras:**
- places a seyya: **dkṭ**
- sits or lies down: **dkṭ**

**Doing so in a puggalikavihāra:**
- saṅghikasaññī: **dkṭ**
- vematiko: **dkṭ**
- puggalikasaññī: **anāpatti**

**Doing so in a puggalikavihāra of another:**
- puggalikasaññī: **dkṭ**

**How are offenses counted?** **each time one of these actions is done, it is an offense**

**Doing so within the upacāra of a vihāra, upaṭṭhānasālā (dining hall), maṇḍapa, rukkhamūla or even abbhokāsa:**
- places a seyya: **dkṭ**
- sits or lies down: **dkṭ**

This is a **tika**-pācittiya, **tika**-dukkaṭa

The saññā is with regard to the vihāra

**Note:**
- even though the offense is sacittaka saññāvimokkha, there can be a tikapācittiya; but when counting offenses another perception is involved; whether it is saṅghika or not, that saññā is irrelevant; 2 saññās

**Vocab:**
- visabhāgapuggala→a person with the nature of opposition
- visabhāga [vi + sa + √bhaj + *a]↔adj. different; opposite; not the same; lit. not sharing

> Evarūpena hi visabhāgapuggalena ekavihāre vā ekapariveṇe vā vasantena attho natthi, tasmā sabbatthevassa nivāso vārito. (V-a. III. 46).

---

### Anāpatti

- if it is a **personal vihāra**
- if done due to being **sick** or oppressed by **cold / heat**
- if done due to paḷibodha / āpadā

---

### Nature

This is **sacittaka**, **saññāvimokkha** offence (though the saṅghikasaññā does not matter here, the sacittaka and saññāvimokkha is said with regard to the bad intention of encroaching)

---

### Aṅga

1. **saṅghike vihāre:** the vihāra being saṅghika
2. knowing that the monk is an anupaṭṭhānīya
   - Note: here there could be another saññā involved
3. desire to obstruct (**sambādhetukāmatā**)
4. **sitting** or **lying down** within the **upacāra**
## 17 - Nikkaḍḍhanasikkhāpadaṃ 

> **Yo pana bhikkhu bhikkhuṃ kupito anattamano saṅghikā vihārā nikkaḍḍheyya vā nikkaḍḍhāpeyya vā, pācittiyaṃ.**
> If any bhikkhu, being resentful and displeased, should drive out a bhikkhu or have [him] driven out from a dwelling belonging to the community, [this is a case] involving expiation.

**Vocab:**
- nikkaḍḍhati [nī + √kaḍḍh + a + ti]↔pr. (+acc & +abl) expels (from); throws out (from); removes (from); lit. drags out

---

### Background

The Buddha was residing in the Jetavana monastery near the city of Sāvatthi. During this time the Sattarasavaggiya monks decided to observe vassāna in a particular remote vihāra and started renovating it. The Chabbaggiya monks, seeing the Sattarasavaggiya monks attending to the repairs, went up to them once the repairs were completed and said "Friends, we need the renovated vihāra", and fought with them and drove the Sattarasavaggiya monks from the vihāra. Then the other monks, who saw them crying and got to know about the issue, informed the matter to the Tathāgata. Then the Buddha laid down the nikkaḍḍhana training rule.

---

### Description

Buildings (vihāra) that have been offered to the Saṅgha belong to every bhikkhu. A bhikkhu has no right to drive out another bhikkhu from a vihāra belonging to the Saṅgha. Therefore, if a bhikkhu drives out another bhikkhu from a saṅghika building out of anger, a pācittiya offence will entail.

---

### Offenses

**Driving out physically:**
- through one door: **1 pc**
- through many doors: **pc according to the number of doors**
- with one effort: **1 pc**
- with many efforts: **pc according to the number of doors**

**Driving out verbally:**
- one command "leave this vihāra": **1 pc**
- multiple commands: **multiple pc**

**Getting someone else to drive out:**
- ordering: **dkṭ**
- the order is fulfilled: **pc for the one who ordered**
- ordering "drive them out from this many doors" / "until the main door": **pc according to the number of doors**

**Doing so from the upacāra of a vihāra, from the upaṭṭhānasālā, maṇḍapa, rukkhamūla:** **dkṭ**

**Taking out (/c) the parikkhāra of another:** **dkṭ for each parikkhāra - if 1 bundle, 1 dkṭ**

**Driving (/c) an anupa out:** **dkṭ**
**Taking an anupa's parikkhāra out:** **dkṭ**

**While driving out someone righteously, if anger arises:** **anāpatti**

---

**Driving out from saṅghika vihāra:**
- saṅghikasaññī: **pc**
- vematiko: **pc**
- puggalikasaññī: **pc**

**Driving out from puggalika vihāra:**
- saṅghikasaññī: **dkṭ**
- vematiko: **dkṭ**
- puggalika of someone else: **dkṭ**
- personal puggalika: **anāpatti**

This is a **tika**-pācittiya, **tika**-dukkaṭa offense

Saññā is with regard to the vihāra

---

### Anāpatti

- if it is **one's own private vihāra**
- if they are **alajjī**, **misbehaving bhikkhu / sāmaṇera** (na sammā vattanta), they and their requisites can be driven / taken out from the dwelling, but not from the entire monastery
- bhikkhus who cause **fights, quarrels, disputes and arguments** and their requisites can be driven out / taken out from the entire monastery, since they may cause schisms

---

### Nature

This is **sacittaka**, **saññāvimokkha** offence

---

### Aṅga

1. **bhikkhuṁ:** being an upa who does not cause disputes among the Saṅgha etc.
2. **saṅghikā vihārā**
3. **kupito anattamano...nikkaḍḍheyya vā nikkaḍḍhāpeyya vā:** driving out or getting someone else to drive out, **out of anger.**
## 18 - Vehāsakuṭisikkhāpadaṃ 

> **Yo pana bhikkhu saṅghike vihāre uparivehāsakuṭiyā āhaccapādakaṃ mañcaṃ vā pīṭhaṃ vā abhinisīdeyya vā abhinipajjeyya vā pācittiyaṃ.**
> If any bhikkhu should [brusquely] sit down or lie down on a bed or seat with detachable legs in a hut with an upper-floor in a dwelling belonging to the community, [this is a case] involving expiation.

---

### Background

The Buddha was residing in the Jetavana monastery near the city of Sāvatthi. During this time, two bhikkhus were residing in a vehāsa kuṭi (a kuṭi with an upper floor) of a saṅghika vihāra. One was living on the upper floor while the other on the floor below. The monk on the upper floor once suddenly sat on an āhaccapādaka bed. Legs of the bed came off and the bed fell onto the monk below. This monk screamed in that instance. Bhikkhus ran to him and inquired, "Friend, why did you shout?" Then the monk explained the issue to the bhikkhus. Knowing the incident, the Buddha laid down the vehāsakuṭi sikkhāpada.

---

### Description

**Purpose:** it is a dangerous act to sit or sleep on an āhaccapādaka bed or chair on an upper floor of a vehāsakuṭi. Therefore, this training rule has been imposed by the Tathāgata preventing this act.

**vehāsakuṭi:** a dwelling that has an upper floor with merely the joists fixed but no floor, and stands at a height that does not touch the head of a majjhima purisa - pamāṇamajjhimapurisa (about 4 hattha)

**Note:**
- the height of a man is normally 4 times the cubit
- vehāsa [viha + āya + sa] masc.↔air; sky; heaven

**āhaccapādaka mañca / pīṭha:** a bed/chair with detachable legs that are not fixed to the frame with nails but simply put together

---

### Offenses

**Sitting / lying down on an āhaccapādaka mañca / pīṭha in a saṅghika vehāsakuṭi:**
- saṅghikasaññī: **pc**
- vematiko: **pc**
- puggalikasaññī: **pc**

**Doing so in a puggalika vehāsakuṭi of another:**
- saṅghikasaññī: **dkṭ**
- vematiko: **dkṭ**
- puggalikasaññī: **dkṭ**

**Doing so in a puggalika vehāsakuṭi that belongs to another:** **dkṭ**

**Doing so in one's own vehāsakuṭi:** **anāpatti**

Therefore this is a **tika**-pācittiya, **tika**-dukkaṭa

Saññā is with regard to the vihāra where the kuṭi is located belonging or not to the saṅgha

---

### Anāpatti

- if it is **avehāsakuṭi** (not an upper-floor dwelling)
- if it is **lower** than the size of a majjhimapurisa (4 hattha)
- if the **floor below is not used** as a dwelling
- if the upper floor has been **floored** with compact wooden boards (**dāruphalakehi ghanasanthata**) or made of limestone
- if the bed / chair has their **legs fixed** to the frame using nails (not āhaccapādaka)
- if one stands intending **to take a robe** hanging on the wall or to hang something on the wall
- if it is **one's personal kuṭi**

---

### Nature

This is **acittaka**, **nosaññāvimokkha** training rule

---

### Aṅga

1. **saṅghike vihāre**
2. **uparivehāsakuṭiyā:**
   - the upper floor being located at a height that does not touch the head of a man of middle size
   - the floor below being used by monks as a dwelling
3. **āhaccapādakaṃ mañcaṃ vā pīṭhaṃ vā abhinisīdeyya vā abhinipajjeyya vā**
## 19 - Mahallakavihārasikkhāpadaṃ 

> **Mahallakaṃ pana bhikkhunā vihāraṃ kārayamānena, yāva dvārakosā aggalaṭṭhapanāya ālokasandhiparikammāya dvatticchadanassa pariyāyaṃ appaharite ṭhitena adhiṭṭhātabbaṃ, tato ce uttariṃ appaharite pi ṭhito adhiṭṭhaheyya pācittiyaṃ.**
> By a bhikkhu who is having a large dwelling built, a layer of two or three coverings can be ordered [to be applied onto the dwelling], [while] standing on [a place which has] few crops, up to the frame of the door for [the purpose of] fixing the bolt, [and] for plastering the window. If he should order more than that, even [when] standing on [a place which has] few crops, [this is a case] involving expiation.

**Vocab:**
- ālokasandhi [āloka + sandhi] masc.↔window; window-shutters; lit. breach for light
- sandhi [saṃ + √dhā + i] masc.↔break; hole; gap; lit. join
- dvārakosa [dvāra + kosa] masc.↔door frame; doorway
- kosa↔sheath, cocoon
- aggalaṭṭhapana nt.↔placing or fixing of a door-bolt or door-panel
- aggala nt.↔bolt, latch

---

### Background

The Buddha was residing in the Ghoshita monastery in the city of Kosambi. During this time, the caregiver (donor) of Venerable Channa, who was a chief minister, constructed a vihāra for him. Venerable Channa started to repeatedly cover the roof of the completed vihāra. He repeatedly plastered the walls as well. The building that could not bear its weight collapsed. Then Venerable Channa started collecting grass and firewood and spoiled the barley field of a brahmin during the process. Then the brahmin started to bad mouth monks. Having known the incident, Buddha laid a training rule prohibiting monks from repeatedly covering the roof or plastering the walls of a kuṭi.

---

### Description

A monk, having a vihāra constructed by a donor, must not have it built near harita fields.

> IIT - Nissayamuttaka Course

**harita fields:** pubbaṇṇa / aparaṇṇa crop fields

**harita:** pubbaṇṇa and aparaṇṇa grains - both the plants and the seeds
- **pubbaṇṇa:** seven types of grains - **sāli, vīhi, yava, godhuma, kaṅgu, varaka and kudrūsaka**
- **aparaṇṇa:** grains other than pubbaṇṇa - **mugga, māsa, tila**, etc

---

**Thatching the roof:**

**5 materials allowed for thatching:**
1. **iṭṭhakā** (clay tiles or bricks)
2. **silā** (stones)
3. **sudhā** (limestone)
4. **tiṇa** (grass)
5. **paṇṇa** (leaves)

**While giving the orders for thatching, the monk should be standing where he can be seen by the person who is sitting either:**
- on the **central beam** of the roof (**piṭṭhivaṃsa**)
- or on the **pinnacle** of the roof (**kūṭāgāraethūpikā**)
- or sitting on the **edge** of the roof border (**chadanamukavaṭṭiyanta**)

**Vocab:**
- vaṃsa masc.↔bamboo
- piṭṭhi [pa + √ṭhā + i]↔fem. back; lit. what stands forward; 2 fem. top; upper side; lit. what stands forward; 3 fem. surface; lit. what stands forward

---

**Plastering the walls:**

**Area normally plastered in order to be strengthened:**
- around the **door frame** (**yāva dvārakosā aggalaṭṭhapanāya**): **2½ hattha around it**
- around the **window frame** (**ālokasandhiparikammāya**): **the width of the window on all sides from the window frame**

**7 colourings and designs allowed:**

**3 colors:**
1. **setavaṇṇa** - to apply white colour
2. **kāḷavaṇṇa** - to apply black colour
3. **gerukaparikamma** - to apply soft brownish-coloured powder

**4 designs:**
1. **mālākamma** - to draw designs of garlands
2. **latākamma** - to draw designs of creepers
3. **makaradantaka** – to draw shark's tooth pattern
4. **pañcapaṭika** – to draw a design of the shape of five fingers of palm in five colours

**Vocab:**
- geruka [giri + *uka] nt.→red chalk; red ochre; lit. from the mountain

---

### Offenses

**During the first 2 times the roof is thatched, the monk can stand: near the kuṭi**

**During the 3rd covering: he must leave the vicinity of the kuṭi**

**If he does so while standing too close to the vihāra:** **dkṭ, as it may collapse**

**Giving orders to cover the roof while standing on harita fields:** **dkṭ**

**Thatching more than 3 times:** **pc**

**How offenses are counted:** **every clay tile / stone / lump of limestone / handful of grass / leaf used**

**Plastering the areas recommended around the doors and windows more than 3 times:** **anāpatti**

**Plastering other areas of the walls 3 times:** **anāpatti**

**Plastering other areas of the walls more than 3 times:** **srthdpn: some say pc, while 3 gaṇṭhipadas say dkṭ, since only a prohibition was laid down**

**Srthdpn:**
- all teachers agree one can plaster those areas around the doors and windows as much as one likes, since the Buddha recommended it
- plastering other areas more than 3 times is prohibited but the offense is not given:
  - some say: pc
  - 3 gaṇṭhipadas: dkṭ, since only a prohibition was laid down

> "Punappunaṃ chādāpesi, punappunaṃ limpāpesīti imasmiṃ vatthusmiṃ uppannadosena sikkhāpadassa paññattattā lepaṃ anujānantena ca dvārabandhassa samantā aḍḍhateyyahatthappamāṇeyeva padese punappunaṃ lepassa anuññātattā tato aññattha punappunaṃ limpentassa vā limpāpentassa vā bhittiyaṃ mattikāya kattabbakiccaṃ niṭṭhāpetvā puna catutthalepe dinne pācittiyena bhavitabba"nti vadanti. Gaṇṭhipadesu pana tīsupi "punappunaṃ lepadānassa vuttappamāṇato aññattha paṭikkhittamattaṃ ṭhapetvā pācittiyassa avuttattā dukkaṭaṃ anurūpa"nti vuttaṃ. (Srd-ṭ. III. 32).

**Is it 3 times after the initial one, or 3 times in total?** → 3 times after the initial plastering, totalling 4

---

**Thatching the roof that has already been thatched 3 times:**
- atirekasaññī (thinking that the allowed times for roofing has been exceeded): **pc**
- vematiko: **pc**
- ūṇakasaññī (thinking that the allowed times for roofing has not been exceeded): **pc**

**Thatching the roof that has not been thatched 3 times yet:**
- atirekasaññī: **dkṭ**
- vematiko: **dkṭ**
- ūṇakasaññī: **anāpatti**

Therefore this is a **tika**-pācittiya, **duka**-dukkaṭa offense

This is **acittaka**, **nosaññāvimokkha** offense

---

### Anāpatti

**If the kuṭi is saññācika (built by the monk without a donor):**
> Navame "mahallako nāma vihāro sasāmiko"ti vuttattā saññācikāya kuṭiyā anāpatti. (Srd-ṭ. III. 31).

- if it is **not for the purpose of dwelling place**
- if it is built for **another**
- if it is built using **personal wealth**
- if it is a **leṇa / guhā / tiṇakuṭi**
  - **leṇa** (a dwelling made by digging a mountain or by building a wall beneath an overhanging rock and which has a door), **guhā** (a tunnel, mountain cave or earth cave)
  - **tiṇakuṭi** (dwelling made of leaves)
  - > Leṇanti dvārabaddhaṃ. (Kṅkh-pu-ṭ. 372)

---

### Aṅga

1. **Mahallakaṃ pana bhikkhunā vihāraṃ:** being a mahallaka vihāra
2. being a vihāra that is built for the purpose of **one's own dwelling**
3. **tato ce uttari appaharitepi ṭhito adhiṭṭhaheyya:** ordering more than **three times** to cover the roof
## 20 - Sappāṇakasikkhāpadaṃ 

> **Yo pana bhikkhu jānaṃ sappāṇakaṃ udakaṃ tiṇaṃ vā mattikaṃ vā siñceyya vā siñcāpeyya vā, pācittiyaṃ.**
> If any bhikkhu should knowingly pour out, or should have [someone else] pour out, water containing living beings on grass or clay, [this is a case] involving expiation.

---

### Background

The Buddha was residing at the **Aggalāva Cetiya** in the city of **Āḷavi**. During this time, the bhikkhus who were residing in the city of Āḷavi, while **constructing monasteries**, **poured water containing worms onto grass and clay**, and got others to do so. They were aware that living beings were in such water and they would eventually die by pouring. Having known this the Buddha laid down the sappāṇaka training rule.

---

### Description

**Purpose:** to prevent pouring of water containing living beings onto grass or soil in the absence of intention of killing (**vadhaka cetanā**); if intentionally it would fall under pc 71

**Does the number of offenses increase when more living beings die?** no, offenses are not according to the number of animals killed

**What is the difference between this sappāṇaka rule and that which is found in the seventh vagga?**

This rule is enforced to prevent the destruction of aquatic beings due to external usages such as construction (**bāhiraparibhoga**), while the latter was laid to minimize the damage to aquatic beings due to monks' personal use, such as bathing or drinking (**attano paribhoga**)

> Dasame imassa sikkhāpadassa "siñceyya vā siñcāpeyya vā"ti bāhiraparibhogavasena paṭhamaṃ paññattattā "sappāṇakaṃ udakaṃ paribhuñjeyyā"ti sikkhāpadaṃ attano nahānapānādiparibhogavasena paññattanti veditabbaṃ. Tasmiṃ vā paṭhamaṃ paññattepi attano paribhogavaseneva paññattattā puna imaṃ sikkhāpadaṃ bāhiraparibhogavaseneva paññattanti gahetabbaṃ. (Srd-ṭ. III. 33-4)

---

### Offenses

**Knowing that aquatic beings inhabit the water, pouring (/c) it onto grass or clay, understanding that it may kill the aquatic beings:** **pc**

**Pouring water from a pot in one attempt without breaking the line of water:** **1 pc**

**Doing so in many attempts breaking the line:** **multiple pc according to the number of attempts**

**Redirecting a stream of water onto the ground:** **pc**

**Making a dam and redirecting water through many channels:** **pc according to the number of channels**

**Dumping large heaps of grass or clay into water that contains living beings:** **pc, but only if they could die due to such act**

**Dumping them in a big body of water where living beings would not die:** **anāpatti**

**Dumping them with many attempts:** **pc according to the number of attempts**

**Pouring water with animals that won't die on soil (such as frogs):** **anāpatti**

---

**Pouring sappāṇakaudaka on the ground:**
- with sappāṇakasaññā (the thought that there are living beings in the water which could die due to the pouring of the water): **pc**
- with vematika: **dkṭ**
- with appāṇakasaññā: **anāpatti**

**Pouring appāṇakaudaka:**
- with sappāṇakasaññā: **dkṭ**
- with vematika: **dkṭ**
- with appāṇakasaññā: **anāpatti**

Therefore it is **ekaka**-pācittiya and **tika**-dukkaṭa

This is a **sacittaka**, **saññāvimokkha** offense

---

### Aṅga

1. **jānaṃ:** knowing that pouring water would kill the living beings who are in it
2. **sappāṇakaṃ udakaṃ:** presence of living beings in the water
3. *(3). Water that could cause death if such an act is committed. (?)* #q 🙋🏼‍♂
4. **tiṇaṃ vā mattikaṃ vā siñceyya vā siñcāpeyya vā:** pouring onto grass or clay **sans intention of killing**
