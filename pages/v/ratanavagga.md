## Rāja- / antepura- sikkhāpadaṁ


<a id="org5259c7e"></a>

### background


<a id="orge74d00a"></a>

### Pāli rule


**Yo pana bhikkhu rañño khattiyassa muddhābhisittassa anikkhantarājake aniggataratanake pubbe appaṭisaṃvidito indakhīlaṃ atikkameyya, pācittiyaṃ.**
If any bhikkhu, without prior announcement, crosses the boundary post of a noble consecrated king’s bedroom when the king and queen have not departed, it is a case involving expiation.



<a id="orgc548951"></a>

### description

1.  muddhābhisitta khattiya rājā

    
    
    -   **muddhābhisitta**: consecrated via khattiya abhiseka
    -   **khattiya**: of the warrior clan (khattiya)
        -   of pure lineage for seven generations
            -   ubhato sujāto, mātito ca pitito ca saṃsuddhagahaṇiko, yāva sattamā pitāmahayugā akkhitto anupakuṭṭho jātivādena
    -   **rājā**: a king
    
      

2.  sayanighara

    
    Enclosed space with the king’s bed, even if only with cloth screens.
    
    -   yattha katthaci rañño sayanaṃ paññattaṃ hoti, antamaso sāṇipākāraparikkhittampi
    
      

3.  anikkhantarājake

    
    
    -   The occasion in which the king has not left
        -   anikkhanto rājā ito ti anikkhantarājakaṃ
    
        

4.  aniggataratanake

    
    
    -   The occasion in which the queen consort (mahesī, consecrated, brings pleasure to king) has not left
        -   ratanaṃ vuccati mahesī, aniggataṃ ratanaṃ ito ti aniggataratanakaṃ
    
    

5.  appaṭisaṁvidito

    
    
    -   Unnanounced: One who has not informed the king or queen about his arrival.
    
    


<a id="org5648d6d"></a>

### āpattivāra

-   entering the room unannounced while king and queen are present:  **pc** 
-   crossing the threshold (ummāra / indhakhīla) with one step:  **dkṭ** 
-   doing so with the second step:  **pc** 


<a id="orge3cf671"></a>

### anāpattivāra



-   **Paṭisaṃvidite**: Entering after informing in advance. (*:q: add as vocab*)
-   **Na khattiyo hoti**: King not of warrior clan.
-   **Na khattiyābhisekena abhisitto hoti**: King not consecrated by warrior customs. (*:q: add as term*)
-   **Rājā sayanigharā nikkhanto hoti**: King has left the bedroom.
-   **Mahesī sayanigharā nikkhantā hoti**: Queen consort has left the bedroom.
-   **Ubho vā nikkhantā honti**: Both king and queen have left.
-   **Na sayanighare**: Entering a place not designated as a bedroom.

## Ratana-sikkhāpadaṁ


<a id="org26d6781"></a>

### background


<a id="org3966a63"></a>

### Pāli rule


**Yo pana bhikkhu ratanaṃ vā ratanasammataṃ vā, aññatra ajjhārāmā vā ajjhāvasathā vā, uggaṇheyya vā uggaṇhāpeyya vā, pācittiyaṃ. Ratanaṃ vā pana bhikkhunā ratanasammataṃ vā ajjhārāme vā ajjhāvasathe vā uggahetvā vā uggahāpetvā vā nikkhipitabbaṃ, ‘Yassa bhavissati, so harissatīti. Ayaṃ tattha sāmīci.**
If any bhikkhu picks up or has someone pick up a treasure or what is considered a treasure, except within a monastery  or dwelling, it is a case involving expiation. Within ārāma or āvasatha, it must be kept aside, thinking, “He to whom it belongs will take it.



<a id="org0ae7f8d"></a>

### description

1.  10 ratana

    
    
    -   **Muttā**: Pearl
    -   **Maṇi**: Gem
    -   **Veḷuriyo**: Cat’s eye, lapis lazuli
    -   **Saṅkho**: Conch shell
    -   **Silā**: Semi-precious stone
    -   **Pavālaṃ**: Coral
    -   **Rajataṃ**: Silver
    -   **Jātarūpaṃ**: Gold
    -   **Lohitaṅko**: Ruby
    -   **Masāragallaṃ**: Emerald, sapphire, crystal
    
    **mem aid**: a pearl studded gem on a drawn cat with blue eyes painted by pencil (lapis) on the surface of a conch-shell lying inside a semi-precious stone (amethyst) surrounded by a coral snake with red strips, locked inside a silver casket locked by a golden key with a ruby on its handle on the neck of sara going to a gala.
    

2.  ratanasammata

    Consumable/usable objects (e.g.: thread, cloths, caps, umbrellas).

3.  ārāma and its boundary

    
    
    -   **parikkhitta** (enclosed monastery): the enclosure marks the boundary
    -   **aparikkhitta** (unenclosed monastery): 2 leḍḍupātā from the furthest / outermost dwelling
        -   *:q: is it measured like an unenclosed village (gharupacara>gama>gamupacara)?*
    
      

4.  āvasatha and its boundary

    
    
    -   Monk’s dwelling (temporary or permanent)
        -   **Parikkhitta**: the enclosure marks the boundary
        -   **Aparikkhitta**: suppapāta / musalapāta from door
    
    
    (*:q: clarify*)

5.  safekeeping procedure inside monastery

    
    
    -   **Examine** object’s characteristics (unwrap, count money); if akappiya, some suggest not to take it directly, but to have an upāsaka take it
    -   **Announce**: “May the person whose belonging is lost come!” (yassa bhaṇḍaṃ naṭṭhaṃ so āgacchatū)
    -   **Verify** owner’s description; hand over if matched, else advise to search elsewhere.
    -   **Delegate**: If leaving monastery, hand it over to suitable monk (lajjī, kukkuccaka) or layperson, not a restless monk (lolajātika)
    -   **Use**: If owner doesn’t appear, use for communal purposes (e.g., stupa, pond); inform owner and transfer merits if they return
    -   **Compensate**: If owner is unhappy, request equivalent from others to return.

6.  safekeeping procedure when travelling alone on an empty road (acc. to Kurundi)

    
    
    -   sit nearby and wait
    -   if owners come: inform
    -   if owners don't come: act appropriately
        -   srtdpn:
            -   if ratana: walk away without expectation (**nirāpekkhagamanaṁ**)
            -   if ratanasammata:
                -   if anāmasa: leave it thinking there is no owner
                -   if āmasa: you can collect it thinking there is no owner
        -   vmtvndn:
            -   if unallowable object:
                -   show it to a suitable lay devotees, etc. and arrange for it to be used in a meritorious manner (such as renovating a pagoda, etc.)
                -   or walk away without expectation
            -   if allowable object: you can collect it with paṁsukūlasaññā
    -   *:q: incomplete, add more information and clarify*
    
    

7.  bhaṇḍāgārasīsena gahaṇaṃ / gāhāpaṇaṃ

    Collect / making sb collect something with the intention of safekeeping it


<a id="orgb8620cd"></a>

### āpattivāra

-   collecting or having sb collect ratana / ratanasammata for safekeeping outside ārāma / āvasatha:  **pc** 
-   doing so within ārāma / āvasatha:  **anāpatti**: yassa bhavissati so harissati
-   doing so to the property of the monk’s mother who is a lay woman:  **pc** 
-   not taking a ratana / ratanasammata in the monastery: seems to be anāpatti (offense not mentioned) (*:q: clarify*)


<a id="orgb1059dc"></a>

### anāpattivāra

-    **ratanaṃ / ratanasammataṃ ajjhārāme / ajjhāvasathe nikkhipati**: collecting or having someone collect within ārāma / āvasatha for safekeeping
-    **ratanasammataṃ vissāsaṃ gaṇhāti**: taking allowable ratanasammata out of trust
-    **ratanasammataṃ tāvakālikaṃ gaṇhāti**: taking allowable ratanasammata for temporary use
-    **ratanasammataṃ paṃsukūlasaññissa**: taking allowable ratanasammata as discarded


<a id="orgb5afcde"></a>

### aṅga

-    **Ananuññātakaraṇaṃ** – absence of an approved reason to protect the object
-    **Parasantakatā** – object being owned by another (lay person)
-    **Vissāsaggāhapaṃsukūlasaññānaṃ abhāvo** – not taking out of trust, (not taking for temporary use) and absence of the perception that the object has been discarded
-    **Uggahaṇaṃ vā uggahāpanaṃ vā** – collecting or making someone else collect it

### other elements

**Kamma**: kāyakamma or vacīkamma

## Vikālegāmapavisana-sikkhāpadaṁ


<a id="org510e302"></a>

### background



-   part 1 &#x2013; Jetavana, Chabbaggiyas, tiracchānakathā in the village hall
    -   Tathāgata: **Jetavana**, Sāvatthi
    -   **Chabbaggiyas** visited a village during the vikāla, sat at the village hall, and engaged in various worldly talks
    -   People criticized
    -   bhikkhus informed the Buddha
    -   Buddha reprimanded the Chabbaggiyas and declared the rule &#x2013; **yo pana bhikkhu vikāle gāmaṃ paviseyya, pācittiyaṁ**
-   part 2 &#x2013; Kosala to Sāvatthi, thieves, anāpucchā
    -   bhikkhus travelling from **Kosala to Sāvatthi** reached one village in the evening
    -   People invited them in
    -   the bhikkhus were concerned about the rule and did not enter the village
    -   **thieves** attacked them
    -   the monks went to Sāvatthi and informed other monks, who informed the Buddha
    -   the Buddha amended the rule &#x2013; **yo pana bhikkhu <span class="underline">anāpucchā</span> vikāle gāmaṃ paviseyya, pācittiyaṁ**
-   part 3 &#x2013; Kosala to sāvatthi, thieves, santaṁ bhikkhuṁ
    -   **one monk**, travelling from Kosala to Sāvatthi, reached one village in the evening
    -   People invited him in
    -   the monk was concerned about the rule and did not enter
    -   thieves attacked him
    -   he went to Sāvatthi and informed the other monks, who then informed the Buddha
    -   the Buddha amended the rule &#x2013; **yo pana bhikkhu <span class="underline">santaṃ bhikkhuṃ</span> anāpucchā vikāle gāmaṃ paviseyya, pācittiyaṁ**
-   part 4 &#x2013; snake bite, fire, aññatra tathārūpā accāyikā karaṇīyā
    
    -   a **monk** got bitten by a serpent
    -   another monk went to the village to fetch fire but, concerned about the rule, did not enter
    -   the Buddha was notified and amended the rule for the final version
    
    


<a id="org85229cb"></a>

### Pāli Rule


**Yo pana bhikkhu santaṃ bhikkhuṃ anāpucchā vikāle gāmaṃ paviseyya, aññatra tathārūpā accāyikā karaṇīyā, pācittiyaṃ.**
(If any bhikkhu, not having informed a present bhikkhu, enters a village at the wrong time, except with an appropriate urgent duty, it is a case involving expiation).



<a id="orgc5cbf86"></a>

### description

1.  vikāla &#x2013; definition

    Time from noon’s end to dawn the next day (Vikālo nāma majjhaṇhike vītivatte yāva aruṇuggamanā).

2.  santa / asanta bhikkhu &#x2013; definition

    
    
    -   **santa** (√as): a "present" monk; one who is within savanūpacāra (~12 cubits) (normally in the monastery, but not necessarily)
    -   **asanta**: an "absent" monk; one who requires search or special approach (beyond savaṇūpacāra)
    -   (*:q: acc. to the background story, even after the allowance for informing a santa bhikkhu had been laid down, the monk did not enter the village, even though he was alone, so it seems that if there was no other bhikkhu to inform, one could not enter the village.  has it changed?*)
    
    

3.  pariccheda and upacāra

    1.  measurements
    
        
        
        -   **majjhima purisa leḍḍupāta** (lḍp): the distance of a stone-throw (where it first hits the ground) with stretched arm by a young man of average strength (thāma majjhima purisa)
            -   stone size: can be held with 1 hand
            -   throw: just as a young man throws a stone to demonstrate their strength
                -   not "upwards" like shooing a crow (Suttantika follows this method)
                -   not an "underarm-throw" like throwing water
            -   distance: excluding the distance the stone rolls after hitting the ground
        -   **suppapāta**: a woman’s winnowing-fan throw
        -   **sammuñjanī pāta**: a woman’s broom-throw
        -   **musalapāta**: pestle-throw (woman's or average man, per commentaries)
        -   **bhājanadhovana udakapāta**: woman’s washing water-throw from the gharapariccheda (rainwater line)
        
        
    
    2.  2 upacāras
    
        
        
        -   **abbhantara upacāra** (internal vicinity): area of the village itself, which is marked by the fence or the parikkheparahaṭṭhāna
            -   in the context of avippavāsa sīmā, gāmūpacāra in “ṭhapetvā gāmañca gāmūpacārañca” means abbhantara upacāra
        -   **bāhira upacāra** (external vicinity)
            -   parikkhitta gāma: 1 lḍp from the indakhila
            -   aparikkhitta gāma: 1 lḍp from the gāmūpacāra
        -   kkvtrn: both are indicated in the Pārājikāpāḷi
            -   gāmūpacāro nāma parikkhittassa gāmassa indakhīle ṭhitassa majjhimassa purisassa leḍḍupāto, aparikkhittassa gāmassa gharūpacāre ṭhitassa majjhimassa purisassa leḍḍupāto

    12. Eight Key Concepts to Understand
    
        1.  1. Village (Gāma) &#x2013; 10 Types
        
            1.  **Ekakuṭiko gāmo** &#x2013; village of one house/kuṭi (existed in Malaya
                region of ancient Sri Lanka)
            2.  **Dvikuṭiko gāmo** &#x2013; village of two houses/kuṭis
            3.  **Tikuṭiko gāmo** &#x2013; village of three houses/kuṭis
            4.  **Catukkuṭiko gāmo** &#x2013; village of four houses/kuṭis
                -   Note: newly established village NOT designated as a village until
                    people start residing there
                -   Village of more than one house = **Anekakuṭiko gāmo**
            5.  **Samanusso gāmo** &#x2013; village where people reside
            6.  **Amanusso gāmo** &#x2013; two types:
                -   Village occupied by non-humans (yakkas); if boutiques/shops visible
                    → must inform a santa bhikkhu before entering during vikāla; if not
                    visible → informing not necessary
                -   Village where all people have temporarily left but **intend to
                    return** (sāpekkhabhāva); if left with NO intention to return →
                    ceases to be a village
            7.  **Parikkhitto gāmo** &#x2013; village surrounded by wall, rampart, or thorny
                branches
            8.  **Aparikkhitto gāmo** &#x2013; village NOT surrounded by wall, rampart, or
                thorny branches
            9.  **Gonisadiniviṭṭho gāmo** &#x2013; houses built in small clusters of 2&#x2013;3 in
                various places (like cattle lying in groups)
            10. **Atirekacatumāsaniviṭṭho gāmo** &#x2013; caravan remaining in one place for
                more than four months = considered a village
        
        2.  2. Border of the Village (Gāma Pariccheda)
        
            -   **Walled village**: the wall/rampart/enclosure itself = the border;
                inner area = the village
            -   **Unwalled village**: the distance of one leḍḍupāta thrown from the
                upacāra of the furthest house = the border; also called
                **parikkheparahaṭṭhāna** (site suitable for building a wall)
        
        3.  3. Vicinity of the Village (Gāmūpacāra) &#x2013; Two Types
        
            -   **Internal vicinity (abbhantara upacāra)**:
                -   = the village boundary (pariccheda) itself
                -   For walled village = the wall/fence
                -   For unwalled village = the parikkheparahaṭṭhāna
                -   Used in context of **avippavāsa sīmā** ("ṭhapetvā gāmañca
                    gāmūpacārañca")
            -   **External vicinity (bahira upacāra)**:
                -   = the place where a leḍḍupāta falls when thrown from the village
                    boundary
                -   For walled village = leḍḍupāta from gatepost (indakhīla)
                -   For unwalled village = leḍḍupāta from parikkheparahaṭṭhāna
            -   **Leḍḍupāta** = distance a stone falls when thrown by a man of middle
                strength (thāma majjhima purisa) with outstretched hand, stone held in
                fist; rolling distance NOT counted; Suttantika Theras: stone thrown to
                chase away crows
        
        4.  4. Boundary of a House (Ghara Pariccheda)
        
            -   = the place where rainwater falls from the roof edge; inner area = the
                house
        
        5.  5. Vicinity of a House (Gharūpacāra)
        
            -   If surrounded by fence → fence = vicinity
            -   If no fence → distance from the roof-drip edge to where water lands
                when a woman throws water standing at the roof-drip edge
        
        6.  6. Boundary of a Monastery (Vihāra Pariccheda)
        
            -   **Walled monastery**: wall/fence = boundary; inner area = monastery
            -   **Unwalled monastery** &#x2013; two methods:
                -   From furthest senāsana (dāna sālā, gilāna sālā etc.): leḍḍupāta from
                    that senāsana's upacāra = monastery boundary
                -   From cetiyaṅgaṇa/bodhiyaṅgaṇa at monastery edge: leḍḍupāta from that
                    courtyard = monastery boundary
            -   This boundary = **vihāra parikkheparahaṭṭhāna** = area inside = the
                monastery
        
        7.  7. Vicinity of a Monastery (Vihārūpacāra) &#x2013; Two Types
        
            -   **Internal vicinity (abbhantara upacāra)**:
                -   = the monastery boundary (pariccheda) itself
                -   Walled: the fence/wall
                -   Unwalled: the parikkheparahaṭṭhāna
                -   Used in passages like "Ajjhārāmo nāma parikkhittassa ārāmassa
                    antōārāmo, aparikkhittassa upacārō"
            -   **External vicinity (bāhira upacāra)**:
                -   = leḍḍupāta from the monastery boundary
                -   Walled: leḍḍupāta from the fence
                -   Unwalled: leḍḍupāta from the parikkheparahaṭṭhāna
                -   Used in: "Anujānāmi, bhikkhave, agilānenapi ārāme ārāmūpacāre
                    chattaṃ dhāretuṃ"
        
        8.  8. Arañña / Non-Village Area (Agāma) &#x2013; Three Definitions
        
            -   **(1) According to Vinaya:**
                -   All areas excluding village AND village vicinity = arañña
                -   "ṭhapetvā gāmañca gāmūpacārañca sabbametaṃ araññaṃ"
                -   Village vicinity counted as part of village by common convention
                    (loka vohāra)
            
            -   **(2) According to Abhidhamma:**
                -   All areas outside the gatepost (indakhīla) = arañña
                -   "nikkhamitvā bahi indakhīlā sabbametaṃ araññaṃ"
                -   Gāmūpacāra of Vinaya approach also = arañña here (non-village in
                    straightforward sense)
            
            -   **(3) According to Suttanta (most relevant for āraññikaṅga):**
                -   A senāsana located beyond **500 bow-lengths (dhanu)** from the village
                    = arañña senāsana
                -   "Āraññakaṃ nāma senāsanaṃ pañcadhanusatikaṃ pacchimaṃ"
                -   **Ācariya dhanu** = bow of specific type; some ancient teachers: 9
                    handspans of ordinary man (pakati purisa) without bowstring; 4
                    handspans with bowstring applied
                -   Arañña areas need NOT contain jungle (unlike general worldly usage)
            
            ---
    
    13. Two Methods for Measuring 500 Bow-Lengths
    
        1.  (a) Vinaya Aṭṭhakathā Method
        
            -   Measure from **village boundary (internal vicinity)** to **monastery
                boundary (internal vicinity)**
            -   Minimum = **2,000 handspans** of ordinary man
            -   Modern approximation:
                -   If 1 handspan = 8 inches → minimum **406.4 meters** from gāma
                    pariccheda
                -   If 1 handspan = 7 inches → minimum **355.6 meters** from gāma
                    pariccheda
        
        2.  (b) Majjhima Nikāya Aṭṭhakathā Method (used for āraññikaṅga)
        
            -   Measure between **two leḍḍupāta points**: one from village's external
                vicinity + one from monastery's external vicinity
            -   "Vihārassapi gāmasseva upacāraṃ nīharitvā ubhinnaṃ leḍḍupātānaṃ antarā
                minitabba"
            -   = measurement taken **between** the two leḍḍupāta throws, **excluding**
                the external vicinity of both village and monastery
            
            ---
    
    14. Procedure (Vidhāna)
    
        -   Must break dawn in monastery where **500 bow-lengths** exist between the
            two leḍḍupāta points (Majjhima method)
        -   If village is close but path is obstructed (rocks etc.) → measure
            **along the natural pathway**
        -   If must travel by ship/boat → measure along that required route
        -   If main road is far but small paths are shorter → measure along the
            **closest possible natural route** (ignoring only main road = "dhutaṅga
            cora")
        -   If **preceptor or teacher is ailing** and forest lacks necessary comfort
            → may take teacher to village monastery for care
            -   To keep āraññikaṅga unbroken: must **leave village monastery before
                dawn** and break dawn in arañña
            -   If teacher's ailment worsens exactly at dawn → must attend to
                teacher; must NOT strive to keep practice pure at that moment
            -   "Wise bhikkhus do not neglect assistance to parents and teachers in
                order to keep the austerity practice pure"

### background


Buddha: Nigrodhārāma, Kapilavatthu, Sākya region

### Pāli rule


**Yo pana bhikkhu aṭṭhimayaṃ vā dantamayaṃ vā visāṇamayaṃ vā sūcigharaṃ kārāpeyya bhedanakaṃ, pācittiyaṃ.**

## Mañcapīṭha-sikkhāpadaṁ


<a id="org494d16f"></a>

### background



-   Buddha: Jetavana
-   V. Upananda used to lie down on a tall bed
-   while the Buddha was touring the dwelling, the V. called Him and showed him his bed
-   he went back and said a mogha purisa is known from his dwelling place and laid down the rule




<a id="orga5f8ce9"></a>

### Pāli rule


**Navaṃ pana bhikkhunā mañcaṃ vā pīṭhaṃ vā kārayamānena aṭṭhaṅgulapādakaṃ kāretabbaṃ sugataṅgulena, aññatra heṭṭhimāya aṭaniyā, taṃ atikkāmayato chedanakaṃ pācittiyaṃ.**



<a id="org9ca6b32"></a>

### description

1.  5 mañcas

    
    
    -   **masāraka**: legs are pierced and the edges of the frame are fixed to them
        -   in the time of vyvncy, it was made of cane
    -   **bundikābaddha**: legs inserted into frame edges
        -   made with cane or wood
    -   **kuḷīrapādaka**: curved legs like horse hooves or crab legs; any bed with curved legs
    -   **āhaccapādaka**: frame drilled, sharpened leg tops inserted, nailed
        -   frame is drilled tops of legs are sharpened (pāda sīse sikhaṃ katvā) and inserted into the drilled holes of the frame. then, the portion of the legs that extends above the frame is drilled horizontally, and nails are fixed 3
    -   **sattaṅga**: bed with seven parts (four legs, three supports); no offence for any leg height; bed that has support to lean upon (apassaya) from three sides
        -   **anāpatti**
    
    **mem aid**: sara in a couch, a bound rear, a lyre inside the hole, removable feet, and the feet become side rails for a sattaṅga
    

2.  5 pīṭha

    
    
    -   **masāraka**
    -   **bundikābaddha**
    -   **kuḷīrapādaka**
    -   **āhaccapādaka**
    -   **āsandika/caturassa pīṭha**: square shaped
        -   no offence for any leg height
        -   not considered a pīṭha under this rule (Cv-a)
    -   note:
        -   long seats
    
    

3.  # of monks allowed to sit on a bed/seat

    
    **anujānāmi, bhikkhave, duvaggassa mañcaṃ, duvaggassa pīṭhaṃ**
    


<a id="org1a4f5de"></a>

### āpattivāra

-   making/ordering bed/seat with legs > eight sugataṅguli for oneself:  **dkṭ** per effort, **pc** upon completion or receipt
-   making/ordering for others:  **dkṭ** 
-   using one made by another:  **dkṭ** 
-   making a too-high sattaṅga bed:  **anāpatti** 
-   (*:q: what if it's not our property?*)

1.  what should be done in the case of offense

    
    Excess leg length must be cut before confessing (**chedanaka pācittiya**)
    


<a id="org831b8f6"></a>

### anāpattivāra



-   **pamāṇikaṃ karoti**: bed/seat with legs exactly eight sugataṅguli
-   **ūnakaṃ karoti**: bed/seat with legs < eight sugataṅguli
-   **aññena kataṃ pamāṇātikkantaṃ paṭilabhitvā chinditvā paribhuñjati**: receiving over-height bed/seat, cutting excess before use
