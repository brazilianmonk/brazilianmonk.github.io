## Paṭhama Pāṭidesanīya Sikkhāpadaṁ


<a id="org9b633d0"></a>

### Background


A bhikkhunī offered her entire alms meal to an unrelated monk on
three consecutive days, leading to her hunger and weakness; a layman
learned of this, fed her, and criticized monks, prompting the Buddha
to establish the rule.



<a id="orgfacd2b0"></a>

### Pāli Rule


Yo pana bhikkhu aññātikāya bhikkhuniyā antaragharaṃ paviṭṭhāya hatthato 
khādanīyaṃ vā bhojanīyaṃ vā sahatthā paṭiggahetvā khādeyya vā bhuñjeyya vā, 
paṭidesetabbaṃ tena bhikkhunā - “Gārayhaṃ, āvuso, Dhammaṃ āpajjiṃ 
asappāyaṃ pāṭidesanīyaṃ, taṃ paṭidesemī” ti. 

If any bhikkhu, having accepted it with his own hand from the hand of an unrelated 
bhikkhunī who has entered an inhabited area for alms, should chew uncooked food 
or eat cooked food, it is to be acknowledged by that bhikkhu saying: “Friends, 
I have committed a blameworthy act which is unsuitable, which is to be 
acknowledged; I acknowledge it.” 



<a id="orgf149eeb"></a>

### Description

Bhikkhus must exercise caution accepting food from bhikkhunīs due to their scarcity of requisites

1.  Aññātikā Bhikkhunī &#x2013; definition and exceptions

    
    
    -   Non-relative (not blood-related within **seven generations**)
    -   Ubhato saṅghe upasampannā (fully ordained by both Saṅghas)
    -   Exceptions:
        -   Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī and 500 Sākyan bhikkhunīs (deemed full
            despite single ordination);
        -   natural sex-change monk becoming bhikkhunī (full despite single
            ordination).
    -   **Notes**: if ekato upasampannā, dkṭ for both accepting and consuming.
    
    

2.  Antaraghara &#x2013; areas included

    
    
    -   Village area including:
        -   **rathikā** (connecting vehicular road; main road)
        -   **byūhaṃ** (non-connecting vehicular road; alleyway)
        -   **siṅghāṭakaṃ** (road junction; intersection)
        -   **gharaṃ** (lay house)
        -   comm.: also **hatthisālā** (elephant stalls)
    
    

3.  Kappiya Bhūmi &#x2013; areas exempted

    
    
    -   **Antarārāma** (monks' monastery inside a village)
    -   **Bhikkhunūpassaya** (bhikkhunīs' residence)
    -   **Titthiyaseyya** (non-Buddhist monastery)
        -   *:q: is modern-day mahāyana monasteries under this?*
    -   **Paṭikkamana** (dining hall)
        -   *:q: discussion of whether it includes restaurants and hotels; maybe yes*
    -   **Note**: the offence is based on bhikkhunī's offering location, not the bhikkhu's consumption site.
    
    

4.  Edible Items &#x2013; pātidesanīya-, dukkaṭa-, and kappiya- vatthu

    
    
    -   **Pātidesanīya Vatthu**: Yāvakālika (khādanīya/bhojanīya);
        -   acceptance: dukkaṭa
        -   each swallow: pāṭidesanīya
    -   **Dukkaṭa Vatthu**: Non-yāvakālika (yāmakālika, sattāhakālika,
        yāvajīvika) for āhārattha
        -   acceptance and each swallow: dukkaṭa
    -   **Kappiya Vatthu**: Yāmakālika for thirst/sickness;
        sattāhakālika/yāvajīvika for medicine
        -   anāpatti
    
    

5.  Dāna &#x2013; 2 factors

    
    
    -   **sahatthā** (by hand, including utensil in hand)
    -   **anupanikkhipitvā** (direct: into bhikkhu's vessel or body
        part)
        -   if upanikkhipitvā (placed on ground/table not touching
            bhikkhu): **anāpatti**
    
    


<a id="org4cefcce"></a>

### Offences

-   Accepting yāvakālika intending to consume it:  **dkṭ** 
    -   Swallowing it:  **ptd** per swallow 
-   Accepting non-yāvakālika edible for hunger:  **dkṭ** 
    -   Swallowing it:  **dkṭ** per swallow 
-   Accepting from ekatoupasampanna:  all **dkṭ** 


<a id="orgfbf7999"></a>

### Non-Offences



-   aññātikāya
    -   **Ñātikāya** (relative bhikkhunī).
-   bhikkhuniyā
    -   **Sikkhamānāya/sāmaṇeriyā** (trainee or novice nun).
        -   ekatoupasampanna: dkṭ
-   antaragharaṁ paviṭṭhāya
    -   **Gāmato nīharitvā deti** (offered outside village).
    -   **Offerings in kappiya bhūmi** (antarārāma, etc.).
-   hattatho…patiggahetvā
    -   **Dāpeti na deti** (offered via intermediary).
    -   **Upanikkhipitvā deti** (indirect placement near bhikkhu).
-   khādanīyaṁ vā bhojanīyaṁ vā
    -   **Yāmakālika/sattāhakālika/yāvajīvika "sati paccaye paribhuñjā"ti** (for
        non-hunger purposes)

-   **Ajjhoharaṇaṃ** (swallowing).

## Dutiya Pāṭidesanīya Sikkhāpadaṁ


<a id="org4f4e8e6"></a>

### Background


At alms events, Chabbaggiyā bhikkhunīs directed lay offerings to
favor Chabbaggiyā bhikkhus, leaving others dissatisfied; monks
criticized this, leading the Buddha to establish the rule prohibiting
failure to dismiss such bhikkhunīs.



<a id="org2d71fd1"></a>

### Pāli Rule


Bhikkhū paneva kulesu nimantitā bhuñjanti, tatra ce sā bhikkhunī
vosāsamānarūpā ṭhitā hoti – ‘Idha sūpaṃ detha, idha odanaṃ dethā’ti,
tehi bhikkhūhi sā bhikkhunī apasādetabbā – ‘Apasakka tāva, bhagini,
yāva bhikkhū bhuñjantī’ti. Ekassa cepi bhikkhuno na paṭibhāseyya taṃ
bhikkhuniṃ apasādetuṃ – ‘Apasakka tāva, bhagini, yāva bhikkhū
bhuñjantī’ti paṭidesetabbaṃ tehi bhikkhūhi – ‘Gārayhaṃ, āvuso, dhammaṃ
āpajjimhā asappāyaṃ pāṭidesanīyaṃ, taṃ paṭidesemā”ti.

Now, bhikkhus who have been invited eat among families, and if a
bhikkhunī who is giving directions is standing there saying, “Give
curry here, give rice here!”  then by those bhikkhus that bhikkhunī
is to be dismissed saying, “Go away, sister, for as long as the
bhikkhus eat!” and if not even one bhikkhu would speak against it, so
as to dismiss that bhikkhunī saying, “Go away, sister, for as long
as the bhikkhus eat!” then it is to be acknowledged by those
bhikkhus, “Friends, we have committed a blameworthy act which is
unsuitable, which is to be acknowledged; we acknowledge it.”



<a id="org7f621b5"></a>

### Description

-   Applies to nimantita (named bhojana invitation) pañca bhojana in kula (lay households in antaraghara); prohibits failure to dismiss full bhikkhunī giving vosāsanā (favoritism-based directions).

1.  Kulesu nimantitā &#x2013; definition

    
    
    -   **kula**: lay household in antaraghara
    -   **nimantitā**: monks have been invited with the mention of a pañcabhojana
    
    

2.  Vosāsanā &#x2013; definition

    
    
    -   Favoritism Instructing &#x2013; e.g., "Idha sūpaṃ detha, idha odanaṃ dethā"ti;
    
    
    
    1.  5 bases of favoritism
    
        
        
        -   Yathāmittatā (friendship)
        -   yathāsandiṭṭhatā (familiarity)
        -   yathāsambhattatā (association)
        -   yathāsamānupajjhāyakatā (same preceptor)
        -   yathāsamānācariyakatā (same teacher)
        
        
        
        1.  Vosāsamānarūpā &#x2013; definition
        
            The instructing nun.


<a id="org7cac08d"></a>

### Offences

-   Failure to dismiss a vosāsamānarūpā bhikkhunī:  **ptd** for all eating bhikkhus if none act ("Apasakka tāva, bhagini, yāva bhikkhū bhuñjantī"ti)
    -   After failing to dismiss, accepting intending to consume it:  **dkṭ** 
    -   Swallowig it:  **ptd** per swallow 
-   Failing to dismiss an ekato upasampannā:  **dkṭ** 
    -   Accepting it from her intending to consume it:  **dkṭ** 
    -   Swallowing it:  **dkṭ** per swallow 


<a id="orge11e5ea"></a>

### Non-Offences



-   **Attano bhattaṃ dāpeti na deti**: if the nun makes someone else distribute her own food
    -   If she gives by herself, ptds offense under the 1st ptds rule.
-   **Aññesaṃ bhattaṃ deti na dāpeti**: if she gives pañcabhojana that belongs to someone else
    -   If she offers someone else's pañcabhojana through someone else: ptds offense under this rule
-   **Yaṃ na dinnaṃ taṃ dāpeti**: if she makes others offer unoffered pañcabhojana (*:q: clarify*)
    -   nothing is mentioned in the books; bhante's opinion: she finds out that some food items have not been offered and she has them offered
-   **Yattha na dinnaṃ tattha dāpeti**: if she makes others offer pañcabhojana to monks to whom they were not offered.
-   **Sabbesaṃ samakaṃ dāpeti**: if she instructs others to give equally
-   **Sikkhamānā / Sāmaṇerī vosāsati**
-   **Pañca bhojanāni ṭhapetvā sabbattha**: if it's not pañcabhojana

## Tatiya Pāṭidesanīya Sikkhāpadaṁ


<a id="orgc7cf4f5"></a>

### Background


A sotāpanna couple gave excessively to monks, declining in wealth while growing in faith; criticism arose, leading to sekkha sammuti procedure; amendments allowed prior-invited or sick monks to accept food.



<a id="org99d9cd6"></a>

### Rule in Pāḷi


Yāni kho pana tāni sekkhasammatāni kulāni, yo pana bhikkhu tathārūpesu sekkhasammatesu kulesu pubbe animantito agilāno khādanīyaṃ vā bhojanīyaṃ vā sahatthā paṭiggahetvā khādeyya vā bhuñjeyya vā, paṭidesetabbaṃ tena bhikkhunā – ‘Gārayhaṃ, āvuso, dhammaṃ āpajjiṃ asappāyaṃ pāṭidesanīyaṃ, taṃ paṭidesemī”ti. 

Now, there are those families which are agreed upon as trainees: if any bhikkhu who has not been invited beforehand, who is not ill, should chew uncooked food or eat cooked food having accepted it with his own hand in families who are of such a kind, who are considered trainees, then it is to be acknowledged by that bhikkhu: “Friends, I have committed a blameworthy act which is unsuitable, which is to be acknowledged; I acknowledge it.” 



<a id="org91c5f79"></a>

### Description

Prohibits uninvited, non-sick acceptance/consumption of yāvakālika from sekkha sammata kula (faith-growing, wealth-declining noble household) to protect donors.

1.  Sekkha Sammata Kula &#x2013; definition

    
    
    -   a faith-growing, wealth-declining-for-giving noble household
        -   kula: family in which both spouses are sotāpannas (*:q: what about higher ariyas?*)
        -   saddhāya vaḍḍhati: growing in saddhā
        -   bhogena hāyati: declining wealth due to giving, not squandering
    
    
    
    1.  How the Sekkhasammata status is conferred
    
        
        Status conferred via **sekkha sammuti kammavācā** (Ñatti-dutiya-kamma: "Suṇātu me, bhante,
        saṅgho. Itthannāmaṃ kulaṃ saddhāya vaḍḍhati, bhogena hāyati&#x2026;").
        

2.  Nimantana &#x2013; definition

    
    
    -   Invitation for yāvakālika meal
        -   time period:
            -   for today (**ajjatanāya**)
            -   or tomorrow (**svātanāya**)
        -   location:
            -   before entering gharūpacāra (house vicinity)
                -   if the invitation is made after the monk has entered the gharūpacāra, it doesn't count as invitation under this rule.
    -   **notes**: differs from standard nimantana (here no bhojana naming, includes khādanīya)
    
    

3.  Gilāna &#x2013; definition

    Illness preventing going for piṇḍapāta.


<a id="org6781ec5"></a>

### Offenses

-   Yāvakālika (uninvited, non-sick, entering gharūpacāra):
    -   Acceptance:  **dkṭ** 
    -   Swallow:  **ptd** per swallow 
-   Non-yāvakālika for hunger:
    -   Acceptance:  **dkṭ** 
    -   Swallow:  **dkṭ** per swallow 


<a id="org5f788b0"></a>

### Non-Offences



-   monk
    -   **Nimantitassa** (prior invitation before gharūpacāra).
    -   **Gilānassa** (illness preventing piṇḍapāta).
-   food
    -   **Nimantitassa/gilānassa sesakaṃ bhuñjati** (leftovers from invited/sick
        monk).
    -   **Aññesaṃ bhikkhā tattha paññattā** (others' prepared food).
    -   **Niccabhatte/salākabhatte/pakkhike/uposathike/pāṭipadike**
        (regular/lot/biweekly/uposatha/post-uposatha meals).
    -   **Yāmakālika/sattāhakālika/yāvajīvika "sati paccaye paribhuñjā"ti** (for
        specific non-hunger reasons).
-   acceptance
    -   **Gharato nīharitvā denti** (brought outside house pre-arrival; not if
        post-arrival).




<a id="orgc1589a5"></a>

### Factors



-   **Sekkhasammatatā** (sekkha-conferred household).
-   **Pubbe animantitatā** (no prior invitation).
-   **Gharūpacārokkamanaṃ** (entering house vicinity).
-   **Agilānatā** (not sick).
-   **Ṭhapetvā niccabhattādīni aññaṃ āmisaṃ gahetvā bhuñjanaṃ** (consuming
    non-exempt yāvakālika).




<a id="orgd861b59"></a>

### Other Elements

**Citta and Saññā**: acittaka, nosaññāvimokkha

## Catuttha Pāṭidesanīya Sikkhāpadaṁ


<a id="org1e81063"></a>

### Background


Sakyan women offering meals to forest monasteries were robbed and assaulted by slaves due to lack of monk warning; Buddha established rule for risky monasteries; amendment allowed sick
monks to accept unannounced food.



<a id="orgae1a997"></a>

### Rule in Pāḷi


Yāni kho pana tāni āraññakāni senāsanāni sāsaṅkasammatāni sappaṭibhayāni, | yo pana bhikkhu tathārūpesu senāsanesu pubbe appaṭisaṃviditaṃ khādanīyaṃ vā bhojanīyaṃ vā ajjhārāme sahatthā paṭiggahetvā | agilāno khādeyya vā bhuñjeyya vā, paṭidesetabbaṃ tena bhikkhunā | – “Gārayhaṃ, āvuso, dhammaṃ āpajjiṃ asappāyaṃ pāṭidesanīyaṃ, taṃ paṭidesemī”ti. 

Now, there are those wilderness lodgings which are considered risky, which are dangerous: if any bhikkhu, staying in lodgings which are of such a kind, without having announced the danger beforehand, having accepted the food with his own hand inside the monastery, and then not being ill, should chew uncooked food or eat cooked food, then it is to be acknowledged by that bhikkhu, “Friends, I have committed a blameworthy act which is unsuitable, which is to be acknowledged; I acknowledge it.”

chanting style: 1b-l



<a id="orgb9185dd"></a>

### Description

-   Prohibits acceptance/consumption of unannounced yāvakālika inside
    sāsaṅka sammata sappaṭibhaya ārañña senāsana to protect sponsors.

1.  Ārañña Senāsana &#x2013; definition

    Forest monastery 500 dhanu (1 dhanu = 9 pakatipurisavidatthi) to 1 gāvuta from village through the road

2.  Sāsaṅka Sammata Sappaṭibhaya &#x2013; definition

    
    
    -   **Sāsaṅka Sammata**: suspicious; thief signs: living/eating/standing/sitting/sleeping places nearby
    -   **Sappaṭibhaya**: dangerous; dead bodies or stolen goods nearby from thieves.
    
    

3.  Ajjhārāma &#x2013; \`efinition

    
    
    -   Bounded: inside boundary is ajjhārāma
        -   *:q: from the boundary, 1 ldpt is ārāmupacāra? is it used in vinaya?*
    -   Unbounded: inside the ārāma and its upacāra is considered ajjhārāma
        -   this means that accepting the food in the upacāra would also constitute an offense
    
    

4.  Paṭisaṃvidita and Appaṭisaṁvidita

    
    
    -   **Paṭisaṁvidita**: Pre-notified yāvakālika (e.g., by non-sahadhammika messenger: "Itthannāmassa khādanīyaṃ/bhojanīyaṁ āharissantī"ti)
        -   notification happens in the ārāma / upacāra
        -   includes accompaniments, family/village/group members' food, extra food from unannounced joiners
        -   Kurundi: if yāgu has been announced but cakes or rice is brought: allowable
    -   **Appaṭisaṃvidita**:
        -   unannounced
        -   announced by sahadhammika
        -   announced outside ārāma/upacāra
    
        
    
    1.  procedure when notified of incoming yāvakālika
    
        
        
        -   monk must warn of sāsaṅka/sappaṭibhaya nature of the monastery
        -   if the food will be brought anyways, the monk must inform the thieves and ask
            them not to harm the donors
        
         
    
    2.  procedure when appaṭisaṁvidita food is brought
    
        
        
        -   option 1:
            -   send it out of the ārāma and its upacāra
            -   have the donors send a messenger inside the monastery and announce the meal
            -   have them bring it and offer the food
        -   option 2:
            -   go outside the ārāma and its upacāra and accept it there
            -   bring it back and consume it (pc-a)
        
        

5.  Gilāna &#x2013; definition

    Illness preventing going for piṇḍapāta.


<a id="org81d6c1f"></a>

### Offenses

-   **Appaṭisaṃvidita** yāvakālika (non-sick, inside):
    -   Accepting intending to consume it:  **dkṭ** 
    -   Swallowing:  **ptd** per swallow 
-   Non-yāvakālika for hunger:
    -   Accepting:  **dkṭ** 
    -   Swallowing:  **dkṭ** per swallow 
-   Accepting it inside the aparikkhitta ārāmūpacāra:  **offense** 
-   Handling Unannounced: Send out for re-announcement; or accept outside and bring in.


<a id="orgdeb005f"></a>

### Non-Offences



-   **Paṭisaṃvidite** (announced).
-   **Gilānassa** (illness preventing piṇḍapāta).
-   **Paṭisaṃvidite/gilānassa sesakaṃ bhuñjati** (leftovers from
    announced/sick; extras for others; excess storable sans sannidhi).
-   **Bahārāme paṭiggahetvā antoārāme bhuñjati** (accept outside, eat
    inside).
-   **Tattha jātakaṃ mūlaṃ/tacaṃ/pattaṃ/pupphaṃ/phalaṃ** (monastery-grown
    organics, raw or cooked inside; not if village-cooked without
    announcement).
-   **Yāmakālika/sattāhakālika/yāvajīvika "sati paccaye paribhuñjā"ti** (for
    non-hunger).
