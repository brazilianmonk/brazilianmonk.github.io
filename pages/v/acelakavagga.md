<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>background</b></mark><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"> </mark></h3>
<ul>
<li>Buddha: Kūṭāgāra, Vesāli; V. Ānanda informed Him about the surplus khādanīya; He instructed that it be donated to those consuming leftover food (<b>vighāsāda</b>); he distributed one cake (pūva) for each but mistakenly gave two cakes to a female wandering ascetic (<b>paribbājikā</b>) three times; this led to an argument among the ascetics when they questioned her about the monk's intentions; a wandering ascetic received a lump of rice mixed with ghee from a bhikkhu and mentioned it came from Gotama’s food distribution; several upāsakas, hearing his insult,, reported it, leading to the rule</li>
<li><b>summary</b>: distributing food led to arguments over favoritism as well as criticism from upāsakas; rule helps keep some distance from titthiyas</li>
<li><b>vighāsāda</b>↔those consuming leftover food</li>
</ul>

<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>background</b></mark></h3>
<ul>
<li>part 1
                            <ul>
<li>Buddha: Veḷuvana Monastery, Rājagaha</li>
<li>V. Upananda’s caretaker invited monks for rice meal with akappiya nimantana</li>
<li>V. Upananda visited other households before the meal, delaying monks and donors</li>
<li>Buddah banned pre-meal visits (purebhatta)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>part 2
                            <ul>
<li>Upananda’s caregiving household sent khādanīya to the monastery for the Saṅgha, but V. Upananda delayed return</li>
<li>Buddha banned post-meal visits (pacchābhatta) except to inviting house</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>part 3
                            <ul>
<li>monks hesitated to visit during cīvaradānasamaya, reducing robes</li>
<li>Buddha allowed exceptions</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>part 4
                            <ul>
<li>while making robes (cīvarakārasamaya), monks needed tools but hesitated</li>
<li>Buddha allowed visits</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>part 5
                            <ul>
<li>sick monks needed medicine but hesitated</li>
<li>Buddha allowed permitted visits after informing a monk (santa bhikkhu)</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>

<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>background</b></mark><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"> </mark></h3>
<ul>
<li>B in jetavana; king pasenadī kosala left with army; chabbaggiyas went to see them. king asked why; monks said to see him. king questioned their intent, suggesting they see tathāgata. soldiers criticized buddha’s followers. tathāgata set uyyutta senā rule, banning monks from seeing armies heading to battle. later, a soldier, a bhikkhu’s uncle, fell ill, sent for his nephew monk to visit. buddha added sub-rule allowing visits to army for valid reasons.  </li>

<ul>
<li>The Buddha was residing in the Jetavana monastery near the city of Sāvatthi.  During this time the Chabbaggiya bhikkhus had visited the army troops for a certain reason and stayed with them for more than three nights. The people belonging to the army criticized the monks, inquiring how they could be staying with the troops. Based on this incident, the Tathāgata declared the senāvāsa training rule prohibiting monks from staying with the troops for a longer time</li>
</ul>

spending 3 dawns and then leaving before sunset: <mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>anāpatti</b></mark> 
if its caturaṅganī senā: <mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: purple;"><i>?</i></mark>
if its a non-caturaṅganī senā: <mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: purple;"><i>?</i></mark>  #<mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: purple;"><b>q</b></mark>🙋🏼‍♂</li>
