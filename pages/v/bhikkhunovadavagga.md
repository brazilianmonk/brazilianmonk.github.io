<ul>
<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><b>Yo pana bhikkhu asammato bhikkhunīyo ovadeyya, pācittiyaṃ.  </b>
If any bhikkhu who has not been authorised should exhort the bhikkhunīs, [this is a case] involving  expiation.    </h3></li>
<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: yellow; color: inherit;"><b>background</b></mark></h3>
<ul>
<li>The Buddha was residing in the Jetavana monastery near the city of Sāvatti. During this time, senior  bhikkhus used to advise bhikkhunīs. Such bhikkhus who were advising the bhikkhunīs received the four  requisites such as robes in abundance. The Chabbaggiya monks who saw this incident decided to offer  advice to the bhikkhunīs. They informed the bhikkhunīs to come to them to obtain advice. Then the  bhikkhunīs also went to the Cabbaaggiya bhikkhus seeking advice. However, the Cabbaggiya bhikkhus  spent most of the day talking about unnecessary matters with the bhikkhunīs and did not spend a lot of  time preaching the Dhamma. The Tathāgata inquired about this matter from the bhikkhunīs, and they  explained the incident to him. Subsequently, the Tathāgata declared a bhikkhunovādaka sammuti (a  convention of appointing a monk to advice bhikkhunīs) based on this incident as: “Anujānami,  bhikkhave, bhikkhunovādakaṃ sammannitum”.  Afterwards, the six types of bhikkhus continued to advise the bhikkhunīs without obtaining the  convention. Based on this incident, the Tathāgata declared ovāda sikkhāpada preventing a bhikkhu who  does not have the bhikkhunovādaka sammuti from advising bhikkhunīs.  Then the Chabbaggiya monks went into various sīmas and declared bhikkhunovādaka sammuti amongst  themselves (they gave the sammuti to each other). Thereafter, they informed the bhikkhunīs that they  have now obtained the bhikkhunovādaka convention, and that bhikkhunīs can come to them once again  for advice. When bhikkhunīs approached them, they continued to discuss unnecessary matters more  than discussing the Dhamma, just as before. The Tathāgata, having got to know about this matter,  preached eight qualities a bhikkhu should have in order to be able to obtain the bhikkhunovādaka  sammuti.  </li>
</ul>
</li>

<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><b>Sammatopi ce bhikkhu atthaṅgate suriye bhikkhunīyo ovadeyya, pācittiyaṃ</b>.
Even if a bhikkhu who has been authorised should exhort the bhikkhunīs after the sun has set, [this  is a case] involving expiation.  </h3></li>
<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: yellow; color: inherit;"><b>background</b></mark></h3>
<ul>
<li>The Tathāgata was residing in the Jetavana monastery near Sāvatthi. During this time, elderly monks  used to admonish bhikkhunīs in turns. Once it was the turn of Venerable Cullapanthaka. That day he  admonished nuns, illustrating his psychic powers, till the sunset. Therefore, bhikkhunīs could not go  back into the city that day. They spent the night outside the city walls and entered the city early in the  morning. Seeing the nuns who were entering the city early in the morning, people started the bad mouth  nuns and monks saying, “These nuns, having spent the night in the monastery with monks, now enter  the city. They are not celibate”. Knowing this incident, the Tathāgata imposed the atthaṅgata training rule.</li>
</ul>
</li>

<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: yellow; color: inherit;"><b>background</b></mark></h3>
<ul>
<li>The Tathāgata was residing in the Nīgrodha monastery in the city of Kapilavattu in the region of Sakka.  During this time, the Chabbaggiya monks were in the habit of visiting the Chabbaggiya bhikkhunīs at  their residential quarters (bhikkhunupassaya) and offering advice (ovāda). One day, the rest of the  bhikkhunīs called on the six types of bhikkhunīs to go up to the bhikkhus to obtain advice. Then the  Chabbaggiya bhikkhunīs informed them that the Chabbaggiya monks come to them to offer advice. The  bhikkhunīs then informed this matter to the bhikkhus. The Tathāgata who came to know of this incident  from the bhikkhus, declared the bhikkhunupassaya training rule.  Once when Venerable Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī fell ill, bhikkhus came to see her. She requested the  bhikkhus to preach the Dhamma to her as she was sick. However, the bhikkhus refused to do so due to  this training rule. The Buddha also visited Venerable Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī to inquire after her. In this  instance she said to the Buddha, “Earlier it was very easy for me as bhikkhus who visited preached the  Dhamma to me, but now they do not do so saying that such acts have been prohibited by the Buddha”.  Thereafter, based on this incident, the Tathāgata declared a sub-charter to the training rule and amended  it, allowing bhikkhus to visit bhikkhunīs at their residential quarters when they are sick, to offer advice.   </li>

</b>If any bhikkhu should knowingly eat alms-food which a bhikkhunī has caused to be prepared, except through previous arrangement of householders, [this is a case] involving expiation.    
                    </h3><ul>
<li>vocab: #[[<mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: red;"><b>pāḷi</b></mark> vocab (2nd year)]] 
                            <ul>
<li>samārambha [saṃ + ā + √rabh + a + a] masc.→initiative; undertaking; activity</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: yellow; color: inherit;"><b>background</b></mark></h3>
<ul>
<li>The Tathāgata was residing in the Veḷuvana monastery in the city of Rājagaha. During this time, the  Bhiikkhunī named Venerable Thullanandā was in the habit of visiting a certain household for meals.  One day, the head of the house invited bhikkhus for a meal. Venerable Thullanandā, having visited the  house that morning was informed of this donation of food, and she inquired about the bhikkhus who  had been invited. Then the head of the house replied with a list of names of the bhikkhus: Sāriputta,  Mahāmoggallāna, Mahākaccāna, Mahākotthita, Mahākappina, Mahācunda, Anuruddha, Revata, Upāli,  Ānanda and Rāhula. Then Venerable Thullanandā asked why he invited such worthless monks (ceṭaka)  when he could invite great men (mahānāga). The man then asked who these mahānāgas are, and to this  Venerable Thullanandā replied saying, “Venerable Devadatta, Venerable Kokālika, Venerable  Katamodakatissa, Venerable Khaṇḍadeviyāputta and Venerable Samuddadatta”. Venerable Sāriputta  and the rest of the bhikkhus arrived at the residence when this conversation was taking place. Then  Venerable Thullanandā told the head of the house, “It is true that you have invited mahānāgas”. The  man got angry and said: “You just called these bhikkhus worthless and now you are saying that they are  great men”. He immediately got her to leave his house and cancelled the regular meals he had been  offering her.   </li>
<li>The bhikkhus began criticizing Venerable Devadatta saying, “How dare Venerable Devadatta consumes  meals that bhikkhunīs had instructed lay people to prepare”. The Buddha then called Venerable  Devadatta and inquired after this matter after which he accepted the claim. The Tathāgata reprimanded  Devadatta and declared the paripācita training rule.  </li>
<li>One day, a bhikkhu who had got ordained in Rājagaha was travelling to a relative’s house. The relative  prepared dāna for him. A bhikkhunī who is frequently visiting this household, told them “Please offer  alms to the Noble monk”. Then the bhikkhu, being suspicious if the dāna had been cooked under the  instruction of that bhikkhunī, refused to accept the alms. He could not have his meal that day as he was  unable to go on piṇḍapāta as well. The Tathāgata who got to know about this incident from other  bhikkhus, redeclared the training rule including a sub-charter allowing monks to have a meal initially  organized by the lay devotees even if a nun asks the lay devotees to offer the meal to a monk.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: yellow; color: inherit;"><b>description</b></mark></h3>
<ul>
<li><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: yellow; color: inherit;"><b>bhikkhuniparipācita </b></mark>bhojana
                            <ul>
<li>a meal of one of the <b>pañca bhojanas </b> </li>
<li>prepared by a devotee who is <b>aññātaka appavārita</b> of both the bhkī and the bhk</li>
<li>initially, the devotee was <b>not intending</b> to offer or make the meal</li>
<li>the devotee only does so after a <b>bhkī praises</b> the bhk and <b>encourages</b> the devotee to give / make it saying <b>detha / karotha</b> </li>
<li>note:
                                    <ul>
<li><b>Bhikkhunī nāma ubhatosaṅghe upasampannā. Paripāceti nāma pubbe adātukāmānaṃ akattukāmānaṃ – "ayyo bhāṇako, ayyo bahussuto, ayyo suttantiko, ayyo vinayadharo, ayyo dhammakathiko, detha ayyassa, karotha ayyassā"ti esā paripāceti nāma.   </b> </li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: yellow; color: inherit;"><b>gihisamārambha </b></mark>bhojana

<li>a meal of one of the <b>five bhojanas</b> that is prepared for a bhk by a devotee <b>before a bhkī gets involved</b> in the preparation</li>
<li>meals of <b>ñātaka</b> and <b>pavārita</b> individuals are gihisamārambha even before being cooked - they are considered as if they have begun to be cooked - and can be requested and arranged as desired: "<b>Gihisamārambho nāma ñātakā vā honti pavāritā vā pakatipaṭiyattaṃ vā"</b>

<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: yellow; color: inherit;"><b>background</b></mark></h3>
<ul>
<li>The Tathāgata was residing in the Jetavana monastery near the city of Sāvatti. During this time,  Venerable Udāyī’s lay-time wife was also ordained and was in the habit of visiting him. He too was  constantly visiting her. Venerable Udāyī even spent time with this bhikkhunī alone in secrecy. The  Tathāgata who got to know about this matter from other bhikkhus, imposed the rahonisajjana training  rule.  </li>
</ul>
