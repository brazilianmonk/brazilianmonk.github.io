<h1>
<mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: yellow; color: inherit;"><b>Adinnādāna</b></mark><b>sikkhāpadaṁ</b>  #<mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>editing</b></mark>
</h1>
<br/>
<ul>
<li><h2 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: yellow; color: inherit;"><b>background</b></mark></h2>
<ul>
<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;">while the Buddha was residing in the city of <b>Rājagaha</b>, the King Bimbisāra of Magadha issued a statement saying that all timbre in the forests of his kingdom could be used by the monks.  Ven. Dhaniya who then had built several kuṭis for himself that had been subsequently destroyed, (did what?) used the statement to steal timber from the king's reserves. The Buddha then declared the second prjk and set the value of the object of prjk to be 5 pādas or more.

A group of chabbaggiya bhikkhus (did what?) stole cloth from a forest area and the Buddha amended the rule adding "gāmā vā araññā vā" to include both village and forest.</h3></li>
<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;">attitude of a <b>judging</b> bhikkhu
                    </h3><ul>
<li>a judging bhikkhu should always consider the monk as <b>innocent</b> until proven guilty and should try to find righteous ways his innocence is proven. If concluded as being guilty the offender should be sent to several vinaya teachers for their opinion and given a meditation subject before taking conclusions. The conclusion should be done by the offender himself.</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><h2 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: yellow; color: inherit;"><b>explanation of pāḷi words</b></mark></h2>
<ul>
<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><b>"adinnaṃ theyyasaṅkhātaṃ ādiyeyya"</b></h3>
<ul>
<li><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: yellow; color: inherit;"><b>adinna</b></mark> (not given)
                            <ul>
<li>according to doors:
not <b>given </b>(physically or verbally) or
not <b>discarded </b>or forsaken (physically, verbally or mentally)</li>
<li><b>child example</b>: a child inherits his inheritance but does not know. Is that propery adinna or not?→since by law he is the owner and he hasn't given it up it is adinna</li>
<li>5 characteristics of <mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: yellow; color: inherit;"><b>adinna</b></mark><b> </b>(not given)
                                    <ul>
<li><b>anissaṭṭha</b> (not relinquished)</li>
<li><b>apariccatta</b> (not abandoned)</li>
<li><b>rakkhita</b> (protected)</li>
<li><b>gopita</b> (guarded)</li>
<li><b>mamāyita</b> (something thought of as mine)</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><b>stealing</b> means taking something that belongs to another, knowing that it does not belong to oneself, without the owner’s consent. The group of minds that perform this act is called <mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: yellow; color: inherit;"><b>theyyasaṅkhāta</b></mark> </li>
<li><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: yellow; color: inherit;"><b>ādiyeyya</b></mark> is a verb that means if taken / should take </li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><b>"yathārūpe adinnādāne…tathārūpaṃ bhikkhu adinnaṃ ādiyamāno"</b></h3>
<ul>
<li>this clause refers to the value of the object of pārājika which is one pāda or more </li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: yellow; color: inherit;"><b>avaharaṇacitta</b></mark> (stealing mind) is→the thought of taking ownership of an object that one perceives to belong to another, not one's own, not discarded, not taking on trust or temporarily, without the consent of the owner
                    </h3><ul>
<li>4 situations in which the avaharaṇacitta <b>does not arise</b>
<ul>
<li><b>sakkasaññā</b> (one perceives the object as belonging to oneself)</li>
<li><b>paṁsukūlasaññā</b> (one perceives the object as discarded)</li>
<li><b>vissāsaggāhī</b> (one takes the object out of trust on the owner)</li>
<li><b>tāvakālikasaññā</b> (one takes the object temporarily)</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: yellow; color: inherit;"><b>pāda</b></mark>  #<mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>editing</b></mark></h3>
<ul>
<li>value for prjk:<b> “</b><b>pādaṃ vā pādārahaṃ vā atirekapādaṃ vā</b><b>”</b> </li>
<li><b>nīlakahapaṇa</b>
<ul>
<li>it is called nīlakahāpaṇa, not because it is blue (nīla) in color, but because it has been made without defects through ancient sciences  </li>
<li>this <b>kahāpaṇa</b> is described in the Vimativinodanī Tīkā as: “<i>Porāṇasatthānurūpaṃ uppādito vīsatimāsappamāṇauttama-suvaṇṇagghanakelakkhaṇa-sampanno nīlakahāpaṇo</i>” which means a gold coin that weighs 20 mañcāḍi (a seed used as measurement) and its composition is not described 
                                    <ul>
<li>1 <b>mañcāḍi</b> = 1 madaṭiya = 8 rice grains (viyaṭa in SL)</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>1 <b>pāda</b> = 1/4 <b>nīlakahapana</b> of Rājagaha = 5 <b>māsaka</b> of gold 🥇 </li>
<li>1 <b>māsaka</b> = 1 <b>mañcāḍi </b>=  the weight of <b>8</b> rice grains (of gold)</li>
<li>1 <b>pāda</b> = 5 māsaka = the weight of <b>40</b> rice grains (of gold)</li>
<li>1 <b>kahāpana</b> = 4 <b>pāda</b> = 20 <b>māsaka</b> of gold
                            <ul>
<li>according to this assumption one pāda equals five māsaka/mañcāḍi of gold</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>2 <b>main interpretations</b> +2 others
                            <ul>
<li>1st interpretation: <mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: yellow; color: inherit;"><b>a coin of pure gold</b></mark>
<ul>
<li>1 <b>kahāpana</b> = 20 <b>mañcāḍi</b> ((a seed used as measurement of weight) that were used in the city of Rājagaha)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>2nd interpretation (more accepted): <mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: yellow; color: inherit;"><b>mixture of metals</b></mark>
<ul>
<li>1 <b>kahāpana</b> = 5 <b>māsaka</b> of gold, 5 of silver, 10 of copper, 1 <b>rice grain</b> of iron (for strengthening)

1 <b>kahāpana</b> = 40 <b>rice grains</b> of gold, 40 of silver, 80 of copper and 1 of iron

1 <b>pāda</b> = 1/4 kahāpana = 10 rice grain of gold, 10 silver, 20 copper, 1/4 iron</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>other ideas: 
                                    <ul>
<li>the value of the coin differs from its value as a currency (it is decided by the government)</li>
<li>according to what the local government decides as a minimum for punishment</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
