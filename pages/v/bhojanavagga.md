<li><h2 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>introduction</b></mark></h2>

<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;">4 + 1 <mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>kālika vibhāga </b></mark>(classification related to time) - purpose and period
                    </h3><ul>
<li><b>yāva</b>kālika↔edibles consumed for the purpose of <b>āhārakicca</b> that can be accepted and consumed from <b>dawn</b> till the <b>end of noon</b> (yāva majjhaṇhikā) of the same day - <b>Aruṇuggamanato yāva ṭhitamajjhanhikā bhuñjitabbato yāvakālikaṃ</b>
<ul>
<li><b>yāma</b>kālika↔essences of certain yāvakālikas consumed for <b>pipāsattha</b> from <b>dawn</b> till the <b>end of the last watch</b> of the night - <b>Aruṇuggamanato yāva yāmāvasānā pipāsāya sati pipāsacchedanatthaṃ pātabbato yāmo kālo assāti.</b> (Khs.Mls. 111.) 
                                    <ul>
<li>for bhesajjattha also seems ok</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><b>sattāha</b>kālika↔edibles that perform the āhārakicca but are not gross food, that are recognized conventionally as medicine and can be consumed for <b>bhesajjattha </b>for a period of <b>seven days</b> - "<b>Tena upasametabbe ābādhe sati yāva sattāhā paribhuñjitabbato sattāhakālikaṃ." </b>(Khs.Mls. 111.).  
                                    <ul>
<li><b>notes</b>:
                                            <ul>
<li>was originally food, became yāvajīvika, and then sattāhakālika</li>
<li>oil extracted from <b>yāvajīvika</b> is also sattāhakālika</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><b>yāvajīvika</b>↔<b> </b>edibles that do not perform āhārakicca, do not fall under other categories, and are consumed for <b>bhesajjattha </b>that can be kept for life (yāvajīvaṁ)‒<b>Ābādhe sati yāvajīvaṃ pariharitvā bhuñjitabbato yāvajīvikaṃ</b>.  </li>
<li><b>akālika</b>↔water - it does not fall under any of the above categories; can be consumed as long as it exists</li>
<li><b>notes</b>: 
                            <ul>
<li>yāvakālika, yāvajīvika and akālika are the first three main categories</li>
<li>5 <b>yāma </b>(watches) 
                                    <ul>
<li>from <b>dawn</b> to <b>noon</b> </li>
<li>from <b>noon</b> to <b>sunset</b> </li>
<li>3 watches of the night
                                            <ul>
<li>from 6 pm to 10 pm</li>
<li>from 10 pm to 2 am</li>

<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>ajjhoharaṇīya</b></mark>↔edible / consumable items - that can be consumed internally #\[\[<mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: red;"><b>pāḷi</b></mark> vocab (2nd year)]] </h3></li>
<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>time periods</b></mark></h3>
<ul>
<li><b>kāla </b>/ <b>purebhatta</b> - from <b>dawn</b> to the <b>end of noon</b> (noon included) - 2 parts
                            <ul>
<li><b>pubbaṇha</b> – from <b>dawn</b> till <b>the beginning</b> of the ṭhitamajjhaṇha</li>
<li><b>ṭithamajjhaṇha / majjhaṇha</b> – the noon; the period in which the sun is at the peak of its path in the sky
                                    <ul>
<li>note: in dhammasanganī a mahathera is said to have eaten a whole meal at this time, giving the impression that it is a (short) period of time, not a brief moment</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><b>extra notes</b>:
                                    <ul>
<li><b>purebhatta</b> has various meanings according to the context it is used but in general, purebhatta is the time <b>before pacchābhatta - </b>4 types of <b>pacchābhatta</b>
<ul>
<li>from the end of the meal till the end of noon (purebhattaṃ vā pacchābhattaṃ vā kulesu cārittaṃ āpajjeyya)</li>
<li>from the end of the meal till the dawn of the following day (pacchābhattaṃ  piṇḍapātapaṭikkanto)</li>
<li><b>vikāla</b> (Ettha madhukapuppharaso aggipāko vā hotu ādiccapāko vā, pacchābhattaṃ na vaṭṭati)   </li>
<li>after the end of noon till the sunset (Pacchābhattaṃ nāma majjhanhike vītivatte yāva atthaṅgate sūriye).   </li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><b>vikāla (/pacchābhatta / aparaṇha)</b> – from the <b>end of the noon</b> till the <b>break of the next dawn</b>
<ul>
<li><b>suriyuggamana</b>↔sunrise</li>
<li><b>aruṇuggamana</b>↔the break of dawn </li>
<li><b>suriyatthaṅgamana</b>↔sunset</li>
<li><b>notes</b>:
                                    <ul>
<li>how time was calculated: using columns (<b>kālatambha</b>) and other signs</li>
<li>“<b>Jambudīpassa kālena paricchedoti evaṃ kira”</b>. (4Kṅk-pu-ṭ. 93.) some say that kāla applies to the time according to Jambudīpa, which implies that consuming food in Uttarakurudīpa during kāla in Jambudīpa is deemed acceptable, even though it is believed to be vikāla there</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><b>yāma‒</b>from one <b>break of dawn</b> to the next</li>
</ul>

<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>kālika</b></mark></h3>
<ul>
<li>how to determine whether an edible is <b>yāvakālika or yāvajīvika</b>? 
                            <ul>
<li>(vnysgh-a) "...<b>yaṃ kiñci mūlaṃ tesu tesu janapadesu pakatiāhāravasena manussānaṃ khādanīyatthaṃ bhojanīyatthañca pharati, taṃ yāvakālikaṃ, itaraṃ yāvajīvikanti veditabbaṁ.</b>"
if it performs the conventional āhārakicca for humans in a particular region, it should be considered a yāvakālika in that region - if not, yāvajīvika
                                    <ul>
<li>pharati \[√phar + a + ti] 2↔pr. (+acc) serves (as); is useful (as) </li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>vnylkr: some edibles may be yāvakālika in one region and yāvajīvika in another - that is due to the differences in the types of nutrients in the soil (bhūmirasa/paṭhavirasa)</li>
<li>srtdpn: because in one region it may serve as food, while in others, is may not suit as food</li>
<li><b>note</b>: some teachers are of the opinion that if a certain food perform āhārakicca for humans in a certain region, it must be taken as yāvakālika in all other regions
                                    <ul>
<li>"<b>neva pharanti na nipphādenti. Ekasmiṃ dese āhārakiccaṃ sādhentaṃ vā asādhentaṃ vā aparasmiṃ dese uṭṭhitabhūmirasādibhedena āhārajighacchāharaṇakiccaṃ asādhentampi vā sambhaveyyāti āha "tesu tesu janapadesū"tiādi." (Vlṅk-ṭ. I. 187.).  </b>
</li>
<li><b>Tesu tesu janapadesūti ettha ekasmiṃ janapade āhārakiccaṃ sādhentaṃ sesajanapadesupi na kappatī"ti vadanti. (Srd-ṭ. III. 65.).  </b> </li>
<li><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>vocab:</b></mark>  #\[\[<mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: red;"><b>pāḷi</b></mark> vocab (2nd year)]] 
                                            <ul>
<li>bhūmirasa / paṭhavirasa↔nutrients of the soil</li>
<li>kappati \[√kapp + a + ti]↔pr. (+dat) it is suitable (for); it is proper (for); it is fitting (for); it is allowable </li>
<li>sādhenta \[√sādh + \*e + nta]↔prp. (+acc) successfully accomplishing; concluding; proving  2 prp. accomplishing, preparing</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<ul>
<li>what is their main <b>characteristic</b>?
                                            <ul>
<li>they are conventionally consumed for āhāratthāya for certain benefits of food
                                                    <ul>
<li><b>Khādanīyatthaṃ bhojanīyatthañca pharaṇābhāvoyeva pana tesaṃ lakkhaṇaṃ. Tasmā yaṃkiñci mūlaṃ tesu tesu janapadesu pakatiāhāravasena manussānaṃ khādanīyatthaṃ  bhojanīyatthañca pharati, taṃ yāvakālikaṃ, itaraṃ yāvajīvikanti veditabbaṃ. Tesu bahuṃ vatvāpi hi imasmiṃyeva lakkhaṇe ṭhātabbaṃ. Nāmasaññāsu pana vuccamānāsu taṃ taṃ nāmaṃ ajānantānaṃ sammohoyeva hoti, tasmā nāmasaññāya ādaraṃ akatvā lakkhaṇameva dassitaṃ. (V-a. III. 99.).    </b> </li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<li>is it an offense to consume it for <b>rasatthāya</b> (for the taste), for <b>davatthāya </b>(for fun) or to satisfy the desires of the donors?

<li>what happens if it is <b>kept</b> after <b>midday</b>? and if then <b>consumed</b>? what if it is kept <b>overnight</b>? and if then <b>consumed</b>?

<li>if kept after <b>midday</b>, nothing happens, but if consumed during the vikāla, it entails a <mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>pct
</b></mark>if kept <b>overnight</b>, it becomes <mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>sannidhi</b></mark>, which is not an offense, but if consumed, it entails a <mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>pct</b></mark> </li>

<li>Buddha in Jetavana; Kāṇamātā offered her daughter in marriage; she came to visit; Kāṇamātā made cakes as gifts; a monk came and she gave him the cake; he informed another and he also came; she gave more cakes; 3rd time the same; cakes were finished; again she made cakes, again they were offered; again the same for the 3rd time; Kāṇā's husband, tired of waiting for her, married another girl; Tathāgata came, taught the dhamma and both became sotāpannas
a monk approached a caravan for alms; he was given sattu; he informed another monk who also came and got sattu; a third time it happened; the upāsaka prepared more sattu and missed the caravan; travelling alone he got robbed; people criticized; Tathāgata laid the rule</li>

<li><h2 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>paṭhamapavāraṇā</b></mark><b>s</b><b>ikkhāpadaṃ</b></h2>
<ul>
<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>background</b></mark></h3>
<ul>
<li>Buddha in Jtvn; brahmin offered alms; bhikkhus later went to their families and ate again; other bhikkhus went piṇḍapāta; brahmin asked neighbors to give alms; they informed him that the first set of monks had again eaten in their houses; brahmin criticized; Tathāgata laid the rule
bhikkhus disposed of gilānātiritta because of the rule; Tathāgata allowed</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><h3 data-toc-injected="true" style="display:inline; font-size:inherit; font-weight:inherit; font-style:inherit; font-family:inherit; color:inherit; margin:0; padding:0; line-height:inherit;"><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>description</b></mark></h3>
<ul>
<li><b>reason</b> for the rule
                            <ul>
<li>to foster contentment with whatever is received, and to prevent offending donors by rejecting their food and looking for better food</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><b>bhojana pavāraṇā</b>
<ul>
<li>2 meanings of <b>pavāraṇā</b>
<ul>
<li>invitation ("saṅghaṁ bhante pavāremi diṭṭhena vā sutena vā")</li>
<li>refusal (yo pana bhikkhu bhuttāvī pavārito): the act of refusing one of the pañca bhojanas offered at his hatthapāsa after having started eating pañcabhojana</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>after having started eating, refusing a bhojana offered within hatthapāsa: <mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>one becomes pavārita, but there is no offense</b></mark> 
accepting food afterwards while still in the same iriyāpatha: <mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>anāpatti</b></mark>
consuming it: <mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>anāpatti</b></mark>
accepting food intending to consume it after having changed the iriyāpatha: <mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>dkṭ</b></mark>
consuming it: <mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>pc for every swallow</b></mark>
</li>
<li>rejecting foods other than pañcabhojana: <mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>not pavāraṇā</b></mark> 
rejecting unallowable foods: <mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>not pavāraṇa</b></mark> </li>
</ul>
</li>

<li><b>5 factors</b> of pavāraṇā‒<b>Pañcahi upāli ākārehi pavāraṇā paññāyati – </b>
<ul>
<li><b>asanaṃ paññāyati: </b>having swallowed even a tiny bit‒chewed or not‒of one of the pañcabhojana; from then onwards, one is called <b>bhuttāvī </b>(one who has eaten)</li>
<li><b>bhojanaṃ paññāyati: </b>having bhojana to be consumed in the next minutes in one's mouth, hands, or bowl, and intending to eat it (<b>vippakatabhojano</b>)
                                    <ul>
<li>what if one has bhojana, but intends to eat it somewhere else? is he vippakatabhojana?
                                            <ul>
<li>no</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>one has food on the hand or bowl, but not in the mouth, and intends to go somewhere else to consume it, give it away, or dispose of it, and rejects bhojana: (pavārita or not?): <mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>no</b></mark> 
another has food in the mouth, but not in the bowl or on the hands, intends not to swallow the food, but spit it out, and refuses bhojana: (pavārita or not?): <mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;">no</mark> </li>
</ul>

<li><b>hatthapāse ṭhito hoti:</b> the donor being within hatthapāsa (2 1/2 hattha)

<li>how to measure it?

<li><b>hatthapāse ṭhitoti ettha pana 
sace bhikkhu </b><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>nisinno </b></mark>(if the monk is sitting)<b> hoti: āsanassa pacchimantato paṭṭhāya</b> (measured starting from the back of the monk)</li>
<li><b>sace </b><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>ṭhito </b></mark>(standing)<b>:</b><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b> </b></mark><b>paṇhiantato paṭṭhāya</b> (from the back of the heel of the monk)</li>
<li><b>sace </b><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>nipanno </b></mark>(lying):<b> yena passena nipanno, tassa pārimantato paṭṭhāya</b> (from the end of his back)</li>
<li><b>dāyakassa nisinnassa vā ṭhitassa vā nipannassa vā ṭhapetvā pasāritahatthaṃ </b>(except the outstretched arm of the donor offering food)<b> yaṃ āsannataraṃ aṅgaṃ </b>(the closest limb)<b>, tassa orimantena </b>(to the monk)<b> paricchinditvā </b>(having delimited)<b> aḍḍhateyyahattho </b>(2 1/2 hattha)<b> “hatthapāso”ti veditabbo. </b> </li>

<li><b>if the donor is in a higher place,</b> like a tree, and the monk is on the ground, the hatthapāsa must be measured from the monk’s head; the closest body part of the donor, except the offering arm, must be within hatthapāsa</li>

<li><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>after pavāraṇā</b></mark>
<ul>
<li>3 ways a monk could eat yāvakālika after pavāraṇā till noon / majjhaṇha of that day
                                    <ul>
<li>eating‒even newly offered food‒<b>without changing the posture</b> (iriyāpatha) in which pavāraṇā occurred
                                            <ul>
<li>5 iriyāpathas here
                                                    <ul>
<li>sitting, standing, walking, lying and <b>squatting</b> </li>
<li><b>note: </b>changing directions does not break the posture; while lying, one may roll in the same location, but not to another location; a short stool may be given to one squatting</li>
</ul>
</li>

<li>eating <b>vinayakammātiritta</b> </li>
<li>eating <b>gilānātiritta</b> </li>

<li><b>anatiritta</b> bhojana↔non-leftover yāvakālika</li>
<li><b>atiritta</b> bhojana↔leftover yāvakālika‒either vinayakammātiritta or gilānātiritta (not leftover food that remains after dāna)
                                    <ul>
<li><b>2 types</b> of atiritta bhojana
                                            <ul>
<li><b>vinayakammā</b>tiritta: food that is made leftover through a minor vinayakamma</li>
<li><b>gilānā</b>tiritta: leftover food from what is brought for or consumed by a sick monk</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><b>8 characteristics</b> of atiritta (7 for vinayakammātiritta and 1 for gilānātiritta)
                                            <ul>
<li><b>kappiyakataṃ hoti:</b> it must be allowable; if bhūtagāma / bījagāma, it must be be made allowable by one of the five samaṇakappa</li>
<li><b>paṭiggahitakataṃ hoti:</b> it must have been properly accepted‒the pavārita monk cannot accept it intending to eat it (dkṭ), only intending to make it atiritta (anāpatti)</li>
<li><b>hatthapāse kataṃ hoti: </b>pavārita and vinayakamma monk must be within hatthapāsa</li>
<li><b>uccāritakataṃ hoti: </b>it must be served by the pavārita monk to the monk who is going to perform the vinayakamma   </li>
<li><b>bhuttāvinā kataṃ hoti: </b>vinayakamma monk must be bhuttāvī
                                                    <ul>
<li>if he is bhuttāvī from eating his own food, it seems unnecessary for him to eat the food of the pavārita monk</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><b>bhuttāvinā pavāritena āsanā avuṭṭhitena kataṃ hoti:</b> if vinayakamma monk is also pavārita, he must not have broken his posture</li>
<li>"<b>Alametaṃ sabba</b>"<b>nti vuttaṃ hoti: </b>this must be said<b>
note: </b>2 possible meanings‒"this food is enough for me" (preferred), or "this food is enough for you"</li>
<li><b>gilānātirittaṃ hoti: </b>(definition) </li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><b>atiritta </b><mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>vinayakamma</b></mark>
<ul>
<li>pavārita monk takes <b>kappiya</b> <b>paṭiggahita</b> food, enters the hatthapāsa of another monk (B), and while holding it doing <b>uccārita</b>, says: "<mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>atirittaṁ karotha, bhante</b></mark>"
B takes at least a grain of rice, eats it (<b>bhuttāvī</b>), hands the bowl back, saying: "<mark remnotemark="true" style="background-color: transparent; color: yellow;"><b>alametaṁ sabbaṁ</b></mark>"
that food becomes vinayakamma atiritta
</li>
