        • musāvādasikkhāpadaṃ #edited
          • Sampajānamusāvāde pācittiyaṃ. In deliberate false speech, [there is a case] involving expiation.
          • description of the training rule
            • what is musāvāda? #[[vibhaṅga exam]]
              • the intentional act (cetanā) (1) to deceive another (2) done bodily or verbally (3) by expressing a subjectively false information (diṭṭha, suta, muta viññāta as adiṭṭhādi...or vice-versa) (4) mem aid: intention (1, 2), doors (3), content (4)
                • 2 types of lies according to attha
                  • atthabhañjaka (a lie that destroys other's benefits): these are heavier lies, for example: lying in court; such lies have never been uttered by bodhisattas
                  • anatthabhañjaka (a lie that does not destroy other's benefits): these are lighter ones; these might be committed by bodhisattas but not by ariyas
                  • note:
                    • bhañjaka↔adj. who breaks or spoils #[[pāḷi vocab (2nd year)]]
                    • bodhisattas, after the prophecy, never utter atthabhañjakamusāvāda
                    • sotāpanna doesn't commit both (acc. to Vajirabuddhi)
                    • sammappayoga / sammāpayoga↔skillful means to avoid lying while concealing information #[[pāḷi vocab (2nd year)]]
            • this is a (sacittaka / acittaka?) sacittaka (saññāvimokkha / nosaññāvimokkha?) saññāvimokkha offence
          • instances of offenses
            • 6 types of musāvādas recorded in Vinaya
              • the pārājika offence (prjk 4 - uttarimanussadhammasikkhāpada)
                • the thullaccaya for indirectly claiming uttarimanussadhamma
              • the saṅghadisesa offences (sghds 8 and 9 - paṭhama and dutiya duṭṭhādosasikkhāpada)
              • the pācittiya offence (pc 1): all other lies
              • the pācittiya offence that occurs when groundlessly accusing another monk of a sghds offence (pc 76)
                • extra ones (TBC): #q🙋🏼‍♂
                  • Pc 24: wrongfully accusing a properly authorized bhikkhu of exhorting bhikkhunīs for worldly gain.
                  • Pc 73: feigning ignorance of a pāṭimokkha rule that you broke.
                  • need to check in more detail, maybe it could be done lying or not
                • the dukkaṭa offence that occurs when groundlessly accusing a bhikkhu of committing a minor offence (offences other than pārājikā and saṅghādisesa)
              • note:
                • keeping silence: dkṭ offense called musāvāda, but not an actual musāvāda it applies during the pātimokkha recitation (and aññāvādaka vihesaka pācittiya, though the latter is not classified as musāvāda) note: one should not interrupt the pātimokkha recitation - if an offense is recalled one should report it to a nearby monk and confess it later #q🙋🏼‍♂
                • bmc1: when a lie would come under another rule (e.g., Pr 4; Sg 8 & 9; Pc 13, 24, 76), the penalties assigned by that rule take precedence over the ones assigned by this rule #q🙋🏼‍♂
            • saying a lie in jest: pc
          • non-offense
            • instances where a lie does not occur
              • davā bhaṇati / sahasā bhaṇati: without having investigated or considered, one says something in haste
              • ravā bhaṇati: while intending to say one thing, accidentaly saying something else, such as saying "cīraṁ" instead of "cīvaraṁ"
          • factors
            • sampajāna: intention to deceive another
            • musāvāde: expressing the lie bodily or verbally to another
            • note:
              • vijānana: the listener's understanding is not a mitigating factor (for upasampanna)
                • for sāmaṇera, understanding is necessary for the sīla to be broken
                • for upasampanna, the other does not need to understand it
                • for kammapatha, the other must be deceived by it (acc. to bhāsaṭīkā)
        • omasavādasikkhāpadaṁ #edited
          • Omasavāde pācittiyaṃ.
          • description
            • what is omasavāda? #[[vibhaṅga exam]]
              • it is the intentional act (cetanā) rooted in anger (1) of belittling (omasati), insulting (khuṁseti) or despises (vambheti) another (2) done bodily or verbally (3) by using one of the ten akkosa vatthu (4)
            • 3 types of omasavāda:
              • omasati - belittles, lit. rubs down
              • khuṃseti - insults
              • vambheti - despises
            • 10 akkosavatthu #[[vibhaṅga exam]]
              • jāti (social class) - example of hīna / ukkaṭṭha
                • hīna: caṇḍāla
                • ukkaṭṭha: khattiya
              • nāma (name) - example of hīna / ukkaṭṭha
                • hīna: avakaṇṇaka (droppy ear)
                • ukkaṭṭha: buddhappaṭisaṁyutta
              • gotta (tribe, family, clan) - example of hīna / ukkaṭṭha
                • hīna: kosiya
                • ukkaṭṭha: gotama
              • kamma (occupation, job) - example of hīna / ukkaṭṭha
                • hīna: pupphachaḍḍakakamma (toilet cleaner)
                • ukkaṭṭha: kasi
              • sippa (craft) - example of hīna / ukkaṭṭha
                • hīna: naḷakārasippa
                • ukkaṭṭha: gaṇanā
              • ābādha (ailments) - example of hīna / ukkaṭṭha
                • hīna: sabbe ābādha
                • ukkaṭṭha: madhumeha
              • liṅga (physical features, gender) - example of hīna / ukkaṭṭha
                • hīna: atidīgha, atirassa, atikaṇha, accodāta
                • ukkaṭṭha: nātidīgha, nātirassa, nātikaṇha, nāccodāta
              • kilesa (defilements) - example of hīna / ukkaṭṭha
                • hīna: sabbe kilesā
              • āpatti (offences / attainments) - example of hīna / ukkaṭṭha
                • hīna: sabbā āpattiyo
                • ukkaṭṭha: sotāpattisamāpatti
              • akkosa (insults) - example of hīna / ukkaṭṭha
                • hīna: goṇosi
                • ukkaṭṭha: paṇḍitosi
              • note: #[[vibhaṅga exam]]
                • hīna: those that are belittled, humiliated, disgusted, indulged and disrespected
                • ukkuṭṭha: names etc. that are not belittled, not humiliated, not disgusted, not indulged and not disrespected
                • note:
              • 40 omāsavāda offences #[[vibhaṅga exam]]
                • low ⇒ low (insulting a caṇḍāla as caṇḍāla)
                  • insulting a bhikkhu of a lower social class with a lower social class topic
                • low ⇒ high (insulting a caṇḍāla as khattiya)

                • high ⇒ low (insulting a khattiya as caṇḍāla)
                  • insulting a bhikkhu of a higher social class with a lower social class topic
                • high ⇒ high (insulting a khattiya as khattiya)
                  • nsulting a bhikkhu of a higher social class with a higher social class topic

              • this is a (sacittaka / acittaka) sacittaka, (saññāvimokkha / nosaññāvimokkha) saññāvimokkha offence

              • directly offending a bhikkhu ⇒ pct doing so indirectly, saying "someone here is a caṇḍāla" ⇒ dkṭ directly offending an anupasampanna (including bhikkhunīs) ⇒ dkṭ doing so indirectly ⇒ dkṭ doing so to an upa / anupa in jest ⇒ dbhs

        • pesuññasikkhāpadaṁ #edited
          • Bhikkhu pesuññe pācittiyaṃ. In the backbiting of a bhikkhu, [there is a case] involving expiation
            • vocab: #[[pāḷi vocab (2nd year)]]
              • pesuñña [√pis + uṇa + *ya] nt.→divisive speech; slander; defamation; denigration; back-biting
          • description
            • what is pisuṇāvācā?→it is the slanderous speech that disrupts courteous relationships between others causing quarrels and division - A belittles (omasavāda) B, and C, intending to slander A, informs B about it
            • 2 objectives behind slandering
              • bhedanādhippāya: intention to break trust between parties by undermining or damaging positive relationships
              • piyakamyatā: intention to manipulate others into extending pleasant interactions or favorable treatment (gaining favors)
          • perception and intention
            • this is a (sacittaka / acittaka?) sacittaka (saññāvimokkha / nosaññāvimokkha?) saññāvimokkha offence
          • instances of offences
            • A does omasavāda towards B to C, and C, intending to slander A, informs B about it or A does omasavāda towards B (by himself or to anyone), C gets to know it and, intending to slander A, informs B about it (C is always upa since this is a rule for upa) if A, B and C were upa: pc saying 1 divisive word: 1 pc saying 2 divisive words: 2 pc if B were a bhī: dkṭ (bhī are anupa here) if B were an anupa: dkṭ if A and B were anupa: dkṭ (if there were anupa involved it is dkṭ) informing while intending to look down (pāpagarahitāya): anāpatti
          • anāpatti
            • na bhedādhippāyena na piyakamyatāya
            • pāpagarahitā vadantassa
            • ummattakādi
          • aṅgāni

        • padasodhammasikkhāpadam #edited
          • Yo pana bhikkhu anusampannaṁ padaso dhammaṁ vāceyya pācittiyam. If any bhikkhu should have one who has not been fully admitted recite the Dhamma [line] by line, [this is a case] involving expiation.
          • background
            • Buddha in Jetavana, Sāvatthi chabbaggiyas were reciting the Dhamma together with upāsakas and the upāsakas started to not be respectful, not be humble and to be malicious towards bhikkhus this lead to the promulgation of the rule
          • description
            • 'Dhamma', according to this training rule, refers to teachings that are recorded in the Tipiṭaka and were expounded either by
              • buddhabhāsita: the Buddha
              • sāvakabhāsita: his disciples
              • isibhāsita: by hermits
              • devatā bhāsita: by devas
              • (original) aṭṭhakathā: explanations found in commentaries; the modern aṭṭhakathā, though not the original aṭṭhakathā, is still included as so for containing the essence of the original
                • the aṭṭhakathā in pāḷi was translated into sinhala and then again translated back into pāḷi and summarized
                • when abhayagiri started teaching dhamma mixed with other teachings, the aṭṭhakathā in pāḷi was translated into sinhalese in order to preserve it
            • pada is fourfold as follows
              • pada: the 1st word (ending with a case ending [vibhattyantaṁ padaṁ]) of a phrase or of a sentence, or the first line of a verse
              • anupada: the 2nd word of a phrase or of a sentence, or the 2nd line of a verse
                • what about the third? #q🙋🏼‍♂
              • anubyañjana: a group of letters which are either not a complete stem like the morpheme ‘pubbaṅ’ of the word ‘pubbaṅgamā’, or a stem - word without the case ending - like ‘pubbaṅgama’
              • anvakkhara: a single letter or syllable
            • note:
              • English terms for verses according to their length:
                • 1 line: monostich
                • 2 lines: couplet
                • 3 lines: tercet / triplet
                • 4 lines: quatrain
                • 5 lines: quintain (kwĭn′tən) / cinquain (sĭng′kān′, săng′-)
                • 6 lines: sestet (sĕ-stĕt′) / hexastich (ˈhɛksəˌstɪk)
                • 7 lines: septet / septette (sĕp-tĕt′) / heptastich
                • 8 lines: octave
                • 9 lines: spenserian
                • 10 lines: dizain (dɪˈzeɪn) / rondeau
                • 12 lines : roundeau prime
                • 13 lines: roundel / rondeau (rŏn′dō, rŏn-dō′)
                • 14 lines: sonnet (sŏn′ĭt)
            • what is the purpose of this rule?→to avoid situations in which the anupa may know a text the upa does not, where the anupa's respect for the upa may diminish
          • instances of offenses

        • paṭhamasahaseyyasikkhāpadaṃ #edited
          • Yo pana bhikkhu anupasampannena uttariṃ dirattatirattaṃ sahaseyyaṃ kappeyya, pācittiyaṃ. If any bhikkhu should make use of a sleeping place for more than two nights or three nights together with one who has not been fully admitted [into the bhikkhu-community], [this is a case] involving expiation.
          • background
            • Buddha: Aggālava Stupa, Ālavi bhikkhus slept in the same area as devotees after dhammatalks; devotees criticized their behavior during the night Buddha: Badarikārāma, Kosambhi Sāmaṇera Rāhula ended up without sleeping quarters during the night due to the previous ruling and slept in the Buddha's toilet; the Buddha, getting to know about it in the morning, amended the rule allowing a maximum of 3 consecutive nights
          • description

        • dutiyasahaseyyasikkhāpadaṃ #edited
          • Yo pana bhikkhu mātugāmena sahaseyyaṃ kappeyya, pācittiyaṃ. If any bhikkhu should make use of a sleeping place together with a woman, [this is a case] involving expiation.
          • background #editing
            • The Tathāgata was residing at Jetavana monastery near the city of Sāvatthi. During this time, while Anuruddha Thera was travelling from a region of the kingdom of Kosala to the city of Sāvatthi, he reached a certain village in the night. There was a rest house run by a lady in this village. Even though the Thera sought permission from her to spend the night in the rest house, she prepared seating for him inside the house and invited him to stay inside, out of desire towards the Thera. While the Thera was residing inside, the lady made many attempts to seduce him but failed since he was an Arahant. Subsequently, having been inspired and affected by great qualities of the Thera Anuruddha, the lady apologised, offered alms the next morning and listened to the Dhamma and became a lifetime follower of the Triple Gem. The Thera visited Sāvatthi and conveyed this incident to the other bhikkhus. Having known the story, the Buddha laid down the dutiya sahaseyya sikkhāpada.
          • description
            • purpose of the rule→a monk who sleeps in the same residence where a lady is sleeping may easily become target of criticism and accusations as well as there being potential dangers to a monk's celibacy due to the presence of unrestrained ladies

          • ujjhāpanake khiyyanake, pācittiyaṃ. In making [another bhikkhu] find fault, in criticising, [there is a case] involving expiation.
          • background
            • The Buddha was residing at the Veḷuvana monastery in the city of Rājagaha. During this time Venerable Dabbamallaputta Thera was preparing lodgings for the Saṅgha (senāsana paññāpaka). He was also in charge of distribution of meals (bhattuddesaka). Two monks named Mettiya and Bhummajaka, who were among the group of Chabbaggiya monks, at the time were juniors and lacked good kamma to obtain good lodgings and food. They received inferior lodgings and food of the Saṅgha. Then they got other bhikkhus to think and say, “Dabbamallaputta Thera favours when preparing quarters. He also favours, when distributing food”. Based on this incident the Buddha imposed the ujjhāpanaka training rule prohibiting monks from getting other monks to think and say bad things about officials appointed by the Saṅgha. When this rule was imposed, Mettiya and Bhummajaka bhikkhus started accusing Dabbamallaputta Thera in the presence of the other bhikkhus saying, “Dabbamallaputta Thera favours when preparing lodgings and distributing food”. Having known this, the Buddha added an amendment to this rule prohibiting monks from speaking ill about Saṅgha officials in the presence of other monks. This amendment, too, is like another training rule.
            • types of anuppaññatti
              • anāpattikara: creates an exception
              • daḷhīkaraṇa: makes it stricter
              • āpattikara: makes another offense, extends the scope of the rule (vihesaka under aññavādaka and this one)

          • Yo pana bhikkhu asammato bhikkhunīyo ovadeyya, pācittiyaṃ. If any bhikkhu who has not been authorised should exhort the bhikkhunīs, [this is a case] involving expiation.
          • background
            • The Buddha was residing in the Jetavana monastery near the city of Sāvatti. During this time, senior bhikkhus used to advise bhikkhunīs. Such bhikkhus who were advising the bhikkhunīs received the four requisites such as robes in abundance. The Chabbaggiya monks who saw this incident decided to offer advice to the bhikkhunīs. They informed the bhikkhunīs to come to them to obtain advice. Then the bhikkhunīs also went to the Cabbaaggiya bhikkhus seeking advice. However, the Cabbaggiya bhikkhus spent most of the day talking about unnecessary matters with the bhikkhunīs and did not spend a lot of time preaching the Dhamma. The Tathāgata inquired about this matter from the bhikkhunīs, and they explained the incident to him. Subsequently, the Tathāgata declared a bhikkhunovādaka sammuti (a convention of appointing a monk to advice bhikkhunīs) based on this incident as: “Anujānami, bhikkhave, bhikkhunovādakaṃ sammannitum”. Afterwards, the six types of bhikkhus continued to advise the bhikkhunīs without obtaining the convention. Based on this incident, the Tathāgata declared ovāda sikkhāpada preventing a bhikkhu who does not have the bhikkhunovādaka sammuti from advising bhikkhunīs. Then the Chabbaggiya monks went into various sīmas and declared bhikkhunovādaka sammuti amongst themselves (they gave the sammuti to each other). Thereafter, they informed the bhikkhunīs that they have now obtained the bhikkhunovādaka convention, and that bhikkhunīs can come to them once again for advice. When bhikkhunīs approached them, they continued to discuss unnecessary matters more than discussing the Dhamma, just as before.
