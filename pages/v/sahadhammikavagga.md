## Sahadhammika-sikkhāpadaṁ


<a id="orgea3eec8"></a>

### background



-   Buddha: Gositārāma, Kosambī
-   V. Channa was misbehaving
-   Other monks admonished him
-   He said he would not abide by the rules until he had inquired about it from a vinayadhara monk
-   Buddha reprimanded and laid down the rule




<a id="orge8ab0a0"></a>

### Pāḷi rule


**yo pana bhikkhu bhikkhūhi sahadhammikaṃ vuccamāno evaṃ vadeyya - ‘na tāvāhaṃ, āvuso, etasmiṃ sikkhāpade sikkhissāmi yāva na aññaṃ bhikkhuṃ vyattaṃ vinayadharaṃ paripucchāmī’ti, pācittiyaṃ.  sikkhamānena, bhikkhave, bhikkhunā aññātabbaṃ paripucchitabbaṃ paripañhitabbaṃ.  ayaṃ tattha sāmīci.**



<a id="org215788a"></a>

### description

**purpose**: to prevent disrespectful monks from not following rules with indirect statements

1.  2 types of anādariya

    
    
    -   **puggala** anādariya: disrespecting the person who admonishes
    -   **dhamma** anādariya: disrespecting the teaching
    -   **note**: in this rule, disrespect in not shown directly as in the anādariyasikkhāpada
    
    

2.  sahadhammika

    
    sahadhammikaṃ nāma **yaṃ bhagavatā paññattaṃ sikkhāpadaṃ** etaṃ sahadhammikaṃ nāma (the rules laid down by the Buddha)
	(*:q: why are the rules called so in this place? are there other places where this name is used for rules?*)
    

3.  2 main and 7 detailed basis of admonishment

    
    
    -   **paññatta**: promulgated vinaya rules
    -   **appaññatta**: non-vinaya rules
        -   **sallekhāya**: the lessening of defilements
        -   **dhūtattāya**: the shaking off of defilements
        -   **pāsādikatāya**: the becoming pleasing to others
        -   **apacayāya**: the destruction of defilements; in some places: for the destruction of the three cycles of saṃsāra
        -   **vīriyārambhāya**: the arousing of wholesome effort
        -   **ovādasikkhāpada**: facts / instructions from the Suttanta or Abhidhamma
        -   mem aid: a dog comes down a hill writing on a paper (coming down -> lessening; writing -> lekha), shakes off his dirt, people like him, (as they pet him) his hair is dispersed (apa ci), he runs away with effort, someone admonishes him (ovāda)
    
            

4.  monks included under "vinayadhara" in this rule

    a monk competent, learned in vinaya and Dhamma

5.  how to act when being thus advised

    
    one can question the background story, first offender, charter, meaning, think wisely, compare and analize it with other vinaya rules, or say "jānissāmi sikkhissāmi"
    


<a id="org6fd267b"></a>

### āpattivāra

-   when being admonished by an upa. with regard to
    -   a vinaya rule, saying one will confirm it with a vinayadhara intending not to follow it:  **pc** 
    -   **sallekhayā, dhutattāya, pāsādikatāya, apacayāya, viriyārambhāya, ovādasikkhāpada**, saying one will confirm it with a vinayadhara with such intention:  **dkṭ** 
-   when being admonished by an anupa. about a vinaya rule or the rest:  **dkṭ** 
-   asking questions about the rule:  **anāpatti** 
-   saying that one will study and practice it:  **anāpatti** 


<a id="org284300e"></a>

### anāpattivāra



-   **jānissāmi sikkhissāmī”ti bhaṇati**: without disrespect, saying one will study / follow the rule




<a id="org1abe9fd"></a>

### aṅga



-   **upasampannena paññattena vacanaṃ**: a fully ordained advising with a training rule promulgated in Vinaya
-   **asikkhitukāmatāya evaṃ vacanaṃ**: saying as aforesaid not willing to follow the rule




<a id="org390ad95"></a>

### other elements

-   **citta and saññā**: sacittaka and saññāvimokkha
-   **āpatti and its saññā**: tika-pācittiya and tika-dukkaṭa; upasampanna / anupasampanna saññā (the perception of the admonisher as upa. / anupa.)
-   **kusalattika and vedanāttika**: akusala citta and dukkha vedanā
-   **vajja**: lokavajja
-   **kiriya**: kiriya
-   **samuṭṭhāna**: kāya-citta, vācā-citta and kāya-vācā-citta
-   **kamma**: kāyakamma or vacīkamma

## Vilekhana S.


<a id="org28d263b"></a>

### Background



-   Buddha: jetavana
-   Buddha giving vinaya discourses praising vinaya, its study, and v. upāli.
-   monks began to study under him
-   chabbaggiyas became afraid of them being knowledgeable and putting them on trials and began to criticize the vinaya to discourage them saying it only brings kukkucca, vihesā (due to doubt), and vilekha
-   Buddha laid down the rule
-   comm: etāni hi ye uddisanti, uddisāpenti sajjhāyanti vā, tesaṃ tāva saṃvattanti yāva "kappati nu kho, na kappati nu kho"ti kukkuccasaṅkhāto vippaṭisāro vihesā vicikicchāsaṅkhāto manovilekho ca uppajjatiyeva




<a id="org4ec1d4b"></a>

### Rule in Pāli


**Yo pana bhikkhu pātimokkhe uddissamāne evaṃ vadeyya - ‘Kiṃ panimehi khuddānukhuddakehi  sikkhāpadehi uddiṭṭhehi, yāvadeva kukkuccāya vihesāya vilekhāya saṃvattantī’ti,  sikkhāpadavivaṇṇake, pācittiyaṃ.**
    


<a id="orgecb481e"></a>

### Description

1.  sikkhāpadavivaṇṇaka &#x2013; definition

    
    the act of criticizing the Vinaya rules mainly done with the intention of discouraging other monks from studying Vinaya
    

2.  3 occasions included in "uddissamāne"

    
    
    -   **pātimokkhaṃ uddisente**: when the pātimokkha is being recited by a teacher to his students as a means of teaching by his will
    -   **pātimokkhaṃ uddisāpente**: when doing so due to the students' request
    -   **pātimokkhaṃ sajjhāyaṃ karonte**: when doing so to memorize it
    
    

3.  comments included in the criticism of the Chabbaggiyas

    
    
    -   **kiṃ panimehi khuddānukhuddakehi sikkhāpadehi uddiṭṭhehi** (what is the benefit of teaching, learning and memorizing these minor and very minor training rules?)
    -   **yāvadeva kukkuccāya vihesāya vilekhāya saṃvattanti** (they only lead to doubt; if not taught, studied, memorized, they don't lead to doubt)
    -   **anuddiṭṭhaṁ idaṁ varaṁ** (it is best if it is not taught)
    -   **anuggahitaṁ idaṁ varaṁ** (&#x2026;not learned)
    -   **apariyāpuṭaṁ idaṃ varaṃ** (&#x2026;not mastered)
    -   **adhāritaṃ idaṃ varaṃ** (&#x2026;not memorized)
    -   **vinayo vā antaradhāyatu** (may it disappear)
    -   **ime vā bhikkhū ataññuno hontu** (may the monks not be well-versed in it)
    -   note: opinions within the vinaya are not included as criticism here
    
    

4.  benefits of studying the Vinaya &#x2013; Vinaya pariyattiyā vaṇṇo

    1.  5 merits of a vinayadhara
    
        
        
        -   **attano sīlakkhandho sugutto hoti surakkhito**: one’s own virtue is well-protected
        -   **kukkuccapakatānaṃ paṭisaraṇaṃ hoti**: he becomes a refuge for monks oppressed by vinaya doubt
        -   **visārado saṅghamajjhe voharati**: he preaches in the midst of the Saṅgha without any fear
        -   **paccatthike sahadhammena suniggahitaṃ niggaṇhāti**: he refutes individuals opposed to the monks or the sāsana with words associating Dhamma and reason
        -   **saddhammaṭṭhitiyā paṭipanno hoti**: he is someone who has undertaken the practice for the prolongation of the threefold Saddhamma
        -   **mem aid**: his own purity, refuge for others, preaches, refutes, prolongs 3 saddhamma
        
        
    
    2.  2 (5 in detail) types of paccatthika
    
        
        
        -   **atta** paccatthika:
            -   those who accused monks with baseless pārājikā offences (e.g.: Vs. Mettiya and Bhummajaka and the layman Vaḍḍha, who was a Licchavī)
            -   any monk who has committed pārājikā offence and lives an unvirtuous life
        -   **sāsana** paccatthika:
            -   monks who hold wrong views (e.g.: V. Ariṭṭha and sāmaṇera Kaṇṭhaka)
            -   monks who hold wrong opinions (e.g.: Vesālika Vajjiputtaka monks)
            -   monks who consider the teachings not taught by the Buddha as taught by the Buddha, etc. (followers of other Buddhist sects such as Mahāsaṅghikas)
        
        
    
    3.  3 saddhamma
    
        
        
        -   **pariyatti** saddhamma: tepiṭakaṃ buddhavacanaṃ
        -   **paṭipatti** saddhamma:
            -   13 dhutaguṇa (dhutaṅgas)
            -   14 khandhakavattāni
            -   82 mahāvattāni
        -   **adhigama** saddhamma:
            -   6 abhiññā
            -   4 magga and phalas (highest)
            -   3 vijjā
            -   4 paṭisambhidā
        
        **note**: the vinayadhara, when there are few virtuous monks in the world, could properly ordain monks (outside majjhadesa first, then inside) and then purify their virtue, keeping the 3 saddhammas
        
    
    4.  6 benefits of being a vinayadhara
    
        
        
        -   **tassādheyyo uposatho / pavāraṇā / saṅghakammaṃ / pabbajjā / upasampadā**: these kammas belong to him
        -   **nissayaṃ deti sāmaṇeraṃ upaṭṭhāpeti**: only he gives nissaya and becomes an upajjhāya for the sāmaṇeras
        
        

5.  desire connected to the offense

    
    **garahitukāmatā**: desire to criticize
    


<a id="org281e262"></a>

### Offenses

-   in the presence of another upa.
    -   criticizing the **vinaya**:  **pc** 
    -   criticizing **other teachings**:  **dkṭ** 
-   in the presence of an anupa.
    -   criticizing the vinaya / other teachings:  **dkṭ** 


<a id="orga624617"></a>

### Non-offences



-   **na vivaṇṇetukāmo "Iṅgha tvaṃ suttante vā gāthāyo vā abhidhammaṃ vā pariyāpuṇassu pacchā vinayaṃ pariyāpuṇissasī"ti bhaṇati**: without the intention to criticize the vinaya, encouraging others to first study suttas, gāthās, or Abhidhamma, and vinaya later




<a id="org7d93128"></a>

### Factors



-   **garahitukāmatā**: desire to criticize
-   **upasampannassa santike sikkhāpada-vivaṇṇanaṃ**: criticizing the vinaya rules in front of a fully ordained monk
    -   (*:q: what about the pātimokkha being recited? isn't that a factor?*)
    -   (*:q: what about the pātimokkhe uddissamāne*)




<a id="org8deb387"></a>

### Other elements

**citta and saññā**: sacittaka and saññāvimokkha

**āpatti and its saññā**: tika-pācittiya and tika-dukkaṭa; upasampanna / anupasampanna saññā (the perception that the person near whom Vinaya rules are criticized is an upa. / anupa.)

**kusalattika and vedanāttika**: akusala citta and dukkha vedanā

## Mohana S.


<a id="org662c1c0"></a>

### Background


chabbaggiyas claimed ignorance of rules to excuse past offences



<a id="org062f6e7"></a>

### Pāli Rule


**Yo pana bhikkhu anvaḍḍhamāsaṃ pātimokkhe uddissamāne evaṃ vadeyya - ‘Idāneva kho ahaṃ jānāmi, ayampi kira dhammo suttāgato suttapariyāpanno anvaḍḍhamāsaṃ uddesaṃ āgacchatī’ti. Tañce bhikkhuṃ aññe bhikkhū jāneyyum nisinnapubbaṃ iminā bhikkhunā dvattikkhattuṃ pātimokkhe uddissamāne, ko pana vādo bhiyyoti, na ca tassa bhikkhuno aññāṇakena mutti atthi, yañca tattha āpattiṃ āpanno tañca yathā dhammo kāretabbo, uttari cassa moho āropetabbo - ‘Tassa te āvuso, alābhā, tassa te dulladdhaṃ, yaṃ tvaṃ pātimokkhe uddissamāne na sādhukaṃ aṭṭhikatvā manasi karosī’ti. Idaṃ tasmiṃ mohanake, pācittiyaṃ.**
(*:q: uddissamāne is the same as the previous rule or the actual recitation of the patimokkha?*)



<a id="org08d509d"></a>

### Description

-   **purpose**: to prevent deception (mohana) regarding knowledge of vinaya
-   **summary**: Pretending not to know a training rule during pātimokkha recitation, though one has heard it before

1.  mohana &#x2013; definition

    
    
    -   **deceiving** other bhikkhus
    -   by feigning ignorance of training rules
    -   during pātimokkha
    -   after having committed an offense
    
    (*:q: is the offense necessary, or just feigning ignorance without having an offense enough here?*)
    
    
    1.  what if one truly did not understand the rule?
    
        
        if there is the intention to deceive, he is **not exempt** from the offense
        

2.  mohāropana &#x2013; definition

    
    ñattidutiyakamma to declare a bhikkhu's deception
    
    
    1.  Kammavācā &#x2013; Pāḷi
    
        
        **Suṇātu me, bhante, saṅgho. Ayaṃ itthannāmo bhikkhu pātimokkhe uddissamāne na sādhukaṃ aṭṭhiṃ katvā manasi karoti. Yadi saṅghassa pattakallaṃ, saṅgho itthannāmassa bhikkhuno mohaṃ āropeyya. Esā ñatti.**
        
        **Suṇātu me&#x2026;manasi karoti. Saṅgho itthannāmassa bhikkhuno mohaṃ āropeti. Yassāyasmato khamati itthannāmassa bhikkhuno mohassa āropanā, so tuṇhassa, yassa nakkhamati, so bhāseyya.**
        
        **Āropito saṅghena itthannāmassa bhikkhuno moho. Khamati saṅghassa, tasmā tuṇhī, evametaṃ dhārayāmi.**
        

3.  aññāṇakena mutti &#x2013; definition

    
    
    -   freedom from an offense due to not having knowledge of the training rules
    -   only applicable if one has not yet heard the vitthāruddesa 2 or 3 times (3 is taken here, so 2 is still anāpatti)
    
    


<a id="orgb980961"></a>

### Offences

-   when the pātimokkha is being recited, intentionally deceiving 
    -   without **mohāropanakamma**:  **dkṭ** 
    -   after **mohāropanakamma**:  **pc** 
    -   monks are not deceived:  irrelevant: **pc** 

1.  what if the monks are not deceived?

    
    actual deception is not needed; **intent** is enough for the offense to occur
    


<a id="orga5d7d9b"></a>

### Non-Offences



-   **na vitthārena sutaṃ hoti**: did not fully hear pātimokkha before
-   **ūnakadvattikkhattuṃ sutaṃ**: heard less than 2–3 times (3 times is taken here, so 2 times is still anāpatti)
-   **na mohetukāmassa**: no intent to deceive

   


<a id="org820eb33"></a>

### Factors



-   **mohāropanaṃ**: mohāropana vinayakamma done
-   **mohetukāmatā**: desire to deceive
-   **vuttanayena sutabhāvo**: heard at least 3 times in full (vitthāruddesa)
-   **mohanaṃ**: actual attempt to deceive




<a id="org43cc1e6"></a>

### Vocabulary

1.  mohetuṁ

    to deceive

## Pahāradāna S.


<a id="orged69a88"></a>

### Background


chabbaggiya monks hit sattarasavaggiya monks out of anger



<a id="org8a3a687"></a>

### Pāli Rule


**Yo pana bhikkhu bhikkhussa kupito anattamano pahāraṃ dadeyya, pācittiyaṃ.**



<a id="org1b39066"></a>

### Description

-   **Purpose**: To restrain physical aggression in the Saṅgha
-   **Summary of the offence**: Hitting another bhikkhu out of anger

1.  kopa &#x2013; definition

    
    **kupito anattamano**: āhatacitto khilajāto (disturbed and roughened mind)
    

2.  3 types of pahāradāna (hitting)

    
    
    -   **kāyena**: with body (e.g., hand, leg)
    -   **kāyapaṭibaddhena**: with object held in body (e.g., stick)
    -   **nissaggiyena**: thrown object (e.g., rock, even lotus petal with angry mind)
    
    


<a id="org105b69b"></a>

### Offences

-   when hitting:
    -   an upa.:  **pc** 
    -   an anupa. (laity, woman, animal, etc.):  **dkṭ** 
    -   a woman with kāyasaṁsaggarāga:  **sgds** 
    -   a woman with methunarāga:  **dkṭ** 
    -   an upa. who dies due to the strike:  **pc**, unless there was the intent to kill, in which case: **prjk** 
        -   (*:q: what about hitting trying to kill under parajika?*)
    -   intending to free oneself from harm:  **anāpatti**: mokkhādhippāya 


<a id="orgaad8018"></a>

### Non-Offences



-   **mokkhādhippāya**: hitting with the intent to escape from harm (e.g. thief, wild animal), even if the other dies




<a id="orgb2e1eb9"></a>

### Factors



-   bhikkhussa … pahāraṁ dadeyya
    -   **upasampannassa pahāradānaṁ**: striking an upa.
-   kupito anattamano
    -   **kupitattā**: presence of anger
    -   **na mokkhādhippāyatā**: not intending to free oneself from harm

  


<a id="orgf83928e"></a>

### Other elements

**Citta and Saññā**: sacittaka, saññāvimokkha

**Āpatti and its saññā**: tika-pācittiya, tika-dukkaṭa

**Vajja**: lokavajja

**Kiriya**: kiriya

**Samuṭṭhāna**: kāya-citta

**Kamma**: kāyakamma


<a id="orgacedd22"></a>

### Vocabulary

1.  āhata

    -   pp. struck; beaten; hit; whacked ā + √han + ta
    -   pp. affected; afflicted; stricken
    -   nt. application; employment; effort; lit. striking
    -   pp. brought; gathered ā + √har + ta

2.  khila

    -   masc. (+loc) hardness (towards); coldness (towards); hard-heartedness (towards) √khī + la
    -   nt. (+loc) hardness (towards); coldness (towards); hard-heartedness (towards)

## Talasattika S.


<a id="org72531f1"></a>

### Background



-   Buddha in Jetavana, Sāvatthi
-   chabbaggiyas raised their hands threateningly at sattarasavaggiya bhikkhus, making them cry




<a id="org1bea7d9"></a>

### Pāli Rule


**yo pana bhikkhu bhikkhussa kupito anattamano talasattikaṃ uggireyya, pācittiyaṃ**



<a id="org021354a"></a>

### Description

-   **Purpose**: To avoid displays of anger and violence among monks

-   **Summary of the offence**: Raising the hand or an object in a threatening gesture (without hitting), out of anger, toward a bhikkhu.

1.  talasattika &#x2013; definition

    
    
    -   **tala**: lifting kāya or kāyapaṭibaddha in a threatening manner; lit.: palm weapon, using the hand as a weapon
        -   notes: a **nissaggiya** (thrown) object is not explicitly included in this rule
            -   *:q: so pointing a gun/bow at someone would not be a pc?*
    -   **sattika**: implies potential to strike, even if the object is soft or harmless, e.g., a lotus petal
        -   some teachers: tala = talasattika &#x2013; talameva talasattikaṁ
    
    


<a id="org4f45393"></a>

### Offences

-   raising kāya / kāyapaṭibaddha with anger, implying intent to hit
    -   an upa.:  **pc** 
    -   an anupa.:  **dkṭ** 
-   when it results in an accidental hit without the intention to strike:
    -   p-a:  **dkṭ for the strike under pahāradāna**: it doesn't fall under talasattika but under pahāradāna because there was a strike (the strike brings the whole action under pahāradāna); under pahāradāna, hitting intentionally is a pc, but since there is no intention here, only a dkṭ is assigned even if it causes injury because of the improper action done with an unwholesome mind; “na paharitukāmatāya dinnattā dukkaṭaṃ"; this dkṭ appear to be added by the commentary, not in the original rule
    -   **vjrbd**: 
        -   lifting: **pc** under talasattika
        -   accidentally hitting: **dkṭ** because it results from the initial angry action
        -   disagrees with the comm.; the accidental strike should not nullify the lifting's offense
    -   **srtdpn & vmtvndn**:
        -   only **dkṭ for lifting under talasattika**; the strike is not an offense since it is unintentional


<a id="orge5137ee"></a>

### Non-Offences



-   **kenaci viheṭhīyamāno mokkhādhippāyo talasattikaṃ uggirati**: if raising the hand / object to escape physical abuse (from human or animal), even if with anger
-   notes: some teachers include acts like chasing away animals (e.g. defecating near the monastery) under this factor




<a id="org914de9f"></a>

### Factors



-   **kupitatā**: presence of anger
-   **na mokkhādhippāyatā**: no intention to escape
-   **upasampannassa talasattiuggiraṇaṃ**: gesture of hitting toward a fully ordained bhikkhu

<a id="orgf7b4e74"></a>

### Background

<a id="orgf264b29"></a>

### Description

1.  bhaṇḍanajāta &#x2013; definition

    
    
    -   **bhaṇḍana**: in a quarrel, planning with one's group to say or do this or that when the opposing group says or does this or that
    -   **bhaṇḍanajāta**: one who is involved in bhaṇḍana; one in whom bhaṇḍana has occurred; nt. blaming; accusing; conflicting √bhaṇḍ + ana
    
    

2.  kalahajāta &#x2013; definition

    
    
    -   **kalaha**: fighting either verbally or physically
    -   **kalahajāta**: one who is involved in such a fight; one in  whom a kalaha has occurred
    
    

3.  vivādāpanna &#x2013; definition

    
    
    -   **vivāda (vi + √vad) / vivādādhirakaṇa**: argument between groups about who is right and wrong
    -   **vivādāpanna**: one who is involved in (entered into) such argument
    
    

4.  upassuti &#x2013; definition

    
    
    -   **upassutiṁ tiṭṭheyya**: stands (or any other posture) where the conversation can be heard
    -   **upassuti**: standing close to the sound (upa + suti); listening in, eavesdropping, snooping
        -   **srtdpn**: favors 1st  definition based on comm: upassuti: **yattha ṭhatvā sakkā hoti tesaṃ vacanaṃ sotuṃ** (the place where one can stand and listen to their voice); aṭṭhame sutisamīpanti saddasamīpaṃ&#x2026;tatthāti evamattho gahetabbo)
        -   **some teachers**: the area closer to the area where one can stand and listen to the sound (*:q: clarify*)
    
    

5.  intention related to the offense

    
    
    -   **codanādhippāya**: the intention to
        -   **accuse them later**
            or
        -   **remind them later of what they have said**
            -   *:q: clarify*
    
    

6.  etadeva paccayaṁ karitvā anaññaṁ pācittiyaṁ &#x2013; definition

    
    
    -   **etadeva paccayaṃ karitvā … pācittiyaṃ**: it is a pc only (eva) for listening (karitvā) with codanādhippāya (etaṁ paccayaṁ)
    -   **anaññaṃ**: not in other cases &#x2013; there is no offense if not under those conditions
        -   etadeva paccayaṃ karitvā anaññanti na añño koci paccayo hoti upassutiṃ tiṭṭhituṃ
    
    

7.  4 situations in which the offense can happen

    
    
    -   taking steps to where one can hear
    -   when walking behind those discussing
    -   when walking ahead of those discussing
    -   when the monks involved in the argument come to one's presence

    1.  with the respective intention,

        -    **show one's presence bodily or verbally such as clearing one's throat**

-   according to **intention**:
    -   intending to accuse them later (codanādhippāya):  **pc** 
    -   intending to remind them later (codanādhippāya):  **pc**

## Kammappaṭibāhana S.


<a id="org6c8fe49"></a>

### Background



-   chabbaggiyas gave consent to a vinayakamma
-   after a monk was punished, they claimed they hadn't agreed
-   Buddha reprimanded them and established this rule




<a id="org7d6945b"></a>

### Pāli Rule


**Yo pana bhikkhu dhammikānaṃ kammānaṃ chandaṃ datvā pacchā khiyyanadhammaṃ āpajjeyya, pācittiyaṃ**
if a bhikkhu consents to a legitimate Saṅgha action and later criticizes it, he commits a pācittiya offence



<a id="org3dd6381"></a>

### Description

-   **purpose**: to prevent monks from criticizing vinayakammas they have given consent to
-   **summary of the offence**: criticizing a Saṅgha decision after consenting to it

1.  dhammika kamma &#x2013; definition

    
    legitimate / righteous disciplinary action
    

2.  chandaṁ datvā &#x2013; definition

    
    giving formal consent
    

3.  khiyyanadhammaṁ āpajjeyya &#x2013; definition

    
    criticizing (*:q: what is included under criticism here?*)
    

4.  vaggakamma &#x2013; definition

    
    a disciplinary action done without the unity of the saṅgha, with a divided saṅgha
    

5.  kammacatuvidha &#x2013; 4 types of vinayakamma with an example

    
    
    -   **apalokana** kamma: act for obtaining permission / giving notices that has 3 characteristics:
        -   **vatthuṃ kittetvā**: a vatthu is mentioned to the saṅgha
        -   **ruccati saṅghassa** 3x: “the saṅgha prefers (likes) this”, and so on
        -   **anumati**: approval of the united saṅgha
    -   **ñatti** kamma: with one motion and anumati of the united saṅgha (e.g.: uposathakamma)
    -   **ñattidutiya** kamma: one motion + one proclamation and anumati (e.g.: mohāropana)
    -   **ñatticatuttha** kamma: one motion + three proclamations and anumati (e.g.: upasampadā, parivāsadāna, mānattadāna, abbhānadāna…)
    
    

6.  5 types of apalokanakamma

    
    
    -   **nissāraṇa**: suspending a sāmaṇera, like Kaṇṭhaka, who criticizes the Buddha, Dhamma or Saṅgha, or holds a wrong view; suspending an ill-behaved sāmaṇera from the community
    -   **osāraṇa**: reinstating the suspended sāmaṇera who has corrected himself
    -   **bhaṇḍukamma**: getting permission to shave a candidate for novicehood
    -   **brahmadaṇḍa**: deciding that the Saṅgha should not speak with or admonish a rude monk like V. Channa
    -   **kammalakkhaṇa**: any kamma of which the characteristic is the mere act of the vinayakamma; initially declared to bhikkhunīs
    
    

7.  kammalakkhaṇa apalokanakamma &#x2013; background and examples

    
    
    -   **background**:
        -   chabbaggiyas doused bhikkhunīs with muddy water, exposed them their body, calves and penis, talked to them with lustful words, and connected them with men
        -   buddha asked bhikknunīs to do a vinayakamma, which can be done in the bhikkhunopassaya, so the monks who did so should not be worshiped by them
        -   e.g.:
            -   **avandiyakamma** (the one mentioned above)
            -   **bhaṇḍabhājana** (distribution of requisites to such monks as acchinnacīvaro, naṭṭhacīvaro, gilāno, bahussuto, saṅghabhāranittharako (one who bears the responsibilities of the saṅgha), and so on, or requisites for the purpose of lodging
            -   consumption of monastery requisites such as roots, barks, leaves, sprouts, flowers, fruits of trees in a monastery, removing trees that provide shade or fruits for the construction of lodgings and so on
    
    


<a id="org0834350"></a>

### Offences

-   giving consent to a kamma and later criticizing it
    -   without knowing any fault in it (and it was actually a dhammika kamma):  **pc** 
    -   knowing it was faulty (adhamma, vagga, or to an akammārahapuggala):  **anāpatti** 


<a id="orgcee8d95"></a>

### Non-Offences


if the criticism is due to knowledge of:

-   **adhamma** (illegal)
-   **vagga** (lack of Saṅgha unity)
-   **akammāraha** (person not deserving a kamma)




<a id="org7c320b0"></a>

### Factors



-   **dhammakammatā**: the vinayakamma is righteous
-   **dhammakammanti saññā**: one believes it was righteous
-   **chandaṃ datvā khiyyanaṃ**: consents and then criticizes
