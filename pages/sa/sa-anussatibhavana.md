# 10 Anussatis



-   **Buddhānussati**: sati taking the (qualities of the) Buddha as object (arahaṁ…)
    -   Buddhaṃ ārabbha uppannā anussati Buddhānussati: Buddhaguṇārammaṇāya satiyā etamadhivacanaṃ
-   **Dhammānussati**: sati taking pariyattidhamma and 9 lokuttaradhammas as object (svākkhāto…)
    -   Nibbāna under Dhammānussati vs under Upasamānussati:
        -   under Dhammānussati: focusing on the common characteristic of being paṭivedhadhamma
        -   under Upasamānussati: focusing on the unique characteristics of Nibbāna (asaṅkhata, amata, etc)
-   **Saṅghānussati**: sati taking the (qualities of the) Saṅgha as object (supaṭipanno…)
-   **Sīlānussati**: sati taking one's own qualities of virtue (unbroken, etc)
-   **Cāgānussati**: sati taking one's own quality of generosity (muttacāga, etc)
-   **Devatānussati**: sati taking one's own qualities (saddhā, etc) as object, while placing the devas in the position of witnesses / as basis
-   **Maraṇānussati**: sati taking death, the cutting off of jīvitindriya, as object
-   **Kāyagatāsati**: sati which has "gone into" one’s own rūpa kāya (32 body parts) or into one’s own body, taking the nimitta of the body parts as repulsive
    -   aluttasamāsa
-   **Ānāpānassati**: sati taking the nimitta of inhalation and exhalation as object
-   **Upasamānussati**: sati taking peace (Nibbāna), which is the cessation of all suffering, as object
-   Notes:
    -   sīla, cāga, and devatā: good for people who have a low perception of themselves
        
        udayabaya, bhayatupatthana, nibbida, adinvva, : qualities of nibbana contemplated
    -   2 types of upasamanussati
        -   freedom from suffering
        -   absence of kilesa
    -   no samathakammaṭṭhāna based on sound, smell, akusala states
    -   no kammaṭṭhāna thinking "tree, tree"
    -   of the anussatis, only kāyagatāsati and ānāpāna have nimitta
        -   that's why nimitta appears in their description

# Buddhānussati

## General Steps



-   Understand the qualities of the Buddha correctly
-   **Aveccappasāda**: develop unshakable confidence in these qualities, which arises from correctly understanding them
    -   fully established in ariyas
    -   puthujjanas can also successfully develop buddhānussati with firm confidence
    -   doesn't become fully successful without such confidence
-   **Rahogata** (kāyaviveka) in a suitable dwelling and withdrawing the mind from various other objects (paṭisallīna)
    -   suitable dwelling: free from 18 defects and possessing 5 suitable qualities
    -   paṭisallīna: withdrawn from different objects and is properly fixed on the meditation object
-   Repeatedly recollect the qualities of the Blessed One

> **Iti pi so Bhagavā Arahaṃ Sammāsambuddho Vijjācaraṇasampanno Sugato Lokavidū Anuttaro Purisadammasārathi Satthā Devamanussānaṃ Buddho Bhagavā**

## Recollections


**So Bhagavā iti pi Arahaṁ&#x2026;Bhagavā**

-   So: Buddha, one who completed the pāramīs, etc.
-   1st Bhagavā: a name of the Teacher; acquired at the bodhimaṇḍa upon the realization of sabbaññutañāṇa
    -   sacchikāpaññatti: designation that arises together with the direct realization of all dhammas
-   last Bhagavā: expresses a particular quality
-   pi: sampiṇḍa (inclusion): shows each title expresses many aspects of the Buddha’s qualities

## Arahaṃ


<a id="org8ca6b2d"></a>

### Vsm: 5 ways



1.  **Ārakattā hatattā**: very far from the faults of defilements because He destroyed them together with their vāsanā through the Ariyamagga
    -   So tato ārakā nāma – yassa yenāsamaṅgitā,
        Asamaṅgī ca dosehi – Nātho tenārahaṃ mato.
    -   notes: kusalavāsanas remain: e.g., karuṇā
2.  **Kilesārina so muni**: he has destroyed (through ariyamagga) the enemies called defilements
    -   Yasmā rāgādisaṅkhātā – sabbepi arayo hatā,
        Paññāsatthena Nāthena – tasmāpi Arahaṃ mato.
3.  **Hatasaṁsāracakkāro**: Has broken the spokes of the wheel of saṃsāra, beginning with avijjā
    -   Arā saṃsāracakkassa – hatā ñāṇāsinā yato,
        Lokanāthena tenesa – Arahanti pavuccati.
4.  **Paccayādina cāraho**: Worthy of receiving all requisites and special offerings
    -   Aggadakkhiṇeyya: the One most worthy of offerings
    -   e.g.,
        -   Brahmā Sahampati: jewelled garland as large as Meru
        -   devas/humans, Bimbisāra, Kosala
        -   Asoka: 96 koṭis, 84.000 monasteries in Jambudīpa
    -   Pūjāvisesaṃ saha paccayehi – yasmā ayaṃ arahati lokanātho,
        atthānurūpaṃ arahanti loke – tasmā jino arahati nāmametaṃ.
5.  **Na raho karoti pāpāni**: does not commit evil even in secret
    -   Fools commit evil secretly, afraid of criticism but the Buddha does not commit evil, even in secret
    -   Note: Buddha could hide even from Brahmas but did not do evil in secret
    -   Yasmā natthi raho nāma – pāpakammesu Tādino,
        Rahābhāvena tenesa – Arahaṃ iti vissuto.

> **Ārakattā hatattā ca, kilesārīna so muni,
> Hatasaṃsāracakkāro, paccayādīna cāraho,**
> **Na raho karoti pāpāni, Arahaṃ tena vuccatīti.**



1.  Hatasaṁsāracakka

    
    **Hatasaṁsāracakka**: Having Broken the Wheel of Saṃsāra 
    
    -   **Hata**: knowing through dhammaṭṭhitiñāṇa the true nature of dhammas, becoming disenchanted toward, detached from, and liberated from them
        -   At the bodhimaṇḍa:
            -   He stood firmly on the ground of sīla
            -   with the feet of viriya
            -   with the hand of saddhā
            -   took up the axe of ñāṇa that destroys kamma
            -   and completely broke all the spokes of this wheel of saṃsāra
        -   **paccayapariggahe paññā dhammaṭṭhitiñāṇaṃ**: wisdom that understands this cause-and-effect relationship (paccayapariggahañāṇa), its existence in the past, future, and present and impermanent nature
            -   Avijjāpaccayā saṅkhārāti ñāṇaṃ, asati avijjāya natthi saṅkhārāti ñāṇaṃ; atītampi addhānaṃ&#x2026; anāgatampi addhānaṃ&#x2026; yampissa taṃ dhammaṭṭhitiñāṇaṃ tampi khayadhammaṃ vayadhammaṃ virāgadhammaṃ nirodhadhammanti ñāṇaṃ
            -   dhammaṭṭhiti: fem. real nature of the Dhamma
    -   **Saṁsāracakka**: paṭiccasamuppāda with 4 saṅkhepas, 3 addhās, 20 ākāras, 3 sandhis

### Vsm-ṭ: 6 other ways



1.  **Ārakā mandabuddhīnaṁ**: very far from an evil person, even if they follow His every step behind Him while holding the corner of His robe
2.  **Ārakā ca vijānataṁ**: close to a good person even if a hundred yojanas away
3.  **Rahānaṁ suppahīnattā**: raha (defilements) have been completely removed
4.  **Vidūnamaraheyyato**: should never be abandoned, but should be approached and associated with again and again, even by good puthujjanas, sekhas, and asekhas, who have completed all that has to be done
5.  **Bhavesu ca rahābhāvā**: the journey (raho), saṁsāric wandering, no longer exists for Him
6.  **Pasaṁsā**: so praiseworthy that even Sammāsambuddhas cannot fully describe His qualities or reach the end of their praise, let alone others
7.  (Extra) **Āṇacakka-dhammacakka**: He is worthy of setting in motion both the wheel of authority and the Dhamma wheel

> **Ārakā mandabuddhīnaṃ – ārakā ca vijānataṃ,
> Rahānaṃ suppahīnattā – vidūnamaraheyyato,**
> **Bhavesu ca rahābhāvā – pāsaṃsā arahaṃ jino.**

Notes:

-   some add additional ways
-   rarely is it used as araho istead of arahaṁ



1.  Ārakā mandabuddhīnaṁ ārakā ca vijānataṁ

    
    **Ārakāti Arahaṃ, suvidūrabhāvato icceva attho**: because He is extremely distant from those:
    
    -   whose body, conduct, mind, and wisdom are untrained
    -   whose rāga, dosa, and moha have not been abandoned
    -   unskilled in the Ariyadhamma
    -   who follow the wrong way of practice
    -   note:
        -   even if such individual held the corner of the Buddha’s robe and followed directly behind Him, he would still be very far from Him because **Dhammaṃ apassanto maṃ na passati** (one who does not see the Dhamma does not see Me)
        -   likewise, the Teacher is far from them
    
    > **Sammā na paṭipajjanti – ye nihīnāsayā narā,
    > Ārakā tehi Bhagavā – dūre tenārahaṃ mato.**
    
    **Ārakāti Arahaṃ, āsannabhāvatoti attho**: because He is very close to (the opposite of the previous)
    
    -   even if 100 yojanas away:
        -   he is close to the Teacher
        -   the Teacher is close to Him
        -   **Dhammaṃ passanto maṃ passati**
    -   note: here ārakā is used in 2 opposite meanings
    
    > **Ye sammā paṭipajjanti – suppaṇītādhimuttikā,
    > Ārakā tehi āsanne – tenāpi Arahaṃ Jino.** 
    
    

2.  Rahānaṃ suppahīnattā

    
    
    -   **Rahitabbo raho**: what should be kept far away, abandoned is raha &#x2013; defilements
    -   **Suppahīnattā**: because He completely abandoned and destroyed them through ariyamagga
        -   pahīnā, ucchinnamūlā, tālāvatthukatā, anabhāvaṁkatā, āyatiṁ anuppādadhammā
    -   Notes: normally, araho (paṭ ekv), but here, o > aṃ = arahaṃ
    
    > **Pāpadhammā rahā nāma – sādhūhi rahitabbato,
    > tesaṃ suṭṭhu pahīnattā – bhagavā arahaṃ mato.**
    
    
    
    1.  Arasarūpādi
    
        
        
        > V-p: **Atthi khvesa, brāhmaṇa, pariyāyo yena maṃ pariyāyena sammā vadamāno vadeyya — ‘Arasarūpo Samaṇo Gotamo’ti. Ye te, Brāhmaṇa, rūparasā saddarasā gandharasā rasarasā phoṭṭhabbarasā te Tathāgatassa pahīnā ucchinnamūlā tālāvatthukatā anabhāvaṃkatā āyatiṃ anuppādadhammā…**
        
        -   Rūparasa, saddarasa, etc: lobhamūla sukhavedanā enjoying visible forms, sounds, etc.
        -   **Pahīnā**:
            -   He abandoned this sukhavedanā
            -   He abandoned and removed it from His mental continuum
        -   **Ucchinnamūlā**:
            -   mūla: roots of saṁsāra and of unwholesome dhammas &#x2013; avijjā & taṇhā
            -   the roots of this sukhavedanā &#x2013; taṇhā & avijjā &#x2013; have been completely cut off
                -   uchinnā mūlā etesanti ucchinnamūlā
            -   notes:
                -   arahat doesn't have akusalacittajarūpas
                    -   kusalacittajarūpas as well, but not normally mentioned since the kiriya is like the same citta as kusala but without the potency to produce results
                -   1st happiness: of removing nīvaraṇas
                -   2nd happiness: happiness of the jhānacitta
                -   3rd happiness: body affected by excellent cittajarūpas
                -   DN mahāparinibbānasutta-a:
                    -   normal phalasamāpatti: do vipassanā normally and enter phalasamāpatti
                    -   **jīvitasaṅkhāraṭhapana phalasamāpatti**: do rūpasattaka arūpasattaka in paṭisaṅkhānupassanā level and then enter phalasamāpatti
                        -   effects remain for a longer period
                        -   can suppress sickness for a long period
                        -   Buddha: no lohitapakkhandika sickness for 7 months
        -   **Tālāvatthukatā**
            -   method 1:
                -   tālavatthu: the (bare) site where a palm tree once stood
                -   rūparasavatthu: mental continuum, the site where rūparasa once stood
                -   rūparasa was made tālāvatthukata: made bare (without rūparasa)
                -   tālavatthu viya nesaṃ vatthu katanti tālāvatthukatā: those dhammas the basis of which have been made like the bare site of a palm tree are called tālāvatthukata
                    -   normally: tālavatthuvatthukatā
                        -   middle vatthu is removed
                        -   "a" is lengthened
                -   summary: like a bare land where a palm once stood (the mind becomes like that bare land where defilements once stood); it cannot grow again; ariyamaggas uproots rūparasādi with their roots and can not arise again
                    -   mental stream becomes like a tālavatthu
            -   method 2:
                -   tālavatthu: a palm tree the top of which has been cut off and, therefore, cannot grow again
                -   tālāvatthukatā: arūparasādi were destroyed by maggañāṇa and cannot grow again like such palm tree 
                    -   avatthubhūto tālo viya katā avatthutālakatā (the unwholesome dhammas are made like a palm tree that is no longer a basis for producing leaves and fruit) 
                        -   avatthutālakatā => tāla + avatthukatā > tālāvatthukatā
            -   method 3:
                -   rūparasādi: past kusalākusala sukhavedanā capable of producing vipāka
                -   their top (taṇhā and avijjā) has been cut off by maggañāṇa, preventing them from being able to produce vipāka, like such palm tree
        -   **Anabhāvaṃkatā**
            -   anabhāvaṃkata: rūparasādi that has been brought into such a condition that it will not exist again later
                -   anu (pacchā) + abhāvaṃ + katā
                -   NOT "na abhāvaṁ katā" (brought to non-non-existence)
            -   some: anabhāvaṃ gatā: anu + abhāvaṃ + gatā: gone into non-existence in such a way that they will not arise afterwards; destroyed; abandoned
                -   like anuacchariyo > anacchariyo
                    -   anacchariya gāthā: verses that produce increasing wonder again and again when heard
        -   **Āyatiṃ anuppādadhammā**
            -   rūparasādi is incapable of arising again in the future
            -   2 together: anabhāvaṃ gatā āyatiṃ anuppādadhammā: they have gone into non-existence and are incapable of arising again in the future
            -   note: does NOT mean some rūparasādi still exist in Him      
                -   Tathāgatassa kho, Brāhmaṇa, rāgo pahīno doso moho, sabbepi pāpakā akusalā dhammā pahīnā ucchinnamūlā tālāvatthukatā anabhāvaṃkatā āyatiṃ anuppādadhammā.
        
        

3.  Vidūnamaraheyyato

    
    
    -   **Na santi etassa rahā pariccajanakā**: there is no (wise) one who abandons Him

### Dhammāṇacakkaṁ pavattetuṁ arahati ("Sūvisi Mahā Guṇaya" by V. Cdvm)


**Anuttaraṃ dhammacakkañca āṇācakkañca pavattetuṃ arahatīti arahā**: worthy and capable of setting in motion the unsurpassed Dhamma wheel and the wheel of authority


1.  Dhammacakka

    
    
    -   **Paṭivedha Dhammacakka** (Dhamma-Wheel of Realization): dhammas that destroy defilements set in motion at the bodhimaṇḍa

    -   **Desanā Dhammacakka** (Dhamma-Wheel of Teaching): by means of preaching, He cuts off others' defilements like the cakkāyudha
        -   set rolling at Isipatana in Bārāṇasī
            -   set rolling: teaching
            -   that teaching is also called Dhammacakka
                -   note: any of Buddha's teachings could be called Dhammacakka in this sense
        -   due to His wisdom, He could teach without impediments, hesitation, etc., like a river pouring from the sky, and make the Dhamma enter the mind of beings like no other
            -   even 100 yojanas away, one could hear Him as if sitting nearby
    -   Notes:
        -   cakkāyudha / cakkāvudha: a weapon like a wheel spinned to cut down enemies
        -   24 spokes desanā cakka
        -   28 pativedha cakka (tbc)
        -   10 spokes of saṁsāra

## Sammāsambuddho


**Sammā sāmañca sabbadhammānaṃ buddhattā pana Sammāsambuddho**: because He understood all dhammas

-   **Avabodha**: He understood them correctly and by Himself
    -   **sammā**: correctly, as they are
    -   **sāmaṁ**: by Himself, without instruction from another teacher
        -   paropadesarahita: without depending on the instruction of another person
-   **Anubodha**: He understood them how they should be understood
-   **Paṭibodha**: He understood them and penetrated them thoroughly
-   Notes: sabbaññutañāṇa: capable of knowing all dhammas in all their aspects whenever He wishes to know them

He became Sammāsambuddha by

-   Knowing according to their individual characteristics abhiññeyya dhammas (all)
    -   abhiññeyyadhammas: dhammas to be known by superior knowledge &#x2013; 4 NT
        -   He did so through
            -   vipassanāñāṇa (before magga)
            -   maggañāṇa (at magga)
            -   sabbaññutañāṇādi (after magga)
        -   4 NT here includes all dhammas: there is no knowable dhamma (ñeyya) outside the 4 NT
            -   29 maggacittuppādas (exc. 8 maggaṅgas) can be included under maggasacca
            -   37 phalacittuppādas can be included under nirodhasacca
                -   sometimes, Nibbāna refers to phalacittuppāda &#x2013; Maṅgalasutta: nibbānasacchikiriyāca: arising of phalacittuppāda
-   Fully understanding according to their general characteristics pariññeyya dhammas &#x2013; dukkhaariyasaccadhammas
-   Abandoning pahātabbadhammas &#x2013; samudayapakkhiyadhammas (all akusaladhammas)
    -   sabbe hi akusalā samudayapakkhiyā
-   Directly realizing the sacchikātabbadhammas &#x2013; nirodhasaccadhammas (Nibbāna and phaladhammas)
    -   why plural?
        -   Nibbāna can be spoken of in different ways, such as saupādisesa and anupādisesa
        -   because this is a general statement concerning the Four Noble Truths that does not limit the exact number of dhammas referred to
            -   e.g., appaccayā dhammā and asaṅkhatā dhammā
        -   phala vimutti may also be included among the dhammas to be directly realized, including phaladhammas here
-   Developing bhāvetabbadhammas &#x2013; all kusaladhammas

-   Saṁsāra
    -   great paññā: shows His disenchantment with the suffering of saṃsāra
        great karuṇā: shows His willingness to endure the suffering of saṃsāra for the sake of others
    -   great paññā: show His complete understanding of the suffering of others
        great karuṇā: shows His effort to provide a remedy for the suffering of others
    -   great paññā: shows His own crossing beyond the suffering of saṃsāra
        great karuṇā: shows His helping others to cross beyond the suffering of saṃsāra
    -   great paññā: shows His lack of delight in saṃsāra

    -   great paññā: shows His inclination towards Parinibbāna

-   Fear
    -   great paññā: shows He Himself is not afraid because of others
        great karuṇā: shows He does not cause fear in others
-   Protection
    -   great paññā: shows that while protecting Himself, He protects others
        great karuṇā: shows that while protecting others, He protects Himself
    -   great paññā: shows He is anattantapa, one who does not torment Himself

-   Refuge
    -   great paññā: shows His quality of being Attanātha, one who is a refuge to Himself

    -   great paññā: shows He is completely unattached to all dhammas

-   **Appiya**: unpleasant to others
-   **Amanāpa**: disagreeable

-   **False, Unbeneficial, and Unpleasant**: He does not speak them

-   **True, Unbeneficial and Unpleasant**: He does not speak them 
    -   e.g., merely insulting someone
-   **True, Beneficial, but Unpleasant**: He knows the proper time to speak these
    -   e.g., declaring someone to be certainly destined for niraya, as for Devadatta: “Āpāyiko devadatto nerayiko.”
-   **False, Unbeneficial, but Pleasant**: He does not speak them
    -   e.g., deceptive statements: “By following the sacrificial rituals taught in the Vedas and killing living beings, one can reach a good destination.”
-   **True, Pleasant, but Unbeneficial**: He does not speak them
    -   e.g., pisuṇāvācā, even if true
-   **True, Beneficial, and Pleasant**: He knows the proper time to speak them

# Dhammānussati

A yogi who wishes to develop Dhammānussati should, while dwelling in a suitable residence, remain
in seclusion (rahogatena) and withdraw from various external objects (paṭisallīnena). He should
recollect the qualities of both the Pariyatti Dhamma and the nine supramundane Dhammas in the
following manner:

“The Dhamma taught by the Blessed One is Svākkhāta, Sandiṭṭhika, Ehipassika, Opanayika, and
Paccattaṃ Veditabbo Viññūhi.”

Here a question arises: Why, in the explanation of Dhammānussati, is it not stated, as in Buddhānussati,
that the meditation should be developed by “a yogi endowed with unshakable confidence”?
Although all six recollections are taught primarily with reference to Noble Disciples, the expression
aveccappasādasamannāgatena – “by one endowed with unshakable confidence” – is not used in the
explanation of Dhammānussati and the remaining five recollections in order to show that these
recollections may also be successfully developed by ordinary persons who possess such qualities as
purified virtue.1
Among the six qualities of the Dhamma, the term Svākkhāta includes both the Pariyatti Dhamma and
the supramundane Dhamma. The remaining five qualities beginning with Sandiṭṭhika, in the strict and
direct sense, refer only to the supramundane Dhamma.2 However, it should be understood that, in a
secondary or extended sense, these five qualities can also apply to the Pariyatti Dhamma.3

1 Kasmā panettha buddhānussatiyaṃ viya "aveccappasādasamannāgatenā"ti na vuttaṃ. Nanu etā paṭipāṭiyā
cha anussatiyo ariyasāvakānaṃ vasena desanaṃ āruḷhāti? Saccametaṃ, evaṃ santepi
"parisuddhasīlādiguṇānaṃ puthujjanānampi ijjhantī"ti dassanatthaṃ "aveccappasādasamannāgatenā"ti
idha na vuttaṃ, ariyasāvakānaṃ pana sukhena ijjhantīti. Tathāpi pāḷiyaṃ āgatattā ca buddhānussatiniddese
vuttanti daṭṭhabbaṃ, ayañca vicāro parato āgamissateva. (Vsm-ṭ. I. 267.).

2 Svākkhātoti imasmiṃ hi pade pariyattidhammopi saṅgahaṃ gacchati, itaresu lokuttaradhammova. (Vsm. I. 206.).

3 Pariyattidhammopīti pi-saddena lokuttaradhammaṃ sampiṇḍeti. Itaresūti sandiṭṭhikādipadesu.
Lokuttaradhammovāti avadhāraṇaṃ nippariyāyena sandiṭṭhikādiatthaṃ sandhāya vuttaṃ, pariyāyato panete
pariyattidhammepi sambhavanteva. (Vsm-ṭ. I. 267.).

## Svākkhāta

Both the Pariyatti Dhamma and the nine supramundane Dhammas (Paṭivedha Dhamma) are Svākkhāta.
Let us first understand the quality of Svākkhāta as it applies to the Pariyatti Dhamma.

The Quality of “Svākkhāta” in the Pariyatti Dhamma

It was widely known in ancient India that the Tathāgata teaches a well-proclaimed Dhamma in the
following manner:

**So dhammaṃ deseti ādikalyāṇaṃ majjhekalyāṇaṃ pariyosānakalyāṇaṃ sātthaṃ sabyañjanaṃ, kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ parisuddhaṃ brahmacariyaṃ pakāseti.**

The Blessed One teaches a Dhamma that is good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good
in the end, . He proclaims a holy life that is complete in meaning and expression entirely complete and pure.

### 2 Brahmacariyaṁ

-   **Sāsana brahmacariya** (practice of the holy life)
-   **Magga brahmacariya** (the path of the holy life)

-   **Sāttha** (endowed with meaning)
    -   because its meaning is complete
    -   because it possesses the meaningful modes of exposition known as saṅkāsana, pakāsana, vivaraṇa, vibhajana, and uttānīkaraṇa
    -   because its meaning is profound and its penetration (paṭivedha) is profound
    -   because it is the object of the analytical knowledges of meaning (attha paṭisambhidā) and discriminative knowledge of intellect (paṭibhāna paṭisambhidā)
    -   because it is to be known by the wise and produces confidence in those who investigate what is wholesome
    -   because it possesses profound intention (adhippāya)
    -   because it gives rise to adhigamaveyyattiya, proficiency in attainment through practice
-   **Sabyañjana** (endowed with expression):
    -   because its verbal expression is complete
    -   because it is complete in letters, words, expressions, forms of exposition, etymology, and detailed explanation
    -   because the Dhamma is profound and its exposition (desanā) is profound
    -   because it is the object of the analytical knowledges of Dhamma (dhamma paṭisambhidā) and language (nirutti paṭisambhidā)
    -   because it is to be accepted with faith and gives rise to confidence in ordinary people
    -   because its words are clear
    -   because through learning it gives rise to āgamaveyyattiya, proficiency based on wide learning and mastery of the textual teaching
-   **Kevalaparipuṇṇa** (entirely complete)
    -   because nothing needs to be added to it
    -   because it is endowed with the five Dhamma aggregates: virtue, concentration, wisdom, liberation, and the knowledge and vision of liberation
-   **Parisuddha** (pure):
    -   because nothing needs to be removed from it and because it is free from defects
    -   because it is free from corrupting impurities
    -   because it leads out from the suffering of the round of existence
    -   because it does not depend upon craving for worldly gain (lokāmisa)
-   Notes:
    -   artha > attha: meaning
    -   astha > attha: go down, get destroyed
        -   suriyo attham gacchati
    -   bahussutas
        -   nissaya bahussuta
        -   parisu bahussuta
        -   bhikkhunova bahussuta
        -   sabbatthakara bahussuta
        -   adhigama bahussuta: ariyas
        -   dhammadhara: one who hold the dhamma, either in terms of study, or in terms of possessing maggaphalas
    -   5 khandhas
        -   kilesa (cause) (mainly avijjā; taṇhā is the most powerful) > lead to
            -   kilesadukkha in present life
            -   khandhadukkha in future life
        -   to attain vimutti from dukkha, remove avijjā with vijjā
        -   for vijjā, you need samādhi
        -   for samādhi, you need sīla
        -   note: pañña is the center
            -   leads to vimutti, then vimuttiñāṇadassana
    -   lokuttara maggas and 3 lower phalas are neither with anusaya (sānusaya), nor with it anusaya

Pariyatti Dhamma is Svākkhāta because it has been well proclaimed without any distortion of meaning

-   durakkhāta teachings:
    -   antarāyikadhammas, things declared by them to be obstructive, are not truly so
    -   niyyānikadhammas, things declared by them to be leading outward from suffering, are not truly so
-   svakkhāta
    -   antarāyikadhamma are truly so
    -   niyyānikadhammas are truly so

### Lokuttaradhammas' Svākkhāta

**Lokuttaradhammas**: 4 magga, 4 phala, Nibbāna

So called because:

-   Nibbāna has been taught in conformity with the practice leading to it, and the practice has been taught in conformity with Nibbāna
    -   Sakka: Supaññattā kho pana tena bhagavatā sāvakānaṃ nibbānagāminī paṭipadā saṃsandati nibbānañca paṭipadā ca. Seyyathāpi nāma gaṅgodakaṃ yamunodakena saṃsandati sameti, evameva supaññattā tena bhagavatā sāvakānaṃ nibbānagāminī paṭipadā saṃsandati nibbānañca paṭipadā ca.
-   The Blessed One has prescribed well for His disciples the practice leading to Nibbāna. That practice and Nibbāna are in complete accord.
    -   like the water of the Ganges meets and harmonizes with the water of the Yamunā, so too Nibbāna and the practice leading to Nibbāna are in complete accord with each other.
    -   Thus, the Blessed One has prescribed well for His disciples the practice leading to Nibbāna.

Magga:

-   because, being in reality the Middle Way that does not approach either self-mortification or indulgence in sensual pleasure, it has been taught precisely as the “Middle Way” (majjhimā paṭipadā)

Phala, aka samaññaphala:

-   because, being states in which the defilements have in fact become tranquillized, they have been taught as states of the tranquillization of defilements (paṭippassaddhakilesa)

Nibbāna:

-   because, being by nature permanent (sassata), deathless (amata), a refuge (tāṇa), and a shelter (leṇa), it has been taught precisely as “permanent,” “deathless,” “a refuge,” “a shelter,” and so forth

## Ehipassiko


<a id="orgd9ba38b"></a>

### Lokuttaradhamma & Pariyattidhamma's

**Ehi passa imaṃ dhamman'ti evaṃ pavattaṃ ehipassavidhiṃ arahatīti ehipassiko**

-   **Ehipassika**: that which is worthy of the invitation "ehipassa" (come and see this Dhamma)
-   **Vijjamānattā**: because it truly exists in the ultimate sense
    -   Buddha and others attained it
-   **Parisuddhattā**: because it is pure, free from the stains of defilements
-   notes: there are occasions when even an unpleasant thing may rightly be shown in order to reveal its true nature for some useful purpose

Pariyattidhamma:

-   because what it teaches truly exists and is pure

"Sūvisi Mahā Guṇaya"

-   merely hearing to Dhamma with a wholesome mind brings good results such as a good rebirth
-   practicing leads to jhānas' happines and brahma lives
-   further practice leads to maggaphalas and the total end of suffering
-   for the multiple benefits of the Dhamma, it is suitable for even cakkavattirājas to renounce and practice it

### Related Stories

****Acela Kassapa and Citta the Householder:****

-   Naked ascetic Acela Kassapa (30 years practice) met lay disciple Citta (30 years).
-   Kassapa reported gaining nothing superior, only nakedness/baldness.
-   Citta described attaining the four jhanas and expecting non-returner status.
-   Impressed, Kassapa sought ordination in Buddha's Dhamma.
-   Citta facilitated Kassapa's ordination and offered support.
-   Kassapa practiced diligently and attained Arahantship.

****Anathapindika's Son, Kisa:****

-   Merchant Anathapindika's son, Kisa, lacked faith and avoided the Dhamma.
-   Father offered 100 kahapanas for Kisa to observe Uposatha and listen to Dhamma.
-   Kisa went for money, slept, and received payment upon return.
-   Father then offered 1000 kahapanas for learning one Dhamma passage.
-   Kisa went, but the Buddha made him forget words, though the meaning remained.

-   Kisa understood the Dhamma and attained Stream-entry.
-   Returned home with the Buddha and the Sangha.
-   Ashamed, Kisa refused the 1000 kahapanas offered before the Buddha.
-   Buddha declared Stream-entry more valuable than universal rule or heavenly rebirth.
-   Buddha uttered a verse emphasizing Stream-entry's superiority.
-   Stream-entry frees one from the four lower realms.

## How the Meditation on the Qualities of the Dhamma Becomes Successful

meditating so > removes hindrances > vitakkavicāra arise > pīti > kāya/mano distress subsides through passaddhi > kāya/manasika sukha > samādhi


<a id="orgb53ae16"></a>

### Why only upacāra

-   because the qualities of the Dhamma are profound
-   because the mind tends to recollect the qualities of the Dhamma in various ways

Since this jhāna arises through recollecting the qualities of the Dhamma, it is called Dhammānussati.


<a id="org7bea576"></a>

### Benefits

-   **Evaṃ opaneyyikassa dhammassa desetāraṃ imināpaṅgena samannāgataṃ satthāraṃ neva atītaṃse samanupassāmi, na panetarahi aññatra tena bhagavatā”ti evaṃ dhammaguṇadassaneneva satthari sagāravo hoti sappatisso**: respect for the Teacher
-   **Dhamme garucittīkāro saddhādivepullaṃ adhigacchati**: growth of faith; He develops reverence for the Dhamma and attains great development in faith and the other wholesome qualities
-   **Pītipāmojjabahulo hoti**: abundance of rapture and joy; because Dhammānussati is a cause for the enlightenment factor of rapture (pīti sambojjhaṅga), the meditator who develops it becomes one who frequently experiences rapture and joy
-   **Bhayabheravasaho**: ability to overcome fear and dread; through mindfulness directed toward the qualities of the Dhamma, he overcomes fear and dread
    -   Dhajagga Sutta: Evaṃ buddhaṃ sarantānaṃ, dhammaṃ saṅghañca bhikkhavo, bhayaṃ vā chambhitattaṃ vā, lomahaṃso na hessati
-   **Dukkhādhivāsanasamattho**: ability to endure pain; furthermore, because bodily suffering rolls away through Dhammānussati like a drop of water on a lotus leaf, the meditator who develops it becomes capable of enduring bodily pain
-   **Dhammena saṃvāsasaññaṃ paṭilabhati**: perception of living together with the Dhamma
-   **Dhammaguṇānussatiyā ajjhāvutthañcassa sarīrampi cetiyagharamiva pūjārahaṃ hoti**: he acquires the perception, “I am living together with the Dhamma.” His Body Becomes Worthy of Veneration; The body of the meditator in whom mindfulness recollecting the qualities of the Dhamma has dwelt becomes worthy of veneration, like a shrine chamber.
-   **Anuttaradhammādhigamāya cittaṃ namati**: the mind Inclines toward the attainment of the supreme dhamma his mind inclines toward the attainment of the unsurpassed Dhamma
-   **Vītikkamitabbavatthusamāyoge cassa dhammasudhammataṃ samanussarato hirottappaṃ paccupaṭṭhāti**: shame and moral dread arise when facing a transgression; Because the qualities of the Dhamma are constantly present in his mind through Dhammānussati meditation, when he encounters an object connected with transgression, shame and moral dread arise in him as he recollects the excellence and purity of the Dhamma.
-   **Uttari appaṭivijjhanto pana sugatiparāyano hoti**: rebirth in a happy destination; If, based upon Dhammānussati, he does not go on to penetrate the Noble Truths, he will nevertheless be reborn in a happy destination.

> **Tasmā have appamādaṃ – kayirātha sumedhaso,  
> Evaṃ mahānubhāvāya – dhammānussatiyā sadāti.**  
> Therefore, the wise should always practice diligently the recollection of the Dhamma, which is of such great power.
