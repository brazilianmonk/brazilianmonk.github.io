# The Role of Monks in Conflict and the Protection of the Sasana

written on June 27th, 2026 by Brazil Ariyañāṇa Bhikkhu
• 🕒 8 min read

### The True Protection of the Buddha Sasana
In an era of global instability, the question often arises whether Buddhist nations or individuals should use force to protect the Dhamma from total destruction. Bhante Ariyañāṇa clarifies that the longevity of the *Sasana* (the dispensation/teachings) does not depend on armaments or physical defense, but on internal factors.

*   **How the Teaching is Protected:** The Dhamma is protected by those who follow it, support the Sangha, practice diligently, study deeply, and propagate the truth [6/26 chat]. The ultimate protection is achieved when practitioners reach the goal of the teachings—the purification of the mind [60, 6/26 chat].
*   **The Vital Role of Vinaya:** For monastics, strictly following the *Vinaya* (the code of discipline) is the single most important factor affecting how long the teachings will survive in the world [6/26 chat].
*   **The Threat of Corruption:** The "True Dhamma" is at greater risk from internal corruption than from external physical threats. One of the main reasons the teaching disappears is the emergence of "corrupted Dhamma"—distorted versions of the teachings that are labeled "Buddhism" but deviate from the original principles [6/26 chat].

### Radical Non-Aversion: The *Kakacūpamasutta*
The Buddha provided a profound ethical framework for responding to violence, most notably in the ***Kakacūpamasutta*** (The Parable of the Saw). This teaching establishes a high standard for mental conduct during conflict:

*   **The Limit of Hatred:** Even if bandits were to dismember a person with a two-handled saw, if that person allows a single thought of hatred to arise in their mind, they are not following the Buddha’s teaching [6/26 chat].
*   **Mental Integrity over Physical Survival:** While a layperson may have different social duties regarding defense, the monastic's priority is to maintain a mind of *Metta* (loving-kindness) and neutrality even under mortal threat [45, 47, 6/26 chat].

### Addressing the Root: Mental States vs. Physical Force
Monastics focus on the **root causes** of war—the "unwholesome mental states" (*akusala dhammas*)—rather than its external symptoms. 

*   **The Source of Conflict:** Wars arise because leaders are driven by greed for power and money, hatred toward others, and delusion [1]. 
*   **Monastic Neutrality:** To protect the Dhamma, monks must maintain a position of neutrality [2, 3]. If a monk takes sides in a political or military conflict (such as the historical examples of Vietnam or Tibet), they risk the Sangha's destruction and the loss of the teaching's integrity [2, 4, 5].
*   **The Ripple Effect:** A monk influences society not through force, but through their example—how they walk, live, and practice—which can inspire a "chain reaction" of peace in others [2, 6].

### Practical Tools: *Upekkha* and *Metta*
For those distressed by global news, the Dhamma offers psychological tools to regain balance:

*   **Upekkha (Equanimity):** This is the ability to maintain a balanced mind by understanding that all beings are the owners of their own *kamma* [7]. Cultivating equanimity prevents one from being overwhelmed by despair or anger when facing a world in conflict [8, 9].
*   **Metta (Loving-Kindness):** Daily practice of *Metta* is a powerful tool to influence one's community and even "Devas" (higher beings) who may have an influence on the world [10, 11].

### Is physical defense never permitted?
While the *Vinaya* prohibits monks from engaging in violence or suicide (even for political protest), Bhante notes that laypeople may feel the need to act or use force to stop a conflict [5, 12]. However, from the perspective of the Dhamma, any action taken with a mind of hatred is considered unwholesome (*akusala*). The monastic role remains fixed: to provide a stable moral foundation and to ensure the path to true liberation remains available for future generations, uncorrupted by the fires of war [48, 60, 6/26 chat].
