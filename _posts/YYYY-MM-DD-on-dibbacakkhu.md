The passage you are thinking of is found in **Majjhima Nikāya 128 (MN 128)**, known as the **Upakkilesa Sutta** (*"The Imperfections of Meditation"* or *"Corruptions of Mind"*).
In this sutta, the Buddha visits Venerable Anuruddha and his companions, who mention that while meditating, they experience a brilliant inner light and the vision of forms (devas/visions), but the visions suddenly vanish.
The Buddha then shares his own personal experience before his full enlightenment (when he was still a Bodhisatta), explaining how he encountered the exact same issue and had to systematically overcome **11 mental imperfections** (*upakkilesas*) that caused his concentration to collapse.
### The Cycle of Doubt and Loss of Vision
The very first imperfection the Buddha describes is **doubt** (*vicikicchā*). He details the process exactly as you remembered:
> "Before my enlightenment, while I was still a bodhisatta... I perceived both the light and the vision of forms. But before long, my light and vision of forms disappeared.
> Then it occurred to me: 'What is the cause, what is the reason why my light and vision of forms disappeared?'
> Then I understood: **'Doubt arose in me, and because of doubt my concentration fell away; when my concentration fell away, the light and the vision of forms disappeared.'** I will act in such a way that doubt will not arise in me again."
> 
### The 11 Imperfections Covered
Whenever the Buddha would resolve one doubt or issue, his mind would settle, the concentration would return, and he would see the light and forms again. However, a *different* subtle mental flaw would then arise and disrupt it.
He systematically overcame 11 of these "corruptions" one by one:
 1. **Doubt** (*vicikicchā*) — Wondering if he's doing it right or what the vision is.
 2. **Inattention** (*amanasikāra*) — Losing focus on the light/form.
 3. **Sloth and Torpor** (*thīna-middha*) — Drowsiness or mental sluggishness.
 4. **Fear / Panic** (*chambhitatta*) — Getting startled or terrified by the intensity of the visions or devas.
 5. **Elation / Excitement** (*ubbilla*) — Getting overly thrilled or ecstatic about seeing the light (*"Wow, look at that!"*), which scatters the mind.
 6. **Inertia / Discomfort** (*duttulla*) — A kind of physical or mental stiffness.
 7. **Excessive Energy** (*accāraddha-viriya*) — Forcing the meditation too hard, leading to restlessness.
 8. **Deficient Energy** (*atilina-viriya*) — Laxity or slacking off too much, slipping back into dullness.
 9. **Longing / Craving** (*abhijjā*) — Developing a desire to hold onto or possess the beautiful vision.
 10. **Perception of Diversity** (*nānatta-saññā*) — Mind wandering off into various multiple thoughts and outer details.
 11. **Excessive Absorption in Forms** (*atinijjhāyitattaṁ rūpānaṁ*) — Staring too intensely at a single form, losing the broad clarity.
### The Ultimate Result
The Buddha explains that by identifying each flaw, realizing it was a corruption, and letting it go, his concentration became steady and unshakable. Once these 11 imperfections were thoroughly cleansed, he was finally able to wield the **Divine Eye** (*dibbacakkhu*) perfectly, allowing him to see light and forms clearly, interact with devas, and ultimately attain full awakening.
