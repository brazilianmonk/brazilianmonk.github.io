# Understanding Pabbajjā, Anagārikas, and the 10 Precepts in the Buddha Sāsana
The path of Buddhist practice encompasses various stages of ethical commitment and renunciation [1]. A crucial distinction exists between the householder life (*gihī*) and the state of renunciation (*pabbajjā*), particularly regarding the observance of the 10 precepts and the role of *anagārikas* and *anagārikās* [1, 2].

---

## 1. The Lay Life (*Gihī*) and Observing 10 Precepts Without Pabbajjā

In the practice of a householder (*gihī* or *gahaṭṭha*), a layperson may follow no precepts, five precepts, or eight precepts. The five precepts constitutes the minimum moral observance in order to have the possibility of attaining jhāna, magga, phala, and Nibbāna. However, a householder can also observe the **10 precepts without taking *pabbajjā*** (without leaving the household life).

* **The Example of Anāgāmīs:** Non-returners (*Anāgāmīs*) naturally observe the 10 precepts while remaining in the householder state. A prominent canonical example is Ghaṭīkāra, a layman recognized by Buddha Kassapa as His foremost supporter, while maintaining the observance of the 10 precepts.
* **Lay Commitment:** Even lay individuals who are not *Anāgāmīs* can choose to observe the 10 precepts while continuing to live as laypeople in the household state.

Thus, observing 10 precepts alone does not automatically constitute leaving the household life.

---

## 2. Defining *Pabbajjā*: The State of Homelessness (*Anagāriya-Bhāva*)

*Pabbajjā* literally refers to leaving the household life and entering the state of homelessness (*Pabbajjāti anagāriyabhāvo*). Anyone who formally leaves household existence enters *anagāriya-bhāva*, regardless of personal views or whether renunciation is undertaken independently or under a monastic teacher.

* **Scope of Renunciation:** Within the Buddha's dispensation (*sāsana*), *pabbajjā* is normally connected to entering the Saṅgha, but is actually not limited exclusively to that; it encompasses all who enter homelessness, specifically including **anagārikas** and **anagārikās** (women renunciates).
* **Position in the Sāsana:** Although *anagārikas* and *anagārikās* are not members of the Saṅgha order, they are:
- 1. true renunciates because they have abandoned the household life and follow the 10 preceps
- 2. included within the sāsana because they follow the Buddha's teachings and can attain jhāna, magga, phala, and Nibbāna. 

---

## 3. The Minimum Ethical Requirement for *Pabbajjā*: The 10 Precepts

For anyone in the state of *pabbajjā*—including *anagārikas* and *anagārikās*—the observance of at least **10 precepts** is the essential minimum requirement.

* **Core Characteristic:** A foundational distinction of any true renunciate (*pabbajita*) is the strict observance of not accepting or using money, gold, or silver.
* **Fulfillment of Renunciate Life:** The Buddha indicates that the life of *pabbajjā* is only complete and fulfilled when the 10 precepts are maintained.
* **Modern Misconceptions:** In contemporary practice, some *anagārikas* and *anagārikās* observe only eight precepts. However, observing only eight precepts while claiming a *pabbajjā* life is insufficient and unaligned with the core teachings. While laypeople can successfully practice eight precepts in household life and be successful in meditation, a *pabbajita* must observe the 10 precepts to fulfill the renunciate standard and attain jhāna, magga, phala, and Nibbāna.

---

## 4. Spiritual Attainments for Renunciates

When *anagārikas* and *anagārikās* follow the 10 precepts and the correct practice, they are fully capable of achieving noble spiritual attainments—including *jhānas*, path and fruition (*maggaphala*), and *Nibbāna* [2, 3]. Conversely, if a *pabbajita* fails to maintain the 10 precepts, their renunciate practice remains incomplete, rendering them incapable of attaining *jhānas* or ultimate liberation [2].

---

### Key Takeaways
1. A layperson (*gihī*) can observe 10 precepts while remaining in household life (e.g., Anāgāmīs like Ghaṭīkāra).
2. *Pabbajjā* means leaving the household state, which includes both *anagārikas* and *anagārikās* as well as other types.
3. For those who undertake *pabbajjā*, observing the 10 precepts—including refraining from money—is the indispensable minimum requirement for spiritual fulfillment and liberation.
