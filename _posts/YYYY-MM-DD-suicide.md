To make it clear: i dont encourage suicde, but even though it's a sensitive topic, there's benefits in analyzing it. Suicde is not the same as what is referred to as killing in the sāsana. Killing is always with dosacitta. Unintentional killing is not "killing". Suicde initially seems to be the same but it is a broader topic. If it were always done with dosacitta, then it would not explain some cases (below). My point is that it is not necessarily done with the vadhakacetanā of dosacitta. Here are some situations related to this topic that argue for the difference: 
- arahats starving themselves (arahats do not take their own lives with their own hands (sappadāsattheravatthu))
- arahats combusting themselves in very long lifespan sāsanas (to be confirmed; mentioned in Koṇḍañña's sāsana)
- ariyas attempted suicide (mahānāma the sakya): ariyas cannot kill, so if suicide was the same, they would not attempt it
- bodhisatta's dānaparamatthapāramī (as a hare, for example, and possibly the case of the tigress): pāramī is kusalacitta with ñāṇasampayutta
- mahābodhisattas as devas or brahmas determining to end their lives  (adhimuttakālakiriya), which is a form of suicde. The case of the brahmas makes it clear that there is no dosa involved in this case since brahmas do not have dosacittas.
- jhānapuggala's suicde: a puthujjana eith jhāna does not kill and this one killed himself (godhikasuttavaṇṇanā)
- non-textual: some monks with pubbenivāsānussati can see situations in their past in which they have given themselves to hungry animals as pāramī for buddhahood without killing intention or self-combusted with iddhis as ascetics (not confirmable, but agree with the case of the hare and tigress).

In our present sāsana, the Buddha laid down a rule against it so monks cannot commit suicde. If they do, the mind of breaking the rules occurs and acts as āṇavītikkamantarāya, which acts as a hindrance for sagga and magga. This seems to conclude that they would be necessarily reborn in duggati. 

Generally, it is something that should not be done but I'm trying to encompass all the possibilities. In most cases, people commit suicide with unwholesome minds and wrong view, which probably takes them to apāya because of the circumstances in which they pass away. 

It's a sensitive topic and there isn't a lot of information about it as far as I and my teachers see.

- don't kill yourself, as you will most probably be reborn in apāya
- If one is a monk, apāya for sure because of the āṇavītikkamantarāya
- killing others: always dosacitta (akusala)
