To make it clear: i dont encourage suicide, but even though it's a sensitive topic, there's benefits in analyzing it. Suicide is not the same as what is referred to as killing in the sāsana. Killing is always with dosacitta. Unintentional killing is not "killing". Suicide initially seems to be the same but it is a broader topic. If it were always done with dosacitta, then it would not explain some cases (below). My point is that it is not necessarily with dosacitta. Here are some situations related to this topic:
- arahats starving themselves
- arahats combusting themselves in very long lifespan sāsanas (to be confirmed; mentioned in Koṇḍañña's sāsana)
- ariyas attempted suicide (mahānāma the sakya): ariyas cannot kill, so if suicide was the same, they would not attempt it
- bodhisatta's dānaparamatthapāramī (as a hare, for example): pāramī is kusalacitta with ñāṇasampayutta
- mahābodhisattas as devas or brahmas determining to end their lives  (adhimuttakālakiriya), which is a form of suicde. The case of the brahmas makes it clear that there is no dosa involved in this case since brahmas do not have dosacittas.

In our present sāsana, the Buddha laid down a rule against it so monks cannot commit suicde. If they do, the mind of breaking the rules occurs and acts as āṇavītikkamantarāya, which acts as a hindrance for sagga and magga. This seems to conclude that they would be necessarily reborn in duggati. 

Generally, it is something that should not be done but I'm trying to encompass all the possibilities. In most cases, people commit suicide with unwholesome minds and wrong view, which probably takes them to apāya because of the circumstances in which they pass away. 

It's a sensitive topic and there isn't a lot of information about it as far as I and my teachers see.
