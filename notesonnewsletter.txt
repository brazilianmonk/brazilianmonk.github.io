Adding a newsletter to a Jekyll site hosted on GitHub Pages is incredibly straightforward. Since your site is static, you don't process data on a server; instead, you just embed a simple HTML form provided by your newsletter service that captures emails and sends them to your provider.
Here are the best pathways to start for free, their limitations, and how to scale them later when your audience grows.
## 1. The Best Free Pathways for Static Sites
### Option A: Kit (Formerly ConvertKit) — *Best for Scaling List Size*
Kit is highly recommended if you expect to grow your subscriber count quickly without paying immediately.
 * **The Free Plan:** Allows up to **10,000 subscribers** with unlimited email sends.
 * **Limitations:** The free tier heavily restricts automated drip campaigns (like automated welcome sequences or multi-day courses). It is mostly meant for sending live broadcasts.
 * **Jekyll Integration:** They provide easy embed scripts or HTML forms to paste directly into your layouts.
### Option B: Sender or EmailOctopus — *Best for Free Automations*
If you want to send an automatic welcome email right after someone joins, these platforms give you advanced features for free.
 * **The Free Plan:** **Sender** gives you up to 2,500 subscribers and 15,000 emails/month. **EmailOctopus** offers up to 2,500 subscribers and 10,000 emails/month.
 * **Limitations:** Unlike Kit, you hit a much lower subscriber ceiling before you have to start paying.
 * **Jekyll Integration:** Both offer straightforward HTML form generation to drop into your site.
### Option C: Buttondown — *Best for Minimalist & Markdown Lovers*
Buttondown is a lightweight, privacy-first newsletter tool built by a solo developer. If you write your Jekyll blog posts in Markdown, you will love Buttondown because it lets you write emails in Markdown, too.
 * **The Free Plan:** Free for up to **100 subscribers**.
 * **Limitations:** The free cap is very small. Advanced features like automation, custom domains, or tracking analytics require paid add-ons.
## 2. General Limitations of "Free" Tiers
When running a newsletter on a free plan, you will almost always face three bottlenecks:
 1. **Branding:** Free plans usually force a "Powered by [Provider]" badge at the bottom of every email.
 2. **No Custom Email Domain:** You can usually send emails from your personal account, but to send from an official domain (e.g., newsletter@yourdomain.org), you often have to authenticate your DNS settings, which some free tiers gate or limit.
 3. **Basic Automation:** Welcome series, branching sequences, and automated RSS-to-email updates are usually locked behind paid walls (except on platforms like Sender).
## 3. How to Scale It Up Later
As your supporters grow and the cost begins to rise, you have two primary scaling strategies:
### Pathway 1: The "Stay on the Platform" Upgrade
Once you exceed your free cap (e.g., crossing 2,500 on Sender or 10,000 on Kit), you simply put a card on file.
 * **Pros:** Seamless transition. Your data stays in one place, your forms don't break, and you unlock full automation features.
 * **Cons:** Can become expensive if your list hits tens of thousands of subscribers.
### Pathway 2: The Technical, High-Scale Route (Amazon SES)
If you reach thousands of subscribers and want to minimize the financial burden on your supporters, you can transition to a **self-hosted or hybrid setup**:
 * **How it works:** You use a platform like **BigMailer** or a self-hosted software like **Ghost** / **Sendy**, and connect it to **Amazon SES (Simple Email Service)**.
 * **The Cost:** Amazon SES charges roughly **$0.10 per 1,000 emails** sent. It is drastically cheaper than commercial platforms at high volumes.
 * **Pros:** Keeps ongoing operational costs close to zero.
 * **Cons:** Requires technical setup, managing your own subscriber lists, and ensuring your domain's email deliverability settings (SPF, DKIM) are perfectly configured so you don't land in spam folders.
**Recommendation to start:** Pick **Kit** if you want the highest subscriber ceiling without paying a cent, or **Sender** if you absolutely need an automated welcome email sequence right from day one. Both will let you export your subscriber list as a .csv file at any time if you decide to switch infrastructure later.
