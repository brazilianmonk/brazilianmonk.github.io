    • 3 types of donors
      • dānadāsa: promising a certain dāna and giving less (or nothing); or giving less/of lower quality than for themselves - one is a 'slave' of the object since one cannot overcome attachment to it
      • dānasahāya: promising a certain dāna and giving as promised; or giving the same amount/of the same quality than for themselves - one is a friend of the object due to not relinquishing the liking for it
      • dānapati: promising a certain dāna and giving more; or giving more/of higher quality than for themselves - one is a master of the object since one can overcome attachment to it

      • 3 types of dukkha
        • a. Dukkha-dukkha: reality that represent physical and mental pain known as suffering.
        • b. Vipariṇāma-dukkha: reality that bring suffering due to change.
        • c. Saṅkhāra-dukkha: the reality that bring suffering due to conditionality
      • 4 senses / aspects of dukkha:
        • i. Pīḷanaṭṭho – in the sense of oppressed
        • ii. Saṅkhataṭṭho – in the sense of conditioned
        • iii. Santāpaṭṭho – in the sense of exhausted
        • iv. Viparināmaṭṭho – in the sense of arisen and fallen
    • 2. Dukkhasamudayaṃ ariyasaccam: noble truth of cause of suffering.
      • Saṃ= saṃyoga (coming together), U = uppatti (becoming) and Aya = kāraṇa (condition).
      • So taṅhā is the main cause of arising of suffering (cause of bhāva). It is three types:
        • a. Kāmataṅhā – craving for sensual pleasure.
        • b. Bhavataṅhā – craving for existence.
        • c. Vibhavataṅhā - craving for annihilation.
      • Four senses / aspects of the cause of suffering:
        • i. āyūhanaṭṭho - in the sense of grasping
        • ii. nidānaṭṭho - in the sense of cause
        • iii. saṃyogaṭṭho – in the sense of attachment
        • iv. paḷibodhaṭṭho - in the sense of impediment

      • Four aspects of cessation:
        • i. Nissaraṇaṭṭho – escape
        • ii. Vivekaṭṭho – peace
        • iii. Asaṅkataṭṭho – unconditioned
