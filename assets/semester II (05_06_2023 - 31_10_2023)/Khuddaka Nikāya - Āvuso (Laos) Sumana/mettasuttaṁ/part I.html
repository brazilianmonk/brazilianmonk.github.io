    • brahmavihāra
      • “loving kindness, compassion, and sympathetic joy safeguard people while equanimity protects righteousness.”
      • mettā
        • characteristic: the mode of friendliness function: to promote friendliness manifestation: the disappearance of ill will proximate cause: recognizing the joyful state of all beings / seeing beings as agreeable
        • sampatti (accomplishment) and vipatti (failure) of mettā

          • vipatti: love as affection
        • enemies of mettā

          • far enemy: byāpāda (non ill-will)
        • 4 types of "love" #[[khuddaka exam]]
          • attapema (self-love): connected to the natural instinct of survival; when strong it may lead to selfishness, and in extreme cases, this selfcenteredness can lead someone to harm or even kill others in order to protect themselves. “{{914552542939246::natthi attasamaṃ pemaṃ}}” (There is no love equal to self-love)
          • lustful love (rāga): connected to the natural reproductive instincts; when one gets what one wants it is considered successful but when not it leads to resentment; Pemasutta: cattārimāni, bhikkhave, (pemāni) jāyanti. katamāni cattāri? {{6283674367332961::pemā pemaṃ jāyati, pemā doso jāyati, dosā pemaṃ jāyati, dosā doso jāyati}} ((1) love is born of love, (2) hate is born of love, (3) love is born of hate, and and (4) hate is born of hate)

      • background #[[khuddaka exam]]
        • once, the Blessed One was staying in {{4955221040580262::Sāvatthī}} ; before the rains retreat He explained various meditation subjects that would suit the {{4955221040580262::84,000}} different types of temperaments (mainly rāga, dosa, moha, vitakka, saddhā and buddhi caritas); a group of 500 monks, after learning their meditation subjects, took His permission and went in search of a suitable place to dwell; wandering by stages they reached {{4955221040580262::a mountain in the Himalayas made of nīla stones, shady forests, silver-like sand, and pools of pure water}}; the nearby village of a {{4955221040580262::1.000}} faithful families welcomed them and invited them to spend the retreat there, building them huts with all needs; after accepting the invitation they remained there practicing diligently; their radiance surpassed that of the tree deities and the deities had to descend from their mansions and roam about; displeased the monks would stay for 3 months, they {{4955221040580262::conjured up frightening sights and sounds, and unpleasant odors, disturbing their practice and causing them bodily and mental distress}}; one day in conclave the elder brought up the issue and they decided to return to the Blessed One for guidance; after proper investigation the Blessed One gave them instructions on how a forest-dwelling monk should practice; there should be two sessions, one in the morning and one in the evening, of the following: {{4955221040580262::mettā, protective discourses, foulness, recollection of death and attention to the eight important reminders}}; after that He taught the mettasutta for cultivating loving-kindness, for their protection and for serving as a vipassanāpadakajjhāna, and sent them back to the very same place. mem aid: In Sāvatthi, before the rains, the Buddha explains kammaṭṭhānas, a group of 500 goes to a mountain and while dwelling there is disturbed by local devas; taking advice from the Buddha they listen to the mettasutta and return to that place
      • 3 purposes of the mettāsutta

        • vipassanāpadakajjhānattha - for the purpose of using the jhāna as a base for vipassanā
      • a forest dweller (āraññakena) should know
        • "apica kho āraññakena pariharaṇaṃ ñātabbaṃ. seyyathidaṃ – sāyaṃ pātaṃ karaṇavasena dve mettā dve parittā dve asubhā dve maraṇassatī aṭṭhamahāsaṃvegavatthusamāvajjanañca, aṭṭha mahāsaṃvegavatthūni nāma jātijarābyādhimaraṇaṃ cattāri apāyadukkhānīti, atha vā jātijarābyādhimaraṇāni cattāri, apāyadukkhaṃ pañcamaṃ, atīte vaṭṭamūlakaṃ dukkhaṃ, anāgate vaṭṭamūlakaṃ dukkhaṃ, paccuppanne āhārapariyeṭṭhimūlakaṃ dukkhaṁ" "A monk who resides in the forest should know how to proceed. In the evening and morning, there should be two sessions of practicing loving-kindness (mettā), two recitations of protection (Paritta), two sessions of contemplating the unattractive nature of the body (asubha), two sessions of mindfulness of death (maraṇānussati), and attention to the eight important reminders (aṭṭhamahāsaṃvegavatthu). The eight important reminders are: birth, old age, illness, death, and the suffering in the four unhappy realms. Alternatively, the first four are birth, old age, illness, and death, followed by the suffering in the unhappy realm, the suffering resulting from past actions, the suffering anticipated in future lives, and the suffering arising from the struggle to find food in the present."

      • anatthakusala (one skillful in what is not good) #[[khuddaka exam]]
        • anesana: one earns one's living by any of the 21 improper means (by gifts of bamboos, leaves, flowers, fruits, toothwood, mouthwash, bathing implements, bath powder, clay; by flattery, beansoupery, and fondling; by conveying messages; by the work of a physician, by the work of a messenger, by going on errands; by presenting a portion of his almsfood as a gift; by geomancy, astrology, or palmistry)
        • agocara: one moves about in the six kinds of wrong resort (prostitutes, widows, old maids, eunuchs, bhikkhunīs and bars)
        • saṁsaṭṭha: he forms bonds with kings, royal ministers, sectarians, and their disciples, in the inappropriate way that laypeople form bonds
        • assaddhādikula: he associates with, resorts to, and visits families that are without faith and confidence, that are miserly, abusive, and contemptuous, that wish for loss, harm, discomfort, and trouble

      • attha (true benefit / welfare): 4 types of sīla #[[khuddaka exam]]
        • pātimokkhasaṃvarasīla: fulfilled by saddhā
        • indriyasaṃvarasīla: not giving rise to covetousness or dejection when objects strike against the six sense doors; fulfilled by sati
        • ājīvapārisuddhisīla: obtaining requisites by righteous means; fulfilled by vīriya
        • paccayasannissitasīla: reflecting on the proper use of requisites and the four sampajañña; fulfilled by paññā
      • 3 meanings of atthakusala (one skilled in what is good) #[[khuddaka exam]]
        • one skilled in sīla: one who purifies the sīla and when reflecting on it, does not find even the slightest fault
        • one skilled in samādhi: one who exerts himself to suppress the defilements and obtains the meditative attainments
        • one skilled in paññā: one who analizes conditioned realities and attains arahantship is the foremost of those skillful in the good
        • in brief: it means one is skillful in good behavior (sīla), attainments (samādhi) and path and fruition (paññā)

    • Assignment - fifteen great qualities in mettāsutta #[[khuddaka exam]]
      • by Ariyañāṇa Bhikkhu
      • sakko
        • One is able (sakko) when one fulfills the second and fourth factors of striving, namely appābādho appātaṅko and āraddhavīriyo
      • uju & suhuju
        • One is considered upright (uju) or very upright (suhuju) according to four methods:
          • uju: occasional uprightness or while one is young suhuju: habitual uprightness
          • uju: asaṭha (not showing qualities one does not possess) suhuju: amāyāvī (not hiding one's faults)
          • uju: abandoning bodily and verbal crookedness suhuju: abandoning mental crookedness
          • uju: not showing the virtues that one does not have (asaṭha) suhuju: not consenting to gains accrued due to non-existent virtues
      • suvaco
        • One is easy to admonish (suvaco) when one does not show anger, displeasure, give excuses or starts criticizing another when being admonished, but listens respectfully, like Sāriputta Mahāthera. It is of two kinds as āmisahetusuvaco and dhammagāravahetusuvaco. The second is meant here
      • mudu
        • One is gentle (mudu) when one acts joyfully and kindly while performing physical and verbal actions, avoiding conflict and opposition, and cultivating harmony, being pleasant and welcoming like a safe harbour
      • anatimānī
        • One if free from superiority conceit (atimāna) due to birth, clan, and so on, like a caṇḍala boy, like a viper without fangs, like a bull without horns or like a floor mat
      • santussako
        • One is content (santussako) when possessed of the twelve types of santosa. Alternatively one is content with what is one's own (sakena tussako), with what is available (santena tussako) and content evenly (samena tussako)
      • subharo
        • One is easy to support (subharo) when one is content with what is offered being it little or much, and having made use of it happily, go about doing their own work
      • appakicco
        • One has few duties (appakicco) when one is not engaged in time consuming duties such as those in relation to building monasteries, managing Saṅgha properties, administrating novices and workers. One is free from the ten impediments:
          • "āvāso ca kulaṃ lābho, gaṇo kammañca pañcamaṃ, addhānaṃ ñāti ābādho, gantho iddhīti te dasāti"
      • sallahukavuttī
        • One has a light livelihood (sallahukavuttī) when one lives depending on few possessions, like a bird, such as the aṭṭhaparikkhāra:
          • "ticīvarañca pattañca vāsi suci ca bandhanaṁ parissāvanena aṭṭhete yuttayogassa bhikkhuno"
      • santindriyo
        • One has calm faculties (santindriyo or anuddhatindriyo) when one restrains lust and other defilements from arising when encountering pleasant or unpleasant objects
      • nipako
        • One is wise (nipako) when one is possessed of wisdom in protecting virtues, finding requisites, in the sattasappāya, or alternatively in the four sampajañña, namely, that of purpose, of suitability, of domain and of non-delusion
      • appagabbho
        • One is not impudent (appagabbho) when free of bodily, verbal and mental impudence such as disrespectful behavior towards teachers, in public places, or towards the Saṅgha.
      • kulesu ananugiddho
        • One is without attachment to families (kulesu ananugiddho) when one approaches suitable families drawing back body and mind, always as a newcomer, without attachment, like the moon that comes and goes without sorrowing with them (na sahasokī), and without rejoicing with them (na sahanandī)
      • na ca khuddamācare kiñci yena viññū pare upavadeyyuṁ
        • One should not perform even small misdeeds of body, speech or mind, for which one would experience the present danger (sandiṭṭhika ādīnava) of being censured by the wise. One should not disregard any evil as little, as it is said in Dhp 121:
          • "māvamaññetha pāpassa, na mantaṃ āgamissati, udabindunipātena, udakumbhopi pūrati, bālo pūrati pāpassa, thokaṃ thokampi ācinaṃ"

    • 3 santussako (content) #[[khuddaka exam]]
      • sakena tussako (one is satisfied with what is one’s own): related to the 4 nissaya (paṁsukūlacīvara, piṇḍapāta, rukkhamūlasesāsana and pūtimuttabhesajja)
      • santena tussako (one is satisfied with what is available)
      • samena tussako (one is evenly satisfied): one is neither attached to desirable objects nor aversed to undesirable ones but is content with all objects with a balanced mind or with intellectual equanimity
      • 4 ariyavaṁsa #[[khuddaka exam]]
        • cīvarasantosa
        • piṇḍapātasantosa
        • senāsanasantosa
        • pahānabhāvanāramatā (delight in the meditation of letting go)
        • note:
          • the first three should be practiced as follows:
            • 1. a monk is content
            • 2. he praises contentment
            • 3. he does not engage in a wrong search (anesanā)
            • 4. he is not agitated when he does not receive them
            • 5. he makes use of them without infatuation, seeing the danger in them and understanding the escape from them
            • 6. he does not extol himself or disparage others because of them
          • while for the fourth: a monk should not be content with a partial liberation and wholesome qualities; he should find delight in their development; he does not extol himself or disparage others based on this

      • 4 types of person according to bhara #[[khuddaka exam]]
        • attano dubbharo (one difficult to support by himself): one who is not satisfied with what is offered and keeps looking for something else, even to the point of coming back empty handed

          • yo pana sālimaṃsodanādīnaṃ patte pūretvā dinnepi dummukhabhāvaṃ anattamanabhāvameva ca dasseti, tesaṃ vā sammukhāva taṃ piṇḍapātaṃ "kiṃ tumhehi dinna"nti apasādento, sāmaṇeragahaṭṭhādīnampi deti, ayaṃ upaṭṭhākānaṃ dubbharo

        • upaṭṭhākānaṃ subharo (one easy to be supported by others): one who is pleased with the donation and does not criticize it
          • yo ca paresampi appaṃ vā bahuṃ vā lūkhaṃ vā paṇītaṃ vā dānaṃ ahīḷetvā attamano vippasannamukho hutvā tesaṃ sammukhāva paribhuñjitvā yāti, ayaṃ upaṭṭhākānaṃ subharo.

        • ākāse pāṇi na sajjati na gayhati na bajjhati (hand in space): the Buddha moved his hand in the empty space and said that just as the hand is not stuck, held or caught in space, in the same way, when a monk approaches families, his mind should not be stuck, held or caught amidst families, thinking, “may those desiring gains obtain gains, may those desiring merits gain merits!” he is happy when others get something as if he himself had gotten it.

        • he often sees a woman
        • when there is the seen, he becomes close
        • being close, one becomes intimate
        • being intimate, lust overcomes him
        • when his mind is overwhelmed by lust, it is to be expected that he will practice the holy life without satisfaction, or commit one of the corrupt offenses, or resign the training and return to a lower life
