      • visayappavatti
        • visayappavatti ‒ definition
          • the presentation of an object to consciousness at one of the six doors, or the occurrence of states of consciousness upon the presentation of an object visaya: object, objective field pavatti: occurance, appearance, manifestation
        • 6 sixes
          • 6 dvāra
          • 6 vatthu
          • 6 viññāṇa
          • 6 ārammaṇa
          • 6 visayappavatti
          • 6 vīthi
        • 6 visayappavatti: 4 types of objects of the five senses and 2 mind-door objects
          • five sense-doors:
            • atimahantārammaṇa (very great / distinguished object)
            • mahantārammaṇa (great / distinguished object)
            • parittārammaṇa (slight / undistinguished object)
            • atiparittārammaṇa (very slight /very undistinguished object)
          • mind-door:
            • vibhūtārammaṇa (clear object)
            • avibhūtārammaṇa (obscure object)
            • note:
              • ledi: there is also ati vibhūta, vibhūta, avibhūta and atiavibhūta
          • maha and paritta in this context refers to what?→the clarity of the object for consciousness, indicating the number of vīthicittas that arise taking it as object
        • 4 main types of objects according to prominence
          • atimahantārammaṇa (1) (very distinguished object)
            • highly prominent / distinguished object
            • impinges on any of the five doors
            • impinges after one mind moment from its genesis (during the second mind moment)
          • mahantārammaṇa (2) (distinguished object)
            • prominent / distinguished object but less than atimahanta
            • impinges on any of the five doors
            • impinges after two or three mind moments from its genesis (during the third or fourth mind moment); therefore it is of 2 types
          • parittārammaṇa (6) (undistinguished object)
            • undistinguished object less prominent than mahanta
            • impinges on any of the five doors
            • impinges after four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine mind moments after genesis (on the 5th, 6th, 7th, 8th, 9th or 10th mind-moment, respectively) therefore it is of 6 types
          • atiparittārammaṇa (6) (very undistinguished object)
            • very undistinguished object / non-prominent objects
            • impinges on any of the five doors
            • impinges after ten, eleven, twelve, thirteen, fourteen, or fifteen mind moments from genesis; therefore it is of 6 types

            • weak during the uppādakkhaṇa strong during the ṭhitikkhaṇa very weak during the bhaṅgakkhaṇa why? bc it has the support of tejo, oja, jīvitindriya, citta & cetasikas (pacchājātapaccaya) during the ṭhitikkhaṇa; not during upppādakkhaṇa

            • strong during the uppādakkhaṇa weaker during ṭhitikkhaṇa very weak during bhaṅgakkhaṇa why? bc it has the support of anantara-, samanantara-, anantarupanissaya-paccayas during the uppādakkhaṇa; not during ṭhitikkhaṇa

            • the impingement of sense objects with pasāda (such as in the case of rūpārammaṇa in which it produces an image while being far away)

      • causes for the arising of manodvāravīthi
        • 4 main causes for the arising of manodvāravīthi
          • diṭṭhavasena: manodvāravīthi caused by an object experienced through the 5 doors (diṭṭha here includes all 5 doors); it is of 2 types:
            • immediately upon experiencing it: one sees with cakkhudvāravīthi rūpārammaṇas of a tree and the following manodvāravīthis occur recognizing and thinking about the tree
            • by recalling it at a later time: one remembers having seen the tree (tbc)
          • diṭṭhasambandhavasena: manodvāravīthi caused by an object connected to what was diṭṭha, by inference from what was directly perceived: e.g.: after seeing the tree, one remember a song about trees; one sees smoke, and understands there is fire
          • sutavasena: manodvāravīthi caused by an object acquired by oral or written report, not direct experience - e.g.: one who has never seen snow, hears and thinks about it (note: under diṭṭha, hearing a sound is included; here the mind focuses on what is meant, not the sound itself)
            • hearing
              • diṭṭhavasena: hear the sound of a peacock; mdv occur thinknng "this is the sound of the peacock"
              • diṭṭhasambandhavasena: hear the sound of the peacock; mdv occur taking something connected to that sound: "the sound of a cat"
              • sutavasena: hear the teacher talking about the ocean; mdv occur taking the ocean as object
              • sutasambandhavasena: hear the teacher talking about the ocean; mdv occur taking something else connected to the ocean "a boat"
          • sutasambandhavasena: manodvāravīthi caused by an object connected to what was suta - e.g.: after hearing about snow, one remembers a friend who lives in a cold place
          • note:
            • diṭṭha, in other cases, can mean objects seen by the eye-door only
            • moreover, manodvāravīthis can occur due to:
              • paripucchāvasena: in response to others asking questions
              • parivitakkavasena: during contemplation and thinking
              • nijjhānakkhantivasena: when one comes into a conclusion
              • anubodhavasena: during vipassanā
              • paṭivedhavasena: magga/phalañāṇa
              • samādhivasena: abhiññāṇa
              • devatopasaṁharaṇavasena: obects made manifest by deities perticularly during dreams
              • kammavasena: gatinimitta in maraṇāsanna
              • dhātukhobhavasena: due to imbalances in physical elements (vāta, pitta, and semha)
              • as well as experiences of hunger, thirst, physical pains, meeting enemies, and meeting friends

                • bhavaṅga, though having its own object, is in a constant state of excitement or "buzz", inclining towards other objects, seeking to acquire a clear cognition of an object manasikāra, present in the bhavaṅga, constantly causes it to vibrate, directing consciousness repeatedly to advert to other objects which have gained conditions to appear when an object acquires sufficient prominence through various operative conditions, it draws the mental continuum out of bhavaṅga

            • consideration of one's remaining lifespan
