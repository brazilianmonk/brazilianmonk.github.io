  • introduction #edited
    • what is the purpose of the nissaggiya pācittiya rules?
      • monks should be careful when receiving, preparing and using the 4 requisites and these rules are to avoid inappropriate acts related to these requisites
    • what happens when a N. pācittiya rule is broken?
      • the item related to the training rule becomes a nissaggiya item which is an item that has to be forfeited, in addition to the monk committing a pct offence
    • what is a nissaggiya item / requisite?
      • it is the item related to the NP rule transgressed by a monk which has to be forfeited
    • what is nissajjana?
      • the forfeiture of a nissaggiya item to a single bhikkhu, to a gaṇa, or to the saṅgha, or its abandonment in the midst of the saṅgha as a vinayakamma
    • when purposely transgressing any rule a {{6377387643295387::dosamūla}}citta arises (anādariya)
    • making use of a nissaggiya item prior to the vinayakamma entails a {{38962323968689505::dkṭ}}
    • types of nissaggiya items according to what happens after nissajjana
      • some must be returned to the offender (np1...)
      • some cannot be made use of by the offender
      • some must be forfeited and another object received in return must be used by the offender (np22)
      • gold, silver and currency (rūpiya) accepted by a monk must be relinquished amidst the Saṅgha
    • 3 types of NP rules according to how the object is forfeited
      • bhikkhu / gaṇa / Saṅgha: rules in which the object is forfeited to the Saṅgha, a group of bhikkhus or a single bhikkhu
      • Saṅgha: rules in which the object is forfeited to the Saṅgha
      • Saṅgha majjhe: rules in which the object is forfeited in the midst of the Saṅgha (gold, silver or money) (not to the Saṅgha)

    • np 2 - dutiyakaṭhinasikkhāpada (uddositasikkhāpada)
      • background #editing
        • The Tathāgata was residing one time at the {{2098409862527062::Jetavana temple, Sāvatthi}}. During this period, most bhikkhus were in the habit of leaving their robes with other bhikkhus, and travelling to the many surrounding districts wearing the uttarāsaṅga (upper robe) and antarāvāsaka (inner robe) robes. Those robes that were left behind would become moldy. Having been aware of this situation, the Buddha declared this training rule On another occasion, in the city of {{2098409862527062::Kosambi}}, a bhikkhu became ill. A messenger was sent by his relatives inviting him to be taken care of. The monk stated: “The Buddha has preached that a dawn must not be passed, separated from the three robes. I have fallen ill”. The Buddha who came to know about this proclaimed: “Anujānāmi, bhikkhave, gilānassa bhikkhuno ticīvarena avippavāsasammutiṃ dātuṃ'' and declared a sub statute as “aññatra bhikkhusammutiyā” allowing monks to stay apart from the three robes if an allowance from the Saṅgha is obtained.
      • description
        • a monk should be responsible of his ticīvara at all times, but specially at night; if unable to be with the robes during the night, at the very least the monk should be with it at the break of dawn, otherwise the robe will become nissaggiya and a pācittiya offense will be committed

      • background
        • The Tathāgata was residing in the Veḷuvana monastery in the city of Rājagaha. One day, Bhikkhuni Uppalavanna meditated after her meal at the forest called Andhavana.     • np 16 - eḷakalomasikkhāpada
      • background
        • The Tathāgata was residing at the Jetavana temple near the city of Sāvatthi. At this time, a bhikkhu who was on his way to Sāvatthi received goat fur (or wool). He made a pile of it, covered it with his upper robe and carried it. The people who saw this asked him mockingly: “How much have you taken? What is the profit you made?” This made the bhikkhu feel shy and speechless. The Buddha who came to know about this incident declared this training rule.
      • Training rule in Pali
        • Bhikkhuno paneva addhānamaggappaṭipannassa eḷakalomāni uppajjeyyuṃ. Ākaṅkhamānena bhikkhunā paṭiggahetabbāni. Paṭiggahetvā tiyojanaparamaṃ sahatthā haritabbāni, asante hārake. Tato ce uttari hareyya, asantepi hārake, nissaggiyaṃ pācittiyaṃ. If a monk receives goat fur while travelling, he may accept it if desired. Once accepted, if there’s no one else to carry it, he can only carry it by himself, up to three yojanas. If he happens to carry it beyond the limit of three yojanas in the absence of a carrier, a nissaggiya pācittiya offence will occur.
      • Description of the training rule
        • A bhikkhu who receives goat fur must not carry it beyond {{9826691569749155::three yojanas}} by himself. The Tathāgata imposed this training rule because it is inappropriate for bhikkhus to do so. When taken the goat fur beyond three yojanas, the goat fur becomes {{9826691569749155::nissaggiya}} while the bhikkhu will have committed a {{9826691569749155::pācittiya}} offence. When the three-yojana boundary is passed, by taking the initial step therein, a {{9826691569749155::dukkaṭa}} offence will be committed. {{9826691569749155::Pācittiya}} offence will be committed upon the second step. The bhikkhu can carry the goat fur within the {{9826691569749155::three-yojana}} distance without committing any offence.
        • If the goat fur is carried back and forth within the area between the location of receiving the goat fur and the three-yojana mark, even if the monk walks more than three yojanas in total, {{5069783648336348::there is no offense}}. An offence will not be committed under this training rule as long as the monk does not cross the threeyojana boundary (from the receiving location). If the bhikkhu who received the goat fur gets another person to carry it, passing the three-yojana boundary will {{5069783648336348::not result in an offence}}. Further, it should be known that carrying an object made out of goat fur will {{5069783648336348::not result in an offence}}
        • if the initial three-yojana mark is crossed while carrying fur that is stolen by thieves and returned, and while carrying goat fur that has been returned after being forfeited through the nissajjana vinayakamma {{18109269204126632::an offence will not occur}}. If the goat fur is received this way and had to be carried again, an additional three yojanas can be covered from the point of the second receiving. The initial three-yojana mark is not taken into consideration. This is the opinion demonstrated in the Gaṇṭhipada and Vimativinodanī. According to the Sāratthadīpanī, there is no limit in the distance the goat fur could be carried when received the second time.
        • If the goat fur is received in one particular place, a distance of three yojanas can be travelled forward from thereon, and take up residence therein. From that point onwards, goat fur can be carried for another three yojanas. This training rule is {{4658933700398924::acittaka}} and {{4658933700398924::nosaññāvimokkha}}. Even if one is unaware of or doubting the distance travelled while the three yojana miles are exceeded, an offence {{4658933700398924::will occur}}
      • Factors
        • 1. Goat fur that has not been made into an object
        • 2. Receiving it for the first time
        • 3. Passing the three-yojana mark
        • 4. Not being a journey in which the monk travels more than three yojana back and forth within the three-yojana boundary.
        • 5. Not a journey that was initiated after residing in a place within the three-yojana boundary after the first receiving
      • Forfeiture
        • Imāni me, bhante, eḷakalomāni tiyojanaṃ atikkāmitāni nissaggiyāni. Imānāhaṃ āyasmato nissajjāmi.

      • background

        • Jātarūpa specifically denotes {{5828879301133194::gold}}, while in certain contexts, the term "rajata" pertains to {{5828879301133194::silver}}.       • background
        • One pot maker invited monks to ask him for bowls if needed. The bhikkhus exceeded their limit and kept asking for more bowls. This made the potter unable to support his family. Seeing this, people started to scold the bhikkhus. Then the Buddha promulgated a rule prohibiting monks from asking for bowls (from aññātaka and appavārita lay devotees) Due to this rule, monks whose bowls were broken went for piṇḍapāta and received alms with their palms. Seeing this, people started to criticise monks saying, “Disciples of the Gotama go for alms and receive food with their palms like ascetics of other religions.” Knowing this, the Buddha amended the rule allowing monks whose bowls are broken to ask for bowls from lay devotees.
