  • np 11 - kosiyasikkhāpada
    • background
      • The Tathāgata was residing at the Aggalāva Cetiya in the city of Āḷavī. At this time, Chabbaggiya monks approached silk-makers and stated: “Friends! Please cook more silkworms and make silk thread. May you give them to us as well. We also would like to prepare silk blankets/rugs”. Then the silkmakers criticised monks since such advice is inappropriate for monks who should abstain from causing harm to living beings. The training rule was imposed based on this incident.
    • training rule in pāḷi
      • Yo pana bhikkhu kosiyamissakaṃ santhataṃ kārāpeyya, nissaggiyaṃ pācittiyaṃ. (If a monk prepares a blanket/rug that contains silk thread, it will result in a nissaggiya pācittiya offence.)
    • description
      • purpose of the rule→the Tathāgata prohibited bhikkhus from preparing blankets or rugs using such thread as it would indirectly encourage silkworms to be cooked; this rule shows the Buddha's consideration for the implications of the process behind the production of products
    • pāḷi terms
      • santhata (non-woven blanket/rug/covering)
        • according to the commentaries, a santhata is like a mat / rug that can be used for sitting and lying down and carried here and there made with layers of thread stacked on top of each other (any type of thread), and by sprinkling gruel on its surface; it is not woven; santhata mentioned in other training rules are also non-woven rugs
        • kosa↔cocoon
        • kosiya↔thread prepared by cooking silkworms
        • kosiyakāraka↔one who makes the silk, silk-maker, or silk-worm
    • instances of offenses
      • preparing or making others prepare a blanket, mixing silk thread: {{06570485861705522::dkṭ}} once the making of the blanket is complete: {{06570485861705522::np}} if only a single silk thread carried by the wind ends up on it: {{06570485861705522::np}} this offence is (cittaka / acittaka?) {{06570485861705522::acittaka}} and (saññāvimokkha / nosaññāvimokkha?) {{06570485861705522::nosaññāvimokkha}} making it for another unknowingly using silk: {{06570485861705522::dkṭ for preliminary steps and dkṭ when concluded}} making use of one woven by another: {{06570485861705522::dkṭ}} making robes or other items such as canopy, a carpet, a wall screen, a mattress, or a pillow using silk thread: {{06570485861705522::no offense (maybe because it is senāsana)}} weaving blankets using silk thread: {{06570485861705522::no offense }}{{06570485861705522::(because technically it only applies to santhata which is not woven; however, when considering the reasons for promulgating the training rule, it is obvious that abstaining from weaving silk rugs is commendable)}}
    • aṅgāni
      • kosiyamissakaṁ: silk thread having been mixed
      • santhataṁ kārāpeyya: Preparing or getting it prepared for oneself
        • completing the covering
    • nissajjana
      • Idaṃ me, bhante, kosiyamissakaṃ santhataṃ kārāpitaṃ nissaggiyaṃ. Imāhaṃ ayasmato nissajjāmi.
  • np 12 - suddhakāḷakasikkhāpada
    • background
      • The Tathāgata was residing at the Kūṭāgāra hall in the city of Vesalī. At that time Chabbaggiya monks were making rugs entirely out of black goat wool (or fur); people who had been on pilgrimage happened to see this and criticised the act; bhikkhus who heard this, informed the Buddha about it, and he declared this training rule.
    • training rule in pāḷi
      • Yo pana bhikkhu suddhakāḷakānaṃ eḷakalomānaṃ santhataṃ kārāpeyya, nissaggiyaṃ pācittiyaṃ. If a monk prepares a blanket only out of black goat wool, it will result in a nissaggiya pācittiya offence.
    • description of the training rule
      • preparing or making another prepare a santhata with only black wool: {{532237942234052::dkṭ}} once it is completed: {{532237942234052::np}} if it is done with coloured wool but completely covered by black wool: {{532237942234052::np}} doing it for another: {{532237942234052::dkṭ for the efforts and dkṭ once completed}} using one made by another: {{532237942234052::dkṭ}} if a canopy (vitāna), a carpet (bhummattharaṇa), a wallscreen (sāṇipākāra), a mattress (bhisi) or a pillow (bibbohana) is made purely out of black goat wool: {{532237942234052::no offense}}
    • pāḷi terms
      • vitānaṁ (nt.) [vi + √tan + *a] ✓↔canopy, awning; cover; lit. stretching out
      • bhūmattharaṇa / bhummattharaṇa↔carpet
      • sāṇipākāra↔wallscreen, tapestry (mem aid: "sunny wall")
      • bhisi↔mattress (mem aid: uma bici na cama)
      • bibbohana↔pillow
    • aṅgāni
      • suddhakālakānaṁ eḷakalomānaṁ: constituting of only black goat wool
      • kārāpeyya: preparing or getting it prepared for oneself
        • completing the blanket
    • nissajjana
      • Idaṃ me, bhante, suddhakāḷakānaṃ eḷakalomānaṃ santhataṃ kārāpitaṃ nissaggiyaṃ. Imāhaṃ ayasmato nissajjāmi.

  • np 13 - dvebhāgasikkhāpada
    • background
      • The Tathāgata was residing at the Jetavana temple near the city of Sāvatthi. At that time, since the Buddha had prohibited making rugs using only black goat wool, Chabbaggiya monks, in order to avoid the offence, were making rugs using a larger percentage of black goat wool and a lesser percentage of goat wool of different colours. People who had been on pilgrimage happened to see this and had been criticising the act. Bhikkhus who heard this, informed the Buddha about it, and He declared the following training rule.
    • training rule in pāḷi
      • Navaṃ pana bhikkhunā santhataṃ kārayamānena dve bhāgā suddhakāḷakānaṃ eḷakalomānaṃ ādātabbā tatiyaṃ odātānaṃ catutthaṃ gocariyānaṃ. Anādā ce bhikkhu dve bhāge suddhakāḷakānaṃ eḷakalomānaṃ tatiyaṃ odātānaṃ catutthaṃ gocariyānaṃ navaṃ santhataṃ kārāpeyya, nissaggiyaṃ pācittiyaṃ. (If a monk prepares a new blanket, he must obtain two parts of black goat wool, one part of white goat wool and another one part of brown goat wool. If a new blanket is made outside this formula using black goat wool more than stipulated, it will result in a nissaggiya pācittiya offence.)
    • description
      • If a santhata is being made the amount of black wool that is taken must be {{2656766654579614::2 quarters or less (dvebhāgā)}} of the total wool requirement by weight; the rest must be of other colors such as white or brown (it does not matter the percentage of which extra color) making it with more black wool than that: {{2656766654579614::dkṭ during the process and np when completed}} making it or having it made for another using more black wool than the stipulated: {{2656766654579614::dkṭ during the process and dkṭ when completed}} using one made by another: {{2656766654579614::dkṭ}} making a vitāna (canopy), bhummattharaṇa (carpet), sāṇipākāra (wallscreen), bhisi (mattress) or a pillow (bibbohana) using more black wool than the stipulated: {{2656766654579614::no offense}} making it while not being aware of there being more black wool than the stipulated: {{2656766654579614::np}} this offense is (sacittaka / acittaka?) {{2656766654579614::acittaka}} and (saññāvimokkha / nosaññāvimokkha?) {{2656766654579614::nosaññāvimokkha}}
    • aṅgāni
      • dvebhāgā suddhakāḷakānaṁ eḷakalomānaṁ...: having included black wool more than the specified quantity (more than half)
      • kārayamānena: reparing or getting it prepared for oneself
        • completing the rug
    • nissajjana
      • Idaṃ me, bhante, santhataṃ anādiyitvā dve tule suddhakāḷakānaṃ eḷakalomānaṃ tulaṃ odātānaṃ tulaṃ gocariyānaṃ kārāpitaṃ nissaggiyaṃ. Imāhaṃ ayasmato nissajjāmi.
    • note:
      • what happens to the nissaggiya items under np11 (kosiya), 12 (suddhakālaka) and 13 (eḷakaloma) after the nissajjana is done?→the item cannot be used by the monk who made the santhata
  • np 14 - chabbassasikkhāpada
    • background
      • The Tathāgata was residing at the Jetavana monastery near the city of Sāvatthi. At that time, most bhikkhus in Sāvatthi were in the habit of getting new blankets done every year. Monks often asked for goat wool and demanded for it saying “Give us goat wool” (dehi me elakalomaṁ...) or “We are in need of goat wool.” (elakalomena me attho) (both of these are viññatti); (in rāja there is a difference, no difference in their power, in that sikkha the buddha said which is appropriate; more suitable but both are viññatti; ) This resulted in criticism of the bhikkhus by laymen. The Tathāgata who learnt about this matter declared a training rule prohibiting monks from making a new blanket before elapsing six years from when one blanket is done. At that time, one monk became ill in the city of Kosambhi. His relatives sent a messenger to accompany him to be brought to be cared for. This particular bhikkhu did not go because new blankets could not be made, his old blanket could not be taken and it was difficult for him to be without a blanket. The Tathāgata who got to know about this declared: “Anujānāmi, bhikkhave, gilānassa bhikkhuno santhatasammutiṃ dātuṃ”, “I allow the Saṅgha to give an allowance to a sick monk to make a new rug within six years” and amended the rule
      • notes:
        • what are the rules related to gilānasammuti?→uddosita (np2), chabbassa (np14), sāsaṅkha (np29)
        • in sekhiya, gilāna is anāpatti, not sammuti
    • training rule in pāḷi
      • Navaṃ pana bhikkhunā santhataṃ kārāpetvā chabbassāni dhāretabbaṃ. Orena ce channaṃ vassānaṃ taṃ santhataṃ vissajjetvā vā avissajjetvā vā aññaṃ navaṃ santhataṃ kārāpeyya, aññatra bhikkhusammutiyā, nissaggiyaṃ pācittiyaṃ. A monk must consume a new blanket which he made or got someone else to make for six years. Therefore, if a monk gets a new blanket made, having or not having given up the original rug before the lapse of six years, without a bhikkhu sammuti, a nissaggiya pācittiya offence will be committed.
    • description of the training rule
      • a santhata should be used for a period of {{015242360415399281::six years}} making a new one before the pasing of six years from the day the last one was made: {{015242360415399281::dkṭ for the process and np when concluded}} making it on or after the day 6 years are completed: {{015242360415399281::no offense}} the counting is done by the moon calendar if sick a monk may obtain a {{015242360415399281::gilānasammuti}} and may make new santhatas every {{015242360415399281::year}} when recovered, the sammuti does not apply, but once again sick it automatically applies once again reagardless of the kind of sickness making it for someone else before the six years: {{015242360415399281::no offense}} receiving, using a second santhata made by another before the 6 year period is over: {{015242360415399281::no offense}} if a canopy, a carpet, a wall screen, a mattress, or a pillow is made within the six years: {{015242360415399281::no offence}} making one mistakenly believing 6 years have passed: {{015242360415399281::np}} this training rule is (acittaka / sacittaka?) {{015242360415399281::acittaka}} and (saññāvimokkha / nosaññāvimokkha?) {{015242360415399281::nosaññāvimokka}}
      • comparing santhatas
        • kosiya santhata (np1) making for another: {{5685330955438559::dkṭ}} using one made by others: {{5685330955438559::dkṭ}} suddhakālaka santhata (np2) making for another: {{5685330955438559::dkṭ}} using one made by others: {{5685330955438559::dkṭ}} atirekadvebhāga santhata (np3) making for another: {{5685330955438559::dkṭ}} using one made by others: {{5685330955438559::dkṭ}} ūnakachabbassa santhata (np4) making for another: {{5685330955438559::anāpatti}} using one made by others: {{5685330955438559::anāpatti}} nisīdana santhata (np5) making for another: {{5685330955438559::dkṭ}} using one made by others: {{5685330955438559::anāpatti}}
    • aṅgāni
      • orena ce channaṃ vassānaṃ: making or getting the blanket made before the designated time period
      • aññatra bhikkhusammutiyā: not having obtained the gilānasammuti
      • kārāpetvā: preparing or getting it prepared for oneself
        • completing the blanket
    • nissajjana
      • Idaṃ me, bhante, santhataṃ ūnakachabbassāni kārāpitaṃ, aññatra bhikkhusammutiyā, nissaggiyaṃ. Imāhaṃ ayasmato nissajjāmi
  • np 15 - nisīdanasanthatasikkhāpada
    • background
      • the agreement the Tathāgata was residing at the {{5876846637346893::Jetavana, Anāthapiṇḍika's park, Sāvatthi}} when He informed the monks He would go into seclusion and only the one who would bring piṇḍapāta could approach Him; monks made an agreement that one who approach Him would be committing a {{5876846637346893::pācittiya offense}}; the visit then {{5876846637346893::Vaṅgantaputta Upasena Thera}} approached the Buddha together with his students and after having a cordial conversation they discussed the fact that {{5876846637346893::one of the monks in the assembly was wearing a paṁsukūla robe he did not like because of following his teacher's practice}}; the Buddha praised the monks and inquired about Upasena's training to which he said he only ordained or gave nissaya to those who could follow his practices of {{5876846637346893::araññikaṅga, piṇḍapātikaṅga and paṁsukūlikaṅga}}; the Buddha praised him; the allowance He then inquired about the monk's agreement; the ven. explained it and said {{5876846637346893::he only abided by the rules promulgated by the Buddha}} which the Buddha praised, giving the monks who followed those austerities the allowance to approach Him as they wish; informing the others the ven. then informed the others of their fault and of the allowance, leading many to observe austerities; some while undertaking tecīvarikaṅga discarded their santhatas believing it to be a hindrance for it; promulgation of the rule emerging from seclusion and wandering through the monastery, the Buddha noticed many discarded santhatas and promulgated the rule mem aid: for Vaṅgantaputta Upasena, and araññika, piṇḍapātika and paṁsukūlikaṅga
      • do santhatas have to be determined?→a non-woven rug is not necessary to be determined, so technically, keeping one does not break the tecīvarikaṅga dhutaṅga
    • training rule in pāḷi
      • Nisīdanasanthataṃ pana bhikkhunā kārayamānena purāṇasanthatassa sāmantā sugatavidatthi ādātabbā dubbaṇṇakaraṇāya, anādā ce bhikkhu purāṇasanthatassa sāmantā sugatavidatthiṃ navaṃ nisīdanasanthataṃ kārāpeyya, nissaggiyaṃ pācittiyaṃ. A monk getting a sitting-rug made, should take a piece of cloth of the size of a sugata handspan, from an old rug for the purpose of discolouring the sitting-rug. If a monk gets a new sitting-rug made without obtaining a piece of cloth of the size of a sugata hand span, from an old rug, a nissaggiya pācittiya offence will be committed.
    • description of the training rule
      • When a bhikkhu makes or orders a new nisīdanasanthata to be made, and if he has an old santhata, he must incorporate a piece of cloth of the size of {{14137350091670164::a sugata handspan}} from the latter. This is done so to make use of the old santhata when a new nisīdana santhata is made after six years from the manufacturing date of the old existing santhata. If a bhikkhu makes a new sitting-rug while the old rug can still be made use of and without taking a piece of cloth of such size from it, a {{14137350091670164::np}} offence will be committed if the old one is completely destroyed: {{14137350091670164::one does not have to use it}}
      • note:
        • whether can nisidanasanthata be made before 6 years is not clear
        • the purpose is to use the material of the old one that is still usable, if all destroyed no need to use
    • Nisīdana santhata (non-woven sitting-rug)
      • a nisīdana santhata is a santhata that has borders like in a normal nisīdana. Therefore, the definition of nisīdana santhata is given in the padabhājanīya as, “Nisīdanaṃ nāma sadasaṃ (with borders) vuccati. Santhataṃ nāma santharitvā kataṃ hoti avāyimaṃ. ”Therefore, nisīdana santhata is a rug that is not woven, but with borders. This too needs not to be determined like a non-woven santhata. It is mentioned in sub-commentaries that some scholars held the idea that nisīdana santhata and nisīdana cīvara are identical. But Vimativinodanī does not appreciate this idea.
      • note:
        • some teachers think (not strongly refuted but not appreciated) but comm thinks not the same as nisīdana for sitting;
        • size not mentioned
        • normal santhata is not woven
    • The procedure of making a nisīdana santhata and how and how not offences are committed
      • The portion of cloth that must be taken from the old rug to the new sitting-rug can be either in the form of a circle or a square. If the portion is a circle, its {{6161144944113413::diameter}} must be at least {{6161144944113413::one Sugata handspan}}, if a square, {{6161144944113413::the length of one side must be at least a Sugata handspan}}. It can be attached to one or several sides of the new sitting-rug. For some reason if the designated size cannot be extracted from the old rug, it is allowed to {{6161144944113413::take a piece that is smaller than a Sugata handspan}}. If the old rug is totally destroyed, an offence will not be committed if the new sitting-rug is made or ordered to be made without obtaining a single piece from the old santhata. As indicated in the Vinaya Pāḷi, if a new sitting-rug is made or ordered to be made for another without taking a piece of cloth of the size of a Sugata hand span from the old rug, the bhikkhu will be committing {{6161144944113413::dukkaṭa offences while making efforts and at the completion of the rug}}. Consuming such a sitting-rug prepared by another {{6161144944113413::will not result in an offence}}.11 A canopy, a carpet, a wall screen, a mattress, or a pillow is made or ordered to be made without taking a piece of cloth the size of a Sugata handspan from the old santhata, {{6161144944113413::it will not result in any offence}}. This is an (acittaka / sacittaka?) {{6161144944113413::acittaka}}, (saññāvimokkha / nosaññāvimokkha?) {{6161144944113413::nosaññāvimokkha}} offence
    • Factors
      • purāṇasanthatassa sāmantā sugatavidatthi ādātabbā: not taking a piece of cloth the size of a Sugata handspan from the old santhata when it is possible to do so
      • kārayamānena: preparing or getting it prepared for oneself
      • nisīdanasanthataṁ: completing the sitting-rug
    • Forfeiture
      • Idaṃ me, bhante, nisīdanasanthataṃ anādiyitvā purāṇasanthatassa sāmantā sugatavidatthiṃ kārāpitaṃ nissaggiyaṃ. Imāhaṃ āyasmato nissajjāmi.

  • np 16 - eḷakalomasikkhāpada
    • background
      • The Tathāgata was residing at the Jetavana temple near the city of Sāvatthi. At this time, a bhikkhu who was on his way to Sāvatthi received goat fur (or wool). He made a pile of it, covered it with his upper robe and carried it. The people who saw this asked him mockingly: “How much have you taken? What is the profit you made?” This made the bhikkhu feel shy and speechless. The Buddha who came to know about this incident declared this training rule.
    • Training rule in Pali
      • Bhikkhuno paneva addhānamaggappaṭipannassa eḷakalomāni uppajjeyyuṃ. Ākaṅkhamānena bhikkhunā paṭiggahetabbāni. Paṭiggahetvā tiyojanaparamaṃ sahatthā haritabbāni, asante hārake. Tato ce uttari hareyya, asantepi hārake, nissaggiyaṃ pācittiyaṃ. If a monk receives goat fur while travelling, he may accept it if desired. Once accepted, if there’s no one else to carry it, he can only carry it by himself, up to three yojanas. If he happens to carry it beyond the limit of three yojanas in the absence of a carrier, a nissaggiya pācittiya offence will occur.
    • Description of the training rule
      • A bhikkhu who receives goat fur must not carry it beyond {{9826691569749155::three yojanas}} by himself. The Tathāgata imposed this training rule because it is inappropriate for bhikkhus to do so. When taken the goat fur beyond three yojanas, the goat fur becomes {{9826691569749155::nissaggiya}} while the bhikkhu will have committed a {{9826691569749155::pācittiya}} offence. When the three-yojana boundary is passed, by taking the initial step therein, a {{9826691569749155::dukkaṭa}} offence will be committed. {{9826691569749155::Pācittiya}} offence will be committed upon the second step. The bhikkhu can carry the goat fur within the {{9826691569749155::three-yojana}} distance without committing any offence.
      • If the goat fur is carried back and forth within the area between the location of receiving the goat fur and the three-yojana mark, even if the monk walks more than three yojanas in total, {{5069783648336348::there is no offense}}. An offence will not be committed under this training rule as long as the monk does not cross the threeyojana boundary (from the receiving location). If the bhikkhu who received the goat fur gets another person to carry it, passing the three-yojana boundary will {{5069783648336348::not result in an offence}}. Further, it should be known that carrying an object made out of goat fur will {{5069783648336348::not result in an offence}}
      • if the initial three-yojana mark is crossed while carrying fur that is stolen by thieves and returned, and while carrying goat fur that has been returned after being forfeited through the nissajjana vinayakamma {{18109269204126632::an offence will not occur}}. If the goat fur is received this way and had to be carried again, an additional three yojanas can be covered from the point of the second receiving. The initial three-yojana mark is not taken into consideration. This is the opinion demonstrated in the Gaṇṭhipada and Vimativinodanī. According to the Sāratthadīpanī, there is no limit in the distance the goat fur could be carried when received the second time.
      • If the goat fur is received in one particular place, a distance of three yojanas can be travelled forward from thereon, and take up residence therein. From that point onwards, goat fur can be carried for another three yojanas. This training rule is {{4658933700398924::acittaka}} and {{4658933700398924::nosaññāvimokkha}}. Even if one is unaware of or doubting the distance travelled while the three yojana miles are exceeded, an offence {{4658933700398924::will occur}}
    • Factors
      • 1. Goat fur that has not been made into an object
      • 2. Receiving it for the first time
      • 3. Passing the three-yojana mark
      • 4. Not being a journey in which the monk travels more than three yojana back and forth within the three-yojana boundary.
      • 5. Not a journey that was initiated after residing in a place within the three-yojana boundary after the first receiving
    • Forfeiture
      • Imāni me, bhante, eḷakalomāni tiyojanaṃ atikkāmitāni nissaggiyāni. Imānāhaṃ āyasmato nissajjāmi.
  • np 17 - eḷakalomadhovāpanasikkhāpada
    • background
      • The Tathāgata was residing at the Nigrodha monastery in the city of Kapilavatthu. At this time, Chabbaggiya monks got bhikkhunis to wash goat fur. They even asked them to dye and untangle them (card the wool). This resulted in the deterioration of the bhikkhunis' Dhamma studies and training of adhisīla, adhicitta and adhipaññā. Mahā Pajāpatī Gotamī informed this matter to the Buddha one day. As a result, this training rule was declared.
    • Training rule in Pali
      • Yo pana bhikkhu aññātikāya bhikkhuniyā eḷakalomāni dhovāpeyya vā rajāpeyya vā vijaṭāpeyya vā, nissaggiyaṃ pācittiyaṃ. If a monk gets an unrelated bhikkhuni to wash or dye or untangle (card) goat fur, a nissaggiya pācittiya offence will be committed
    • Description of the training rule
      • If a bhikkhu asks an unrelated bhikkhuni to wash or dye or untangle goat fur owned by him, it will result in a {{7070308360194955::pācittiya}} offence for the bhikkhu while the goat fur will become {{7070308360194955::nissaggiya}}. The rest of the information pertaining to this training rule must be understood as per the {{7070308360194955::purāṇacīvara training rule (np4)}}
    • Factors
      • eḷakalomāni: being fur / wool
      • aññātikāya bhikkhuniyā: ordering an unrelated bhikkhuni who is in the vicinity of twelve cubits (upacāra)
      • dhovāpeyya vā rajāpeyya vā vijaṭāpeyya vā: the same bhikkhuni executing any of the three tasks, i.e., washing, dyeing and carding (combing out, untangling)
    • Forfeiture
      • Imāni me, bhante, eḷakalomāni aññātikāya bhikkhuniyā dhovāpitāni (rajāpitāni/vijaṭāpitāni) nissaggiyāni. Imānāhaṃ āyasmato nissajjāmi.
  • np 18 - rūpiyasikkhāpadaṃ
    • background
      • The Tathāgata was residing at the Veḷuvana monastery in the city of Rājagaha. At this time, Venerable Upananda, a son of Sākyans became friendly with a certain family. If any food or drink item is prepared in that house, Upananda Thera also had a portion set aside for him. One day, meat was prepared in the evening in this household. Even though Venerable Upananda’s portion was reserved, it had to be given to the child in the house who was crying early in the morning. When Venerable Upananda arrived at the household, the matter was explained and had been asked what kind of meat he would like for the value of one gold coin. Then Venerable Upananda, instead of mentioning the preferred meat, requested the gold coin. The householder was displeased and started bad-mouthing the bhikkhu. When this matter was informed, the Buddha imposed the rūpiya training rule.
      • note:
        • money is the cause / root of all requisites
    • Training rule in Pali
      • Yo pana bhikkhu jātarūparajataṃ uggaṇheyya vā uggaṇhāpeyya vā upanikkhittaṃ vā sādiyeyya nissaggiyaṃ pācittiyaṃ. If a monk accepts gold, silver or money (rūpiya) himself or gets someone else to accept on his behalf or maintains silence implying acceptance when kept in front of him to offer him, the rūpiya must be forfeited. A pācittiya offence will be committed.
    • Description of the training rule
      • Jātarūpa specifically denotes {{5828879301133194::gold}}, while in certain contexts, the term "rajata" pertains to {{5828879301133194::silver}}. In this instance, "rajata" refers to {{5828879301133194::not only silver but also encompasses various forms of currency employed in trade apart from gold}}. A comprehensive term for these precious mediums is "rūpiya," encompassing {{5828879301133194::gold, silver, and currency fashioned from diverse metals, paper or any other material, all intended for use in transactions}}. The training rule is breached when a monk accepts or makes someone accept on his behalf rūpiya that is directly offered to him or collects or makes someone collect on his behalf rūpiya that has been discarded. When rūpiya is kept close to the bhikkhu with the intention of offering, and if the bhikkhu does not reject the offering by a gesture or words or at least with a rejecting thought: “These are not allowable to us,” and remains to be tacitly approving, a {{5828879301133194::nissaggiya pācittiya}} offence will be committed.
    • Akappiya vatthu, kappiya vatthu and related offences
      • all physical things, according to Vinaya, are categorised into two groups (vatthu) according to their inherent nature of being allowable or not for monks
        • kappiyavatthu / arūpiya - objects that are allowable for monks to accept ((piṇḍapāta, robes, umbrella, slippers...)
        • akappiyavatthu - objects which if accepted a monk entails an offense
          • dukkaṭavatthu - objects which if accepted a monk entails a dukkaṭa offence (8 / 15)
          • nissaggiyavatthu / rūpiya - objects which if accepted a monk entails a nissaggiyapācittiya offence (3): rūpiya, jātarūpa, rajata (gold, silver and currencies)
        • note:
          • jātarūpa→gold
          • rajata↔silver in most contexts but here any currency made of any material as well
          • rūpiya↔jātarūparajata
      • 8 / 15 dukkaṭavatthu
        • 1 - 8 'precious items' (muttādi)
          • 1. Mutta - Pearl
          • 2. Maṇi - Gems
          • 3. Viḷuriya - Lapis lazuli
          • 4. Saṅkha - Chank and conch shells
          • 5. Silā - Quartz
          • 6. Pavāla - Corals
          • 7. Lohitaṅka - Ruby
          • 8. Māsāragalla - cat’s eye
        • 9. Satta dhañña - Seven types of grains (with the husk)
        • 10. Taḷāka-vāpi-pokkharaṇī-udakavāhaka - Lakes, tanks, ponds, man-made streams carrying water (water bodies)
        • 11. Khetta - Paddy fields
        • 12. Pupphārāma - Flower gardens
        • 13. Phalārāma - Orchards
        • 14. Dāsadāsī - Male and female slaves
        • 15. Gavādi tiracchānagatā - Animals such as oxen, goats etc
        • note:
          • dhañña is not allowed with the husk, without it it is allowed

      • Nissaggiyavatthus become nissaggiya after accepting for {{40550412343321063::one’s own use}}. And the monk commits a {{40550412343321063::pācittiya}} offence for doing so. Accepting them on behalf of others results in a {{40550412343321063::dukkaṭa}} offence. On the other hand, by accepting dukkaṭavatthu for one’s own use or for others, a monk commits a {{40550412343321063::dukkaṭa}} offence. When accepting these unallowable objects, multiple offences occur according to {{40550412343321063::the number of items accepted}}. If any of these objects are collected for safekeeping, outside the monastery or residence of monks, a {{40550412343321063::pācittiya}} offence will be committed through the {{40550412343321063::ratana sikkhāpada}} if a sāmaṇera accepts it for others {{40550412343321063::it doesn't break the rule}}

          • 3 ways of accepting + 2

            • taking a discarded object

    • Forfeiture
      • forfeiting in the midst of the Saṅgha A bhikkhu who accepts money, must {{18350945701038146::go to the Saṅgha, wear the uttarāsaṅga properly over one shoulder, worship senior bhikkhus at their feet, squat and keep the money in front of the Saṅgha.}} Then the money must be forfeited in the midst of the Saṅgha (not to the Saṅgha; but the forfeiture sentence say so) saying the following: {{18350945701038146::“Ahaṃ bhante rūpiyaṃ paṭiggahesiṃ, idaṃ me nissaggiyaṃ, imāhaṃ saṃghassa nissajjāmi”}} confession then he should confess the offence (or offences) and a senior bhikkhu must accept the offence. disposing by a layperson If an upāsaka is present, he must be told, {{18350945701038146::“Friend, take this.”}} When he asks, “What should I buy from this?” he must not be told: {{18350945701038146::“Bring this and that particular object.”}} He must be told: {{18350945701038146::“Such and such item is appropriate for bhikkhus.”}} making use of the allowable items Thereafter, if he brings something appropriate like oil or ghee or honey from that money, it should be consumed by {{18350945701038146::all bhikkhus except the bhikkhu who accepted the money}}. Even if the offender receives something from another monk who received it, {{18350945701038146::he must not accept it}}. The light shone by a lamp that is lit from the oil bought from that money is {{18350945701038146::also inappropriate }}for this monk. If this money is used to dig a well or pond, the water that is generated through them is {{18350945701038146::inappropriate}} for this monk. If a bed or chair is bought from that money, such will be {{18350945701038146::inappropriate}} for this bhikkhu. if not smart enough If the layman to whom the money was forfeited is not smart enough to make it useful for the Saṅgha, he must be told to {{18350945701038146::take and dispose of the inappropriate object.}} If he takes away the money without disposing of the object, {{18350945701038146::he must not be stopped}}. if no laymen or the laymen leaves If he just leaves the rūpiya object without taking it, the Saṅgha must {{18350945701038146::appoint one monk to have the money disposed of.}} That monk is called {{18350945701038146::rūpiyachaḍḍaka}}.
      • Disposing by the rūpiyachaḍḍaka has to be done as follows.
        • The bhikkhu must be requested to dispose of the money.
        • Soon after, the following kammavācā must be recited in a vinayakamma.
        • (not for exam) Suṇātu me bhante, saṃgho. Yadi saṃghassa pattakallaṃ saṃgho (itthaṃ nāmaṃ) bhikkhuṃ rūpiyachaḍḍakaṃ sammanneyya, esā ñatti. Suṇātu me bhante, saṃgho. (itthaṃ nāmaṃ) bhikkhuṃ rūpiyachaḍḍakaṃ sammannati. Yassāyasmato khamati. (itthannāmassa) bhikkhuno rūpiyachaḍḍakassa sammuti, so tuṇhassa, yassa nakkhamati so bhāseyya. Sammato saṃghena (itthannāmo) bhikkhu rūpiyachaḍḍako, khamati saṃghassa tasmā tuṇhi. Evametaṃ dhārayāmi.
        • The rūpiyachaḍḍaka monk must then take the money and throw it to deep waters, precipice or jungle where it cannot be found again, closing his eyes
        • note: at present, this is not allowed to be done for currencies as it is against the law
    • Factors
      • jātarūparajataṁ: 1. Being an object of rūpiya: Gold or silver or any type of money
      • uggaṇheyya vā uggaṇhāpeyya vā upanikkhittaṁ vā sādiyeyya: 3. Accepting it, getting it accepted or tacitly accepting without rejecting when offered
        • Being for oneself
  • np 19 - rūpiyasaṃvohārasikkhāpada
    • background
      • The Tathāgata was residing one time at the Jetavana monastery near the city of Sāvatthi. Chabbaggiya monks traded (buying and selling) with various rūpiya items, i.e., gold, silver and money. People started blaming them. Minimalistic bhikkhus informed this to the Buddha. The Buddha in this instance imposed the rūpiyasaṃvohāra training rule.

  • np 20 - kayavikkayasikkhāpada
    • background
      • The Tathāgata was residing one time at the Jetavana temple near the city of Sāvatthi. Venerable Upananda, the Sākyan son was proficient in making robes. One wandering mendicant (paribbājaka) gave a silk shawl to Venerable Upananda and took a beautifully dyed saṅghāṭi from him. Subsequently, the paribbājaka asked for the silk shawl again knowing that the exchange was not profitable to him. However, Upananda Thera did not return the shawl. The mendicant started to bad-mouth bhikkhus. Based on this incident, this training rule was declared.
    • Training rule in Pali
      • Yo pana bhikkhu nānāppakārakaṃ kayavikkayaṃ samāpajjeyya nissaggiyaṃ pācittiyaṃ. If a monk trades all sorts of allowable objects (exchange allowable requisites for other allowable requisites), a nissaggiya pācittiya offence will be committed
      • vocab: #[[pāḷi vocab (1st year) ]]
        • kayavikkayaṃ samāpajjeyya→engaging in an exchange by saying: “Take this object and give me that object"; Kaya means to take an object of another. Vikaya means to give in return something that belongs to oneself
    • pāḷi terms
      • saddhādeyya vinipāta↔a monk giving his own belonging to a lay person other than his parents (and other exceptions such as ārāmikas)
