np 19 - rūpiyasaṃvohārasikkhāpada


  • background
    • The Tathāgata was residing one time at the Jetavana monastery near the city of Sāvatthi. Chabbaggiya monks traded (buying and selling) with various rūpiya items, i.e., gold, silver and money. People started blaming them. Minimalistic bhikkhus informed this to the Buddha. The Buddha in this instance imposed the rūpiyasaṃvohāra training rule.
  • Training rule in Pali
    • Yo pana bhikkhu nānappakārakaṃ rūpiyasaṃvohāraṃ samāpajjeyya, nissaggiyaṃ pācittiyaṃ. If a monk trades with gold or silver or money in various ways, a nissaggiya pācittiya offence will be committed
    • vocab: #[[pāḷi vocab (1st year) ]]
      • saṃvohāro [saṃ + vi + ava + √har + *a] masc.↔business; trading; dealing ; 2 masc. interacting; transacting; dealing (with); (comm) communicating [saṃ + vi + ava + √har + *a] ✓
  • Description of the training rule
    • This training rule prohibits exchange

  • Forfeiture
    • for the trades in which one receives a nissaggiyavatthu the forfeiture is the same as in rūpiyasikkhāpada apart from the sentence being different: "{{0599841888494248::Ahaṃ bhante nānāppakārakaṃ rūpiyasaṃvohāraṃ samāpajjiṃ. Idaṃ me nissaggiyaṃ, imāhaṃ saṃghassa nissajāmi}}. If the received object is a dukkaṭa vatthu or a kappiya vatthu, the offence must be confessed and there is no specific procedure given for their forfeiture (dv such as lakes can be given away to local leaders)
  • Important details related to the training rule
    • following are 5 types of patta received in exchange with rūpiya
      • paṭhamapatta (mahā akappiya patta / 'rūpiya'ayo'patta')
        • procedure: {{3859481629611272::accepting money, buying iron and getting a bowl made}} allowance: {{3859481629611272::not allowed for anyone and cannot be made allowable by any means; anything made from it or with it is also not allowed}}
        • notes:
          • there are different opinions about this idea in different sub-commentaries saratthadipani: no way for a monk, but any unallowable requisite is given to someone beyond the circle of sahadhammika and kappiya (including relatives), if it comes back it is allowable vimativinodani: no way to make it allowable, it says saratthadipani is wrong
      • dutiyapatta ('rūpiyapatta')
        • procedure: {{9839599526588334::accepting money and buying a bowl}} allowance: {{9839599526588334::not allowed for anyone but can be made allowable by reversing the process (the money is returned to its owner and the bowl to the seller, then it is appropriate for that bhikkhu to get it back again via proper means)}}
      • tatiyapatta ('rūpiya'kappiyavohāra'patta')
        • procedure: {{7660776414284867::accepting money, keeping it with a caretaker, using kappiyavohāra saying "this is good for me" in a shop and the kappiya buying the bowl and offering it to the monk}} allowance: {{7660776414284867::not allowed for anyone but if the money had been properly forfeited beforehand the bowl would be suitable for all sahadhammikas except for the bhikkhu who accepted the money}}
      • catutthapatta ('akappiyavohāra'patta')
        • procedure: {{6366935519075319::in a shop, with a kappiya, using akappiyavohāra saying, "Please, take this money and give me the bowl / Please, take this bowl for me giving this money to the shopkeeper"}} allowance: {{6366935519075319::not allowed for that monk but allowed for other sahadhammikas}}
        • note:
          • teeling a kappiya to take money for oneself is NP, but to give it to someone else (even if the word used is take but the money is going away) it is a different offense (what offense?) #q🙋🏼‍♂
          • some ideas say it has to go through a vinayakamma but normally not
      • pañcamapatta (kappiyapatta / 'kappiyavohāra'patta')
        • procedure: {{5628004763239463::in a shop, with a kappiya, using kappiyavohāra saying "This is good for me"}} allowance: {{5628004763239463::allowed to everyone}}
      • it is not allowable for oneself when {{5918677634781169::it was bought with the money one accepted}} it is not allowable for the other sahadhammikas when {{5918677634781169::it was bought with the money that had not been properly forfeited}}
      • to whom each object is allowed:
        • rūpiya accepted, not forfeited, kappiyavohāra: allowed for {{26037609476591683::no one}} rūpiya accepted, not forfeited, akappiyavohāra: allowed for {{26037609476591683::no one}} rūpiya accepted, forfeited, akappiyavohāra: allowed for {{26037609476591683::no one}} rūpiya accepted, forfeited, kappiyavohāra: allowed for {{26037609476591683::other sahadhammikas only}} rūpiya not accepted, akappiyavohāra: allowed for {{26037609476591683::other sahadhammikas only}} rūpiya not accepted, kappiyavohāra: allowed for {{26037609476591683::everyone}}
    • Factors
      • 1. The object that is given or taken being rūpiya
      • 2. Exchanging
