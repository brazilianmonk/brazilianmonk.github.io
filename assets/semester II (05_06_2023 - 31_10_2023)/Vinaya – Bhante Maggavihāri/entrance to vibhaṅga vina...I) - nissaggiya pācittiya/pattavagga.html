  • np 21 - pattasikkhāpada #edited
    • background
      • The Tathāgata was residing at the Jetavana monastery near the city of Sāvatthi. At this time, the Chabbaggiya monks used to collect a lot of bowls. People, who visited temples, having seen the collection of bowls, bad-mouthed the monks saying: “These monks are collecting many bowls. Are they trading bowls?” The bhikkhus who heard this, informed the Buddha. Then the Buddha laid down the patta sikkhāpada prohibiting monks from keeping extra bowls. At another time, Venerable Ānanda received an extra bowl. He desired to offer it to Venerable Sāriputta. The latter was residing in Sāketa by then. Venerable Ānanda inquired the Buddha what he should do as the Buddha had barred monks from keeping extra bowls. When asked, the Buddha inquired from Venerable Ānanda: “When will Sāriputta come here?” Ānanda replied; “Sir, in nine or ten days.” Then the Buddha amended the patta sikkhāpada allowing monks to keep extra bowls for ten days.
    • Training rule in pāḷi
      • Dasāhaparamaṃ atirekapatto dhāretabbo. Taṃ atikkāmayato nissaggiyaṃ pācittiyaṃ. An extra bowl must only be kept for ten days. If that is exceeded the bowl becomes nissaggiya. A pācittiya offence will be committed
    • pāḷi terms
      • atirekapatta↔a bowl that has not been determined or subjected to vikappana
      • adhiṭṭhitapatta↔a bowl that has been determined
      • belt area of the patta↔an area of 2 aṅguli from its rim
    • when a bhikkhu receives a bowl, it must be determined or subjected to vikappana within {{03367198651117742::ten days}}, otherwise: {{03367198651117742::np}} not all bowls received will be subjected to this offence. An offence will only occur if the bowl is {{03367198651117742::of the appropriate }}{{03367198651117742::material}}{{03367198651117742::, }}{{03367198651117742::size}}{{03367198651117742:: and }}{{03367198651117742::burnt }}{{03367198651117742::(for it to be suitable to be determined it has to be properly}}{{03367198651117742:: }}{{03367198651117742::burnt) }} determining a second bowl: {{03367198651117742::the determination is not valid and the second bowl is still atirekapatta}} after nissajjana keeping the atirekapatta for over 10 days: {{03367198651117742::np}} if the adhiṭṭhāna is broken the bowl should be redetermined within 10 days but if not fit for adhiṭṭhāna the days only count after it has been fixed (in case of a hole) using a nissaggiya patta: {{03367198651117742::dkṭ}}
    • nissajjana procedure
      • approach another monk
      • wear the robe over one shoulder, worship the senior (if so), crouch with the bowl in hands
      • nissajjana: “{{48449692168707226::Ayaṃ me, bhante, patto dasāhātikkanto nissaggiyo. Imāhaṃ āyasmato nissajjāmi}}.”
      • forfeit the bowl to the other monk
      • confess the pācittiya offense
      • the other monk says, “Imaṃ pattaṃ āyasmato dammi”, and hands over the bowl back
    • Method of relinquishment and determination
      • paccuddharaṇa for patta within hatthapāsa, in contact with it: “{{1379372512101118::Imaṃ pattaṃ paccuddharāmi}}" (3x) at a distance, thinking of its location: "{{1379372512101118::Etaṁ pattaṁ paccuddharāmi}}" (3x) adhiṭṭhāna for patta being in contact with the bowl: “{{1379372512101118::Imaṃ pattaṃ adhiṭṭhāmi}}" (3x) if at a distance, thinking of its location: "{{1379372512101118::Etaṁ patta adhiṭṭhāmi}}" (3x)
      • 9 reasons due to which the adhiṭṭhāna of a bowl is broken
        • 1. Acchedena / coraharaṇa- If stolen by others
        • 2. Dānena - By giving it to another
        • 3. Vissāsaggāhena - By someone taking out of trust (not tāvakālikaggāha)
        • 4. Vibbhamena - By becoming a layman
        • 5. Maraṇena - Due to death
        • 6. Paccuddharaṇena - By removing the determination
        • 7. Liṅgaparivattanena - Due to sex change
        • 8. Sikkhāpaccakkhānena - By relinquishing the upasampadā
        • 9. Chiddena - Due to a hole big enough for a grain of millet (kaṅgu) to pass below the belt area (2 aṅguli from the rim of the bowl)
          • 7 types of grains

            • vīhi (other rice categories)
            • yava (barley)
            • godhuma (wheat)
            • kaṅgu (millet)
            • varaka (?)
            • kudrūsaka (finger millet)

          • vibbhamati [vi + √bham + a + ti]↔pr. leaves monkhood; disrobes; lit. spins /turns away; 2 pr. goes astray
          • ujjhati↔pr. leaves, forsakes
          • uddhaṭa [ud + √dhar + ta] pp.↔pulled out; removed; extracted; 2 pp. raised, lifted

    • vikappana (sharing ownership)
      • 2 method of vikappana
        • sammukhā vikappana; procedure:
          • preliminary steps as in forfeiture
          • say “Imaṃ pattaṃ tuyhaṃ vikappemi.” / plural: “Ime patte tuyhaṃ vikappemi”
          • after this the bowl can be kept but not used
          • if the second monk does paccuddhāra: “Mayhaṃ santakaṃ paribhuñja vā vissajjehi vā yathāpaccayaṃ karohi,” and hands it back / plural: "Mayhaṁ santake..."
          • then it can be used as one likes
        • asammukhā vikappana; procedure:
          • preliminary steps as in forfeiture
          • inform the other monk where the bowl is
          • say “Etaṃ pattaṃ tuyhaṃ vikappemi” / plural: "Ete patte..."

        • A bowl that is made out of proper material and size and properly burnt
  • np 22 - ūṇapañcabandhanasikkhāpada #edited
    • background
      • One pot maker invited monks to ask him for bowls if needed. The bhikkhus exceeded their limit and kept asking for more bowls. This made the potter unable to support his family. Seeing this, people started to scold the bhikkhus. Then the Buddha promulgated a rule prohibiting monks from asking for bowls (from aññātaka and appavārita lay devotees) Due to this rule, monks whose bowls were broken went for piṇḍapāta and received alms with their palms. Seeing this, people started to criticise monks saying, “Disciples of the Gotama go for alms and receive food with their palms like ascetics of other religions.” Knowing this, the Buddha amended the rule allowing monks whose bowls are broken to ask for bowls from lay devotees. Subsequently, Chabbaggiya bhikkhus began discarding their bowls due to the slightest scratch and hole, and kept asking for new bowls from the same potter. This made the potter unable to support his family. Seeing this, people started to scold the bhikkhus. Then the Buddha amended the training rule for the second time and promulgated it again as follows.
    • Training rule in Pali
      • Yo pana bhikkhu ūnapañcabandhanena pattena aññaṃ navaṃ pattaṃ cetāpeyya, nissaggiyaṃ pācittiyaṃ. Tena bhikkhunā so patto bhikkhuparisāya nissajitabbo. Yo ca tassā bhikkhuparisāya pattapariyanto so tassa bhikkhuno padātabbo ‘Ayaṃ te bhikkhu, patto yāva bhedanāya dhāretabbo’ ti. Ayaṃ tattha sāmīci. If a monk requests for and receives a new bowl while using a bowl that has not been repaired in five places, such bowl (newly received one) will become nissaggiya. A pācittiya offence will also be committed. This bhikkhu must forfeit the bowl in front of a group of bhikkhus. The final bowl left after the procedure of forfeiture is done, must be given to that bhikkhu saying, “Monk, you must use this bowl till it’s broken”. This is how the training rule must be followed.
    • Description of the training rule
      • the purpose of this training rule is to {{39855350572820214::avoid bhikkhus from requesting aññātaka appavārita donors for new bowls }}{{39855350572820214::when there is no actual need}}
    • pāḷi terms
      • 2 types of patta
        • mattikapatta↔clay bowl
          • ūṇapañcabandhanapatta↔a mattikapatta that has less than five bandhana or bandhanokāsa
          • pañcabandhanapatta↔a mattikapatta that has 5 bandhanas or bandhanokāsas and is not considered a patta (apatta)
          • apatta↔not considered a patta (used for mattikapatta that has 5 or more bandhanas or bandhanokāsas)
        • ayopatta↔an iron / steel bowl
          • akappiyapatta (under this rule)↔ayopatta that is broken beyond repair or that, after repair, remains with an uneven surface that retains food
          • kappiyapatta (under this rule)↔an ayopatta that has an even surface that does not retain food, regardless of whether it is broken or not, as long as it can be fixed
      • bandhana↔a mend which is at least 2 aṅguli long
      • bandhanokāsa↔a crack that should be mended which is at least 2 aṅguli long
      • pattagāhāpaka bhikkhu↔the monk appointed to make others take the forfeited patta
    • counting bandhanas / bandhanokāsas
      • principles: every 2 aṅguli of crack / mend ⇒ 1 bandhana / bandhanokāsa is counted a crack / mend must be at least 2 aṅguli long to be considered as bandhana / bandhanokāsa a crack / mend of less than 2 aṅguli long = {{39757109291825943::not bandhana / bandhanokāsa}} 1 crack / mend of 2 aṅguli = {{39757109291825943::1 bandhana / bandhanokāsa}} 2 cracks / mends of 2 aṅguli = {{39757109291825943::2 bandhana / bandhanokāsa}} 1 crack / mend of 4 aṅguli = {{39757109291825943::2 bandhana / bandhanokāsa}} etc...
      • what is a patta with 4 bandhanas or bandhanokāsas?
        • is a mattikapatta with a crack / mend of 8 aṅguli, 4 cracks / mends of 2 aṅguli, or any combination of cracks / mends amounting to 4 bandhanas total
    • instances of offenses
      • asking for a new bowl from an aññātaka and appavārita lay sponsor while still having an ūṇapañcabandhana patta (only asking): {{47682826537927614::dkṭ}} when the bowl is received: {{47682826537927614::np}} asking while having a pañcabandhanapatta: {{47682826537927614::no offense}} asking while having a kappiya ayopatta: {{47682826537927614::np}} asking while having an akkapiya ayopatta: {{47682826537927614::no offense}} asking for another: {{47682826537927614::not np (which offense?)}} during the forfeiture vinayakamma not taking the forfeited patta for not liking it or for effacement: {{47682826537927614::no offense}} not taking the forfeited patta out of compassion for the offender: {{47682826537927614::dkṭ}} after the forfeiture if the offender uses the bowl he receives from the Saṅgha in a way that it would get damaged or destroyed: {{47682826537927614::dkṭ}} if he gives it away: {{47682826537927614::dkṭ}} if he tries to exchange it with another monk: {{47682826537927614::dkṭ}} if another bhikkhu takes the offender's bowl and gives him his own bowl in exchange: {{47682826537927614::no offense}}
        • asking for another is which offense? #q🙋🏼‍♂
    • nissajjana procedure
      • the offender approaches the Saṅgha with the suitable preliminary steps, all bowls must be brought
      • he forfeits the bowl saying: “{{16058609946807012::Ayaṃ me, bhante, patto ūnapañcabandhanena pattena cetāpito nissaggiyo. Imāhaṃ saṅghassa nissajjāmi}}.”
      • the Saṅgha appoints a pattagāhāpaka bhikkhu
      • the pattagāhāpaka bhikkhu gives the forfeited patta to the most senior monk in the gathering saying, “Sir, may you take this!”
      • if the senior takes the bowl, his own bowl must be given to the second in line
      • if the senior does not take the bowl the pattagāhāpaka bhikkhu should offer it to the next in line of seniority
      • the process must continue until the most junior upasampadā monk either take or pass a bowl
      • finally, the bowl that remains at the end (probably the worst bowl) must be given to the offender saying, "{{41865132277750083::ayaṁ te patto, yāva bhedanāya dhāretabbo}}"
    • aṅgāni
      • ūṇapañcabandhanena pattena: being a clay bowl that has less than five bandhanas or bandhanokāsas, or a kappiya iron bowl
      • being for oneself
      • aññaṁ navaṁ pattaṁ cetāpeyya: Requesting a new bowl
      • receiving a new bowl
    • note:
      • this rule is moslty applicable to mattikapatta
  • np 23 - bhesajjasikkhāpada #edited
    • background
      • The Tathāgata was residing at the Jetavana monastery near the city of Sāvatthi. At this time, Venerable Piḷindavaccha Thera was residing in the city of Rājagaha. One day, with the intention of freeing a poor family who were their attendants the Thera determined and made King Bimbisāra’s palace gold. The people who saw this and were moved by this act offered ghee, fresh butter etc. to Piḷindavaccha Thera. The Thera distributed most of such offerings among the brethren. Some monks stored them in pots and pans. As a result, the dwellings of monks got dirty due to spilling of ghee, butter etc. Moreover, dwellings were overrun with rats. The people who visited the temple seeing this, criticised the bhikkhus’ act. The Buddha, becoming aware of this matter, imposed this training rule.
    • training rule in pāḷi
      • Yāni kho pana tāni gilānānaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ paṭisāyanīyāni bhesajjāni, seyyathīdaṃ, sappi, navanītaṃ, telaṃ, madhu, phāṇitaṃ. Tāni paṭiggahetvā sattāhaparamaṃ sannidhikārakaṃ paribhuñjitabbāni, taṃ atikkāmayato, nissaggiyaṃ pācittiyaṃ. Medicinal items such as ghee, butter, sesame oil, bee honey and jaggery that should be used by sick bhikkhus must be stored and consumed within seven days of accepting them. If seven days have passed, the bhikkhu will have committed a nissaggiya pācittiya offence
      • vocab: #[[pāḷi vocab (1st year) ]]
        • paṭisāyanīya [pati + √sāy + anīya]↔ptp. edible; palatable; tasty; delicious; lit. to be tasted
        • sappiṁ nt.↔ghee; clarified butter
        • navanītaṁ nt.↔fresh butter
        • phāṇitaṁ nt. [√phaṇ + *e + ita]↔sugar treacle; jaggery; molasses
    • description

  • np 24 - vassikasāṭikasikkhāpada #edited
    • background
      • The Tathāgata was residing at the Jetavana monastery near the city of Sāvatthi. The Chabbaggiya bhikkhus went in search of vassikasāṭika (rain robes) well ahead of rainy season saying that the Buddha had recommended vassikasāṭika for bhikkhus. The stitching was done and the robes were used well in advance. Due to have been used in advance, vassikasāṭika robes decayed during the rainy season. As a result, some monks of the Chabbaggiya group took baths naked in open areas. Other bhikkhus started criticising this act. They informed the Tathāgata also. It is then that this training rule was imposed. mem aid: Jetavana, chabbaggiyas procured, made and worn vassikasāṭikacīvaras well before vassāna and the robes decayed during the rainy season, leading them to bathe naked in public
    • training rule in pāḷi
      • ‘Māso seso gimhānanti, bhikkhunā vassikasāṭikacīvaraṃ pariyesitabbam, ‘aḍḍhamāso seso gimhāna’nti, katvā nivāsetabbaṃ. ‘Orena ce māso seso gimhāna’nti, vassikasāṭikacīvaraṃ pariyeseyya, ‘orenaḍḍhamāso seso gimhāna’nti, katvā nivāseyya, nissaggiyaṃ pācittiyaṃ. Rain robes must be looked for during the last month of the summer. It could be put to use from the last half month of the summer. If one searches for (and obtains) or wears a rain robe before this time frame, a nissaggiya pācittiya offence will be committed.
      • vocab: #[[pāḷi vocab (1st year) ]]
        • orena [ora + ena]↔ind. (+gen) earlier (than); sooner (than); lit. by low
        • ora [ava > o + ra]↔adj. lower; inferior; 2 nt. this side; this bank; near side
    • description
      • a vassikasāṭikacīvara can be procured (pariyesati) during which period?→from the last month of the gimhāna season (āsāḷhī) to the end of the vassāna season; this 5 month-period is called pariyesanakhetta
      • a vassikasāṭikacīvara can be procured (pariyesati), made (karoti) and worn (nivāseti) during which period?→from the sukkapakkha of āsāḷhī (the last pakkha of the gimhāna season) to the end of the vassāna season; this period is called kāraṇa-nivāsana-khetta
      • a vassikasāṭikacīvara can be determined (adhiṭṭhāti) during which period?→only during the vassāna season
      • note:
        • there is a disagreement about the word karaṇa
    • pāḷi terms
