np 4 - purāṇacīvarasikkhāpada


  • background
    • the Tathāgata was residing in the Jetavana, Sāvatthi at one time; Ven. Udāyī's ex-wife went forth among the bhikkhunis and they would frequently visit each other; one day, he sat on a seat in front of her exposing his genitals and she also did the same; while looking at her genitals lustfully, he emitted semen; then the inner robe was taken by her to wash, and she took some of the semen in her mouth. A portion of it was inserted into her genitals. This made her pregnant. Following this particular incident, the Buddha declared a training rule prohibiting monks from getting their robes washed by unrelated bhikkhunis
    • notes:
      • the bhikkhunis end up having to be protected by the monks which increases their association
      • the bhikkhunis are encouraged to associate monks and the monks protect the bhikkhunis, but at the same time bhikkhunis are agocara for bhikkhus
      • 2 rules to control the distance between unrelated monks and nuns
      • the rules only refers to bhikkhunis, that is maybe because of the difference in sīla which naturally distances bhikkhus from the other non-bhikkhunis (the anupasampannas might also be more under watch which naturally distances them from the bhikkhus)
      • payanti (breastfeeding) bhikkhuni: she can take care of the child but after some period the child has to be given to someone's custody
      • one is naturally not attracted to a relative and an unrelated one does not know the limits, therefore the limitations were imposed on unrelated ones
  • training rule in pāḷi
    • yo pana bhikkhu aññātikāya bhikkhuniyā purāṇacīvaraṃ dhovāpeyya vā rajāpeyya vā ākoṭāpeyya vā, nissaggiyaṃ pācittiyaṃ (If a monk gets an unrelated female monk to wash, die or beat a used robe a nissaggiya pācittiya offence will be committed)
    • vocab: #[[pāḷi vocab (1st year) ]]
      • purāṇa [pura + aṇa]↔adj. previous; old; ancient; 2 adj. worn out; old; used
      • ākoṭāpeti [ā + √kuṭ + *āpe + ti]↔pr. (+acc) makes beat; causes to pound; makes pummel
  • description
    • what is the purpose of this training rule?→to avoid bhikkhus from getting their robes washed by unrelated bhikkhunis since the brahmacariya could be adversely impacted by innapropriate relationships with unrelated bhikkhunis

    • bhikkhuni
      • 4 types of bhikkhunis
        • aṭṭhagarudhammehi upasampannā: this is Mahā Pajāpatī Gotamī, the younger sister of the Buddha’s mother (who was also his stepmother), who received the higher ordination by accepting the eight cardinal rules made by the Buddha
        • ekato (paripuṇṇa) upasampannā: nuns ordained following Mahāpajāpatī gotamī (500) until aṭṭhavācikupasampadā was established and bhikkhus who went through a natural sex change (only one case recorded)
        • ekato (aparipuṇṇa) upasampannā: after aṭṭhavācikupasampadā was established, nuns who had been ordained by the bhikkhunī saṅgha but not yet from the bhikkhu saṅgha were so called; they are not a full-fledged bhikkhunī
        • ubhato upasampannā: a bhikkhuni ordained by both saṅghas
        • note: the aparipuṇṇa is not included under the term bhikkhuni in bhikkhu rules; offenses regarding aparipuṇṇas are lighter
    • purāṇacīvara (used robe)
      • a nivāsana parūpana robe that is dyed and marked (kappabindu) that was used at least once for wearing or covering, making contact with the body (kāyaparibhoga)
      • note:
        • it includes kāyaparibhoga such as carrying it on the shoulder, on the head, using it as a pillow, etc
        • it does not include using it without coming into contact with it, such as using it as a shade (holding it over one's head) or as a sheet under the paccattharaṇa
    • nivāsana robe↔robe that is worn on the body as antaravāsaka
    • pārupana robe↔robe that is worn on the body as uttarāsaṅga
    • kāyaparibhoga↔using with the body, including lying / sitting on it, putting it on the head, etc

  • aṅgāni
    • aññātikāya bhikkhunīya: ordering an aññātika bhikkhuni, who is within the proximity of 12 cubits, to wash, dye or beat the robe
    • purāṇacīvaraṁ: the robe being a purāṇa cīvara
    • dhovāpeyya vā rajāpeyya vā ākoṭāpeyya vā: that same bhikkhuni carrying one of the ordered activities such as washing, dying or beating
  • nissajjana
    • “Idaṃ me, bhante, puraṇacīvaraṃ aññātikāya bhikkhuniyā dhovāpitaṃ (rajāpitaṃ / ākoṭāpitaṃ) nissaggiyaṃ. Imāhaṃ āyasmato nissajjāmi.”
