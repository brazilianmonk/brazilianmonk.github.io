np 6 - aññātakaviññattisikkhāpada


  • background
    • The Tathāgata was residing at the Jetavana temple near Sāvatthi. Venerable Upananda who was a son of the Sākyan clan is an eloquent Dhamma orator. One rich person (seṭṭhi) who was impressed by his sermons, requested that he be informed if the priest requires any of the requisites. Then, Upananda Thera, requested one of the shawls out of the two he was wearing. Since the rich man could not offer one of them and return home in one shawl, he promised that he would send the shawl after having reached home. Venerable Upananda did not agree to this idea and insisted that the shawl be given immediately. Then, the rich man gave one of his shawls and went home with one shawl. The people who got to know about this incident began to bad-mouth the priest. Therefore, this training rule was declared inhibiting monks from asking for robes from unrelated lay devotees. On another occasion, robes of bhikkhus who had embarked on a long journey were stolen. Since the Buddha had laid down a training rule prohibiting monks from asking for robes from aññātika and appavārita lay devotees, the monks whose robes were looted came back to the Buddha naked. They even paid respect to the Buddha unclothed. Thus, based on this instance, a sub charter was declared for this training rule
    • who is the venerable usually connected with the rules regarding robes?→Upananda Thera
  • training rule in pāḷi
    • Yo pana bhikkhu aññātakaṃ gahapatiṃ vā gahapatāniṃ vā cīvaraṃ viññāpeyya, aññatra samayā, nissaggiyaṃ pācittiyaṃ. Tatthāyaṃ samayo, acchinnacīvaro vā hoti bhikkhu naṭṭhacīvaro vā ayaṃ tattha samayo. (A monk asks for a robe from an unrelated male or female householder during an inappropriate time (and obtains it). For this act the monk commits a nissaggiya pācittiya offence. What is the suitable time to ask for robes? When the robes have been stolen by thieves or when the robes have been destroyed. These are the suitable occasions to ask for robes from lay devotees.)

  • description
    • what is the purpose of this rule?→to prevent monks from making unsuitable requests from a aññātaka appavārita dāyaka
  • pāḷi terms
    • pavārita↔a lay devotee who has invited a monk to request for things within the limits of the invitation (under this rule it must be an invitation with regard to any requisites or at least with regard to robes) note: the donor is not bound to give after inviting; if the donor changes or rescinds the invitation, the donor becomes one who has not invited
    • appavārita dāyaka under this rule is→a lay devotee who has not invited a monk with regard to any requisites or at least with regard to robes
    • acchinnacīvaro samayo↔the occasion in which at least 2 robes have been stolen
    • naṭṭhacīvaro samayo↔the occasion in which at least 2 robes (starting with 3) have been destroyed or decayed beyond a usable state
