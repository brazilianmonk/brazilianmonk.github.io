np 27 - mahāpesakārasikkhāpada #edited


  • background
    • The Tathāgata was residing at the Jetavana monastery near the city of Sāvatthi. One man ready to leave for a different region said as follows to his wife: “Collect some thread and order that particular cloth-weaver to prepare a robe and keep it. I’m hoping to offer it to Venerable Upananda.” A monk who was travelling for alms heard this and informed Venerable Upananda about the gain he is going to get in future. Venerable Upananda then went to the cloth-weaver and instructed him to make the robe longer and broader. The weaver went to the wife of the donor and asked for more thread. Then the wife of the donor gave the weaver another portion of thread that was equal to what was given earlier. The aforementioned man once back at home, got to know the news about the robe, and offered the robe to Upananda Thera. However, he criticised the act of Venerable Upananda for instructing to enlarge the robe. The Buddha who came to know this matter, declared this training rule. mem aid: Jetavana a man, before travelling, arranged for a robe to be weaved and kept intending to offer it to āyasmā Upananda later the ven. got wind of it and asked the weaver for a longer and broader robe who then asked for more thread from the family the donor offered the robe but criticized the thera
  • training rule in pāḷi
    • Bhikkhuṃ paneva uddissa aññātako gahapati vā gahapatānī vā tantavāyehi cīvaraṃ vāyāpeyya, tatra ce so bhikkhu pubbe appavārito tantavāye upasaṅkamitvā cīvare vikappaṃ āpajjeyya, ‘Idaṃ kho, āvuso, cīvaraṃ maṃ uddissa viyyati, āyatañca karotha, vitthatañca, appitañca suvītañca suppavāyitañca suvilekhitañca suvitacchitañca karotha, appeva nāma mayampi āyasmantānaṃ kiñcimattaṃ anupadajjeyyāmā’ ti. Evañca so bhikkhu vatvā kiñcimattaṃ anupadajjeyya antamaso piṇḍapātamattampi, nissaggiyaṃ pācittiyaṃ. An unrelated male or female householder gets a robe woven by a cloth-weaver for a particular bhikkhu. Even before the lay sponsor asked for instructions from the monk on how to make the robe, that bhikkhu goes up to the cloth-weaver and orders: “This robe is being woven for me. Therefore, make it longer, broader, lay the thread in such a way to make it thicker, lay the thread evenly, weave the centre of the robe well. We will give you something in return”, and offers alms at the very least. This results in a nissaggiya pācittiya offence.

  • instances of offenses
    • when an aññātaka appavārita dāyaka is getting a robe woven by a tantavāya, a bhikkhu goes to the tantavāya and request it to be made larger in length (āyataṁ) / width (vitthataṁ) / thickness (appitaṁ), the tantavāya asks for more thread from the donor: every act of the tantavāya after being so requested by the monk: {{032965717156883345::dkṭ}} when the enlarged robe is completed: {{032965717156883345::np}} doing so after the dāyaka invited to ask: {{032965717156883345::no offense}} asking to make it of less value: {{032965717156883345::no offense}} asking for it to be modified in a way that does not require more thread: {{032965717156883345::no offense}} simply giving piṇḍapāta: {{032965717156883345::not np}}
    • the 3 modifications that lead to an offense:
      • āyataṁ (longer)
      • vitthataṁ (wider)
      • appitaṁ (thicker)
      • note: only these three lead to an offense because they cannot be done without adding at least a little more thread; the other 4 prompts (suvītaṁca suppavāyitaṁca suvilekhitañca suvitacchitañca) do not require more thread, therefore do not lead to an offense
