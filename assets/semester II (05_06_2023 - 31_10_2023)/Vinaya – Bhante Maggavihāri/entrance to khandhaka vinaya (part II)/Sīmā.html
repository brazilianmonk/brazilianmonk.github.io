  • The Tathāgata emphasized that disciplinary acts such as uposatha and pavāraṇā must be carried out by the united Saṅgha. The unity of the Saṅgha is determined by three main factors:
    • 1. Participation of all bhikkhus: All bhikkhus residing in a specific area should participate in the assembly for vinayakamma and remain within close proximity of hatthapāsa to each other.
    • 2. Consent from absent monks: If any monks remaining in the specific area are unable to participate, they should send their consent (chanda) to the assembly.
    • 3. Non-opposition: Monks physically present in the vinayakamma should not oppose the act, either verbally or physically.
    • If any of these factors are incomplete, such as when even a single monk from the area fails to attend or provide consent, the vinayakamma becomes invalid. Hence, it is crucial to understand the area where the participation or consent of monks is required for a vinayakamma, which is known as sīmā in Pāḷi.

  • Sīmā Nimitta: Boundary Signs of Sīmās The area of a baddha sīmās are marked by signs known as sīmā nimitta, which act as boundaries or boundary points for the sīmā. The Buddha has recommended eight different inanimate physical objects that can serve as sīmā nimitta. These signs are as follows:
    • Pabbata nimitta - A mountain made entirely of {{9298563369508668::rock or soil}}, or a combination of both, that is the size of {{9298563369508668::an }}{{9298563369508668::elephant or larger. }}{{9298563369508668:: }}
    • 2. Pāsāṇa nimitta - A rock or lump of {{803470063814846::iron }}that ranges in size from {{803470063814846::a minimum of a jaggery lump (about 32 pala) to the size of an elephant}}. If it is a flat rock, it can be even larger.
    • 3. Vana nimitta - Even a thicket with at least {{01782351812731764::four or five tree}}{{01782351812731764::s}} out of which at least three trees containing {{01782351812731764::heartwood }}can be considered a vana (forest) nimitta. A group of arecaceae trees (palm family) cannot be considered as vana nimitta.
    • 4. Rukkha nimitta - A tree, even with a slender trunk as thin as a {{9374691891825326::stylu}}{{9374691891825326::s (a pen used to write on palm leafs)}}, can be considered a rukkha nimitta as long as it has grown to a height of {{9374691891825326::eight inches}} (aṅguli) and contains {{9374691891825326::heartwood}}.
    • 5. Magga nimitta - A footpath or cart-path that passes through {{20310446301945428::two or three}} villages.
    • 6. Vammika nimitta - An ant hill that reaches a height of {{520948976119376::eight inches}} (aṅguli), even if it was built on the same day, with a circumference resembling an {{520948976119376::ox horn}}{{520948976119376::.}}
    • 7. Nadī nimitta - Rivers suitable for udakukkhepa sīmas can be considered nadī nimitta (river). More details about them can be found under udakukkhepa sīmā.
    • 8. Udaka nimitta - Places with non-flowing water, such as natural lakes, ponds, oceans, and lagoons. Even {{19324482861071068::a pit dug just before the vinayakamma and filled with water}} can be used as an udaka nimitta if the water in the pit does not dry out until the vinayakamma is completed. Flowing water or water in a vessel does not suffice as an udaka nimitta.

  • Threefold Sampatti
    • The establishment of a baddha sīmā requires the recitation of a ñatti dutiya kammavācā. Its recitation is called {{9631750216794839::sīmā sammuti vinayakamma}} – disciplinary act done to establish a baddha sīmā. To ensure its effectiveness, three factors known as sampatti must be present. These factors are as follows:
      • 1. Nimitta Sampatti: The sīmā should be established by correctly naming and connecting the signs (nimitta) in the manner described earlier. When the vinayakamma is performed in this way, it is considered to have nimitta sampatti.It must be one of the eight nimittas.
      • 2. Parisa Sampatti: At least four upasampadā monks are required to establish a sīmā. If the sīmā is established within a village, all the monks residing within the boundaries of the village should either participate in the act or provide their consent. The participation or consent of monks who are in {{3508147518381164::already established baddhasīmās, rivers, seas, or natural lakes}} within the village is not necessary. Furthermore, it is crucial that the participating monks do not object to the vinayakamma. When the disciplinary act is performed with these conditions fulfilled, it is said to have parisa sampatti. chanda is from the sima where the kamma is done, not the area that will be established;
      • 3. Kammavācā Sampatti: The recommended ñatti dutiya kammavācā must be correctly recited according to the Vinaya by a competent monk. This recitation should be made in the presence of the gathered assembly of monks. When the disciplinary act is performed with this proper recitation, it is considered to have kammavācā sampatti.
  • Types of Baddha Sīmā
    • 1. Samānasaṃvāsaka Sīmā: All baddha sīmās are considered samānasaṃvāsaka sīmā because {{26872413460797406::monks in communion can perform all types of vinayakammas in any baddha sīmā. }}
    • 2. Avippavāsa Sīmā / ticīvara avippavāsa sīmā: If needed, a baddha sīmā can be designated as an avippavāsa sīmā. In such cases, a bhikkhu can keep the three robes (ticīvara) within the designated baddha sīmā and reside anywhere within its boundaries at dawn. The ticīvara does not need to be within the monk's hatthapāsa. However, when approving a baddha sīmā as an avippavāsa sīmā, the village areas (gāma) and village proximity (gāmūpacāra) within the baddha sīmā should be excluded. These areas, although part of the baddha sīmā, cannot be designated as avippavāsa zones. The avippavāsa sammuti should be given after the proper establishment of the baddha sīmā.
    • 3. Khaṇḍa Sīmā and Mahā Sīmā: When performing a disciplinary act within a baddha sīmā, all bhikkhus present in the area must gather in the assembly or send their consent. However, gathering all the bhikkhus and obtaining their consent can be challenging in large baddha sīmās. To address this, small sīmā zones, known as khaṇḍa sīmā, are established within the larger baddha sīmā to facilitate the performance of vinayakammas other than uposatha and pavāraṇā easily. The larger sīmā that encompasses the khaṇḍa sīmās is called the mahā sīmā.
  • Abaddha Sīmā
    • An abaddha sīmā refers areas which is not a baddha sīmā. There are primarily three types of abaddha sīmās
      • Gāma Sīmā: A gāma sīmā encompasses areas where the inhabitants are required to pay taxes to a king or regional leader. This includes commercial towns (nigama), cities (nagara), which are large administrative areas. The demarcated area by the government as a village, market towns or a city constitutes the gāma sīmā.
        • note:
          • In some cases, kings may declare certain areas as separate villages ({{42887126048120305::visuṃ gāma}}) and hand them over to individuals or temples. These areas are known as {{42887126048120305::visuṃ gāma}} sīmās. Generally, villages set aside for monasteries are exempt from taxes. However, even these sīmās fall under the category of gāma sīmā. This indicates that the imposition of taxes is not the primary criterion for an area to be considered a gāma. Any area declared as a village by a king or government should be regarded as a gāma sīmā. All gāma sīmās are samānasaṃvāsaka sīmās since monks in communion can perform vinayakammas in these areas. However, they do not become avippavāsa sīmās.
          • visuṁgāmasīmā: a separate area within another gāma sīmā given by the king; 3 prominent sayadaws and vinayalankaratika say it is not necessary for the king to say it is a visuṁgāma as long as the king separated the land and offers it
          • between gāmasīmās there is no problem with them being connected

      • Sattabbhantara Sīmā: An abbhantara refers to {{2267895823489534::28 cubits of a majjhima purisa}}, and sattabbhantara signifies seven abbhantaras, equivalent to {{2267895823489534::196 cubits of a majjhima purisa}}. The sattabbhantara sīmā represents the area covered by a circle with a radius of seven abbhantaras. It is counted from the outermost monk of the assembly. This is a dynamic / flexible sīmā. this sīmā does not overlap with the gāmasīmā; There are two types of sattabbhantara sīmās:
        • Arañña Sattabbhantara Sīmā: These sattabbhantara zones are located in areas apart (away) from the proximity of villages (gāmūpacāra) and are typically found in forests (uninhabited area). They can also exist in deserts.
        • Dīpa Sattabbhantara Sīmā: Apart from forests and deserts, sattabbhantara sīmās can also be found in uninhabited islands in the sea. Dīpa sattabbhantara sīmās are found in slands that are far enough from the mainland, making it impossible for fishermen to {{9262078243903662::travel to and return}}{{9262078243903662:: }}{{9262078243903662::within a day}}{{9262078243903662:: using ordinary boats (sailboats, }}{{9262078243903662::not motor}}{{9262078243903662::)}}. Islands accessible to fishermen to go back and forth within a day should be considered {{9262078243903662::part of the gāma sīmā at the shore of the ocean}}.
        • note: According to Vinaya, they are inherently avippavāsa sīmās
      • Udakukkhepa Sīmā: This refers to the demarcated areas found in bodies of water. It is defined by {{8523899390673717::the area where water or sand falls when thrown in all directions from a fixed point by a ordinary strong man (thāma majjhima purisa)}}; it does not overlap with the land on both sides; 3 types according to the type of water body:
        • Nadī Udakukkhepa Sīmā
          • 2 factors for being a river
            • it endures the whole vassāna season during the reign of righteous rulers when rainfall occurs at regular intervals of 5 or 10 days
            • at least 1 or 2 inches of the properly worn antaravāsaka become damp while crossing it
            • note: it is recognized as a river from its source regardless of where it flows into
        • Samudda Udakukkhepa Sīmā
        • Jātassara Udakukkhepa Sīmā: A natural lake, also known as sayaṃ jāta jātassara, refers to a pit that forms naturally and becomes filled with water from various directions. It is crucial that the water remains in the lake without drying up throughout {{23160856928330653::the entire rainy season}} under the reign of righteous kings. If the pit of water is manually dug by humans or animals, it does not qualify as a natural lake. Similarly, if the water in the pit dries up shortly after rainfall, during the rainy season, leaving insufficient water for drinking or washing hands and feet, it cannot be considered a jātassara. However, if the pit forms due to the natural erosion of a riverbank or seashore and possesses the other specified characteristics, it can be identified as a sayaṃ jāta jātassara.
        • note: they are not categorized as avippavāsa sīmās
