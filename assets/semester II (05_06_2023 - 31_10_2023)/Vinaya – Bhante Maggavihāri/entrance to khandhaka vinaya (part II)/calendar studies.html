calendar studies #edited


  • days and hours
    • a {{7123742167393994::divasa}} (day) is the duration between one dawn to the next and comprises {{7123742167393994::60}}{{7123742167393994:: }}{{7123742167393994::ghaṭikā}} (buddhist hours) with each of them being equivalent to {{7123742167393994::24 minutes}} in modern standards
  • fortnights and months
    • one {{9628323819374938::pakkha}} is the period of fifteen consecutive days either between the full and new moon, or between the new and full moon; the waning period is the {{9628323819374938::kālapakkha}} while the waxing period is the {{9628323819374938::sukkapakkha}}; both together constitute a {{9628323819374938::māsa}} (month); the month commences with {{9628323819374938::kālapakkha}} and concludes with {{9628323819374938::sukkapakkha}}, ensuring that each month concludes with a {{9628323819374938::full}} moon; however, according to the Abhidhānappadīpikā, a month begins with {{9628323819374938::sukkapakkha}} and ends with {{9628323819374938::kālapakkha}} in one year there are {{9628323819374938::18}} pakkhas with 15 days and {{9628323819374938::6}} pakkhas with 14 days
  • seasons
    • in one year there are {{2670845918658269::12}} months 4 months collectively form an {{2670845918658269::utu}} (season) in one year there are 3 seasons: {{2670845918658269::vassāna}} / {{2670845918658269::vassa}} / {{2670845918658269::pāvusa}}(rainy season), {{2670845918658269::hemanta }}(winter) and {{2670845918658269::gimhāna}} / {{2670845918658269::gimha}} / {{2670845918658269::uṇha}} / {{2670845918658269::nidāgha }}(summer) each season can be further divided into 2 smaller seasons known as {{2670845918658269::khuddaka utu}} each small season comprises 2 months totalling 6 small seasons: {{2670845918658269::vassāna}}, {{2670845918658269::sarada}}, {{2670845918658269::hemanta}}, {{2670845918658269::sisira}}, {{2670845918658269::vasanta}}, {{2670845918658269::gimhāna}}

  • sun's course
    • during the three small seasons, namely vassāna, sarada, and hemanta, the sun follows the {{3992637552976803::dakkhiṇāyana}}{{3992637552976803:: (southern path)}} during the sisira, vasanta, and gimhāna small seasons, it travels along the {{3992637552976803::uttarāyana }}{{3992637552976803::(northern path)}} A {{3992637552976803::vassa}} (year) is completed when the sun completes its journey along both paths once

  • days of the week
    • in ancient pāḷi books, specific names for days within a pakkha are not mentioned; generally, they are denoted using ordinal numbers such as "aṭṭhamī", "cātuddasī", "paṇṇarasī" and so on; however, A. P. Buddhadatta has assigned pāḷi names to the days of the week in the christian calendar as follows: Monday {{6236092112923388::Candavāra (moon)}} Tuesday {{6236092112923388::Kujavāra (Mars)}} Wednesday {{6236092112923388::Budhavāra (Mercury)}} Thursday {{6236092112923388::Guruvāra (Jupiter)}} Friday {{6236092112923388::Sukkavāra (Venus)}} Saturday {{6236092112923388::Sanivāra / Soravāra (Saturn)}} Sunday {{6236092112923388::Ravivāra (Sun)}}
