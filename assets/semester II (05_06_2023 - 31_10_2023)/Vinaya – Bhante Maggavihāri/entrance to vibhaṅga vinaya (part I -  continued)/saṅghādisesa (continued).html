  • aññabhāgiyasikkhāpadaṁ #edited
    • background
      • once the Buddha was residing at {{4969228568135087::Rājagaha, Veḷuvana monastery, in Kalandakanivāpa }} when Venerables {{4969228568135087::Mettiya and Bhūmajaka (/Bhummajaka)}} while descending from the mount {{4969228568135087::Gijjhakūṭa}} saw {{4969228568135087::a male goat (chagalaka) and a female goat (ajikā) having sex; }}they decided to name them as {{4969228568135087::Dabbamallaputta and bhikkhunī Mettiyā respectively}}; afterwards, they told other monks what they had seen using those names; the monks who did not believe this accusation, advised the duo not to make such allegations and informed the Buddha of the matter; who then gathered the Saṅgha and questioned the accused, Venerable {{4969228568135087::Dabbamallaputta}}, three times, whether he had committed such act, who replied “{{4969228568135087::Yathā maṁ bhante bhagavā jānāti (The Blessed One knows who I am)}}”; the Buddha then said {{4969228568135087::“Dabba, this issue will not be solved with such type of answers. You need to directly say whether you have committed such an inappropriate act or not”}}; to which the venerable replied, {{4969228568135087::“Bhante, I do not recall having had sex even in a dream, let alone while awake”}}; the Buddha then instructed the Saṅgha to investigate the accusers, Venerables {{4969228568135087::Mettiya and Bhummajaka}}, further; the truth was disclosed and the Buddha promulgated the aññabhāgiya training rule
      • chabbaggiya (group of six) refers to six monks who were very good friends but did not live a virtuous life during the Buddha’s time; all the six monks had {{25086052468228104::1.500}} disciple monks, altogether {{25086052468228104::9.000}}; in Vinaya texts, any member of this large group was referred to as chabbaggiya monks in general
    • pāḷi terms
      • aññabhāgiya↔something other than the accused (something external to the accused)
      • adhikaraṇa↔animate or inanimate object that is the base for the lesa; in this case the goat
      • the goat that was named by the chabbaggiyas as Dabbalamallaputta is an {{040302988648710736::aññabhāgiya adhikaraṇa (an object other than the accused) }}
      • lesa↔attribute, name, pretext; there are 10 types (jāti, nāma, gotta, liṅga...); in this case it is the name "Dabbamallaputta" that was bestowed upon the goat by them
      • aññābhāgiyassa adhikaraṇassa kiñcidesaṁ lesamattaṁ upādāya...(taking the lesa from the third party and accusing) which means that they first gave the name (lesa) Dabba to the goat (aññābhāgiya adhikaraṇa) and then used that lesa to make the accusation
    • 6 aṅga
      • ...bhikkhuṁ...: the accused is generally perceived as being a bhikkhu
        • perceived by the accuser or by others in general? #q🙋🏼‍♂
      • suddhasaññā (the accuser perceives the accused as pure with regard to prjk offenses)
      • amūlakacodanā (being a baseless accusation)
      • aññabhāgiyassa...upādāya...: accusing by taking an attribute (lesa) of an external object (aññabhāgiya adhikaraṇa)
      • appeva nāma...cāveyyān'ti: sammukhācodanā (done in the presence of the accused) cāvanādhippāyena (intended on expelling)
      • the accused understands the accusation immediately

    • background
      • once the Buddha was residing at {{8146421347930963::Rājagaha }}{{8146421347930963::in the }}{{8146421347930963::Veḷuvana }}{{8146421347930963::monastery}} when Venerable {{8146421347930963::Devadatta}} approached monks such as {{8146421347930963::Kokālika}} and proposed to cause a schism in the Saṅgha by requesting 5 things from the Buddha that He would certainly reject, and then use that to conquer people's faith; the 5 things were that all monks always be {{8146421347930963::araññaka }}{{8146421347930963::(forest-dwellers), }}{{8146421347930963::piṇḍapātika }}{{8146421347930963::(alms goers), }}{{8146421347930963::paṁsukūlika }}{{8146421347930963::(rag-robe wearers), }}{{8146421347930963::rukkhamūlika }}{{8146421347930963::(root-of-tree dwellers), }}{{8146421347930963::yāva jīvaṁ macchamaṁsaṁ na khādeyyuṁ}}{{8146421347930963:: (vegetarian)}}; being requested thus the Buddha replied that such practices were optional; the ven. then approach the people and said that the Buddha had declined five types of wholesome practices suggested by him and gained followers; coming to know of his plot the Buddha laid down the saṅghabheda training rule which is intended to {{8146421347930963::prevent the person who is attempting to cause a schism from committing such kamma (saṅghabheda kamma which is an ānantariyakamma) which is a prjk offense}}, by inflicting a saṅghādisesa offence beforehand note: lohituppādakamma is also a prjk offense

    • 8 steps leading to saṅghabheda
      • vatthu: bringing up one of the 18 bhedakaravatthus
      • intention: heaving the unwholesome intention to promote one's ideals
      • sīmā: doing so within the sīmā where formal acts are performed
      • followers: converting 4 or more monks to accept one's ideals
      • kamma: performing one of the vinayakammas (any of the fourfold vinayakammas)
      • consent: doing so without the consent of at least 4 bhikkhus within the sīmā
      • number: there being at least 9 bhikkhus within the sīmā
      • sides: 4 of them are bhedanuvattakas, the bhedaka himself and at least 4 monks opposing the bheda

      • once the Buddha was residing at the {{7193962991543492::Jetavana }}{{7193962991543492::monastery in }}{{7193962991543492::Sāvatthi}}; during that time, two monks Venerables {{7193962991543492::Assaji }}{{7193962991543492::and }}{{7193962991543492::Punabbasuka }}{{7193962991543492::and their followers}} were living in {{7193962991543492::Kīṭāgiri}} where they were having improper associations with lay devotees and performing unsuitable acts such as growing flower trees, as well as committing utterly inappropriate acts for monks to do; one day a virtuous monk, after finishing his rains retreat in the county called {{7193962991543492::Kāsī}} was on his way to meet the Buddha, entered the district and went for alms round abiding by the vinaya rules; however, due to associating those monks, lay devotees could not recognize the value of abiding by the Vinaya rules; they criticized the newcomer's behavior and praised the local monks; one wise lay devotee saw the virtuous monk, approached him, asked if he had gotten alms easily, and hearing a negative answer invited the monk to his house and offered him a meal; he then reported the bad behavior of the local monks and requested it to be reported to the Buddha; after doing so the Buddha {{7193962991543492::advised Venerables }}{{7193962991543492::Sāriputta }}{{7193962991543492::and }}{{7193962991543492::Moggallāna }}{{7193962991543492::to go to Kīṭāgiri and perform a }}{{7193962991543492::pabbājanīyakamma }}{{7193962991543492::to all the monks there; with difficulty they did so but the monks were displeased and did not submit to it and instead criticized them back; }}being informed of what had happened the Buddha declared the kuladūsaka training rule prohibiting monks from not submitting to the pabbājanīyakamma that has been done righteously
      • relationship between the laity and the saṅgha
        • the laity is responsible for providing the basic requisites needed by the saṅgha and the saṅgha for providing the laity with the Dhamma; if monks provide the laity with requisites the laity will associate monks for the sake of requisites, favours or improper friendship, causing the laity to lose respect, expect material things from the monks and dwindle in wholesome states while causing the saṅgha to lose support, the monks to be intent on gain, causing the disappearance of the sāsana "{{8505625782263755::agārā anagārāca ubho aññoñña nissitā}}" (householders and renunciants both depend on each other)
        • in the monastery the Buddha always talks first

          • veḷudāna – Giving cane to the laity

      • pāpasamācāra↔engaging in unsuitable acts to please the laity, breaking their correct faith
        • 4 pāpasamācāra
          • mālāvacchaṃ ropeti ropāpeti (planting flowers and getting them planted)
          • mālāvacchaṃ siñcati siñcāpeti (watering flower plants and getting them watered)
          • mālāvacchaṃ ocināti ocināpeti (plucking flowers and getting them plucked)
          • mālāvacchaṃ gantheti ganthāpeti (making garlands and getting them made)
          • they are inappropriate only when done to please the laity; when done for monastic purposes as per rules of Vinaya it is appropriate

      • pabbājanīyakamma↔formal vinayakamma performed by the saṅgha on an upasampanna that repeatedly performs unsuitable acts of kuladūsana or pāpasamācāra to prevent him/her from corrupting the faith of the laity; it consists of banishing him/her from dwelling in the same monastery and of going for alms on the same village, as well as imposing certain duties to follow
        • when one (a bhikkhu or bhikkhunī) constantly engages in acts of kuladūsana, the Saṅgha must perform the {{06069610255587565::pabbājanīyakamma}} which is a kamma that consists of {{06069610255587565::preventing one from residing in the monastery in which one engaged on such acts, going for alms in that village, as well as imposing certain duties one must follow}} after the kamma, if the offender remains residing in the same monastery, can the offender go piṇḍapāta in the same village? what about another nearby village? {{06069610255587565::no, the offender is not allowed to go piṇḍapāta on neither of these villages}} after the kamma, can the offender live in another monastery in the same village? what about piṇḍapāta? {{06069610255587565::yes, the offender can reside in another monastery in the same village but cannot go piṇḍapāta on that village, although a nearby village is allowed}}

    • steps leading to the full offense
      • one repeatedly commits acts of kuladūsana or pāpasamācāra
      • the saṅgha should perform the pabbājanīyakamma on the offender
      • the offender does not conform to the pabbājanīyakamma and accuses the saṅgha of acting biasedly
      • the saṅgha should admonish the offender in private
      • the saṅgha should admonish the offender in the midst of the saṅgha
      • the saṅgha should perform samanubhāsanakamma on the offender
      • if the offender persist through all these steps the offender incurs the full sgds offense at the end of the third anusāvana
