      • sikkhāpada
        • sikkhāpada is {{15126430453517714::the training rules or precepts promulgated by the Bhagavā which are the foundation for achieving higher spiritual states, known as }}{{15126430453517714::adhisīla}}{{15126430453517714::, }}{{15126430453517714::adhicitta}}{{15126430453517714::, and }}{{15126430453517714::adhipaññā}}
        • 2 types of sikkhāpada
          • ovādasikkhāpada: these are exhortations by the Bhagavā that are found mostly in the Suttapiṭaka, where the Bhagavā advises bhikkhus on what actions to perform or avoid such as "pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti" (refrain from taking life) or the ovādapātimokkha
          • āṇāsikkhāpada: these are instructions by the Bhagavā, that are found in the Vinayapiṭaka, that have been established as binding rules
        • the threefold sāsana
          • pariyattisāsana (the study of the doctrine)
          • paṭipattisāsana (the practice)
          • paṭivedhasāsana (supramundane attainments)
        • 10 benefits of promulgating sikkhāpada
          • saṅghasuṭṭhutāya (for the excellence of the Saṅgha): under rājasikkhāpada: “Yo ca tathāgatassa {{7469453387277609::vacanaṃ sampaṭicchati, tassa taṃ dīgharattaṃ hitāya sukhāya hoti}}” (whoever accepts the Tathāgata's words, that would bring them benefit and happiness for a long time); on the other hand, Bhaddāli Sutta in the Majjhima Nikāya, emphasizes the detrimentals one has to face for not accepting them
            • is this the only meaning meant on this case? what about the excellence of the Saṅgha? #q🙋🏼‍♂
          • saṅghaphāsutāya (for the comfort of the Saṅgha): The establishment of precepts enables the Saṅgha to live together peacefully and in unity
          • dummaṅkūnaṃ puggalānaṃ niggahāya (for restraining obdurate individuals)
          • pesalānaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ phāsuvihārāya (for the comfort of well-behaved monks): when the boundaries of right and wrong are explicitly defined, well-behaved monks can practice their morality with ease, eliminating any doubts about what should or should not be done; furthermore, when immoral individuals are restrained, the observance of community acts is unhindered and moral monks can abide comfortably
          • diṭṭhadhammikānaṃ āsavānaṃ saṃvarāya (for the prevention of suffering here and now): adhering to the precepts prevents sufferings here and now from occurring, such as physical punishment, accusations, defamation, loss of respect or losing followers
          • samparāyikānaṃ āsavānaṃ paṭighātāya (for warding off suffering hereafter): adhering to the precepts prevents sufferings hereafter from occurring, such as bad destinations and various unpleasant results in saṁsāra
          • appasannānaṃ pasādāya (for instilling faith in those without faith): learning about the precepts and observing disciplined bhikkhus and bhikkhunīs can inspire faith even in non-Buddhists such as the case of a non-Buddhist brahmin, well-versed in Vedas, who, developed faith in the Buddhasāsana after studying the Vinaya
          • pasannānaṃ bhiyyobhāvāya (for the increase of faith in the faithful): learning about the precepts and observing disciplined bhikkhus and bhikkhunīs can increase the faith of Buddhists themselves
          • saddhammaṭṭhitiyāya (for the endurance of the true Dhamma); how does the promulgation of precepts lead to the endurance of the three saddhammas? #editing
            • saddhamma comprises three aspects:
              • pariyattisaddhamma: the teachings found in the Tipiṭaka
              • paṭipattisaddhamma: austere practices (dhutaṅga), duties (khandhaka vatta and asīti mahāvatta), morality (sīla), concentration (samādhi), and insight (vipassanā)
              • paṭivedhasaddhamma: supramundane path (magga), fruition (phala), and Nibbāna
            • the promulgation of precepts leads to the study of vinaya and in order to gain a deeper understanding of the vinaya, monks and nuns engage in the study of other teachings of the Buddha which strengthens the pariyattisaddhamma the observance of precepts allows the proper development and fulfilment of the practical aspects of the teaching such as austerities, duties, morality, concentration and insight, strengthening the paṭipattisaddhamma the observance of precepts assists disciples to easily fulfill their practice and achieve the paths, fruitions and Nibbāna, which strengthens the paṭivedhasaddhamma
          • vinayānuggahāya (for the fostering of discipline): The establishment of precepts strengthens the fourfold Vinaya within the sāsana
            • vinaya is fourfold
              • saṃvaravinaya (discipline as restraint)
              • pahānavinaya (discipline as eradication)
              • samathavinaya (discipline as settlement of disciplinary issues)
              • paññattivinaya (discipline as promulgated rules)

          • the observance of the uposatha is found among other religions as well
          • there are {{41351940971711754::three}} uposatha days in a fortnight, namely {{41351940971711754::the eighth (aṭṭhamī), fourteenth (cātuddasī), and fifteenth (paṇṇarasī) days}} ; uposathavinayakamma is to be done on the {{41351940971711754::14th or 15th, }}except on the day of{{41351940971711754:: pavāraṇā }}in which the{{41351940971711754:: }}{{41351940971711754::pavāraṇāvinayakamma }}should be done instead

          • is the uposathavinayakamma the same as the pātimokkha recitation?→they are different kammas
          • 9 uposathas
            • divasavasena: 3 uposathas based on the day it is observed
              • cātuddasīuposatha: the one observed on the 14th
              • paṇṇarasīuposatha: the one observed on the 15th
              • sāmaggīuposatha: the one observed on the day a previously disunited saṅgha is reunited; if such day coincides with the pavāraṇa day, the pavāraṇavinayakamma should be performed instead
              • note: one can perform the uposatha on the 14th or 15th but traditionally it is performed on the 15th with the exception of the 3rd and 7th uposatha which is traditionally performed on the 14th
            • puggala- / kāraka-vasena: 3 uposathas based on the individuals who perform it
              • saṅghauposatha: the one observed by four or more bhikkhus/nīs in communion (samānasaṃvāsaka bhikkhu)
              • gaṇauposatha: the one observed by three or two bhikkhus/nīs in communion; it should not be done by obtaining chanda
              • puggalauposatha: the one observed by a single bhikkhu/nī
            • kātabbākāraka vasena: 3 uposathas based on the manner of performance
              • suttuddesauposatha: observed by a saṅgha by reciting the pātimokkha
                • note: any part of the Buddha's teaching can be called sutta
              • pārisuddhiuposatha: observed by three or two bhikkhus/nīs by declaring their purity (pārisuddhi) mutually
              • adhiṭṭhānauposatha: observed by a single bhikkhu/nī by determining, "Ajja me uposatho (paṇṇaraso/cātuddaso)"
              • vocab: #[[pāḷi vocab (1st year) ]]
                • pārisuddhi means {{7743788569267704::purity}}, while parisuddhi means {{7743788569267704::one who is pure}}
          • saṅghuposatha - pubbakaraṇādi (tasks to be performed before the saṅghuposatha)

              • sammajjanakaraṇaṃ: the uposatha venue should be swept
              • padīpujjalanaṃ: lamps should be lit (during the night)
              • udakaṭṭhapanaṃ: water for drinking and other uses should be made available
              • āsanapaññāpanaṃ: seats should be prepared (at very least with leaves)

    • Sīmā #editing
      • The Tathāgata emphasized that disciplinary acts such as uposatha and pavāraṇā must be carried out by the united Saṅgha. The unity of the Saṅgha is determined by three main factors:
        • 1. Participation of all bhikkhus: All bhikkhus residing in a specific area should participate in the assembly for vinayakamma and remain within close proximity of hatthapāsa to each other.
        • 2. Consent from absent monks: If any monks remaining in the specific area are unable to participate, they should send their consent (chanda) to the assembly.
        • 3. Non-opposition: Monks physically present in the vinayakamma should not oppose the act, either verbally or physically.
        • If any of these factors are incomplete, such as when even a single monk from the area fails to attend or provide consent, the vinayakamma becomes invalid. Hence, it is crucial to understand the area where the participation or consent of monks is required for a vinayakamma, which is known as sīmā in Pāḷi.

      • Threefold Sampatti
        • The establishment of a baddha sīmā requires the recitation of a ñatti dutiya kammavācā. Its recitation is called {{9631750216794839::sīmā sammuti vinayakamma}} – disciplinary act done to establish a baddha sīmā. To ensure its effectiveness, three factors known as sampatti must be present. These factors are as follows:
          • 1. Nimitta Sampatti: The sīmā should be established by correctly naming and connecting the signs (nimitta) in the manner described earlier. When the vinayakamma is performed in this way, it is considered to have nimitta sampatti.It must be one of the eight nimittas.
          • 2. Parisa Sampatti: At least four upasampadā monks are required to establish a sīmā. If the sīmā is established within a village, all the monks residing within the boundaries of the village should either participate in the act or provide their consent. The participation or consent of monks who are in {{3508147518381164::already established baddhasīmās, rivers, seas, or natural lakes}} within the village is not necessary. Furthermore, it is crucial that the participating monks do not object to the vinayakamma. When the disciplinary act is performed with these conditions fulfilled, it is said to have parisa sampatti. chanda is from the sima where the kamma is done, not the area that will be established;
          • 3. Kammavācā Sampatti: The recommended ñatti dutiya kammavācā must be correctly recited according to the Vinaya by a competent monk. This recitation should be made in the presence of the gathered assembly of monks. When the disciplinary act is performed with this proper recitation, it is considered to have kammavācā sampatti.
      • Types of Baddha Sīmā
        • 1. Samānasaṃvāsaka Sīmā: All baddha sīmās are considered samānasaṃvāsaka sīmā because {{26872413460797406::monks in communion can perform all types of vinayakammas in any baddha sīmā. }}
        • 2. Avippavāsa Sīmā / ticīvara avippavāsa sīmā: If needed, a baddha sīmā can be designated as an avippavāsa sīmā. In such cases, a bhikkhu can keep the three robes (ticīvara) within the designated baddha sīmā and reside anywhere within its boundaries at dawn. The ticīvara does not need to be within the monk's hatthapāsa. However, when approving a baddha sīmā as an avippavāsa sīmā, the village areas (gāma) and village proximity (gāmūpacāra) within the baddha sīmā should be excluded. These areas, although part of the baddha sīmā, cannot be designated as avippavāsa zones. The avippavāsa sammuti should be given after the proper establishment of the baddha sīmā.
        • 3. Khaṇḍa Sīmā and Mahā Sīmā: When performing a disciplinary act within a baddha sīmā, all bhikkhus present in the area must gather in the assembly or send their consent. However, gathering all the bhikkhus and obtaining their consent can be challenging in large baddha sīmās. To address this, small sīmā zones, known as khaṇḍa sīmā, are established within the larger baddha sīmā to facilitate the performance of vinayakammas other than uposatha and pavāraṇā easily. The larger sīmā that encompasses the khaṇḍa sīmās is called the mahā sīmā.

      • np 2 - dutiyakaṭhinasikkhāpada (uddositasikkhāpada)
        • background #editing
          • The Tathāgata was residing one time at the {{2098409862527062::Jetavana temple, Sāvatthi}}. During this period, most bhikkhus were in the habit of leaving their robes with other bhikkhus, and travelling to the many surrounding districts wearing the uttarāsaṅga (upper robe) and antarāvāsaka (inner robe) robes. Those robes that were left behind would become moldy. Having been aware of this situation, the Buddha declared this training rule On another occasion, in the city of {{2098409862527062::Kosambi}}, a bhikkhu became ill. A messenger was sent by his relatives inviting him to be taken care of. The monk stated: “The Buddha has preached that a dawn must not be passed, separated from the three robes. I have fallen ill”. The Buddha who came to know about this proclaimed: “Anujānāmi, bhikkhave, gilānassa bhikkhuno ticīvarena avippavāsasammutiṃ dātuṃ'' and declared a sub statute as “aññatra bhikkhusammutiyā” allowing monks to stay apart from the three robes if an allowance from the Saṅgha is obtained.

      • np 3 - tatiyakaṭhinasikkhāpada
        • background #editing
        • description #editing
          • When a bhikkhu’s robe has been done or kaṭhina privileges have been removed, if a bhikkhu receives an out-of-season robe he can accept it, if he prefers. Once accepted he must quickly make it a robe (according to NP1). If the cloth received is not enough to be made into a robe and if it is expected that extra cloth will be received from some other source to fulfil the need, the first cloth can be kept for up to one month. Even while expecting extra cloth, if the first cloth is kept for more than a month, it will be nissaggiya. A pācittiya offence will also be committed.

      • np 5 - cīvarapaṭiggahaṇasikkhāpada
        • background

      • np 16 - eḷakalomasikkhāpada
        • background
          • The Tathāgata was residing at the Jetavana temple near the city of Sāvatthi. At this time, a bhikkhu who was on his way to Sāvatthi received goat fur (or wool). He made a pile of it, covered it with his upper robe and carried it. The people who saw this asked him mockingly: “How much have you taken? What is the profit you made?” This made the bhikkhu feel shy and speechless. The Buddha who came to know about this incident declared this training rule.
        • Training rule in Pali
          • Bhikkhuno paneva addhānamaggappaṭipannassa eḷakalomāni uppajjeyyuṃ. Ākaṅkhamānena bhikkhunā paṭiggahetabbāni. Paṭiggahetvā tiyojanaparamaṃ sahatthā haritabbāni, asante hārake. Tato ce uttari hareyya, asantepi hārake, nissaggiyaṃ pācittiyaṃ. If a monk receives goat fur while travelling, he may accept it if desired. Once accepted, if there’s no one else to carry it, he can only carry it by himself, up to three yojanas. If he happens to carry it beyond the limit of three yojanas in the absence of a carrier, a nissaggiya pācittiya offence will occur.
        • Description of the training rule
          • A bhikkhu who receives goat fur must not carry it beyond {{9826691569749155::three yojanas}} by himself. The Tathāgata imposed this training rule because it is inappropriate for bhikkhus to do so. When taken the goat fur beyond three yojanas, the goat fur becomes {{9826691569749155::nissaggiya}} while the bhikkhu will have committed a {{9826691569749155::pācittiya}} offence. When the three-yojana boundary is passed, by taking the initial step therein, a {{9826691569749155::dukkaṭa}} offence will be committed. {{9826691569749155::Pācittiya}} offence will be committed upon the second step. The bhikkhu can carry the goat fur within the {{9826691569749155::three-yojana}} distance without committing any offence.
          • If the goat fur is carried back and forth within the area between the location of receiving the goat fur and the three-yojana mark, even if the monk walks more than three yojanas in total, {{5069783648336348::there is no offense}}. An offence will not be committed under this training rule as long as the monk does not cross the threeyojana boundary (from the receiving location). If the bhikkhu who received the goat fur gets another person to carry it, passing the three-yojana boundary will {{5069783648336348::not result in an offence}}. Further, it should be known that carrying an object made out of goat fur will {{5069783648336348::not result in an offence}}
          • if the initial three-yojana mark is crossed while carrying fur that is stolen by thieves and returned, and while carrying goat fur that has been returned after being forfeited through the nissajjana vinayakamma {{18109269204126632::an offence will not occur}}. If the goat fur is received this way and had to be carried again, an additional three yojanas can be covered from the point of the second receiving. The initial three-yojana mark is not taken into consideration. This is the opinion demonstrated in the Gaṇṭhipada and Vimativinodanī. According to the Sāratthadīpanī, there is no limit in the distance the goat fur could be carried when received the second time.
          • If the goat fur is received in one particular place, a distance of three yojanas can be travelled forward from thereon, and take up residence therein. From that point onwards, goat fur can be carried for another three yojanas. This training rule is {{4658933700398924::acittaka}} and {{4658933700398924::nosaññāvimokkha}}. Even if one is unaware of or doubting the distance travelled while the three yojana miles are exceeded, an offence {{4658933700398924::will occur}}
        • Factors
          • 1. Goat fur that has not been made into an object
          • 2. Receiving it for the first time
          • 3. Passing the three-yojana mark
          • 4. Not being a journey in which the monk travels more than three yojana back and forth within the three-yojana boundary.
          • 5. Not a journey that was initiated after residing in a place within the three-yojana boundary after the first receiving
        • Forfeiture
          • Imāni me, bhante, eḷakalomāni tiyojanaṃ atikkāmitāni nissaggiyāni. Imānāhaṃ āyasmato nissajjāmi.

      • np 22 - ūṇapañcabandhanasikkhāpada #edited
        • background
          • One pot maker invited monks to ask him for bowls if needed. The bhikkhus exceeded their limit and kept asking for more bowls. This made the potter unable to support his family. Seeing this, people started to scold the bhikkhus. Then the Buddha promulgated a rule prohibiting monks from asking for bowls (from aññātaka and appavārita lay devotees) Due to this rule, monks whose bowls were broken went for piṇḍapāta and received alms with their palms. Seeing this, people started to criticise monks saying, “Disciples of the Gotama go for alms and receive food with their palms like ascetics of other religions.” Knowing this, the Buddha amended the rule allowing monks whose bowls are broken to ask for bowls from lay devotees. Subsequently, Chabbaggiya bhikkhus began discarding their bowls due to the slightest scratch and hole, and kept asking for new bowls from the same potter. This made the potter unable to support his family. Seeing this, people started to scold the bhikkhus. Then the Buddha amended the training rule for the second time and promulgated it again as follows.
