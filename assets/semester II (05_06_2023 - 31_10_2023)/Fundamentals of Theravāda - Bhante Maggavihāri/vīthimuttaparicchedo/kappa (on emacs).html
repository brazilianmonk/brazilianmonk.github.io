    • antara- / cūḷa- / manvantara-kappa: period it takes for the human lifespan to vary from asaṅkheyya to 10 years and back
      • antarakappas are also called manvantarakappas due to there being a king named Manu at the beginning of each antarakappa
        • Prd. § 144: Antarakappo nāma ekassa vivaṭṭhaṭṭhāyi-asaṅkheyyassa abbhantare manussānaṃ āyukappassa hāyana-vaḍḍhana-vasena dissamānantarā catusaṭṭhippabhedā cūḷakappā vuccanti.
        • Prd.: … ye loke manvantara-kappā’ti vuccanti. Ekamekena manunāmakena mahāsammatarājena upalakkhitā antarakappā’ti vuttaṃ hoti.
      • tividhā antarakappā (sattavināsa [destruction of beings])
        • satthantarakappa (the interstice of weapons) (our present antarakappa)
          • occurs due to excess {{6624027049963579::dosa}} most beings end up in {{6624027049963579::niraya}} since {{6624027049963579::they die with strong hatred in the mind}}
          • how it happens
            • there is a rainfall, and those who are not in strict seclusion get in contact with it and become infuriated, weapons appear in their hands (or whatever object they take becomes a weapon) and most beings kill each other; those in strict seclusion survive and develop saṁvega and begin to abstain from killing; due to that, the lifespan gradually increases
            • rainfalls related to the topic of kappas
              • sattavināsa's rainfall which happens before the sattavināsa at the end of each antarakappa (confirm whether it happens in all 3 or only in sattha) #q🙋🏼‍♂
              • vipattikara mahāmegha: the rainfall that marks the beginning of the great destruction
              • sampattikara mahāmegha: the rainfall that marks the beginning of the period of genesis
        • rogantarakappa (the interstice of disease)
          • occurs due to excess {{33495370918125955::rāga: overindulgence in food and sense pleasures; cakkavattisutta says moha }} most beings end up in {{33495370918125955::sagga}} since {{33495370918125955::they die with wholesome minds of loving-kindness that tend to occur when experiencing suffering thinking, "may others not experience such suffering!"}}
        • dubbhikkhantarakappa (the interstice of famine)
          • occurs due to excess {{6070289468831485:: moha, causing the destruction of natural resources leading to severe shortages of food and water; cakkavattisutta says rāga}} most beings are reborn in {{6070289468831485::pettivisaya}} since {{6070289468831485::they die with strong desire for food and drinks}}
        • note:
          • comm. to the cakkavattisutta says dosa, moha, and rāga, respectively (tattha lobhussadāya pajāya dubbhikkhantarakappo hoti. mohussadāya rogantarakappo. dosussadāya satthantarakappo)
      • aggaññasutta and cakkavattisīhanādasutta: suttas explaining the beginning of the world and how it evolves
        • humans are born spontaneously from the brahma world; they have no sexual distinction, can move freely, and feed on happiness; with the elapse of time, their glory decreases, and with the increase in defilements, the lifespan gradually reduces down to 10 years; at that time, the sattavināsa occurs (sattha / roga / dubbhikkha); after it, humans reflect on what happened and begin to develop saṁvega and undertake moral precepts of abstaining from killing, etc., and gradually the lifespan increases up to asaṅkheyya, again to decrease, as humanity gradually slacks in virtue
        • cakkavattisutta: in the next antarakappa, when the human lifespan drops down to {{926480733394816::80,000}}, the Buddha {{926480733394816::Metteyya}} will appear in a Kingdom called {{926480733394816::Ketumatī}} in Jambudīpa
        • "Na hi buddhā vaḍḍhamāne āyumhi nibbattanti, hāyamāne pana nibbattanti." (Buddhas only appear during the declining period of the human lifespan)
        • in the darker periods of humanity, those who kill their parents are praised
        • the life span at the end of the antarakappa is not given
      • ratio of drop in human lifespan
        • every 100 years, 1 year drops
        • note:
          • it seems that it may vary, because if only 1 year drops every 100, during the period in which the lifespan of humans is asaṅkheyya, it would be a big difference between generations

              • what happens to beings during this period?
                • kāmaduggati beings: most are born in kāmasugati, attain jhānas, and are reborn in the brahma realms; those who have strong wrong views go to lokantarikaniraya, where they spend the saṁvaṭṭa-, saṁvaṭṭaṭṭhāyī-, and vivaṭṭa-kappas
                • kāmasugati beings: most develop jhāna before the great destruction and are born in brahma realms, where they spend the saṁvaṭṭa-, saṁvaṭṭaṭṭhāyī-, and vivaṭṭa-kappas
                • rūpāvacara beings: those in realms that are to be destroyed attain higher jhānas and ascend to higher realms; those in other realms remain there
          • 3 saṁvaṭṭasīmā (not included in the respective destruction)
            • ābhassarā (tejo saṁvaṭṭo): kāmabhūmi and 1st jhāna realms are destroyed by fire
            • subhakiṇhā (āpo saṁvaṭṭo): kāmabhūmi, 1st and 2nd jhāna realms are destroyed by water
            • vehapphalā (vāyo saṁvaṭṭo): kāmabhūmi, 1st, 2nd, and 3rd jhāna realms are destroyed by wind

    • mahākappa: 4 asaṅkheyyakappas
      • mahākappa = {{3546986952455533::4}} asaṅkheyyakappas
        • length of a mahākappa (2 similes)
          • the 1 cubic yojana mountain rubbed by the kāsi cloth once every 100 years
          • sāsapasutta: the 1 cubic yojana iron city (surrounded with an iron wall 1-yojana long, wide and high) filled with mustard seeds; 1 seed is removed every 100 years
      • kappas in the past (2 similes)
        • sāvakasutta: the 4 disciples who live for a 100 years contemplating 100k kappas each every day (accumulative between them ⇒ 400k kappas a day); they would die before finding a beginning
        • gaṅgāsutta: the sand in the Ganga; there are more kappas in the past
      • 5 types (actually 6) based on the number of Buddhas that appear in them (with gāthā)
        • (suññakappa (empty aeon): no Buddhas appear; most common)
          • comm: no paccekabuddha as well Ledi: yes, based on apadāna and cakkavattirājā
        • sārakappa (excellent?): 1 Buddha appears
        • maṇḍakappa (decorated?): 2 Buddhas appear
        • sāramaṇḍakappa (excellent and decorated?): 3 Buddhas appear
        • varakappa (wonderful?): 4 Buddhas appear
        • bhaddakappa (auspicious): 5 Buddhas appear
        • note:
          • other methods:
            • sāra, vara (2 or 3), maṇḍa, bhadda
            • sāra, maṇḍa, vara, sāramaṇḍa, bhadda
