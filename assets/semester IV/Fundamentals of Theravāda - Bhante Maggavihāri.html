        • paccaya (condition, cause)↔that which supports the arising, continuance or growth of another reality
        • paccayuppanna (conditioned, effect)↔that which has its arising, continuance or growth supported by another reality
        • how should paccaya and paccayuppanna be distinguished? (with simile)
          • the arising of fire depends on the sandpaper and the phosphorus on the matchstick, which with friction, combusts into flames - each of these two supporting the arising of fire in a different way since if they were providing similar support, only one would suffice for the production of fire - likewise, various paccayadhammas assist the arising, continuance or growth of other paccayuppannadhammas in various ways
        • paccayasatti (conditional force)↔the conditional force present in the paccaya of supporting the paccayupanna in various ways
        • difference between paccaya and paccayasatti
          • paccaya is the dhamma that assists the paccayupanna, while paccayasatti is the conditional force, ability, power present in the paccaya of offering the assistence - one single paccaya may assist different paccayuppannas with various different paccayasattis - like any given citta supports the arising of the citta that follows by means of anantarapaccayasatti, as well as supporting concomitant dhammas by means of sahajātapaccayasatti
        • 3 ways a paccaya can support a paccayuppanna with paccayasatti
          • for the arising of a paccayuppanna that has not arisen
          • for the continuance of a paccayuppanna existing in generations
          • for the growth of paccayuppanna, for giving successive paccayuppannas a higher capacity than its predecessor to perform their functions

    • nāma, rūpa and paññatti #edited
      • nāma (definition & realities)
        • realities that have no physical form or appearance and are so called since they are known by hearing or reading names - also known as arūpa if referring to only cittacetasikas, it could be said that they are the realities that bend, incline towards an object
        • 89 cittas, 52 cetasikas and Nibbāna
        • 4 nāmakkhandhas / arūpakkhandhas: vedanā-, saññā-, saṅkhāra- and viññāṇa-kkhandhas
      • rūpa (definition & realities)
        • realities that are visibly formed - rūpayatīti rūpaṃ - and that are deformed due to the strike of opposing realities - ruppatīti rūpaṃ
        • 28 rūpas - rūpakkhandha
      • paññatti
        • ultimate realities - though experienced - are not obvious to beings what is most often seen as existing are paññattis
        • paññatti [pa + √ñā + *āpe + ti] (definition and example)
          • conceptions that are most often perceived by the mind as existing, but that, upon examination - such as reduction - can be understood to be non-existing, mentally fabricated, mere subjective realities - e.g.: a cloth, often perceived as real, if reduced to smaller components, is only a collection of threads
        • 2 types
          • atthapaññatti (concepts-as-meanings / meaning-concepts)
            • what it is→meanings that are known mainly through words, but by other means as well, and do not exist in reality - aka paññattinimitta
            • types
              • saṇṭhānapaññatti (concept of shape): concepts based on the shape / arrangement of mahābhūtās - e.g.: mountain, trees, flowers, leaves, fruits
              • samūhapaññatti (concept of aggregation): concepts based on the various combinations of mahābhūtās - e.g.: tables, chairs, beds, mats, pillows and chariots
              • sattapaññatti (concept of beings): concepts based on the aggregates of beings seen as one thing - e.g.: deity, human, lady, man, elephant, horse, ox, etc
              • disāpaññatti (concept of direction): concepts based on the movement of the sun and moon, etc - e.g.: east, west, north, south, etc)
              • kālapaññatti (concept of time): concepts based on the rotation of the sun and moon, etc - e.g.: morning, evening, noon, night, year, month, day, etc
              • others: ākāsapaññatti, nimittapaññātti...
          • nāmapaññatti (concepts-as-names / name-concepts)
            • what it is→the names that convey meanings of two kinds - atthapaññatti or paramattha
              • ñāpakahetu ([√ñā + *āpe + aka]): words that, when heard, bring ideas to the mind that are either paramattha or paññatti
                • main grammatical idea in India
                  • consonants give or make clear the meaning
                  • vowels help the pronunciation or uttering of the word
            • 2 type of meanings (attha) conveyed by nāmapaññattis
              • atthapaññatti / hīnattha
              • paramattha
            • what is the difference between the sound of a word, the name uttered, and its meaning?
              • the sound is the ultimate materiality that arises due to citta or utu
              • the name is the concept expressed through a combination of sounds
              • the meaning is the concept or reality that is referred to by the name
              • note: when hearing collections of sounds, names are perceived, even though they (names) do not exist in reality
            • how are names conveyed by sound?
              • intending to convey a concept, one utters its combinations of sounds according to the standards of language, and the listener hears the sounds and understands the concept
              • concept ⇒ sound uttered ⇒ sound heard ⇒ concept e.g.: a four-legged chair that can seat two people (atthapaññātti) the name that conveys that meaning - pīṭha (nāmapaññatti) sounds uttered and heard in Pāḷi (pī - ṭha)
              • (extra) mental processes when hearing the word pīṭha and understanding its meaning
                • one of 9 (exc. atiparittārammaṇa) sotadvāravīthis taking present saddārammaṇa (the sound pī)
                • anubandhaka manodvāravīthi <17 ck vibhūta or avibhūta taking the past saddārammaṇa (pī)
                • the same 2 vīthis happen with the sound (ṭha)
                • manodvāravīthi <17 ck vibhūta or avibhūta taking both past sounds together (pīṭha)
                • manodvāravīthi <17 ck avibhūta taking the nāmapaññatti as object
                • manodvāravīthi <17 ck avibhūta taking attha as object
            • 6 types
              • vijjamānapaññatti (name-concepts of real things): names that refer to ultimate realities - e.g.: citta, vedanā, paṭhavi, Nibbāna, etc

            • not being satisfied with whatever is received

            • inability to sustain friendship with others over a long period
            • getting excited (in the negative sense) during all activities
            • being afraid
            • changing mood quickly
            • having less attachment towards people and things
            • liking spicy food

              • sees unreal defects, therefore close to paññā
          • mohacarita - 5 traits

            • tendency to be easily deceived

            • not accepting opinions just because someone has said them
            • doing all activities neatly and properly
            • note:
              • sees actual defects, therefore close to dosa
          • vitakkacarita - 6 traits
            • difficulty in achieving concentration (samādhi)
            • excessive talking
            • associating and being together with many people
            • frequently thinking about various matters
            • not being delighted in doing wholesome deeds

      • paṭhavikasiṇabhāvanā
        • before contemplation
          • prepare the paṭhavikasiṇamaṇḍala (parikammakasiṇa maṇḍala / nimitta)
            • saṁhārima (mobile)
              • gather light red clay (not to resemble the red kasiṇa), remove impurities, mix it well, spread it on a circular mould of about 2 vidatthi in diameter (for the final result to be the right size (1 vidatthi and 4 aṅgula), since it shrinks considerably while drying), flatten it well, let it dry, apply new layers if needed until flat as a drum
            • tattraṭṭhaka (immobile)
              • one method: cut off / draw a circular shape 1 vidatthi and 4 aṅgula in diameter on a wooden plank, partially drive in small nails spread throughout the disk leaving them 1/2 aṅguli out (to hold the clay in place), spread the clay covering the nails, it can be on the ground at a quiet place; it could be fixed or mobile; cement can be used for strengthening; in which case, pure clay should be added as a final layer
          • place the disk on a quiet place
          • prepare a seat 1 vidatthi and 4 aṅguli high
          • place the disk 2 1/2 hattha from the seat
          • clean the area and the body
        • contemplation
          • preparing the mind
            • recollect tiratanaguṇa, or at least Buddhaguṇa to gladden the mind
            • pervade beings with mettā
            • arouse effort determining that one shall tread upon the path walked by Noble Ones
          • contemplation with the parikammanimitta
            • look at the kasiṇamaṇḍala, neither with wide eyes nor squinting
            • determine the mind to be well established on this object, holding it firmly
            • contemplate it as "paṭhavi, paṭhavi", now with opened eyes, and now with closed eyes
            • avoid moving the body
            • avoid giving attention to other objects that may come to mind
            • contemplate in such manner until the uggahanimitta is produced
            • what the object of paṭhavikasiṇa is
              • a disk of clay that has abundant hardness and is of a light red color (concept based on realities - vijjamānena avijjamāna paññatti)
                • why vijjamānena? pathavi in this case is a concept, not the dhātu
                • why is the circle said to be the main object?
              • not possible to avoid its color (light red), but one should not focus on the color / hardness, but simply the idea of earth
              • note:
                • soil / clay / earth is called paṭhavi as it contains abundant hardness
                • the clay disk itself is a helpful means to get the idea of a clay disk
          • contemplation with the uggahanimitta
            • having produced the uggahanimitta, one should not sit in front of the parikammanimitta any further to avoid comparisons between the parikamma and uggaha nimmittas, but should go to another quiet area without delay in order to further the contemplation
            • if the uggahanimitta is lost, one should again contemplate the parikammanimitta
            • with closed eyes, contemplate the uggahanimitta as "paṭhavi, paṭhavi" and protect one's concentration until the paṭibhāganimitta is produced
            • with the uggahanimitta, the nīvaraṇas start to be subdued and samādhi increases
          • contemplation with the paṭibhāganimitta
            • when samādhi is stronger, the paṭibhāganimitta appears very clearly and brightly, as a shining gold circle or the morning sun; upacārasamādhi is attained and with it, the hindrances are at once greatly subdued
            • one should guard one's concentration and continue to contemplate the paṭibhāganimitta
            • with the paṭibhāganimitta, jhāna factors are strengthened, and samādhi increases
          • attaining the 1st jhāna
            • 1st jhāna occurs establishing appaṇāsamādhi as if entering the object
            • in the beginning, the absorption lasts for a mere mind-moment, but with repeated attainment, one is able to remain in it for long periods
            • difference between the upacāra and the appaṇā samādhi #[[fundamentals exam ]]
              • upacāra: kāmāvacara kusala / kiriya cittas; 7 javanas per vīthi; bhavaṅgas in between vīthis; not as well established in the object; jhāna factors are not fully mature and balanced
              • appaṇā (samāpatti): rūpa- or arūpāvacara kusala / kiriya cittas or lokuttaraphalacittas; many javanas in one vīthi (even lasting for hours or days); no bhavaṅgas in between; well established in the object; jhāna factors are mature and balanced
              • what is the similarity?→the object - paṭibhāganimitta
          • mastering the 1st jhāna
            • vasī / vasītā→vasanaṃ vasī, samatthatāti attho‒vasī is dwelling in the attainment, meaning competence, proficiency in it
              • samatthatā [saṃ + √ar + tha + tā]↔fem. ability; capability; competence; proficiency #[[pāḷi vocab (2nd year)]]
              • pañcavidhavasī (the 5 masteries)
                • āvajjanavasī (mastery of advertence): the ability to swiftly reflect on (contemplate) the (five) factors of the (first) jhāna without delay
                • samāpajjanavasī (mastery of attainment): the ability to swiftly attain the jhāna whenever one wishes without delay
                • adhiṭṭhānavasī (mastery of determination): 2 definitions
                  • paramatthadpn: the ability to maintain the jhāna for a short period, such as a fingersnap
                  • vbvn-ṭ: the ability to maintain the jhāna as long as one wishes without letting the mind-stream fall back into bhavaṅga

          • attaining the other rūpavacara jhānas
            • after sufficient mastery, vitakka, which is the coarsest factor of the 1st jhāna, appears to the meditator as gross
            • reflect on the disadvantages of vitakka and the benefits of its absence to remove the nikanti towards it‒until it is subdued, one cannot enter the jhāna without vitakka
            • again contemplate the paṭibhāganimitta intending to attain the 2nd jhāna without vitakka
            • vitakkavirāgabhāvanā
              • meditation done after the mastery of the first jhāna, intending to enter jhāna without vitakka
              • vitakkavirāgabhāvanā chart
                • so long as the nikanti for vitakka is not suppressed, it is parikammabhāvanā for the 2nd jhāna
                • after its suppression, it is upacārabhāvanā until the 2nd jhāna occurs
                • up to the suppression of the nikanti, it is paṭipadā; from then onwards until the appaṇāsamādhi, it is abhiññā
                • note:
                  • the same applies to vicāra-, pīti-, and sukha-virāgabhāvanā

      • āruppabhāvanā
        • after having refined the mental factors, how can the meditator proceed in the path of samatha?
          • after refining the jhānaṅgas as much as possible (aṅgasamatikkama), what is left is to make the saññā subtler by taking subtler objects (ārammaṇasamatikkama)‒saññā is mostly based on the object
        • aṅga samatikkama↔the overcoming of factors
        • ārammaṇa samatikkama↔the overcoming of objects
          • what is the step-by-step of the ārammaṇasamatikkama?
            • rūpa (sign of rūpa): paṭhavīkasiṇa paṭibhāganimitta absence of rūpa: anantākāsa (paññatti) nāma: ākāsānañcāyatanajjhānacitta abscence of nāma: ākiñcañña (paññatti) nāma that cognizes the abscence of nāma: ākiñcaññāyatanajjhānacitta
        • ākāsānañcāyatana
          • how to attain it?
            • after mastering the 5th rūpāvacarajjhāna, emerging from it and spreading the nimitta, in order to distance oneself from rūpa (the sign of rūpa), one should reflect on the disadvantages of rūpa and advantages of arūpa, and should pay attention to the space occupied by the kasiṇanimitta as "ākāso, ākāso" or "anantākāso, anantākāso" until the 1st arūpajjhāna arises
          • what is its object?
            • the ākāsanimitta obtained by removing one of the 9 kasiṇanimittas (exc. ākāsakasiṇa), therefore named kasiṇugghātimākāsa, which is a paññatti
            • what is the difference between the kasiṇigghātimākāsa and the ākāsakasiṇa that leads to the 5 rūpāvacara jhānas?
              • (me): both are ākāsapaññattis, but obtained differently; ākāsakasiṇa is obtained based on vaṇṇarūpa kasiṇugghāṭimākāsa is obtained by removing the mental sign of rūpa (paṭibhāganimitta) the first one can be taken as object by a beginner, and be the object of the rūpāvacarajhānas the latter can only be taken as object once the paṭibhāganimitta was produced, and can only be object of jhāna when all coarse mental factors have been subdued
          • 2 similes for the sign of rūpa at this stage
            • the sign of rūpa is compared to a coil of ropes seen as a snake or as a man with similar features as another man one might be running away from
          • extra notes:
            • kasiṇanimittas are sometimes taken as self or God
            • normally, the expansion of the nimitta is mentioned in the process‒at which stages must the nimitta be expanded? #q🙋🏼‍♂
        • viññāṇañcāyatana
          • how to attain it?
            • intending to distance oneself further away from rūpa and focusing on a subtler object, one reflects on the disadvantages of ākāsānañca and the advantages of viññāṇañca, focuses on the citta that had ākāsānañca as object, and contemplates it as “viññāṇaṃ, viññāṇaṃ” or “anantaṃ viññāṇaṃ, anantaṃ viññāṇaṃ” until the 2nd arūpajjhāna occurs
          • what is its object?
            • its object is what is more subtle than the abscence of rūpa, that is nāma‒in this case, the nāma that just took the abscence of rūpa as object
        • ākiñcaññāyatana
          • how to attain it?
            • after having mastered the viññāṇañcāyatana, one should reflect on its disadvantages and the advantages of ākiñcaññāyatana, overcome the nikanti for it, and focus on what is more subtle than nāma, that is the abscence of nāma‒in this case, the abscence of the nāma that had ākāsānañcāyatana as object‒as “natthi kiñci, natthi kiñci” (there is nothing whatsoever), which is a paññātti

                • while investigating realities taken as a self, being, person or I,
                • nāma: one sees the collection of cittas and cetasikas that arise internally depending on vatthus and that always incline or bend towards objects (namanalakkhaṇa), separating them from other realities and understanding them as nāma (1)
                • rūpa: then, one does the same for the material bases that do not bend to objects that are molested when striken by opposing realities (ruppanalakkhaṇa), understanding them as rūpa (2)
                • nāma ≠ rūpa: one understands that nāma is not rūpa (3) and vice-versa (4), that they are different realities, have different characteristics, are mutually dependent, giving rise to nāmarūpaparicchedañāṇā
                • nāma is a mere reality: it is not a self, being, person or I (5)
                • rūpa is a mere reality: " (6)
                • conclusion: there is no self, no transmigrating self, being, person, I, there is nothing apart from nāma and rūpa (7), there is no kāraka (8), no vedak. (9) (nāmarūpavavatthāna)

                • the same procedure, but applied not to just nāma and rūpa, but to khandha, dhātu, and āyatana

                • the knowledge that defines nāma and rūpa as different realities, and occurs during the nāmarūpapariggaha practice
              • nāmarūpavavatthāna
                • the knowledge that there is no self, being, person, I, kāraka, or vedaka apart from nāma and rūpa, and that occurs during the nāmarūpapariggaha practice after nāmarūpaparicchedañāṇa
