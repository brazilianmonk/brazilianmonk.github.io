          • vacīmayadāna (a dāna which is a vacīkamma): one made by mere instruction; if done so out of lack of interest in the act of giving, it comes under the asappurisadāna "asahatthā dānaṁ deti"

          • dānavatthu / dānakāraṇa→reasons behind giving “dānavatthūnīti dānakāraṇāni”
          • 2 main motivations behind giving
            • anuggahavasena dānaṃ deti: giving in order to assist another - usually less fortunate (someone considered inferior or equal in terms of virtuous) - driven by anuggahabuddhi and often compassion
            • pūjāvasena dānaṃ deti: giving in order to honour another - usually superior in terms of virtue - and accumulate great merit driven by pūjābuddhi; it included viceyyadāna
              • viceyyadāna↔donation given having selected a virtuous recipient
              • viceyya [vi + √ci + eyya]↔ger. (+acc) discriminating; discerning; distinguishing; lit. picking; 2 ger. scrutinizing; judging #[[pāḷi vocab (2nd year)]]
            • note: occasionally both motivations can occur within the act of donation
          • 13 reasons behing giving
            • agati
              • chandā dānaṃ deti: giving out of affection (pema)
              • dosā dānaṃ deti: giving out of anger, as an angry father may give away things belonging t othe family
              • mohā dānaṃ deti: giving with ignorance
              • bhayā dānaṃ deti: giving out of fear of being defamed, criticized or of woeful states or being poor in the future and so on

        • Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī's story
          • Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī, a {{4031962599362735::Koliyan }}princess of the {{4031962599362735::Gotama }}clan, was destined for greatness since birth, prophesied to {{4031962599362735::bear a child of remarkable stature}} . If the child were to be a girl - she would become {{4031962599362735::the chief queen of a universal monarch}} - should it be a son - {{4031962599362735::a universal monarch}} - and in either cases, she would become a leader of a great retinue, and for that fact she was called so - leader (pati) of a grand (mahā) retinue (pajā). Married to King {{4031962599362735::Suddhodana}}, she became the mother of Princess {{4031962599362735::Rūpananda}} and Prince {{4031962599362735::Nanda}}, and later cared for the Bodhisatta after Queen {{4031962599362735::Mahāmāyā}}'s passing. When the Bodhisatta left seeking enlightenment, she felt sadness but later rejoiced at his return as the Sammāsambuddha. Driven by devotion, Mahāpajāpatī personally crafted {{4031962599362735::two}} robes for the Buddha, showcasing her love and dedication and carried them on a fragrant casket on her head to the monastery. She became a {{4031962599362735::stream-enterer}} during the Buddha's visit to Kapilavatthu and, after King Suddhodana's death, sought ordination as a bhikkhunī, becoming the first in the sāsana to do so. Revered as "{{4031962599362735::buddhamātā}}," she earned widespread respect from monks, nuns, and lay followers. Before her passing, Mahāpajāpatī expressed gratitude for her title as "Buddha Mother," a rare and esteemed honor, saying: "Rañño mātā mahesīti, {{4031962599362735::sulabhaṃ nāmamitthinaṃ, Buddhamātāti yaṃ nāmaṃ, etaṃ paramadullabhaṃ}}". While it's not uncommon for women to be bestowed with titles like "Queen Mother" or "Queen Consort," the distinction of "Buddha Mother" is an exceedingly rare and cherished honor. Her funeral, attended by the Buddha himself, was grand and dignified. Her life exemplifies the power of love, devotion, and unwavering faith in the pursuit of truth and enlightenment.
