        • musāvādasikkhāpadaṃ #edited
          • Sampajānamusāvāde pācittiyaṃ. In deliberate false speech, [there is a case] involving expiation.
          • description of the training rule
            • what is musāvāda? #[[vibhaṅga exam]]
              • the intentional act (cetanā) (1) to deceive another (2) done bodily or verbally (3) by expressing a subjectively false information (diṭṭha, suta, muta viññāta as adiṭṭhādi...or vice-versa) (4) mem aid: intention (1, 2), doors (3), content (4)
                • 2 types of lies according to attha
                  • atthabhañjaka (a lie that destroys other's benefits): these are heavier lies, for example: lying in court; such lies have never been uttered by bodhisattas
                  • anatthabhañjaka (a lie that does not destroy other's benefits): these are lighter ones; these might be committed by bodhisattas but not by ariyas
                  • note:
                    • bhañjaka↔adj. who breaks or spoils #[[pāḷi vocab (2nd year)]]
                    • bodhisattas, after the prophecy, never utter atthabhañjakamusāvāda
                    • sotāpanna doesn't commit both (acc. to Vajirabuddhi)
                    • sammappayoga / sammāpayoga↔skillful means to avoid lying while concealing information #[[pāḷi vocab (2nd year)]]

                • the thullaccaya for indirectly claiming uttarimanussadhamma
              • the saṅghadisesa offences (sghds 8 and 9 - paṭhama and dutiya duṭṭhādosasikkhāpada)
              • the pācittiya offence (pc 1): all other lies
              • the pācittiya offence that occurs when groundlessly accusing another monk of a sghds offence (pc 76)
                • extra ones (TBC): #q🙋🏼‍♂

            • instances where a lie does not occur
              • davā bhaṇati / sahasā bhaṇati: without having investigated or considered, one says something in haste
              • ravā bhaṇati: while intending to say one thing, accidentaly saying something else, such as saying "cīraṁ" instead of "cīvaraṁ"

        • omasavādasikkhāpadaṁ #edited
          • Omasavāde pācittiyaṃ.
          • description
            • what is omasavāda? #[[vibhaṅga exam]]
              • it is the intentional act (cetanā) rooted in anger (1) of belittling (omasati), insulting (khuṁseti) or despises (vambheti) another (2) done bodily or verbally (3) by using one of the ten akkosa vatthu (4)
            • 3 types of omasavāda:
              • omasati - belittles, lit. rubs down
              • khuṃseti - insults
              • vambheti - despises
            • 10 akkosavatthu #[[vibhaṅga exam]]
              • jāti (social class) - example of hīna / ukkaṭṭha
                • hīna: caṇḍāla
                • ukkaṭṭha: khattiya
              • nāma (name) - example of hīna / ukkaṭṭha
                • hīna: avakaṇṇaka (droppy ear)
                • ukkaṭṭha: buddhappaṭisaṁyutta
              • gotta (tribe, family, clan) - example of hīna / ukkaṭṭha
                • hīna: kosiya
                • ukkaṭṭha: gotama
              • kamma (occupation, job) - example of hīna / ukkaṭṭha
                • hīna: pupphachaḍḍakakamma (toilet cleaner)
                • ukkaṭṭha: kasi
              • sippa (craft) - example of hīna / ukkaṭṭha
                • hīna: naḷakārasippa
                • ukkaṭṭha: gaṇanā
              • ābādha (ailments) - example of hīna / ukkaṭṭha
                • hīna: sabbe ābādha
                • ukkaṭṭha: madhumeha
              • liṅga (physical features, gender) - example of hīna / ukkaṭṭha
                • hīna: atidīgha, atirassa, atikaṇha, accodāta
                • ukkaṭṭha: nātidīgha, nātirassa, nātikaṇha, nāccodāta
              • kilesa (defilements) - example of hīna / ukkaṭṭha
                • hīna: sabbe kilesā
              • āpatti (offences / attainments) - example of hīna / ukkaṭṭha
                • hīna: sabbā āpattiyo
                • ukkaṭṭha: sotāpattisamāpatti
              • akkosa (insults) - example of hīna / ukkaṭṭha
                • hīna: goṇosi
                • ukkaṭṭha: paṇḍitosi
              • note: #[[vibhaṅga exam]]
                • hīna: those that are belittled, humiliated, disgusted, indulged and disrespected
                • ukkuṭṭha: names etc. that are not belittled, not humiliated, not disgusted, not indulged and not disrespected
                • note:
              • 40 omāsavāda offences #[[vibhaṅga exam]]

        • padasodhammasikkhāpadam #edited
          • Yo pana bhikkhu anusampannaṁ padaso dhammaṁ vāceyya pācittiyam. If any bhikkhu should have one who has not been fully admitted recite the Dhamma [line] by line, [this is a case] involving expiation.
          • background
            • Buddha in Jetavana, Sāvatthi chabbaggiyas were reciting the Dhamma together with upāsakas and the upāsakas started to not be respectful, not be humble and to be malicious towards bhikkhus this lead to the promulgation of the rule
          • description
            • 'Dhamma', according to this training rule, refers to teachings that are recorded in the Tipiṭaka and were expounded either by
              • buddhabhāsita: the Buddha
              • sāvakabhāsita: his disciples
              • isibhāsita: by hermits
              • devatā bhāsita: by devas
              • (original) aṭṭhakathā: explanations found in commentaries; the modern aṭṭhakathā, though not the original aṭṭhakathā, is still included as so for containing the essence of the original
                • the aṭṭhakathā in pāḷi was translated into sinhala and then again translated back into pāḷi and summarized
                • when abhayagiri started teaching dhamma mixed with other teachings, the aṭṭhakathā in pāḷi was translated into sinhalese in order to preserve it
            • pada is fourfold as follows
              • pada: the 1st word (ending with a case ending [vibhattyantaṁ padaṁ]) of a phrase or of a sentence, or the first line of a verse
              • anupada: the 2nd word of a phrase or of a sentence, or the 2nd line of a verse
                • what about the third? #q🙋🏼‍♂
              • anubyañjana: a group of letters which are either not a complete stem like the morpheme ‘pubbaṅ’ of the word ‘pubbaṅgamā’, or a stem - word without the case ending - like ‘pubbaṅgama’
              • anvakkhara: a single letter or syllable
            • note:
              • English terms for verses according to their length:
                • 1 line: monostich
                • 2 lines: couplet
                • 3 lines: tercet / triplet
                • 4 lines: quatrain
                • 5 lines: quintain (kwĭn′tən) / cinquain (sĭng′kān′, săng′-)
                • 6 lines: sestet (sĕ-stĕt′) / hexastich (ˈhɛksəˌstɪk)
                • 7 lines: septet / septette (sĕp-tĕt′) / heptastich
                • 8 lines: octave
                • 9 lines: spenserian
                • 10 lines: dizain (dɪˈzeɪn) / rondeau
                • 12 lines : roundeau prime
                • 13 lines: roundel / rondeau (rŏn′dō, rŏn-dō′)
                • 14 lines: sonnet (sŏn′ĭt)
            • what is the purpose of this rule?→to avoid situations in which the anupa may know a text the upa does not, where the anupa's respect for the upa may diminish

        • paṭhamasahaseyyasikkhāpadaṃ #edited
          • Yo pana bhikkhu anupasampannena uttariṃ dirattatirattaṃ sahaseyyaṃ kappeyya, pācittiyaṃ. If any bhikkhu should make use of a sleeping place for more than two nights or three nights together with one who has not been fully admitted [into the bhikkhu-community], [this is a case] involving expiation.
          • background
            • Buddha: Aggālava Stupa, Ālavi bhikkhus slept in the same area as devotees after dhammatalks; devotees criticized their behavior during the night Buddha: Badarikārāma, Kosambhi Sāmaṇera Rāhula ended up without sleeping quarters during the night due to the previous ruling and slept in the Buddha's toilet; the Buddha, getting to know about it in the morning, amended the rule allowing a maximum of 3 consecutive nights
          • description
            • purpose of the rule→when an upa sleeps with anupa, the anupa could develop disrespect towards the upa, after witnessing inappropriate sleep-related behaviors that may occur during his sleep
            • channa↔roofed
              • sabbacchanna↔entirely roofed
              • yebhuyyacchanna↔mostly roofed (pc) (3/4+)
              • upaḍḍhacchanna↔half-roofed (dkṭ)
              • yebhuyya acchanna↔mostly not roofed (1/4 or less) (dkṭ)
              • acchanna↔unroofed (anāpatti)
            • paricchanna↔screened - by anything from a cloth to a wall (minimum 1 1/2 mph high)
              • sabbaparicchanna↔the entire perimeter is fully screened (pc)
                • a transparent (such as glass) screen would still count, it seems, becuse it prevents entry...but isn't it about concealing / hiding those inside? because the yebhuyyaparicchanna still counts as an offense but allows entry #q🙋🏼‍♂

              • yebhuyya-aparicchanna↔the perimeter is mostly unscreened (dkṭ) (1/4 or less)
              • aparicchanna↔the perimeter is not screened or the screen is not high enough
              • note:
                • minimum height of the screen→1 1/2 mph (it need not be connected to the ceiling)
            • ekūpacāra↔the area accessible from one single entrance; any other entrance must be sealed in order not to count

              • the areas that are only accessible from different entrances, requiring one to leave the building and enter from another entrance in order to access it

            • counting nights
              • how to count nights here?→if both upa and anupa are lying down at dawn time, one night is counted; likewise, the second and third; on the fourth consecutive night - as soon as the sun sets - whenever they are lying down at the same time - whatever time that is during the night - the offense occurs (not at dawn time)
              • what is lying down?
                • above the pelvis should be lying down flat
                • the amount of degrees of reclination that are considered lying down is not given
                • if one keeps the feet on the ground and lies on the bed, it is still not lying down - but if the feet are brought up, it is
                • maybe one foot on the ground is enough not to be lying down
              • what if the anupa is a different person or if the place changes?→that does not matter - what matters is the amount of nights one lies down together with an anupa
              • is lying down during the day-time allowed?→since the ruling says atthaṅgate sūriye, it is allowed, but bmc says ṭīkā includes daytime

            • what if one falls down while walking?→if one falls and immediately gets up it is not considered as lying down
            • what if one is tied to a bed and forced to lie down?→no offense, since it is against one's will

          • background
            • The Buddha was residing at the Veḷuvana monastery in the city of Rājagaha. During this time Venerable Dabbamallaputta Thera was preparing lodgings for the Saṅgha (senāsana paññāpaka). He was also in charge of distribution of meals (bhattuddesaka). Two monks named Mettiya and Bhummajaka, who were among the group of Chabbaggiya monks, at the time were juniors and lacked good kamma to obtain good lodgings and food. They received inferior lodgings and food of the Saṅgha. Then they got other bhikkhus to think and say, “Dabbamallaputta Thera favours when preparing quarters. He also favours, when distributing food”. Based on this incident the Buddha imposed the ujjhāpanaka training rule prohibiting monks from getting other monks to think and say bad things about officials appointed by the Saṅgha. When this rule was imposed, Mettiya and Bhummajaka bhikkhus started accusing Dabbamallaputta Thera in the presence of the other bhikkhus saying, “Dabbamallaputta Thera favours when preparing lodgings and distributing food”.           • Yo pana bhikkhu asammato bhikkhunīyo ovadeyya, pācittiyaṃ. If any bhikkhu who has not been authorised should exhort the bhikkhunīs, [this is a case] involving expiation.
          • background
            • The Buddha was residing in the Jetavana monastery near the city of Sāvatti. During this time, senior bhikkhus used to advise bhikkhunīs. Such bhikkhus who were advising the bhikkhunīs received the four requisites such as robes in abundance. The Chabbaggiya monks who saw this incident decided to offer advice to the bhikkhunīs. They informed the bhikkhunīs to come to them to obtain advice. Then the bhikkhunīs also went to the Cabbaaggiya bhikkhus seeking advice. However, the Cabbaggiya bhikkhus spent most of the day talking about unnecessary matters with the bhikkhunīs and did not spend a lot of time preaching the Dhamma. The Tathāgata inquired about this matter from the bhikkhunīs, and they explained the incident to him. Subsequently, the Tathāgata declared a bhikkhunovādaka sammuti (a convention of appointing a monk to advice bhikkhunīs) based on this incident as: “Anujānami, bhikkhave, bhikkhunovādakaṃ sammannitum”. Afterwards, the six types of bhikkhus continued to advise the bhikkhunīs without obtaining the convention. Based on this incident, the Tathāgata declared ovāda sikkhāpada preventing a bhikkhu who does not have the bhikkhunovādaka sammuti from advising bhikkhunīs. Then the Chabbaggiya monks went into various sīmas and declared bhikkhunovādaka sammuti amongst themselves (they gave the sammuti to each other). Thereafter, they informed the bhikkhunīs that they have now obtained the bhikkhunovādaka convention, and that bhikkhunīs can come to them once again for advice. When bhikkhunīs approached them, they continued to discuss unnecessary matters more than discussing the Dhamma, just as before. The Tathāgata, having got to know about this matter, preached eight qualities a bhikkhu should have in order to be able to obtain the bhikkhunovādaka sammuti.
