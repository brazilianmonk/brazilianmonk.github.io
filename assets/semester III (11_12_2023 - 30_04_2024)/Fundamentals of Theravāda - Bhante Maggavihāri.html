        • pasādarūpa
          • what are pasādarūpa?
            • matters that are dependent on the great primaries born of kamma, and that are sensitive to and instrumental in experiencing gocararūpas pasāda denotes the clarity of a surface, like the surface of a mirror
            • note:
              • daṭṭhu-kāmatā-nidāna-kammajabhūta-ppasāda-lakkhaṇaṁ cakkhuṁ the eye has the characteristic of sensitivity depending on great elements produced by the kamma which was affected by the craving to see
              • lahutā, mudutā and kammaññatā are attributes of the mahābhūtā but pasāda depends on mahābhūtā
          • 5 pasādarūpas (sensitive matter)
            • cakkhuppasāda (visual- / eye- sensitivity): the sensitivity to visible data present in the center of the pupil that is instrumental in and enables the perception of visible data
              • location and size
                • location: in the center of the pupil of the eye
                • size: approximately the size of a louse's head or, according to another ancient teacher, about the size of half a green gram it is spread across seven layers in the eye, akin to oil permeating cotton wool
                • modern perspective: the light goes through the pupil and lens and forms at the back of the retina where the optic nerves are located
              • does cakkhuppasāda see?
                • what is called seeing is done by cakkhuviññāṇa, not cakkhuppasāda - when a visible object impinges on the cakkhuppasāda, cakkhuviññāṇa arises seeing the object - the same applies to the other sensitivities
              • is it separate from the mahābhūta?
                • no, rather, it relies on the four great elements, and thus, it is known as the sensitivity of the great elements "catunnaṃ mahābhūtānaṃ pasādo" - the same applies to the other sensitivities
            • sotappasāda (auditory sensitivity): the sensitivity to sounds present within the ear shaped like a ring with fine copper-colored hair that is instrumental in and enables the perception of sounds
            • ghānappasāda (olfactory sensitivity): the sensitivity to odors present within the nose and shaped like a goat's hoof that is instrumental in and enables the perception of odors
            • jivhāppasāda (gustatory sensitivity): the sensitivity to flavors present on an area of the tonge that resembles the edge of a water lily petal that is instrumental in and enables the perception of flavors
            • kāyappasāda (tactile sensitivity): the sensitivity to tangibles present throughout the body (exc. hair, nails, dry skin...) that is instrumental in and enables the perception of tangibles

                • Dukkha dukkha
                  • Pain occurring in various places of the body due to various reasons and displeasure (pain) arising in the mind due to various reasons are called dukkha dukkha. Many such suffering occur during one human lifetime. There would be none of these sufferings if the five human aggregates were not present. Therefore, it is correct to say that all these mental and physical pain is produced by human aggregates.
                • Saṅkhāra dukkha
                  • Saṅkhāra dukkha is the suffering that arises while producing something for the first time and while maintaining it. There should be a wholesome deed done in the past to be born as a human being. It is impossible to obtain the five aggregates of a human without a wholesome deed. Therefore, one who desires to attain the five human aggregates must perform wholesome deeds. Having to perform wholesome acts is one of the sufferings connected with the five human aggregates. Even if wholesome acts are performed, human aggregates will not be acquired if many unwholesome acts are done. Therefore, one must avoid unwholesome acts in addition to performing good deeds. Avoiding unwholesome acts is also not an easy task. Inhibiting oneself from doing akusala is also a suffering involved in acquiring the five human aggregates. The human body is not automatically protected after birth in the human world. One has to exert a lot of effort in supplying food and drinks for its maintenance and in protecting the body. Such effort is the suffering in the maintenance of human life after birth. All these are the saṅkhāra dukkha produced by human aggregates.
                • Vipariṇāma dukkha
                  • Vipariṇāma dukkha is the suffering that arises when arisen realities decay and break up (vanish) or because of the signs of decay and death vipariṇāmanimitta. However much the five human aggregates are fed, they continue to decay and change. They can get destroyed by death due to not receiving sufficient food or other requisites or due to some other danger. If not, they will surely get destroyed one day due to the expiring of the lifespan. The death of human aggregates cannot be prevented by any means or power. When illnesses and the like, which can kill the five aggregates, are seen, one has to make great effort to protect the human aggregates from such danger. When signs of destruction appear, fear (bhaya) and sadness (soka) arise. All these are the vipariṇāma dukkha related to human aggregates.

              • These eight factors in a lokuttara magga citta are collectively called the supramundane path (lokuttara magga). It is also called the “noble eightfold path”.
              • The only path to the cessation of suffering is the removal of craving because suffering keeps on arising as long as craving is present. The stated supramundane path arises destroying craving. The only thing which has the power to destroy craving is the aforesaid noble eight-fold path. Therefore, it is called the noble truth of the way to (leading to) the cessation of suffering. (It should be noted that, in this lesson of noble truths what was referred to as the five aggregates are only mundane realities.)
